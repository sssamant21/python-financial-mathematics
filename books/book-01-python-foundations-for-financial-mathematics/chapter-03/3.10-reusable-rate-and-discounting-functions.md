## 3.10.1 Purpose

Section 3.9 implemented single-amount present and future values. This section separates the supporting rate conventions and dimensionless factors into reusable functions.

Reusability depends on clear meaning. A nominal annual rate is not an effective annual rate, and an annual effective rate is not automatically a monthly effective rate. A discount factor is a multiplier, not a monetary amount or a quoted rate.

This section implements nominal annual to effective periodic conversion, periodic to effective annual conversion, accumulation factors, and discount factors. General function composition follows in Section 3.11.

## 3.10.2 Learning Objectives

You should be able to:

1. Distinguish a quoted rate, a growth factor, and a discount factor.
2. Convert a nominal annual rate to its effective rate per compounding period.
3. Calculate an effective annual rate from equal periodic rates.
4. Build reusable factor functions with explicit domains.
5. Validate known values and factor relationships.
6. Explain numerical and convention limits before reusing a helper.

## 3.10.3 Rates and Factors Have Different Meanings

| Quantity | Expression | Interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| Effective periodic rate | r | Fractional change over one modeled period |
| One-period growth factor | 1 + r | Multiplier from beginning to end |
| n-period accumulation factor | (1 + r)^n | Multiplier from period 0 to n |
| n-period discount factor | 1 / (1 + r)^n | Multiplier from period n to 0 |

Factors are dimensionless. Multiplying an amount by a factor preserves its currency and amount scale.

For 5% per period and two periods, the accumulation factor is 1.1025. The discount factor is approximately 0.90702948. The periodic rate itself is 0.05.

## 3.10.4 Nominal Annual Rate to Periodic Rate

Let j be a nominal annual rate convertible m times per year. Under that quotation convention:

periodic_rate = j / m.

For a nominal annual rate of 12% compounded monthly, m = 12 and the monthly effective rate is 1%.

This division is justified by the definition of that nominal quotation. Dividing an effective annual rate by 12 does not produce its exact equivalent effective monthly rate.

## 3.10.5 Periodic Rate to Effective Annual Rate

If the same effective rate r applies over m equal compounding periods per year:

effective_annual_rate = (1 + r)^m - 1.

A monthly rate of 1% therefore produces an effective annual rate of:

1.01^12 - 1 = 0.126825030131969720661201.

That is approximately 12.682503%, rather than 12%.

The relationship follows by applying the one-period growth factor m times, then subtracting the original unit amount.

## 3.10.6 What Is Deliberately Outside This Interface?

Converting an effective annual rate R to an equivalent periodic rate requires solving (1 + r)^m = 1 + R, giving r = (1 + R)^(1/m) - 1. A fractional-power implementation needs a deliberate numerical policy and is not included in this lab.

Continuous compounding, day-count fractions, irregular periods, APR disclosures, fees, and jurisdiction-specific quotation rules also require additional contracts.

These helpers encode the exact conventions named in their interfaces. They are not general rate-conversion or borrowing-cost engines.

## 3.10.7 Discount Factors and Negative Rates

A discount factor converts a future amount into its period-0 equivalent under the supplied constant effective periodic rate.

For zero periods, the factor is one. For zero rate, it is also one. With a positive rate and positive horizon it is below one; with a supported negative rate it is above one.

For example, at -10% for one period, a future amount of 900 discounts to 1000 because the discount factor is 1/0.9.

No sign reversal is performed. These factors can mathematically multiply signed amounts, but any public monetary interface must still define its sign and domain policy.

## 3.10.8 Supported Domain and Numerical Policy

The lab uses:

- Decimal rate inputs, finite and consistent with the named convention.
- Effective periodic rates greater than -1.
- Integer compounding frequencies from 1 through 366, excluding bool.
- Integer valuation horizons from 0 through 1000, excluding bool.
- The active Decimal context, without explicit currency quantization.

The frequency and horizon caps are teaching-model operating limits.

For nominal rates, the implied periodic rate must exceed -1. Consequently, a nominal annual rate below -100% is not automatically rejected when its per-period rate still satisfies this domain. Whether such a quotation is meaningful for a particular product is a separate suitability question.

Checks reject non-finite or nonpositive factors if arithmetic returns them without trapping. Decimal arithmetic exceptions can propagate. Positive finite results can still be inaccurate under insufficient precision; underflow, cancellation, and rounding need a context policy.

In particular, a tiny nonzero nominal rate may round to zero during division, and subtracting one from a rounded annual growth factor can lose a tiny effective rate. Finiteness checks cannot establish sufficient accuracy.

## 3.10.9 Hands-On Lab — Rate and Factor Helpers

Save this complete module as rate_discounting_lab.py. It requires Python 3.10 or later and the standard library.

```python
"""Explicit nominal-rate conversion and constant-rate factor helpers."""

from decimal import Decimal, localcontext
import doctest


def _finite_rate(rate: Decimal) -> None:
    """Require a finite Decimal rate representation."""
    if not isinstance(rate, Decimal):
        raise TypeError("rate must be Decimal")
    if not rate.is_finite():
        raise ValueError("rate must be finite")


def _frequency(periods_per_year: int) -> None:
    """Require an integer frequency from 1 through 366, excluding bool."""
    if not isinstance(periods_per_year, int) or isinstance(periods_per_year, bool):
        raise TypeError("periods_per_year must be an integer excluding bool")
    if not 1 <= periods_per_year <= 366:
        raise ValueError("periods_per_year must be between 1 and 366")


def periodic_rate_from_nominal(
    nominal_annual_rate: Decimal,
    *,
    periods_per_year: int,
) -> Decimal:
    """Return j/m for a nominal annual rate convertible m times yearly.

    Require a finite Decimal quotation and integer frequency 1..366,
    excluding bool. The implied effective periodic rate must exceed -1.
    Use the active Decimal context; perform no fee or APR adjustment.
    Raise TypeError for representation errors, ValueError for domain
    errors, and ArithmeticError for an unusable computed rate.
    Decimal arithmetic exceptions may also propagate.

    >>> periodic_rate_from_nominal(Decimal("0.12"), periods_per_year=12) == Decimal("0.01")
    True
    """
    _finite_rate(nominal_annual_rate)
    _frequency(periods_per_year)
    if nominal_annual_rate <= -Decimal(periods_per_year):
        raise ValueError("implied periodic rate must be greater than -1")
    rate = nominal_annual_rate / Decimal(periods_per_year)
    if not rate.is_finite() or rate <= Decimal("-1"):
        raise ArithmeticError("computed periodic rate is unusable")
    return rate


def accumulation_factor(
    periodic_rate: Decimal,
    *,
    periods: int,
) -> Decimal:
    """Return (1+r)**n for a constant effective periodic rate.

    Require finite Decimal r > -1 and integer n in 0..1000, not bool.
    Return a dimensionless finite positive Decimal. Validate even
    when n is zero. Use the active context with no quantization.
    Raise TypeError or ValueError for unsupported inputs; propagate
    Decimal arithmetic exceptions and reject unusable computed factors.

    >>> accumulation_factor(Decimal("0.05"), periods=2) == Decimal("1.1025")
    True
    >>> accumulation_factor(Decimal("0.05"), periods=0) == Decimal("1")
    True
    """
    _finite_rate(periodic_rate)
    if periodic_rate <= Decimal("-1"):
        raise ValueError("periodic_rate must be greater than -1")
    if not isinstance(periods, int) or isinstance(periods, bool):
        raise TypeError("periods must be an integer excluding bool")
    if not 0 <= periods <= 1000:
        raise ValueError("periods must be between 0 and 1000")
    factor = (Decimal("1") + periodic_rate) ** periods
    if not factor.is_finite() or factor <= 0:
        raise ArithmeticError("accumulation factor must be finite and positive")
    return factor


def discount_factor(
    periodic_rate: Decimal,
    *,
    periods: int,
) -> Decimal:
    """Return the reciprocal accumulation factor from period n to 0.

    Input domains match accumulation_factor. Return a dimensionless
    finite positive Decimal using the active context, without currency
    quantization. Inherit input failures and arithmetic exceptions.
    Raise ArithmeticError if the computed reciprocal is unusable.

    >>> discount_factor(Decimal("0"), periods=10) == Decimal("1")
    True
    >>> discount_factor(Decimal("0.25"), periods=1) == Decimal("0.8")
    True
    """
    factor = Decimal("1") / accumulation_factor(periodic_rate, periods=periods)
    if not factor.is_finite() or factor <= 0:
        raise ArithmeticError("discount factor must be finite and positive")
    return factor


def effective_annual_rate(
    periodic_rate: Decimal,
    *,
    periods_per_year: int,
) -> Decimal:
    """Return (1+r)**m - 1 for m equal compounding periods per year.

    Require finite Decimal r > -1 and integer frequency 1..366, not bool.
    Return a Decimal effective annual rate, not a percentage display.
    Apply the active context; inherit validation and arithmetic failures.
    Reject a non-finite annual rate or a rounded rate at or below -1.

    >>> effective_annual_rate(Decimal("0.01"), periods_per_year=12) == Decimal("0.126825030131969720661201")
    True
    """
    _frequency(periods_per_year)
    factor = accumulation_factor(periodic_rate, periods=periods_per_year)
    rate = factor - Decimal("1")
    if not rate.is_finite() or rate <= Decimal("-1"):
        raise ArithmeticError("effective annual rate is unusable")
    return rate


def run_checks() -> None:
    """Verify independent known values, boundaries, and factor identities."""
    monthly = periodic_rate_from_nominal(
        Decimal("0.12"), periods_per_year=12
    )
    assert monthly == Decimal("0.01")
    annual = effective_annual_rate(monthly, periods_per_year=12)
    assert annual == Decimal("0.126825030131969720661201")
    assert effective_annual_rate(Decimal("0"), periods_per_year=12) == 0
    assert periodic_rate_from_nominal(
        Decimal("0.05"), periods_per_year=1
    ) == Decimal("0.05")

    assert accumulation_factor(Decimal("0.05"), periods=2) == Decimal("1.1025")
    assert discount_factor(Decimal("0.25"), periods=1) == Decimal("0.8")
    assert Decimal("125") * discount_factor(
        Decimal("0.25"), periods=1
    ) == Decimal("100")

    for factor_function in (accumulation_factor, discount_factor):
        assert factor_function(Decimal("0.05"), periods=0) == Decimal("1")
        assert factor_function(Decimal("0"), periods=10) == Decimal("1")
        try:
            factor_function(Decimal("-1"), periods=0)
        except ValueError:
            pass
        else:
            raise AssertionError("Expected rate rejection before zero horizon")

    assert accumulation_factor(Decimal("-0.10"), periods=1) == Decimal("0.9")
    assert discount_factor(Decimal("-0.10"), periods=1) > 1
    assert discount_factor(Decimal("0.05"), periods=2) < 1

    with localcontext() as context:
        context.prec = 40
        growth = accumulation_factor(Decimal("0.037"), periods=17)
        discount = discount_factor(Decimal("0.037"), periods=17)
        assert abs(growth * discount - 1) <= Decimal("1e-35")

    try:
        periodic_rate_from_nominal(Decimal("0.12"), periods_per_year=True)
    except TypeError:
        pass
    else:
        raise AssertionError("Expected bool frequency rejection")

    try:
        periodic_rate_from_nominal(Decimal("0.12"), periods_per_year=0)
    except ValueError:
        pass
    else:
        raise AssertionError("Expected zero frequency rejection")

    try:
        periodic_rate_from_nominal(Decimal("-12"), periods_per_year=12)
    except ValueError:
        pass
    else:
        raise AssertionError("Expected implied rate rejection")

    try:
        accumulation_factor(Decimal("NaN"), periods=2)
    except ValueError:
        pass
    else:
        raise AssertionError("Expected non-finite rate rejection")

    try:
        discount_factor(Decimal("0.05"), periods=-1)
    except ValueError:
        pass
    else:
        raise AssertionError("Expected negative horizon rejection")


if __name__ == "__main__":
    results = doctest.testmod()
    if results.failed:
        raise SystemExit(1)
    run_checks()
    monthly = periodic_rate_from_nominal(
        Decimal("0.12"), periods_per_year=12
    )
    annual = effective_annual_rate(monthly, periods_per_year=12)
    discount = discount_factor(Decimal("0.05"), periods=2)
    print(f"Monthly effective rate: {monthly:.2%}")
    print(f"Effective annual rate: {annual:.6%}")
    print(f"Two-period discount factor: {discount:.8f}")
    print(f"PASS: {results.attempted} doctests and rate/factor checks")
```

## 3.10.10 Reusing Factors in Valuation

After saving the lab in the same directory, a caller can use:

```python
from decimal import Decimal
from rate_discounting_lab import accumulation_factor, discount_factor

growth = accumulation_factor(Decimal("0.05"), periods=2)
discount = discount_factor(Decimal("0.05"), periods=2)

future_amount = Decimal("1000") * growth
present_amount = Decimal("1102.50") * discount

assert future_amount == Decimal("1102.50")
assert abs(present_amount - Decimal("1000")) <= Decimal("1e-20")
```

The tolerance acknowledges that the separately computed reciprocal may round. Dividing directly by the growth factor and multiplying by an already rounded reciprocal can differ at finite precision.

This short example assumes valid monetary amounts and does not provide the full amount validation of Section 3.9. Keep such validation in public monetary functions.

## 3.10.11 Avoid Convention-Ambiguous Helpers

Names such as convert_rate(rate) and annualize(rate) hide necessary choices.

Prefer names and parameters that identify the quotation, compounding frequency, and target convention. Do not overload a helper so that an omitted flag changes a nominal quotation into an effective rate.

Rates from a dataset should be accompanied by metadata describing units and conventions before conversion. Neither a Decimal object nor a 0.12 value establishes that metadata.

## 3.10.12 Validation Beyond Reciprocal Checks

An accumulation factor multiplied by its reciprocal should be approximately one within a suitable tolerance. That relationship alone cannot prove the rate convention is correct.

The lab also checks independently calculable values: a nominal 12% rate produces a 1% monthly rate, and a one-period 25% rate produces a discount factor of 0.8.

For a positive amount and a fixed positive horizon, increasing the positive rate should reduce the discount factor. Verify such directional expectations alongside numeric examples.

A static type check can support interface review, but it cannot determine whether a rate quote was interpreted correctly.

## 3.10.13 Scope and Reproducibility

The helpers do not read a global market rate, clock, file, or currency. Inputs make rate and period choices explicit.

They use the active Decimal context and may set its signal flags. They do not change its precision or trap settings. Use localcontext for controlled experiments and record the chosen precision and acceptance tolerance where required.

Avoid describing these helpers as independent of all external state: the numerical context remains an observable dependency.

## 3.10.15 Troubleshooting Guide

| Symptom | Likely cause | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Annual result differs from a quote | Nominal and effective conventions mixed | Identify the source convention |
| Monthly growth is far too large | Annual rate applied every month | Convert the specific quotation correctly |
| Discounted amount grows with a positive rate | Accumulation used instead of discounting | Apply the reciprocal factor |
| Frequency zero causes division problems | Validation omitted | Require a supported positive integer |
| Invalid rate passes at zero horizon | Special-case return precedes validation | Validate first |
| Factor is zero or non-finite | Context limits or extreme inputs | Reject unusable results and review context |
| Tiny converted rate disappears | Finite-precision rounding or cancellation | Review precision and acceptance policy |
| Reciprocal test passes but valuation is wrong | Shared convention mistake | Check independent financial examples |

## 3.10.16 Key Takeaways

Reusable rate functions should name their conventions. Reusable factors should state their direction and timeline.

Keep validation, numerical policy, and monetary interfaces explicit. Verify known values as well as reciprocal relationships, and record rate metadata before applying a conversion.
