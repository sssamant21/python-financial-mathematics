## Learning objectives

Distinguish an effective periodic rate from a nominal annual quote, convert between nominal and effective annual rates, compare compatible offers on a common basis, and use numerically careful Python conversions. State the frequency, units, and assumptions that make a rate meaningful.

## A quoted percentage needs a convention

Earlier sections required an effective rate for the same period counted in the exponent. A quote such as “6% annually, compounded monthly” introduces a different representation: a nominal annual rate together with a compounding frequency.

Let j be a nominal annual rate convertible m times per year. Under the mathematical convention used here, its periodic effective rate is

\[
i_p=\frac{j}{m}.
\]

There are m equal periods in one year, so the annual accumulation factor is (1+j/m)^m. Define the effective annual rate e by matching this factor:

\[
1+e=\left(1+\frac{j}{m}\right)^m,
\qquad
\boxed{e=\left(1+\frac{j}{m}\right)^m-1}.
\]

An effective rate describes proportional change over its named period after compounding within that period. A nominal annual quote scales the periodic rate by frequency; it is not generally the year's compounded proportional change. OpenStax's stated-versus-effective discussion explains the role of within-year compounding [1].

Assume constant rates, equal compounding intervals, retained interest, one currency, and no fees, taxes, interim flows, default, or posting-rounding adjustments. These equations convert a mathematical convention; they do not determine a product's complete contractual or legal disclosure terms.

## An original monthly example

For nominal annual j=.06 with m=12,

\[
i_p=.06/12=.005=0.5\%\text{ per month},
\]

\[
e=(1.005)^{12}-1\approx.0616778118644996.
\]

Thus **6% nominal annually, compounded monthly** corresponds to approximately **6.16778119% effective annually** under the stated assumptions. On 10,000 illustrative USD, one year's model balance is approximately 10,616.78.

| Rate description | Fractional input | Named period or frequency |
|---|---:|---|
| Nominal annual quote j | .06 | Convertible 12 times per year |
| Periodic effective rate i_p | .005 | One month |
| Effective annual rate e | .0616778118644996… | One year |

Multiplying .005 by 12 recovers the nominal quote .06. It does not recover the effective annual rate because it omits interest on retained interest. For positive j and m>1, the effective annual rate exceeds j. At m=1 the two annual representations agree; at zero rate they also agree. Negative rates require their own domain checks rather than a blanket positive-rate interpretation.

## Convert an effective annual rate back

Starting from a known effective annual rate e, solve the factor equation:

\[
i_p=(1+e)^{1/m}-1,
\]

\[
\boxed{j=m\left[(1+e)^{1/m}-1\right]}.
\]

For **6% effective annually**, the equivalent nominal annual quote convertible monthly is approximately **5.84106068%**, and its monthly rate is approximately .48675506%.

This differs from the preceding 6% nominal example. One starts with a nominal annual quote of .06; the other starts with an effective annual rate of .06. Equal printed numbers under different conventions do not imply equivalent growth.

Dividing .06 by 12 is appropriate when .06 is the nominal quote defined by j=mi_p. Dividing an effective annual .06 by 12 does not produce its equivalent monthly effective rate. Use the root formula for that conversion.

## Preserve valuation when changing representation

With a whole-year horizon y and equivalent annual rate e,

\[
P\left(1+\frac{j}{m}\right)^{my}=P(1+e)^y.
\]

For the nominal 6% monthly example, 10,000 accumulated for 36 months agrees with 10,000 accumulated for three years at the converted effective annual rate. The test verifies this with numerical tolerance. Discounting a payment through the corresponding factors also preserves value because both representations use the same accumulation factor.

Feeding the unconverted nominal .06 into an annual-effective valuation function instead computes a different scenario, P(1.06)^y. A mathematically valid function call can still express the wrong financial assumption if the quote convention was not converted first.

The annual factor equality alone does not establish partial-period accrual rules. If a contract defines daily accrual, irregular credit dates, or special settlement rules, use those terms explicitly rather than assuming equal monthly periods or arbitrary fractional powers.

## Compare on a common basis

Consider two synthetic fee-free one-year accumulation alternatives with otherwise identical assumptions:

| Alternative | Quoted rate | Effective annual rate | Year-end value of 10,000 USD |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| A | 6% nominal annual, monthly compounding | Approximately 6.16778119% | Approximately 10,616.78 |
| B | 6.15% effective annual | 6.15% | 10,615.00 |

A produces the slightly larger year-end amount in this narrowly defined comparison. Comparing 6% directly with 6.15% would give the wrong ranking because the quote conventions differ. Equivalent annual conversion creates a common mathematical basis; it does not remove differences in fees, payment restrictions, credit quality, taxes, or rate variability from a real offer.

## Clarify the meaning of nominal

Here, nominal means an annual quote constructed by multiplying a periodic rate by m. In inflation discussions, nominal instead means measured without adjusting for changes in purchasing power. These are different distinctions. An effective annual rate can itself be nominal in purchasing-power terminology.

Similarly, do not identify this fee-free mathematical j automatically with a regulated APR, or e automatically with every advertised yield disclosure. The code implements no disclosure rules, fee allocation, payment-schedule regulation, or jurisdiction-specific requirements. Read the actual convention attached to a quoted product before translating it into model inputs.

## Domains and numerical care

Require m to be a positive integer and 1+j/m>0. The reverse conversion requires e>−1. Rates in (−1,0) on an effective annual basis are supported as mathematical scenarios. Zero rates map to zero; rates at or beyond a zero annual accumulation boundary are outside this model.

Directly evaluating a tiny rate with `(1+j/m)**m - 1` can lose information when floating-point addition rounds 1+j/m to one. The lab evaluates the same expression through

\[
e=\operatorname{expm1}\left(m\operatorname{log1p}(j/m)\right),
\]

and reverses it with

\[
j=m\operatorname{expm1}\left(\operatorname{log1p}(e)/m\right).
\]

`log1p(x)` and `expm1(x)` are designed to improve accuracy for small arguments compared with naive subtraction/addition expressions [2]. Their use does not change the compounding convention and does not introduce continuous compounding; these are computational identities for the discrete formulas.

The functions accept finite real numeric rates and reject booleans. Frequency is a built-in integer limited to 1 through 1,000,000 as a teaching implementation bound, not a financial law. The functions reject selected overflow, complete underflow, and outputs rounded onto an unsupported domain boundary. Very small intermediate values or near-boundary rates can still have limited relative accuracy. These finite-precision checks do not provide arbitrary-range arithmetic or exact settlement rounding. Keep full returned precision until formatting the result.

```python
"""Fee-free nominal/effective conversion with equally spaced periods."""
import math
from numbers import Real
import unittest


def finite(value,name):
    if isinstance(value,bool) or not isinstance(value,Real):
        raise TypeError(f'{name} must be real, not bool')
    try:
        value=float(value)
    except OverflowError as exc:
        raise ValueError(f'{name} outside float range') from exc
    if not math.isfinite(value):
        raise ValueError(f'{name} must be finite')
    return value


def frequency(value):
    if isinstance(value,bool) or not isinstance(value,int):
        raise TypeError('frequency must be an integer, not bool')
    if value < 1 or value > 1000000:
        raise ValueError('supported frequency range is 1 through 1000000')
    return value


def checked(value,source):
    if not math.isfinite(value) or (source != 0 and value == 0):
        raise ValueError('conversion outside supported float range')
    return value


def nominal_to_effective(nominal,m):
    j=finite(nominal,'nominal'); m=frequency(m)
    periodic=j/m
    if periodic <= -1:
        raise ValueError('nominal divided by frequency must exceed -1')
    if j != 0 and periodic == 0:
        raise ValueError('periodic rate underflow')
    try:
        result=math.expm1(m*math.log1p(periodic))
    except OverflowError as exc:
        raise ValueError('effective rate overflow') from exc
    result=checked(result,j)
    if result <= -1:
        raise ValueError('effective rate rounded to unsupported boundary')
    return result


def effective_to_nominal(effective,m):
    e=finite(effective,'effective'); m=frequency(m)
    if e <= -1:
        raise ValueError('effective rate must exceed -1')
    result=checked(m*math.expm1(math.log1p(e)/m),e)
    if result/m <= -1:
        raise ValueError('periodic rate rounded to unsupported boundary')
    return result


class RateTests(unittest.TestCase):
    def test_01_monthly_example(self):
        self.assertAlmostEqual(nominal_to_effective(.06,12),.06167781186449957)

    def test_02_reverse_conversion(self):
        j=effective_to_nominal(.06,12)
        self.assertAlmostEqual(j,.0584106067841163)
        self.assertAlmostEqual(nominal_to_effective(j,12),.06)

    def test_03_round_trip_frequencies(self):
        for m in [1,2,4,12,365]:
            for e in [0,.01,.06,-.02]:
                self.assertTrue(math.isclose(nominal_to_effective(effective_to_nominal(e,m),m),e,
                                             rel_tol=1e-12,abs_tol=1e-15))

    def test_04_comparable_offer_ranking(self):
        a=nominal_to_effective(.06,12); b=.0615
        self.assertGreater(a,b)
        self.assertGreater(10000*(1+.06/12)**12,10000*(1+b))

    def test_05_equivalent_balances(self):
        e=nominal_to_effective(.06,12)
        monthly=10000*(1+.06/12)**36
        annual=10000*(1+e)**3
        self.assertAlmostEqual(monthly,annual,places=8)
        self.assertNotAlmostEqual(monthly,10000*1.06**3)

    def test_06_zero_annual_and_negative(self):
        self.assertEqual(nominal_to_effective(0,12),0)
        self.assertEqual(effective_to_nominal(0,12),0)
        self.assertAlmostEqual(nominal_to_effective(.06,1),.06)
        negative=nominal_to_effective(-.02,12)
        self.assertTrue(-1 < negative < 0)
        self.assertAlmostEqual(effective_to_nominal(negative,12),-.02)

    def test_07_invalid_inputs(self):
        for function in [nominal_to_effective,effective_to_nominal]:
            for bad in [True,'0.06',None]:
                with self.assertRaises(TypeError): function(bad,12)
            for bad in [float('nan'),float('inf')]:
                with self.assertRaises(ValueError): function(bad,12)
            for m in [True,12.0]:
                with self.assertRaises(TypeError): function(.06,m)
            for m in [0,-1,1000001]:
                with self.assertRaises(ValueError): function(.06,m)
        with self.assertRaises(ValueError): nominal_to_effective(-12,12)
        with self.assertRaises(ValueError): effective_to_nominal(-1,12)

    def test_08_small_rates_and_range(self):
        # Directly subtracting one from (1+j/m)**m loses this tiny input.
        e=nominal_to_effective(1e-16,12)
        self.assertGreater(e,0)
        self.assertTrue(math.isclose(e,1e-16,rel_tol=1e-12))
        with self.assertRaises(ValueError): nominal_to_effective(1e308,12)
        with self.assertRaises(ValueError): nominal_to_effective(-11.99,12)
        with self.assertRaises(ValueError): nominal_to_effective(5e-324,12)


if __name__=='__main__':
    print(f'6% nominal, monthly -> effective annual {nominal_to_effective(.06,12):.8%}')
    print(f'6% effective annual -> nominal, monthly {effective_to_nominal(.06,12):.8%}')
    unittest.main(verbosity=2)
```

```text
6% effective annual -> nominal, monthly 5.84106068%
```

## Practice with answers

1. A nominal annual rate is 8% convertible quarterly. Find its periodic rate and effective annual rate. **Answer:** 2% per quarter; (1.02)^4−1=.08243216, or 8.243216% annually.
2. An effective annual rate is 8%. Find an equivalent nominal quote convertible quarterly. **Answer:** 4[(1.08)^(1/4)−1], approximately 7.77061876% nominal annually.
3. At annual compounding, how do the nominal and effective annual rates relate? **Answer:** they are equal under this mathematical convention because m=1.
4. Does 6% effective annually imply .5% effective monthly? **Answer:** no. The equivalent monthly rate is (1.06)^(1/12)−1, approximately .48675506%.
5. Does converting a quote to an effective annual rate account for an upfront fee automatically? **Answer:** no. A fee changes the cash flows and needs a separate consistent valuation or cost calculation.
