## Learning objectives

Represent the timing, sign, and amount of cash flows from a declared viewpoint; distinguish time points from intervals; retain separate events when building period totals; and identify the information needed before a valuation calculation. Build and test a small Python timeline without confusing cash movements with balances or present values.

## Define the clock before placing the money

A financial timeline records when payments occur. Its time axis needs an origin and a unit. In this section, time 0 means the valuation origin immediately before any time-0 payment; one period means one year. Time 1 is one year after the origin, not the first row of an arbitrary input file.

There are n intervals but n+1 boundary points from time 0 through time n. For a three-year horizon, the boundary points are 0, 1, 2, and 3. The first year occupies the interval from 0 to 1. A payment at the end of that year belongs at 1.

| Interval | Beginning boundary | Ending boundary |
|---|---:|---:|
| First year | 0 | 1 |
| Second year | 1 | 2 |
| Third year | 2 | 3 |

A month-based model uses monthly points instead. Never label the axis in years and silently treat each integer as a month. Actual calendar dates need an explicit mapping to the chosen clock. Holidays, business-day adjustments, partial years, and contractual day-count rules are not inferred by this simple integer-period lab.

## Choose one viewpoint

We use positive amounts for receipts and negative amounts for payments, viewed by the investor whose cash movements are being modeled. The California Board of Equalization's timeline introduction describes this viewpoint-dependent convention [1]. A borrower receiving loan proceeds records an initial positive flow; the lender advancing those proceeds records a negative flow. Both descriptions can be correct.

The synthetic USD example below records an initial investment, two income receipts, one fee, and a final return of principal. No receipt is scheduled at time 2. The schedule is assumed complete for the declared horizon.

| Event ID | Time in years | Investor cash flow, USD | Description |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| initial | 0 | −1,000.00 | Initial investment |
| income1 | 1 | +80.00 | First receipt |
| fee1 | 1 | −5.00 | Fee |
| income3 | 3 | +80.00 | Final income receipt |
| principal3 | 3 | +1,000.00 | Principal returned |

These are original teaching inputs, not the terms or valuation of a quoted financial product. The example promises no annual coupon at time 2. Its explicit timing is the definition of the fixture.

Reversing the viewpoint of each identical transfer reverses its sign. This is a representation identity, not a claim that one counterparty necessarily receives every fee: the investor's fee might be paid to a third party. A real counterparty-specific ledger would need the identities and obligations for each transfer.

## Events and period totals serve different purposes

A chronological event ledger retains descriptions and identifiers. A period grid provides one net number per boundary. For the example, netting only events at the same point produces:

| Boundary time | Net investor cash flow, USD | Evidence |
|---|---:|---|
| 0 | −1,000.00 | One initial payment |
| 1 | +75.00 | Receipt 80 minus fee 5 |
| 2 | 0.00 | Confirmed no scheduled event |
| 3 | +1,080.00 | Income 80 plus principal 1,000 |

At time 1, a net receipt of 75 does not mean there was only one transaction. Keep both underlying events for audit and interpretation. Conversely, an incoming 20 and outgoing 20 can net to zero while still representing two events. A zero period total does not identify which of these situations occurred.

Netting assumes a common currency, viewpoint, and modeled time point. Payments at different times cannot be combined as if they occurred simultaneously without a valuation rule. Aggregating to annual boundaries also discards intrayear timing. Use finer timestamps if that distinction matters.

## Confirmed absence and unknown information

The grid can insert zero for an omitted period only under the explicit assumption that the event schedule is complete and omission means no event. An incomplete bank extract, an unconfirmed payment amount, or an unresolved date does not support that assumption.

The lab therefore rejects unknown or nonfinite amounts instead of replacing them with zero. It cannot detect an event that was never supplied. Its empty input means an explicitly empty complete schedule, not evidence that an external account had no activity. Completeness is an input contract supplied by the author, not something inferred from successful code execution.

The demonstration supports USD only and declares currency once for the entire schedule. It does not parse or convert mixed currencies, inspect exchange rates, or infer a record's denomination from its number. A production ingestion layer would need currency metadata per source record and checks before constructing this single-currency schedule.

## Beginning versus end of period

Three equal deposits made at the beginning of three annual intervals occur at times 0, 1, and 2. Three deposits at the end occur at 1, 2, and 3. Both schedules contain three payments, yet they differ in every payment date.

| Deposit number | Beginning schedule time | End schedule time |
|---|---:|---:|
| 1 | 0 | 1 |
| 2 | 1 | 2 |
| 3 | 2 | 3 |

Write the dates first rather than guessing them from a payment count. Later annuity sections use this distinction in valuation formulas. Here, the test checks locations only; it does not calculate annuity values.

## A timeline is not a balance or a valuation

The event at time 0 is a −1,000 investor cash movement. It is not automatically a negative account balance. Determining account balances also requires an opening balance, the definition of the account, and rules for interest, accrual, reinvestment, and other changes.

The signed sum of this schedule is −1,000 + 75 + 0 + 1,080 = 155 USD. Label it an **undiscounted signed cash-flow sum**. It is not a present value, an annual return, or a terminal account balance. The sum omits the time value of the differently dated amounts. Section 9.1 established common-date equivalence; Sections 9.8 and 9.9 will develop multiple-flow valuation and net present value.

`Event` is a frozen dataclass holding one event. Type annotations document the fields but do not validate incoming values; `timeline` performs those checks [2]. It returns a sorted tuple of events plus a dense period-total dictionary. Sorting by ID within a period is deterministic display order, not evidence of intraperiod transaction sequence. Duplicate IDs are rejected even if they carry different amounts; multiple distinct IDs at the same time are permitted.

The examples construct Decimal amounts from text to avoid importing binary floating-point approximations [3]. The function checks the resulting Decimal type, not the history of its construction. Decimal arithmetic still uses a finite precision context; the small supplied amounts are exact under the default context. This teaching implementation does not impose a settlement scale, specify tax rounding, guarantee exact totals for arbitrary large inputs or altered contexts, or support unbounded horizons efficiently.

```python
"""Event ledger and dense period grid; no discounting or balance inference."""
from dataclasses import dataclass
from decimal import Decimal
import unittest


@dataclass(frozen=True)
class Event:
    event_id: str
    period: int
    amount: Decimal
    description: str


def timeline(events, *, horizon, currency):
    """Return sorted events and period totals for one declared currency."""
    if isinstance(horizon, bool) or not isinstance(horizon, int):
        raise TypeError('horizon must be an integer, not bool')
    if horizon < 0:
        raise ValueError('horizon must be nonnegative')
    if currency != 'USD':
        raise ValueError('this teaching fixture supports USD only')
    checked = []
    identifiers = set()
    for event in events:
        if not isinstance(event, Event):
            raise TypeError('each record must be Event')
        if not isinstance(event.event_id, str) or not event.event_id.strip():
            raise ValueError('event_id must be nonblank text')
        if event.event_id in identifiers:
            raise ValueError('duplicate event_id')
        identifiers.add(event.event_id)
        if isinstance(event.period, bool) or not isinstance(event.period, int):
            raise TypeError('period must be an integer, not bool')
        if not 0 <= event.period <= horizon:
            raise ValueError('period outside declared horizon')
        if not isinstance(event.amount, Decimal):
            raise TypeError('amount must be Decimal constructed from text')
        if not event.amount.is_finite():
            raise ValueError('amount must be finite; unknown is not zero')
        if not isinstance(event.description, str) or not event.description.strip():
            raise ValueError('description must be nonblank text')
        checked.append(event)
    ordered = tuple(sorted(checked, key=lambda e: (e.period, e.event_id)))
    totals = {period: Decimal('0') for period in range(horizon + 1)}
    for event in ordered:
        totals[event.period] += event.amount
    return ordered, totals


def fixture():
    # Investor viewpoint. One period is one year; events occur at boundaries.
    return (
        Event('initial', 0, Decimal('-1000'), 'Initial investment'),
        Event('income1', 1, Decimal('80'), 'First receipt'),
        Event('fee1', 1, Decimal('-5'), 'Fee'),
        Event('income3', 3, Decimal('80'), 'Final income receipt'),
        Event('principal3', 3, Decimal('1000'), 'Principal returned'),
    )


class TimelineTests(unittest.TestCase):
    def test_01_expected_period_grid(self):
        ordered, totals = timeline(fixture(), horizon=3, currency='USD')
        self.assertEqual(list(totals), [0, 1, 2, 3])
        self.assertEqual(list(totals.values()), list(map(Decimal, ['-1000','75','0','1080'])))
        self.assertEqual(len(ordered), 5)

    def test_02_sorting_and_same_time_events(self):
        ordered, _ = timeline(reversed(fixture()), horizon=3, currency='USD')
        self.assertEqual([e.period for e in ordered], [0,1,1,3,3])
        self.assertEqual({e.event_id for e in ordered if e.period == 1}, {'fee1','income1'})

    def test_03_perspective_reversal(self):
        opposite = [Event(e.event_id,e.period,-e.amount,e.description) for e in fixture()]
        _, a = timeline(fixture(), horizon=3, currency='USD')
        _, b = timeline(opposite, horizon=3, currency='USD')
        self.assertEqual([a[t]+b[t] for t in a], [Decimal('0')]*4)

    def test_04_beginning_and_end_positions(self):
        beginning = [Event(f'b{k}',k,Decimal('-100'),'Deposit') for k in range(3)]
        end = [Event(f'e{k}',k+1,Decimal('-100'),'Deposit') for k in range(3)]
        a, _ = timeline(beginning, horizon=3, currency='USD')
        b, _ = timeline(end, horizon=3, currency='USD')
        self.assertEqual([e.period for e in a], [0,1,2])
        self.assertEqual([e.period for e in b], [1,2,3])

    def test_05_zero_net_preserves_gross_events(self):
        events = [Event('in',1,Decimal('20'),'Receipt'),Event('out',1,Decimal('-20'),'Payment')]
        ordered, totals = timeline(events, horizon=1, currency='USD')
        self.assertEqual(totals[1], 0)
        self.assertEqual(len(ordered), 2)
        empty, zeros = timeline([], horizon=1, currency='USD')
        self.assertEqual(empty, ())
        self.assertEqual(zeros, totals)

    def test_06_duplicate_and_unknown_rejection(self):
        with self.assertRaises(ValueError): timeline([fixture()[0]]*2,horizon=3,currency='USD')
        for bad in [Decimal('NaN'),Decimal('Infinity')]:
            with self.assertRaises(ValueError):
                timeline([Event('bad',1,bad,'Unknown')],horizon=3,currency='USD')
        with self.assertRaises(TypeError):
            timeline([Event('bad',1,None,'Unknown')],horizon=3,currency='USD')

    def test_07_period_currency_and_record_validation(self):
        for bad in [True,1.5]:
            with self.assertRaises(TypeError): timeline([],horizon=bad,currency='USD')
        with self.assertRaises(ValueError): timeline([],horizon=-1,currency='USD')
        with self.assertRaises(ValueError): timeline([],horizon=1,currency='EUR')
        for bad in [-1,4]:
            with self.assertRaises(ValueError):
                timeline([Event('bad',bad,Decimal('1'),'Bad time')],horizon=3,currency='USD')
        with self.assertRaises(TypeError):
            timeline([Event('bad',True,Decimal('1'),'Bad time')],horizon=3,currency='USD')
        with self.assertRaises(TypeError): timeline([{}],horizon=3,currency='USD')
        with self.assertRaises(ValueError):
            timeline([Event('',0,Decimal('1'),'No ID')],horizon=3,currency='USD')

    def test_08_decimal_totals_and_nominal_sum(self):
        small = [Event('a',0,Decimal('0.1'),'A'),Event('b',0,Decimal('0.2'),'B')]
        _, totals = timeline(small,horizon=0,currency='USD')
        self.assertEqual(totals[0], Decimal('0.3'))
        _, totals = timeline(fixture(),horizon=3,currency='USD')
        self.assertEqual(sum(totals.values(),Decimal('0')), Decimal('155'))
        # 155 is an undiscounted signed sum, not an NPV or an account balance.


if __name__ == '__main__':
    _, totals = timeline(fixture(),horizon=3,currency='USD')
    print('Investor cash flows; USD; annual boundary periods')
    for period, amount in totals.items():
        print(f't={period}: {amount:+.2f}')
    unittest.main(verbosity=2)
```

## Practice with answers

1. A borrower receives 2,000 now and pays 1,100 at the end of each of two years. Write the borrower timeline and reverse it for the lender. **Answer:** borrower {0: +2,000, 1: −1,100, 2: −1,100}; lender signs reversed. The borrower's undiscounted sum is −200, not an interest rate.
2. Move three investor deposits of 100 from year ends to year beginnings. **Answer:** times [1, 2, 3] become [0, 1, 2]; each investor amount remains −100.
3. At time 2, receive 50 and pay a fee of 50. How many events and what total? **Answer:** two events, net zero. Keep their separate IDs.
4. A supplied file contains no time-2 row, but its completeness is unconfirmed. May the model assert zero? **Answer:** no. Resolve completeness or retain an explicit unknown state in a richer model before valuation.
5. Why does the 155 USD signed sum not establish an investment return? **Answer:** it does not incorporate the timing and capital exposure needed for a rate calculation or the common-date discounting needed for valuation.

Before calculating, record the viewpoint, currency, origin, period unit, payment boundaries, event completeness, and any aggregation policy. Next: **9.3 — Simple Interest**.
