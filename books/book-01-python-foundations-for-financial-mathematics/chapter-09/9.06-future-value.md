## Learning objectives

Calculate the future value of a single amount, identify its start and target dates, compare alternatives at a common future date, distinguish an equivalent value from an actual cash flow, and implement checked forward valuation in Python.

## Specify the date of the answer

Future value expresses an amount at a later valuation date under a stated accumulation rule. Section 9.4 derived compound accumulation through changing account balances; this section uses that rule to translate one dated amount and compare alternatives.

For amount P available at time 0, constant effective rate i per period, and n matching whole periods,

\[
\boxed{FV_n=P(1+i)^n}.
\]

The California State Board of Equalization's future-worth lesson describes this single-amount factor and emphasizes matching the rate to the period unit [1]. The formula assumes retained interest. It does not include later deposits or withdrawals.

Our examples use synthetic amounts in one illustrative currency, constant rates, whole periods, and no fees, taxes, default, or interim external payments. The supplied rate defines a scenario; a computed future value is not evidence that the rate will be earned.

## An original worked example

Translate 7,500 USD today to the end of year 4 at an assumed 4.5% effective annual rate:

\[
FV_4=7500(1.045)^4
=7500(1.192518600625)
=8943.8895046875.
\]

The year-4 equivalent is **8,943.89 USD** when displayed to cents. Total model growth is 8,943.8895046875−7,500 = 1,443.8895046875 USD. The future value includes the original amount; growth is only the difference.

| Quantity | Value | Date or unit |
|---|---:|---|
| Starting amount | 7,500 USD | Time 0 |
| Effective rate | .045 | Per year |
| Elapsed term | 4 | Whole years |
| Accumulation factor | 1.192518600625 | Dimensionless |
| Future equivalent | 8,943.8895046875 USD | End of year 4 |

Carry unrounded values through calculations. Formatting a result to cents does not specify a contractual rule for rounding annual interest credits. A contract that rounds each credit separately may produce a different balance.

## Count elapsed periods, not the target label

An amount can begin at time s rather than time 0. With the same effective rate in each intervening period, its equivalent at time T≥s is

\[
V_T=P_s(1+i)^{T-s}.
\]

Suppose 2,000 becomes available at year 2 and the target date is year 5. At 5% effective annually there are **three** accumulation periods:

\[
V_5=2000(1.05)^{5-2}=2315.25.
\]

Using exponent 5 would give 2,552.563125 and incorrectly grant two years of accumulation before the amount becomes available. Timeline labels are coordinates; the exponent counts the intervening intervals.

The Python wrapper `value_at` takes explicit `start` and `target` integers to make this distinction visible. It rejects a target before the start because that would be backward valuation, which Section 9.5 handles through present value. These integer coordinates are period counts, not parsed calendar dates.

## Compare alternatives at a common future date

Consider receiving A=7,500 at time 0 or B=8,000 at year 2. Compare both at year 4, assuming the same 4.5% effective annual accumulation is available from each receipt date until the target.

| Alternative | Receipt time | Accumulation periods to year 4 | Equivalent at year 4, USD |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| A: 7,500 | 0 | 4 | 8,943.8895046875 |
| B: 8,000 | 2 | 2 | 8,736.2000000000 |

A exceeds B by approximately 207.689505 USD at year 4 under these assumptions. Comparing their original printed amounts alone would omit timing. Do not accumulate B for four periods merely because A was accumulated for four; the **target date** is shared, while their elapsed terms differ.

Discounting both year-4 equivalents to the same earlier date at the same rate preserves the ranking because both are divided by the same positive factor. This is a consistency property of the model. It does not settle practical differences in uncertainty, liquidity, taxes, currency, or reinvestment opportunities.

The assumed 4.5% is constant over all intervening periods. Applying one quoted rate to both starting dates is a modeling choice, not an observed forward-rate agreement or a market forecast. Changing-rate models require an explicit sequence of applicable factors.

## Signs and actual transactions

This section's helper translates a signed amount without reversing its sign. A positive receipt has a positive forward equivalent; a negative obligation has a negative forward equivalent. The accumulation factor remains positive for i>−1.

If an investor pays 7,500 into an account now and receives its balance at maturity, the actual timeline records −7,500 now and +8,943.8895046875 at year 4 in this scenario. Passing the initial **payment sign** −7,500 to the helper returns a negative equivalent of that outflow, not an automatically inferred positive maturity receipt. The function translates value; the author constructs the transaction timeline.

An equivalent amount at year 4 is not an additional payment to append to an existing future receipt. It is a representation at another date. Likewise, retained interest is already included in an accumulated account balance; recording it again as external income would double count it.

## Boundaries and rate conventions

For a fixed positive starting amount and n>0, raising i within i>−1 raises future value. For positive i, increasing the whole-period horizon raises future value. At zero rate or zero elapsed periods, the equivalent equals the starting amount. With −1<i<0, a positive amount shrinks as the horizon increases; for example, 1,000 becomes 960.4 after two periods at −2%.

For a negative signed amount, multiplying by a larger positive accumulation factor makes the equivalent more negative. State sign assumptions when describing growth or monotonicity.

The rate must be effective for the same period counted in n. An annual effective rate should not be divided by 12 and treated automatically as its equivalent monthly effective rate. Nominal quotes, compounding frequency, and continuous compounding receive dedicated treatment later in this chapter. The introductory helper does not infer fractional-period rules, contractual day counts, currency conversion, inflation, or cash-flow additions.

A nominal future amount and its purchasing power are different quantities. To discuss purchasing power, add an explicit inflation assumption and label the price-date units. The growth formula by itself calculates no inflation adjustment.

## Standalone Python lab

Save the complete listing as `future_value.py` and run:

```bash

```

On Windows, `py -3.12` may replace `python`. No third-party packages are required. Publication checks used Python 3.12.14 on Linux; Windows execution was not performed.

The standalone function accepts finite real signed amounts and rates greater than −1, rejecting booleans. Periods and date coordinates must be nonnegative integers. The wrapper uses `target-start` as the term. Zero amounts, zero rates, and zero terms return the supplied amount after validation.

This is a finite-precision float implementation. It rejects unsupported accumulation overflow, total underflow, and nonfinite or zero output for a nonzero input. Calculating the factor first can reject extreme cases that another numerical arrangement might support. Very small rates may disappear in 1+i, and nonzero subnormal results can have reduced relative precision. The function does not supply arbitrary-range arithmetic, monetary settlement rounding, or proof of an appropriate financial rate.

```python
"""Forward equivalence of one signed amount under a fixed periodic rate."""
import math
from numbers import Real
import unittest


def real(value, name):
    if isinstance(value, bool) or not isinstance(value, Real):
        raise TypeError(f'{name} must be real, not bool')
    try:
        result=float(value)
    except OverflowError as exc:
        raise ValueError(f'{name} outside float range') from exc
    if not math.isfinite(result):
        raise ValueError(f'{name} must be finite')
    return result


def period(value, name):
    if isinstance(value,bool) or not isinstance(value,int):
        raise TypeError(f'{name} must be an integer, not bool')
    if value < 0:
        raise ValueError(f'{name} must be nonnegative')
    return value


def future_value(amount, rate, periods):
    p=real(amount,'amount'); r=real(rate,'rate'); n=period(periods,'periods')
    if r <= -1:
        raise ValueError('rate must exceed -1')
    if p == 0 or r == 0 or n == 0:
        return p
    try:
        factor=(1+r)**n
        result=p*factor
    except OverflowError as exc:
        raise ValueError('accumulation outside supported float range') from exc
    if factor <= 0 or not math.isfinite(result) or result == 0:
        raise ValueError('future value outside supported float range')
    return result


def value_at(amount, rate, *, start, target):
    start=period(start,'start'); target=period(target,'target')
    if target < start:
        raise ValueError('target must be at or after start')
    return future_value(amount,rate,target-start)


class FutureValueTests(unittest.TestCase):
    def test_01_worked_amount(self):
        self.assertAlmostEqual(future_value(7500,.045,4),8943.8895046875)

    def test_02_target_elapsed_time(self):
        self.assertAlmostEqual(value_at(2000,.05,start=2,target=5),2315.25)
        self.assertNotAlmostEqual(value_at(2000,.05,start=2,target=5),future_value(2000,.05,5))

    def test_03_common_target_comparison(self):
        now=value_at(7500,.045,start=0,target=4)
        later=value_at(8000,.045,start=2,target=4)
        self.assertAlmostEqual(later,8736.2)
        self.assertGreater(now,later)
        self.assertAlmostEqual(now-later,207.6895046875)

    def test_04_inverse_and_composition(self):
        for rate in [.045,0,-.02]:
            result=future_value(7500,rate,4)
            self.assertAlmostEqual(result/(1+rate)**4,7500)
        staged=future_value(future_value(7500,.045,1),.045,3)
        self.assertAlmostEqual(staged,future_value(7500,.045,4))

    def test_05_zero_and_signed_amounts(self):
        self.assertEqual(future_value(7500,.045,0),7500)
        self.assertEqual(future_value(7500,0,4),7500)
        self.assertEqual(future_value(0,.045,4),0)
        self.assertAlmostEqual(future_value(-7500,.045,4),-future_value(7500,.045,4))

    def test_06_ordering_and_negative_rate(self):
        self.assertGreater(future_value(7500,.06,4),future_value(7500,.03,4))
        self.assertGreater(future_value(7500,.045,5),future_value(7500,.045,4))
        self.assertAlmostEqual(future_value(1000,-.02,2),960.4)

    def test_07_rejected_inputs(self):
        for args in [(True,.045,4),(7500,'0.045',4),(7500,.045,True),(7500,.045,4.5)]:
            with self.assertRaises(TypeError): future_value(*args)
        for args in [(float('nan'),.045,4),(7500,float('inf'),4),(7500,-1,4),(7500,.045,-1)]:
            with self.assertRaises(ValueError): future_value(*args)
        with self.assertRaises(ValueError): value_at(2000,.05,start=5,target=2)
        with self.assertRaises(TypeError): value_at(2000,.05,start=True,target=5)

    def test_08_range_and_display(self):
        for args in [(1,1,2000),(1,-.5,2000),(1e308,1,2),(5e-324,-.5,1),(10**1000,.05,1)]:
            with self.assertRaises(ValueError): future_value(*args)
        self.assertEqual(f'{future_value(7500,.045,4):.2f}','8943.89')


if __name__=='__main__':
    print(f'7500 today at 4.5% effective annually: year-4 FV = {future_value(7500,.045,4):.2f}')
    unittest.main(verbosity=2)
```

```text

```
