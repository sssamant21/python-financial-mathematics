## Learning objectives

Derive an unknown periodic, effective annual, nominal annual, or continuously compounded rate from observed present and future balances; identify the required assumptions; distinguish a unique single-payment rate from multi-cash-flow IRR; and implement and test stable Python solutions.

## The inverse time-value-of-money problem

Suppose a nonzero present balance \(PV\) becomes a nonzero future balance \(FV\) after \(n\) complete compounding periods, with a constant periodic rate \(i\) and no intervening cash flows:

\[
FV=PV(1+i)^n.
\]

Dividing by \(PV\), taking the positive \(n\)-th root, and subtracting one gives

\[
\boxed{i=\left(\frac{FV}{PV}\right)^{1/n}-1}.
\]

The real-valued growth model requires \(FV/PV>0\), \(n>0\), and \(i>-1\). In this section, the balances must be nonzero and have the same sign; a negative-to-negative signed balance is mathematically supported, but interpretation as an investment return requires care. Zero or negative time cannot identify a unique rate from this formula.

**Crucial distinction:** The result is a rate **per compounding period**, not automatically an annual rate.

## Worked example: rate per period

An illustrative balance increases from 8,000 USD to 9,680 USD over two complete annual periods. Then

\[
i=(9680/8000)^{1/2}-1=\sqrt{1.21}-1=0.10.
\]

The annual effective rate is **10%**. The same balances over two **quarters** instead imply 10% **per quarter**, corresponding to an effective annual rate of \((1.10)^4-1=46.41\%\), under a constant quarterly reinvestment model. Always identify the period before annualizing.

## Fractional years and effective annual rates

If \(t>0\) is measured in years and the model uses an effective annual rate \(e\),

\[
FV=PV(1+e)^t,\qquad
\boxed{e=(FV/PV)^{1/t}-1}.
\]

A fractional exponent assumes a particular smooth interpolation of annual effective growth. It does **not** establish that a real contract accrues interest fractionally in that way. The time coordinate must use a stated day-count basis.

For an illustrative 1,000 USD growing to 1,210 USD in two years,

\[
e=(1.21)^{1/2}-1=10\%.
\]

If \(FV=PV\), the implied rate is zero for every positive horizon. If \(0<FV/PV<1\), the implied effective rate is negative but greater than \(-100\%\).

## Solving for a continuously compounded rate

For \(FV=PV\,e^{r_ct}\),

\[
\boxed{r_c=\frac{\ln(FV/PV)}{t}}.
\]

For the two-year 1,000-to-1,210 example, \(r_c=\ln(1.21)/2=\ln(1.10)\approx9.53101798\%\) per year. This differs from the 10% effective annual rate but describes the same modeled annual growth.

## Solving for a nominal annual rate

For a nominal annual rate \(j\) convertible \(m\) times a year,

\[
FV=PV\left(1+\frac{j}{m}\right)^{mt}.
\]

Assuming \(m\) is a positive integer, \(t>0\) years, and the specified equal-period model applies,

\[
\boxed{j=m\left[(FV/PV)^{1/(mt)}-1\right]}.
\]

A 6% nominal annual rate convertible monthly implies a 0.5% monthly rate. Given 24 complete months of growth, the inverse formula should recover 6% nominal annually; it should not be labeled 6% effective annually.

## Logarithms and numerical precision

Computing `ratio ** (1/n) - 1` may lose precision when the rate is very small. A more stable floating-point expression is

\[
i=\operatorname{expm1}\left(\frac{\ln(FV/PV)}{n}\right).
\]

Python provides `math.expm1` to evaluate \(e^x-1\) accurately for small \(x\). The teaching implementation checks that the ratio is positive and representable. Very large or tiny ratios can overflow or underflow in ordinary binary floats even when the exact mathematical ratio exists; a production implementation may instead work with logarithms of the absolute balances, using a carefully defined numeric policy.

**Rounding policy:** Do not round an inferred rate before verifying it against the original balances. Quote percentages only at presentation time and disclose the compounding basis.

## What the implied rate does—and does not—mean

The rate inferred from a single beginning balance, ending balance, and elapsed time is a **constant-rate equivalent** under a specified growth convention. It is not automatically an internal rate of return, realized time-weighted return, APR, APY, inflation-adjusted return, or forward-looking forecast. Deposits, withdrawals, dividends, fees, and changing market prices require an appropriate cash-flow or return model. A multi-cash-flow IRR problem may have multiple roots or no real root and is covered later in the curriculum.

```python
"""Solve a constant rate from two positive, same-sign balances."""
import math
from numbers import Real
import unittest

def finite(x, name):
    if isinstance(x, bool) or not isinstance(x, Real):
        raise TypeError(f"{name} must be real")
    try:
        x = float(x)
    except OverflowError as exc:
        raise ValueError(f"{name} out of float range") from exc
    if not math.isfinite(x):
        raise ValueError(f"{name} must be finite")
    return x

def inputs(present, future, years):
    p, f, t = finite(present, "present"), finite(future, "future"), finite(years, "years")
    if p == 0 or f == 0 or (p > 0) != (f > 0):
        raise ValueError("balances must be nonzero and have the same sign")
    if t <= 0:
        raise ValueError("years must be positive")
    ratio = f / p
    if ratio <= 0 or not math.isfinite(ratio):
        raise ValueError("balance ratio outside float range")
    return p, f, t, ratio

def periodic_rate(present, future, periods):
    if isinstance(periods, bool) or not isinstance(periods, int):
        raise TypeError("periods must be a positive integer")
    if periods <= 0:
        raise ValueError("periods must be positive")
    _, _, _, ratio = inputs(present, future, periods)
    rate = math.expm1(math.log(ratio) / periods)
    if not math.isfinite(rate) or rate <= -1:
        raise ValueError("periodic rate outside supported range")
    return rate

def effective_annual(present, future, years):
    _, _, t, ratio = inputs(present, future, years)
    rate = math.expm1(math.log(ratio) / t)
    if not math.isfinite(rate) or rate <= -1:
        raise ValueError("effective rate outside supported range")
    return rate

def continuous_rate(present, future, years):
    _, _, t, ratio = inputs(present, future, years)
    result = math.log(ratio) / t
    if not math.isfinite(result):
        raise ValueError("continuous rate outside supported range")
    return result

def nominal_annual(present, future, years, frequency):
    if isinstance(frequency, bool) or not isinstance(frequency, int):
        raise TypeError("frequency must be a positive integer")
    if frequency <= 0:
        raise ValueError("frequency must be positive")
    _, _, t, ratio = inputs(present, future, years)
    periodic = math.expm1(math.log(ratio) / (frequency * t))
    nominal = frequency * periodic
    if not math.isfinite(nominal) or periodic <= -1:
        raise ValueError("nominal rate outside supported range")
    return nominal

class RateTests(unittest.TestCase):
    def test_01_one_period(self):
        self.assertAlmostEqual(periodic_rate(100, 110, 1), .1)
    def test_02_multi_period(self):
        self.assertAlmostEqual(periodic_rate(100, 121, 2), .1)
    def test_03_effective_annual(self):
        self.assertAlmostEqual(effective_annual(1000, 1210, 2), .1)
    def test_04_continuous(self):
        self.assertAlmostEqual(continuous_rate(100, 100*math.exp(.05*3), 3), .05)
    def test_05_nominal_monthly(self):
        self.assertAlmostEqual(nominal_annual(1000, 1000*(1+.06/12)**24, 2, 12), .06)
    def test_06_negative_rate(self):
        self.assertAlmostEqual(effective_annual(100, 81, 2), -.1)
    def test_07_signed_balances(self):
        self.assertAlmostEqual(periodic_rate(-100, -110, 1), .1)
        self.assertAlmostEqual(effective_annual(100, 100, 4), 0)
    def test_08_invalid(self):
        for args in ((0, 100, 1), (100, -110, 1), (100, 110, 0),
                     (100, 110, -1), (100, float("inf"), 1)):
            with self.assertRaises((TypeError, ValueError)):
                effective_annual(*args)
        with self.assertRaises(TypeError):
            periodic_rate(100, 110, 1.0)
        with self.assertRaises(ValueError):
            nominal_annual(100, 110, 1, 0)
        with self.assertRaises(TypeError):
            continuous_rate(True, 110, 1)

if __name__ == "__main__":
    print(f"Two-year EAR: {effective_annual(1000, 1210, 2):.6%}")
    unittest.main(verbosity=2)
```

## Practice exercises and answers

1. **One period:** 400 becomes 420 over one complete period. **Answer:** \(420/400-1=5\%\) per period.
2. **Two years:** 5,000 becomes 6,050 over two years. **Answer:** \((6050/5000)^{1/2}-1=10\%\) effective annual.
3. **Continuous equivalent:** Convert a 10% effective annual rate. **Answer:** \(\ln(1.10)\approx9.53101798\%\) continuously compounded annually.
4. **Decline:** 100 becomes 81 over two years. **Answer:** \((81/100)^{1/2}-1=-10\%\) effective annual.
5. **No growth:** 1,000 remains 1,000 for three years. **Answer:** 0% under each of the constant-rate conventions.
6. **Conceptual:** Why can the formula not infer a unique investment return after an unrecorded deposit? **Answer:** The final balance combines investment growth with added cash; cash-flow timing and amounts must be incorporated before return attribution.
