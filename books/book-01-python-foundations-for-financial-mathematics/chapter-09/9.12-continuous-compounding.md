## Learning objectives

Derive continuous accumulation as a limit of periodic compounding; distinguish continuously compounded rates from effective annual rates; compute future and present values; convert between rate conventions; and validate assumptions, numerical boundaries, and inverse identities with Python.

## From discrete periods to a continuous model

For principal \(P\), nominal annual rate \(j\), \(m\) equal compounding periods per year, and \(t\) years with \(mt\) complete periods, discrete accumulation is

\[
A_m=P\left(1+\frac{j}{m}\right)^{mt}.
\]

As the number of conversion periods grows without bound while \(j\) and \(t\) remain fixed,

\[
\lim_{m\to\infty}\left(1+\frac{j}{m}\right)^{mt}=e^{jt}.
\]

We denote the continuously compounded annual rate by \(r_c\), giving

\[
\boxed{FV=PV\,e^{r_ct}},\qquad
\boxed{PV=FV\,e^{-r_ct}}.
\]

The rate is an annualized **continuously compounded** quote; \(t\) must be measured in years on a stated basis. The model assumes a constant rate, continuous reinvestment, and no interim cash flows, fees, taxes, or credit losses. Continuous compounding is a mathematical convention, not literal infinite transaction posting.

## Why the exponential appears

Taking logarithms of the discrete factor gives

\[
\log(A_m/P)=mt\log(1+j/m)
=jt\,\frac{\log(1+j/m)}{j/m}.
\]

Since \(\lim_{x\to0}\log(1+x)/x=1\), the log growth approaches \(jt\); exponentiating yields \(e^{jt}\). For \(j=0\), the result follows directly because every accumulation factor is one.

Unlike a discrete quote, a real continuously compounded rate can be negative without making the exponential growth factor negative. For finite real \(r_c,t\), \(e^{r_ct}>0\). A negative rate reduces a positive balance over a positive horizon; it does not create a negative accumulation factor.

## Worked example: one-year accumulation

Invest an illustrative 10,000 USD at **6% continuously compounded** for one year:

\[
FV=10{,}000e^{0.06}\approx 10{,}618.365465.
\]

The end balance is approximately **10,618.37 USD**. Its effective annual rate is

\[
EAR=e^{0.06}-1\approx 0.0618365465=6.18365465\%.
\]

For comparison, a 6% nominal quote with daily conversion (365 equal periods) yields approximately 10,618.31 USD, slightly below the continuous limit. This comparison fixes the **nominal quote**, principal, and horizon. It does not imply every financial product offering daily crediting has the same terms.

## Worked example: present value and time consistency

Suppose a payment of 15,000 USD is due in three years, with a flat continuously compounded discount rate of 4%:

\[
PV=15{,}000e^{-0.04(3)}
\approx 13{,}303.80655.
\]

Thus the illustrative present value is approximately **13,303.81 USD**. Multiplying that unrounded present value by \(e^{0.12}\) recovers 15,000 USD. Rounding the intermediate value to cents can introduce a small reconciliation difference.

Continuous factors compose across adjacent intervals when the same constant rate applies:

\[
e^{r_ct_1}e^{r_ct_2}=e^{r_c(t_1+t_2)}.
\]

If the rate varies over time, the generalization is \(e^{\int_0^t r(s)\,ds}\), which requires an explicitly specified rate path; a single constant rate cannot silently represent changing market conditions.

## Converting rate conventions correctly

The effective annual rate corresponding to a continuously compounded annual rate is

\[
\boxed{EAR=e^{r_c}-1}.
\]

The inverse is

\[
\boxed{r_c=\ln(1+EAR)},\qquad EAR>-1.
\]

A nominal annual rate \(j\) convertible \(m\) times a year is equivalent over one year to

\[
\boxed{r_c=m\ln(1+j/m)},\qquad 1+j/m>0.
\]

These conversions equate **annual accumulation factors**, not the numerical rate quotes. For instance, a 6% effective annual rate corresponds to \(\ln(1.06)\approx5.82689081\%\) continuously compounded, not 6% continuously compounded.

For very small rates, `math.expm1(r)` and `math.log1p(e)` are more accurate than naively computing `math.exp(r)-1` and `math.log(1+e)`.

## Discounting dated cash flows

For cash flows \(C_i\) at year coordinates \(t_i\geq0\), valued at time zero under a constant continuously compounded rate,

\[
PV_{\text{total}}=\sum_i C_i e^{-r_ct_i}.
\]

Keep cash-flow signs and dates explicit. A positive inflow and a negative outflow should not be combined without recording their timing. An actual day-count convention (for example, actual days divided by a specified basis) must be chosen before converting dates into year fractions.

## Numerical and practical boundaries

The exponential can overflow for large positive exponents or underflow to zero for sufficiently negative exponents in ordinary binary floating-point. Even mathematically valid inputs may therefore fall outside the teaching lab's representable range. Input validation rejects nonfinite values, booleans as rates, negative horizons, and underflow/overflow rather than silently returning a misleading result. Zero cash flows remain zero; negative signed cash flows are allowed.

Continuous compounding is common in mathematical finance and log-return modeling. It is not interchangeable with contractual APR, APY, a yield with a different compounding convention, or a term structure of rates. No market data or investment recommendation is implied by the examples.

```python
"""Continuous compounding: educational floating-point TVM model."""
import math
from numbers import Real
import unittest

def finite(x, label):
    if isinstance(x, bool) or not isinstance(x, Real):
        raise TypeError(f"{label} must be real")
    try:
        y = float(x)
    except OverflowError as exc:
        raise ValueError(f"{label} outside float range") from exc
    if not math.isfinite(y):
        raise ValueError(f"{label} must be finite")
    return y

def accumulation(rate, years):
    r, t = finite(rate, "continuous rate"), finite(years, "years")
    if t < 0:
        raise ValueError("horizon must be nonnegative")
    exponent = r * t
    if not math.isfinite(exponent):
        raise ValueError("exponent outside supported range")
    try:
        factor = math.exp(exponent)
    except OverflowError as exc:
        raise ValueError("accumulation overflow") from exc
    if factor == 0 or not math.isfinite(factor):
        raise ValueError("accumulation underflow or overflow")
    return factor

def future_value(principal, rate, years):
    p = finite(principal, "principal")
    result = p * accumulation(rate, years)
    if not math.isfinite(result) or (p != 0 and result == 0):
        raise ValueError("future value outside supported float range")
    return result

def present_value(future, rate, years):
    f = finite(future, "future cash flow")
    result = f * accumulation(-finite(rate, "continuous rate"), years)
    if not math.isfinite(result) or (f != 0 and result == 0):
        raise ValueError("present value outside supported float range")
    return result

def effective_annual(rate):
    r = finite(rate, "continuous rate")
    try:
        e = math.expm1(r)
    except OverflowError as exc:
        raise ValueError("effective rate overflow") from exc
    if not math.isfinite(e) or e <= -1:
        raise ValueError("effective rate outside supported range")
    return e

def continuous_from_effective(effective):
    e = finite(effective, "effective annual rate")
    if e <= -1:
        raise ValueError("effective annual growth must be positive")
    return math.log1p(e)

def continuous_from_nominal(nominal, frequency):
    j = finite(nominal, "nominal annual rate")
    if isinstance(frequency, bool) or not isinstance(frequency, int):
        raise TypeError("frequency must be an integer")
    if frequency <= 0:
        raise ValueError("frequency must be positive")
    p = j / frequency
    if j != 0 and p == 0:
        raise ValueError("periodic rate underflow")
    if p <= -1:
        raise ValueError("periodic growth factor must be positive")
    result = frequency * math.log1p(p)
    if not math.isfinite(result):
        raise ValueError("continuous rate outside supported range")
    return result

class ContinuousTests(unittest.TestCase):
    def test_01_zero_rate(self):
        self.assertEqual(future_value(10000, 0, 3), 10000)
    def test_02_one_year(self):
        self.assertAlmostEqual(future_value(10000, .06, 1), 10000 * math.exp(.06))
    def test_03_inverse(self):
        p = 12750.25
        self.assertAlmostEqual(present_value(future_value(p, .035, 4), .035, 4), p)
    def test_04_effective_conversion(self):
        self.assertAlmostEqual(effective_annual(.06), math.exp(.06)-1)
        self.assertAlmostEqual(continuous_from_effective(effective_annual(.06)), .06)
    def test_05_nominal_equivalence(self):
        for m in (1, 2, 4, 12, 365):
            c = continuous_from_nominal(.06, m)
            self.assertAlmostEqual(math.exp(c), (1+.06/m)**m)
    def test_06_continuous_limit(self):
        discrete = 10000*(1+.06/365)**365
        self.assertLess(discrete, future_value(10000, .06, 1))
    def test_07_negative_and_zero_time(self):
        self.assertLess(future_value(10000, -.02, 2), 10000)
        self.assertEqual(future_value(-200, .04, 0), -200)
    def test_08_validation(self):
        for bad in (float("nan"), float("inf"), True, "0.06"):
            with self.assertRaises((TypeError, ValueError)):
                effective_annual(bad)
        for bad in (0, -1, 12.0, True):
            with self.assertRaises((TypeError, ValueError)):
                continuous_from_nominal(.06, bad)
        with self.assertRaises(ValueError):
            continuous_from_effective(-1)
        with self.assertRaises(ValueError):
            future_value(100, .05, -1)
        with self.assertRaises(ValueError):
            future_value(100, 1000, 1)
        with self.assertRaises(ValueError):
            present_value(100, 1000, 1)

if __name__ == "__main__":
    print(f"Continuous FV: {future_value(10000, .06, 1):,.2f}")
    print(f"Continuous EAR: {effective_annual(.06):.8%}")
    unittest.main(verbosity=2)
```

## Exercises with answers

1. **Compute** the future value of 5,000 USD at 3% continuous for two years. **Answer:** \(5000e^{0.06}\approx 5{,}309.18\) USD.
2. **Convert** 5% continuously compounded into an effective annual rate. **Answer:** \(e^{0.05}-1\approx5.12710964\%\).
3. **Convert** 5% effective annual into a continuous annual rate. **Answer:** \(\ln(1.05)\approx4.87901642\%\).
4. **Discount** 2,000 USD due in 18 months at 4% continuously compounded. **Answer:** \(2000e^{-0.04(1.5)}\approx1{,}883.53\) USD, with 18 months defined as 1.5 years.
5. **Explain** why a 6% continuously compounded quote is not the same as 6% effective annual. **Answer:** The former produces \(e^{0.06}-1\approx6.18365\%\) effective annual growth; the latter has annual growth of exactly 6%.
6. **Show** the interval-composition identity for a constant rate. **Answer:** \(e^{r_ct_1}e^{r_ct_2}=e^{r_c(t_1+t_2)}\), by the exponent addition rule.
