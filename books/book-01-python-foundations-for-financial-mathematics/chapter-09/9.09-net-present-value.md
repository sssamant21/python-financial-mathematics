## Include the initial investment

Section 9.8 valued future net payments without supplying an acquisition price. Net present value brings the investment costs into the same valuation. For a nonnegative time-0 cost magnitude K and future signed cash flows C_j at periods t_j≥1,

\[
\boxed{NPV=-K+\sum_j\frac{C_j}{(1+i)^{t_j}}}.
\]

Alternatively, put every signed payment, including the initial outflow C_0=−K, in a single dated schedule and sum its present equivalents. Both representations describe the same calculation. Use one representation consistently: do not put −K in the schedule and subtract it again.

NPV is measured in currency at the valuation date. It is not a percentage return, the sum of nominal receipts, an accounting earnings figure, or a new cash payment. OpenStax's finance treatment presents NPV as a common-date comparison of inflows and outflows and discusses its conditional decision use [1].

## An original acquisition example

Continue the synthetic USD schedule from Section 9.8 and add a time-0 acquisition cost of 1,100. Use 5% effective annually, whole-year payment dates, one investor viewpoint, and complete deterministic cash flows. Include no additional taxes, fees, financing payments, or uncertainty beyond the explicitly listed fee.

| Event | Year | Signed amount, USD |
|---|---:|---:|
| Acquisition cost | 0 | −1,100 |
| First income | 1 | +300 |
| Fee | 2 | −40 |
| Later income | 3 | +120 |
| Principal receipt | 3 | +900 |

The calculation is

\[
NPV=-1100+\frac{300}{1.05}-\frac{40}{1.05^2}
+\frac{1020}{1.05^3}.
\]

The future net flows have a time-0 equivalent of approximately 1,130.55. After subtracting the 1,100 initial cost, NPV is approximately **+30.55 USD at time 0**. The initial outflow has factor one; it is not discounted for a year simply because it appears in the first input row.

The undiscounted signed sum is 180 USD. That amount differs from NPV because it ignores timing. Total future principal receipts also must not be confused with net value created: recovering invested principal contributes cash without making the entire receipt an economic surplus.

## Interpret the sign within the model

| NPV sign | Model interpretation relative to the supplied valuation rule |
|---|---|
| Positive | Valued inflows exceed valued outflows |
| Zero | The complete schedule balances in present-value terms |
| Negative | Valued outflows exceed valued inflows |

For an independent optional investment, a positive NPV supports undertaking it under the model's cash-flow and opportunity-cost assumptions; negative NPV supports declining it on that basis. Zero means equivalence at the supplied hurdle rate, not zero nominal growth. These interpretations assume the comparison and rate are appropriate. Capital constraints, mutually exclusive alternatives, mandatory obligations, omitted effects, and uncertain inputs require further analysis rather than a mechanical sign-only action.

A tiny floating-point result around zero should be assessed with a stated numerical tolerance and economic materiality threshold. The lab verifies approximate equality in its break-even test; it does not automatically label a computed value as a final investment decision.

## Price sensitivity and break-even acquisition cost

Hold the future schedule and rate fixed. Let V be its present equivalent. Then

\[
NPV(K)=V-K.
\]

Raising the time-0 cost by 50 reduces NPV by exactly 50 mathematically. For the example, a cost of 1,150 produces NPV of approximately −19.45. A cost equal to the unrounded V makes NPV zero.

This break-even acquisition cost is conditional on the stated future cash flows and rate. It is not a guaranteed market price, an appraisal certification, or a universally appropriate bid. Include all relevant time-0 costs in K for this simple interface. More elaborate transactions may require distinct time-0 receipts and outflows, which a general signed ledger can retain separately.

## Rate scenarios and mixed signs

Keep the project's amounts and dates unchanged and vary only the assumed effective annual rate:

| Rate | NPV at time 0, USD, displayed |
|---:|---:|
| 0% | +180.00 |
| 5% | +30.55 |
| 10% | −93.99 |

These are scenarios, not probabilities. The rate grid does not estimate the likelihood of an outcome. Choosing the rate requires a coherent valuation framework; do not select a rate solely to obtain a favorable NPV. Use nominal cash flows with compatible nominal valuation assumptions and real cash flows with compatible real assumptions. The lab neither estimates a cost of capital nor adjusts independently for credit risk or inflation.

For an initial outflow followed solely by positive future receipts, NPV decreases with the discount rate over i>−1. Mixed future signs do not guarantee that shape. As an explicit counterexample, consider cost 100, receipt 230 at year 1, and payment −132 at year 2:

\[
NPV(i)=-100+\frac{230}{1+i}-\frac{132}{(1+i)^2}.
\]

It is zero at both 10% and 20%, and positive at 15%. Thus one cannot assume that an arbitrary schedule has a single zero or a steadily decreasing NPV profile. The test checks these values directly; it does not solve for an internal rate of return. Root finding receives separate treatment later.

## Compare incremental alternatives

For comparable alternatives A and B under the same linear valuation rule,

\[
NPV(B-A)=NPV(B)-NPV(A).
\]

Consider one-year alternatives at 10%: A costs 1,000 and pays 1,200; B costs 1,100 and pays 1,280. A's NPV is approximately 90.91, while B's is approximately 63.64. Moving from A to B requires an additional 100 now for an additional 80 at year 1, with incremental NPV approximately −27.27.

Under these explicitly comparable, mutually exclusive alternatives, A has the larger NPV despite B's larger nominal receipt. For different lives, risk assumptions, resource limits, or repeatability, define a consistent comparison before ranking projects. Independent projects may both be acceptable, while a constrained portfolio of projects needs more than independent sign tests.

The full listing includes its own valuation helper; it imports no prior lab. `net_present_value` accepts a nonnegative initial-cost magnitude and strictly future signed Flow records. It inserts the initial negative payment automatically and returns audit rows plus NPV. Time-0 records in `future_flows` are rejected to keep the input contract explicit. IDs must be unique; `__initial_cost__` is reserved for the inserted record. This design cannot detect a caller hiding an already-counted acquisition cost inside a mislabeled future amount: the author must establish the schedule's economic meaning.

The schedule is assumed complete, single-currency, and expressed in matching whole periods. Unknown amounts are rejected rather than filled with zero; omissions cannot be detected automatically. A zero-cost empty schedule has NPV zero by the explicit empty-schedule contract. This says nothing about the completeness of an unexamined external file.

The function uses finite binary floating-point values and `math.fsum`. Selected overflow, complete-underflow, and nonfinite failures are rejected. Summation accuracy cannot repair inaccurate factors, near-cancellation, unrepresentable intermediate results, or omitted cash flows. Very small rates can be lost in 1+i. Factor-first arithmetic can reject extreme cases that another arrangement might support. The implementation does not impose exact monetary settlement or a contractual rounding rule.

```python
"""Explicit acquisition cost plus strictly future signed project flows."""
from dataclasses import dataclass
import math
from numbers import Real
import unittest


@dataclass(frozen=True)
class Flow:
    event_id: str
    period: int
    amount: float


def real(value,name):
    if isinstance(value,bool) or not isinstance(value,Real):
        raise TypeError(f'{name} must be real, not bool')
    try:
        value=float(value)
    except OverflowError as exc:
        raise ValueError(f'{name} outside float range') from exc
    if not math.isfinite(value):
        raise ValueError(f'{name} must be finite')
    return value


def whole(value,name):
    if isinstance(value,bool) or not isinstance(value,int):
        raise TypeError(f'{name} must be an integer, not bool')
    if value < 0:
        raise ValueError(f'{name} must be nonnegative')
    return value


def value_flows(flows,rate,*,target=0):
    """Return (event audit rows, total equivalent); one declared currency."""
    r=real(rate,'rate'); target=whole(target,'target')
    if r <= -1:
        raise ValueError('rate must exceed -1')
    seen=set(); checked=[]
    for flow in flows:
        if not isinstance(flow,Flow):
            raise TypeError('records must be Flow objects')
        if not isinstance(flow.event_id,str) or not flow.event_id.strip():
            raise ValueError('event ID must be nonblank text')
        if flow.event_id in seen:
            raise ValueError('duplicate event ID')
        seen.add(flow.event_id)
        t=whole(flow.period,'period'); amount=real(flow.amount,'amount')
        checked.append((flow.event_id,t,amount))
    rows=[]
    for event_id,t,amount in sorted(checked,key=lambda x:(x[1],x[0])):
        delta=target-t
        try:
            accumulation=(1+r)**abs(delta)
            if not math.isfinite(accumulation) or accumulation <= 0:
                raise ValueError('accumulation outside supported float range')
            factor=accumulation if delta >= 0 else 1/accumulation
            equivalent=amount*factor
        except OverflowError as exc:
            raise ValueError('valuation outside supported float range') from exc
        if not math.isfinite(factor) or factor <= 0 or not math.isfinite(equivalent):
            raise ValueError('valuation outside supported float range')
        if amount != 0 and equivalent == 0:
            raise ValueError('nonzero event underflowed to zero')
        rows.append((event_id,t,amount,factor,equivalent))
    try:
        total=math.fsum(row[4] for row in rows)
    except OverflowError as exc:
        raise ValueError('total outside supported float range') from exc
    if not math.isfinite(total):
        raise ValueError('total must be finite')
    return rows,total



def net_present_value(initial_cost,future_flows,rate):
    """Cost is a nonnegative magnitude; future events must have period >= 1."""
    cost=real(initial_cost,'initial_cost')
    if cost < 0:
        raise ValueError('initial_cost must be nonnegative')
    future=list(future_flows)
    for flow in future:
        if not isinstance(flow,Flow):
            raise TypeError('future records must be Flow objects')
        if whole(flow.period,'period') == 0:
            raise ValueError('future_flows must exclude time-zero events')
    return value_flows([Flow('__initial_cost__',0,-cost),*future],rate)


def fixture():
    return [Flow('income1',1,300),Flow('fee2',2,-40),
            Flow('principal3',3,900),Flow('income3',3,120)]


class NPVTests(unittest.TestCase):
    def test_01_reference_project(self):
        rows,result=net_present_value(1100,fixture(),.05)
        self.assertEqual(len(rows),5)
        self.assertEqual(rows[0][3],1)
        self.assertEqual(rows[0][4],-1100)
        self.assertAlmostEqual(result,-1100+300/1.05-40/1.05**2+1020/1.05**3)
        self.assertGreater(result,0)

    def test_02_price_and_break_even(self):
        future_pv=value_flows(fixture(),.05)[1]
        self.assertAlmostEqual(net_present_value(future_pv,fixture(),.05)[1],0)
        a=net_present_value(1100,fixture(),.05)[1]
        b=net_present_value(1150,fixture(),.05)[1]
        self.assertAlmostEqual(a-b,50)
        self.assertLess(b,0)

    def test_03_positive_zero_negative(self):
        flows=[Flow('receipt',1,1100)]
        for cost,target in [(900,100),(1000,0),(1100,-100)]:
            self.assertAlmostEqual(net_present_value(cost,flows,.1)[1],target)

    def test_04_zero_rate_and_future_cost(self):
        self.assertEqual(net_present_value(1100,fixture(),0)[1],180)
        self.assertEqual(net_present_value(50,[],.05)[1],-50)
        self.assertEqual(net_present_value(0,[],.05)[1],0)
        self.assertLess(net_present_value(0,[Flow('cleanup',2,-100)],.05)[1],0)

    def test_05_rate_scenarios_and_nonconventional_shape(self):
        values=[net_present_value(1100,fixture(),r)[1] for r in [0,.05,.1]]
        self.assertGreater(values[0],values[1]);self.assertGreater(values[1],values[2])
        # Negative later payments can make NPV nonmonotone.
        flows=[Flow('in',1,230),Flow('cleanup',2,-132)]
        self.assertAlmostEqual(net_present_value(100,flows,.1)[1],0)
        self.assertAlmostEqual(net_present_value(100,flows,.2)[1],0)
        self.assertGreater(net_present_value(100,flows,.15)[1],0)

    def test_06_incremental_comparison(self):
        a=net_present_value(1000,[Flow('a',1,1200)],.1)[1]
        b=net_present_value(1100,[Flow('b',1,1280)],.1)[1]
        incremental=net_present_value(100,[Flow('increment',1,80)],.1)[1]
        self.assertAlmostEqual(b-a,incremental)
        self.assertGreater(a,b)

    def test_07_input_contract(self):
        for cost in [True,'1100',None]:
            with self.assertRaises(TypeError): net_present_value(cost,fixture(),.05)
        for cost in [-1,float('nan')]:
            with self.assertRaises(ValueError): net_present_value(cost,fixture(),.05)
        with self.assertRaises(ValueError): net_present_value(100,[Flow('extra',0,-100)],.05)
        with self.assertRaises(ValueError): net_present_value(100,[fixture()[0]]*2,.05)
        with self.assertRaises(TypeError): net_present_value(100,[Flow('unknown',1,None)],.05)
        with self.assertRaises(ValueError): net_present_value(100,fixture(),-1)
        with self.assertRaises(ValueError): net_present_value(100,[Flow('bad',1,float('nan'))],.05)

    def test_08_range_and_order(self):
        a=net_present_value(1100,fixture(),.05)[1]
        b=net_present_value(1100,reversed(fixture()),.05)[1]
        self.assertEqual(a,b)
        with self.assertRaises(ValueError): net_present_value(100,[Flow('far',2000,1)],1)
        with self.assertRaises(ValueError): net_present_value(100,[Flow('tiny',1,5e-324)],1)


if __name__=='__main__':
    rows,result=net_present_value(1100,fixture(),.05)
    print(f'PV of future net flows: {value_flows(fixture(),.05)[1]:.2f} USD')
    print(f'Initial cost: 1100.00 USD; NPV: {result:+.2f} USD at t=0')
    for rate in [0,.05,.1]:
        print(f'Rate {rate:.0%}: NPV {net_present_value(1100,fixture(),rate)[1]:+.2f} USD')
    unittest.main(verbosity=2)
```

## Practice with answers

1. Pay 1,000 now and receive 1,210 in two years. Use 10% effective annually. **Answer:** NPV=−1,000+1,210/1.1²=0, even though nominal receipts exceed the initial cost by 210.
2. Increase that initial cost to 1,050 without changing the receipt or rate. **Answer:** NPV=−50.
3. The future flows of a schedule have PV 2,400, and all time-0 acquisition costs total 2,250. **Answer:** NPV=+150 in valuation-date currency. Do not subtract acquisition costs again if they were already included in a complete signed total.
4. Can an unknown cleanup obligation be replaced by zero before computing NPV? **Answer:** no. Resolve it or model uncertainty explicitly; missing costs can reverse the conclusion.
5. Does a positive NPV of 100 mean a 100% return? **Answer:** no. NPV is a currency amount at a valuation date. A return is a different quantity requiring its own definition.
