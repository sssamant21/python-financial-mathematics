## The interest-bearing base changes

Under compound interest, retained interest becomes part of the balance used to calculate later interest. The CFPB describes this mechanism in its public explanation of compound interest [1]. In contrast, Section 9.3 kept the original principal as the simple-interest base.

Use a synthetic account with principal P, constant effective interest rate i per period, and n whole periods. All credited interest remains in the account. Assume no deposits, withdrawals, fees, taxes, default, or changes to the rate. The account has a balance at time 0 and credits interest at each period end. A rate input of .04 means 4% per period.

Let B_k be the balance immediately after crediting interest for period k, with B_0=P. Interest for period k is

```math
J_k=iB_{k-1}.
```

The closing balance is the opening balance plus that period's interest:

```math
B_k=B_{k-1}+J_k=B_{k-1}(1+i).
```

Repeated substitution gives

```math
B_n=P(1+i)^n.
```

Total interest over the horizon is B_n−P, not iB_n and not generally Pin. The expression Pin belongs to the unchanged-principal simple-interest model.

## An original three-period schedule

Invest 2,000 illustrative USD at 4% effective annually for three years. One period is one year, and all interest is retained.

| Year ending | Opening balance, USD | Interest for year, USD | Closing balance, USD |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | 2,000.000 | 80.000 | 2,080.000 |
| 2 | 2,080.000 | 83.200 | 2,163.200 |
| 3 | 2,163.200 | 86.528 | 2,249.728 |

The second year's base includes the first year's 80. The third year's base includes both earlier interest credits. Total interest is 80 + 83.2 + 86.528 = 249.728. The closed calculation gives the same maturity amount:

```math
2000(1.04)^3=2249.728.
```

Display the final amount as 2,249.73 if two decimal places are desired. This example retains unrounded intermediate balances; it does not apply a rule that rounds each annual credit to cents. A contractual posting rule could produce a different result and must be stated separately.

## Compare like with like

Use the same principal, 4% numeric annual rate, three-year horizon, and no external cash flows for both models:

| Model | Interest-bearing base | Maturity amount, USD | Total interest, USD |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| Simple | Original 2,000 throughout | 2,240.000 | 240.000 |
| Compound | Prior closing balance | 2,249.728 | 249.728 |

The extra 9.728 arises from the changing base. For a nonnegative rate and a whole number of periods, (1+i)^n ≥ 1+ni. With positive principal, positive rate, and n≥2 the inequality is strict. At n=0 or n=1, or at zero rate, the two models agree. These qualifications matter; comparisons involving fractional horizons need additional assumptions about accrual between crediting dates.

Neither column represents a market forecast. The comparison holds the numeric rate fixed to isolate the accumulation rule. Real offers may quote different rate conventions and payment terms.

## Composition and the rate unit

With the same constant effective rate throughout,

```math
P(1+i)^{m+n}=\bigl[P(1+i)^m\bigr](1+i)^n.
```

Computing five periods at once therefore agrees with computing two periods and then three more while retaining the entire balance. This property follows from exponent addition; it does not imply that reinvestment at a particular market rate will be available.

Rate and term must share a unit. An effective monthly i requires n to count months. An effective annual i requires n to count years in this whole-period model. Using .04 as a monthly rate would mean 4% each month, not 4% annually. Dividing an effective annual rate by 12 does not generally produce its equivalent effective monthly rate. Sections 9.10 and 9.11 develop quote conventions and compounding frequency explicitly.

This section deliberately restricts n to whole periods. The algebraic extension (1+i)^t for fractional t requires an interpolation convention. A real contract might instead use a different partial-period accrual rule; the code does not infer one from a decimal term.

## Zero and negative rates

At zero rate, every period's interest is zero and the balance stays P. At zero periods, the final balance is the initial principal.

The same recurrence also permits −1<i<0. For 1,000 and i=−.02 over two periods, the balances are 980 and 960.4. Interest entries are negative and the positive balance shrinks. This is a mathematical scenario under stated assumptions, not a statement about an available deposit product.

The lab rejects i≤−1. A −100% rate would make the balance zero after a positive period and would break invertibility; rates below it allow sign reversals. Those cases are outside the positive-accumulation model used in this chapter. The principal input is nonnegative; signed investor payments belong in a cash-flow timeline, not in this account-balance helper.

## Account credits and external cash flows

Credited interest changes the balance inside the modeled account. It is not simultaneously an extra cash receipt outside the account. If all funds remain invested until maturity, the investor's external timeline contains an initial payment of P and a final receipt of B_n, with opposite signs. Adding the intermediate interest credits again as external receipts would double count them.

If interest is paid out instead, the account no longer follows the assumed retained-interest schedule. Keep the income payments and principal account separate. Likewise, contributions or withdrawals require a recurrence with explicit external flows and a declared ordering of interest and payments.

`compound_amount` uses the closed formula. `schedule` independently builds period rows containing the period number, opening balance, interest, and closing balance. The tests reconcile both methods on finite fixtures, check continuity between rows, and compare summed interest with the change in principal.

Both helpers reject booleans, nonfinite inputs, negative principal, unsupported rates, and negative or noninteger terms. They detect selected overflow and complete-underflow failures. These are finite-precision float implementations, not arbitrary-range calculators. The closed formula can reject a calculation when its intermediate factor is outside the float range even if a differently arranged calculation might be representable. Very small rates may disappear in 1+i, small credits may be lost when added to a large balance, and tiny nonzero balances can have reduced relative accuracy. The two methods need not agree bit-for-bit or fail at identical numerical boundaries. A schedule also uses time and memory proportional to n. Use suitable precision and resource limits for broader requirements.

```python
"""Whole-period compounding with retained interest and no external flows."""
import math
from numbers import Real
import unittest


def real(value, name):
    if isinstance(value, bool) or not isinstance(value, Real):
        raise TypeError(f'{name} must be real, not bool')
    try:
        result = float(value)
    except OverflowError as exc:
        raise ValueError(f'{name} outside float range') from exc
    if not math.isfinite(result):
        raise ValueError(f'{name} must be finite')
    return result


def inputs(principal, rate, periods):
    p = real(principal, 'principal'); r = real(rate, 'rate')
    if p < 0 or r <= -1:
        raise ValueError('principal must be nonnegative and rate must exceed -1')
    if isinstance(periods, bool) or not isinstance(periods, int):
        raise TypeError('periods must be an integer, not bool')
    if periods < 0:
        raise ValueError('periods must be nonnegative')
    return p, r


def compound_amount(principal, rate, periods):
    p,r = inputs(principal,rate,periods)
    if p == 0 or r == 0 or periods == 0:
        return p
    try:
        factor = (1+r)**periods
        amount = p*factor
    except OverflowError as exc:
        raise ValueError('result outside supported float range') from exc
    if not math.isfinite(amount) or factor == 0 or amount == 0:
        raise ValueError('result outside supported float range')
    return amount


def schedule(principal, rate, periods):
    p,r = inputs(principal,rate,periods)
    rows = []
    balance = p
    for k in range(1,periods+1):
        interest = balance*r
        closing = balance+interest
        if not math.isfinite(closing) or (balance > 0 and closing <= 0):
            raise ValueError('schedule outside supported float range')
        rows.append((k,balance,interest,closing))
        balance = closing
    return rows


class CompoundTests(unittest.TestCase):
    def test_01_worked_schedule(self):
        rows = schedule(2000,.04,3)
        for row,expected in zip(rows,[(1,2000,80,2080),(2,2080,83.2,2163.2),(3,2163.2,86.528,2249.728)]):
            for value,target in zip(row,expected): self.assertAlmostEqual(value,target)
        self.assertAlmostEqual(compound_amount(2000,.04,3),2249.728)

    def test_02_loop_closed_form_and_interest_sum(self):
        rows = schedule(1234.56,.037,12)
        self.assertAlmostEqual(rows[-1][3],compound_amount(1234.56,.037,12))
        self.assertAlmostEqual(math.fsum(row[2] for row in rows),rows[-1][3]-1234.56)
        for prior,later in zip(rows,rows[1:]): self.assertEqual(prior[3],later[1])

    def test_03_composition(self):
        staged = compound_amount(compound_amount(2000,.04,2),.04,3)
        self.assertAlmostEqual(staged,compound_amount(2000,.04,5))

    def test_04_zero_and_one_period(self):
        self.assertEqual(compound_amount(2000,.04,0),2000)
        self.assertEqual(schedule(2000,.04,0),[])
        self.assertEqual(compound_amount(2000,0,10),2000)
        self.assertEqual(compound_amount(0,.04,10),0)
        self.assertAlmostEqual(compound_amount(2000,.04,1),2000*(1+.04))

    def test_05_simple_comparison(self):
        self.assertAlmostEqual(compound_amount(2000,.04,3)-2000*(1+.04*3),9.728)
        self.assertAlmostEqual(compound_amount(2000,.04,1),2000*(1+.04))

    def test_06_negative_rate(self):
        self.assertAlmostEqual(compound_amount(1000,-.02,2),960.4)
        rows = schedule(1000,-.02,2)
        self.assertTrue(all(row[2]<0 and 0<row[3]<row[1] for row in rows))

    def test_07_rejected_inputs(self):
        for function in (compound_amount,schedule):
            for args in [(True,.04,2),(1000,'0.04',2),(1000,.04,True),(1000,.04,2.5)]:
                with self.assertRaises(TypeError): function(*args)
            for args in [(-1,.04,2),(1000,-1,2),(1000,-1.1,2),(1000,.04,-1),
                         (float('nan'),.04,2),(1000,float('inf'),2)]:
                with self.assertRaises(ValueError): function(*args)

    def test_08_numerical_range_and_rounding(self):
        with self.assertRaises(ValueError): compound_amount(1000,1,2000)
        with self.assertRaises(ValueError): compound_amount(1000,-.5,2000)
        with self.assertRaises(ValueError): schedule(1e308,1,2)
        rows=schedule(2000,.04,3)
        self.assertAlmostEqual(rows[-1][3],2249.728)
        self.assertEqual(f'{rows[-1][3]:.2f}','2249.73')


if __name__ == '__main__':
    print('Period | Opening | Interest | Closing (illustrative USD)')
    for k,opening,interest,closing in schedule(2000,.04,3):
        print(f'{k} | {opening:.3f} | {interest:.3f} | {closing:.3f}')
    unittest.main(verbosity=2)
```
