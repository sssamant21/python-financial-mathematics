## Interest on unchanged principal

Simple interest uses the original principal as its calculation base. The base does not grow when interest accrues. This defining feature is described in the California State Board of Equalization's public training material [1].

For principal P, simple rate r per unit of time, and elapsed time t in the same unit, define

\[
I=Prt,\qquad A=P+I=P(1+rt).
\]

I is interest; A is principal plus accrued interest. If P is measured in USD, both I and A are in USD. The product rt is dimensionless. This section assumes constant nonnegative principal, rate, and time, one currency, no fees or taxes, no interim payments, and no capitalization of accrued interest. Negative rates can be considered algebraically, but the teaching implementation intentionally excludes them.

A rate of 6% is entered as .06, not 6. The rate is a **simple annual rate** when time is measured in years. Calling it an effective annual compound rate would imply a different accumulation rule across multiple years.

## An original worked example

Suppose 2,400 USD earns simple interest at 6% annually for 18 months. For this synthetic example, 18 months is explicitly defined as 1.5 years; no particular calendar dates are supplied.

\[
I=2400(0.06)(1.5)=216,
\]

\[
A=2400+216=2616.
\]

| Elapsed years | Original principal, USD | Accrued interest, USD | Principal plus interest, USD |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 0 | 2,400.00 | 0.00 | 2,400.00 |
| 0.5 | 2,400.00 | 72.00 | 2,472.00 |
| 1.0 | 2,400.00 | 144.00 | 2,544.00 |
| 1.5 | 2,400.00 | 216.00 | 2,616.00 |

The equal half-year increments are 72 USD because the base remains 2,400. The table is an accrual illustration, not a schedule of cash receipts. Under the stated no-interim-payment assumption, the lender timeline has −2,400 at time 0 and +2,616 at time 1.5 if the obligation is paid in full. The borrower has the opposite signs. The accrued amounts at intermediate times are not extra payments to add to that final receipt.

## Match the rate and time units

The same simple-interest example can be written with r = .06/12 = .005 per month and t = 18 months:

\[
2400(0.005)(18)=216.
\]

Dividing by 12 here is a unit change for a proportional simple-interest rule. It is not the conversion from an effective annual compound rate to an equivalent effective monthly compound rate. That separate conversion uses a different formula and is developed later.

| Input combination | Product rt | Meaning |
|---|---:|---|
| .06 per year × 1.5 years | .09 | Consistent |
| .005 per month × 18 months | .09 | Consistent |
| .06 per year × 18 months | 1.08 numerically | Units do not match |

A numeric validator cannot infer that the author mistakenly entered months where years were required. Names, documentation, and the exercise's explicit assumptions supply this meaning.

## Day-based examples need a declared denominator

For an illustrative fixed-denominator rule, let t = d/B, where d is a supplied elapsed-day count and B is the declared annual denominator. Consider 10,000 USD at 6% simple annually for 90 supplied days:

| Explicit basis | Year fraction | Interest before display rounding, USD | Displayed interest, USD |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| Fixed 360-day denominator | 90/360 | 150 | 150.00 |
| Fixed 365-day denominator | 90/365 | approximately 147.945205479452 | 147.95 |

The difference comes from the declared year fraction. Neither denominator is universally correct. These are model inputs, not claims about the contractual basis of a loan or security. The lab does not implement 30/360, Actual/Actual, holiday adjustments, settlement rules, leap-year allocation, or rules for including endpoint dates. In particular, supplying the number 90 does not establish how it was counted. Resolve those terms before using a real agreement.

Keep intermediate precision; round only when displaying this example. Actual payment calculations need an explicit contractual rounding policy.

## Additive accrual and the rollover trap

With unchanged principal and rate, interest over two consecutive durations is additive:

\[
Pr(t_1+t_2)=Prt_1+Prt_2.
\]

However, applying a fresh simple-interest contract to the **maturity amount** of the first contract changes the principal for the second. For 1,000 at 5%:

- One two-year simple-interest calculation gives 1,000(1 + .05 × 2) = 1,100.
- One year gives 1,050. Reinvesting the entire 1,050 for another year at 5% gives 1,102.50.

The extra 2.50 is interest on the first year's interest. Algebraically,

\[
(1+rt_1)(1+rt_2)=1+r(t_1+t_2)+r^2t_1t_2.
\]

Thus the simple accumulation factor does not generally have the composition property of the compound factor in Section 9.1. Do not move between arbitrary valuation dates by repeatedly restarting a simple-interest factor and assume that it preserves the original unchanged-principal contract. Use the contract's origin and accrual terms consistently.

If the principal changes because of a repayment or a new advance, this section's single P no longer describes the entire arrangement. A richer schedule must specify the outstanding principal and interest-payment treatment over each interval.

```python
"""Simple interest on unchanged principal, using matched rate/time units."""
import math
from numbers import Real
import unittest


def nonnegative(value, name):
    if isinstance(value, bool) or not isinstance(value, Real):
        raise TypeError(f'{name} must be a real number, not bool')
    try:
        result = float(value)
    except OverflowError as exc:
        raise ValueError(f'{name} outside float range') from exc
    if not math.isfinite(result) or result < 0:
        raise ValueError(f'{name} must be finite and nonnegative')
    return result


def simple_interest(principal, rate, time):
    """Return (interest, maturity amount); no interim payments or rounding."""
    p = nonnegative(principal, 'principal')
    r = nonnegative(rate, 'rate')
    t = nonnegative(time, 'time')
    if p == 0 or r == 0 or t == 0:
        return 0.0, p
    interest = p*r*t
    amount = p+interest
    if not math.isfinite(interest) or not math.isfinite(amount):
        raise ValueError('result outside supported float range')
    if interest == 0:
        raise ValueError('nonzero interest underflowed to zero')
    return interest, amount


def year_fraction(days, basis):
    """Caller supplies elapsed days; fixed illustrative denominator only."""
    if isinstance(days, bool) or not isinstance(days, int):
        raise TypeError('days must be an integer, not bool')
    if days < 0:
        raise ValueError('days must be nonnegative')
    if isinstance(basis, bool) or not isinstance(basis, int):
        raise TypeError('basis must be an integer')
    if basis not in (360, 365):
        raise ValueError('supported fixed bases are 360 and 365')
    return days/basis


class SimpleInterestTests(unittest.TestCase):
    def test_01_reference_case(self):
        self.assertEqual(simple_interest(2400,.06,1.5),(216,2616))

    def test_02_matching_monthly_units(self):
        annual = simple_interest(2400,.06,1.5)
        monthly = simple_interest(2400,.005,18)
        self.assertAlmostEqual(annual[0],monthly[0])
        self.assertAlmostEqual(annual[1],monthly[1])

    def test_03_fixed_day_bases(self):
        self.assertAlmostEqual(simple_interest(10000,.06,year_fraction(90,360))[0],150)
        self.assertAlmostEqual(simple_interest(10000,.06,year_fraction(90,365))[0],147.94520547945206)

    def test_04_zero_boundaries(self):
        self.assertEqual(simple_interest(2400,0,1.5),(0,2400))
        self.assertEqual(simple_interest(2400,.06,0),(0,2400))
        self.assertEqual(simple_interest(0,.06,1.5),(0,0))

    def test_05_additive_interest_not_rollover(self):
        first = simple_interest(1000,.05,1)[0]
        second = simple_interest(1000,.05,1)[0]
        total = simple_interest(1000,.05,2)
        self.assertEqual(first+second,total[0])
        rolled = simple_interest(simple_interest(1000,.05,1)[1],.05,1)[1]
        self.assertEqual(total[1],1100)
        self.assertEqual(rolled,1102.5)
        self.assertNotEqual(rolled,total[1])

    def test_06_scalar_input_rejection(self):
        for field in range(3):
            for bad in [True,'1',None]:
                args=[2400,.06,1.5];args[field]=bad
                with self.assertRaises(TypeError): simple_interest(*args)
            for bad in [-1,float('nan'),float('inf')]:
                args=[2400,.06,1.5];args[field]=bad
                with self.assertRaises(ValueError): simple_interest(*args)

    def test_07_day_input_rejection(self):
        for bad in [True,90.5,'90']:
            with self.assertRaises(TypeError): year_fraction(bad,360)
        with self.assertRaises(ValueError): year_fraction(-1,360)
        with self.assertRaises(ValueError): year_fraction(90,366)
        with self.assertRaises(TypeError): year_fraction(90,True)

    def test_08_numerical_range_and_maturity_identity(self):
        with self.assertRaises(ValueError): simple_interest(1e308,10,2)
        with self.assertRaises(ValueError): simple_interest(5e-324,.1,.1)
        with self.assertRaises(ValueError): simple_interest(10**1000,.05,1)
        interest,amount=simple_interest(3500,.042,2.25)
        self.assertAlmostEqual(amount-3500,interest)
        self.assertAlmostEqual(interest,330.75)


if __name__ == '__main__':
    interest,amount=simple_interest(2400,.06,1.5)
    print(f'Interest: {interest:.2f}; maturity amount: {amount:.2f}')
    unittest.main(verbosity=2)
```


## Practice with answers

2. Calculate interest on 8,000 at 3% annually for six months, explicitly treating six months as half a year. **Answer:** 120; maturity amount 8,120.

4. With 1,000 at 5% simple annually, how much interest accrues during year 2 if the original principal is unchanged? **Answer:** 50. A result of 52.50 implies that the first year's interest became part of the interest-bearing base.
5. Does 216 of interest over 18 months mean a 9% annual rate? **Answer:** no. It is 216/2,400 = 9% over the full 1.5-year term; dividing by 1.5 recovers the stated 6% annual simple rate.
