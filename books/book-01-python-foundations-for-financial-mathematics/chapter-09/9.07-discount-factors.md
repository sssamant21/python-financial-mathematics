## Separate the factor from the cash amount

Section 9.5 calculated the present value of a single future payment F. Write that calculation as

```math
PV=F\,D(0,n),\qquad D(0,n)=\frac{1}{(1+i)^n}.
```

D(0,n) is the **discount factor** from payment time n to valuation time 0 under a constant effective periodic rate i. It is dimensionless. F and PV retain the same currency unit while referring to different dates. The California Board of Equalization's present-worth lesson gives the reciprocal relationship between this factor and the compound accumulation factor [1].

A factor answers: “What is the model-equivalent value at the valuation date of one unit paid at the payment date?” Once the dates and rate convention are fixed, the same factor scales different signed amounts. It is not itself a dollar payment.

This section assumes deterministic payments, one currency, whole matching periods, constant effective rates, and no fees, taxes, default, or intermediate external payments. Factors are computed from supplied assumptions, not calibrated to market instruments.

## An original factor grid

| 1 | 0.961538462 | 4,807.69 |

Each row is a separate single-payment illustration. The table does not describe four promised payments of 5,000. Multiplying 5,000 by the unrounded year-3 factor gives approximately 4,444.981793354574. Multiplying −5,000 by that same factor gives the negative equivalent from the payer's viewpoint.

Displayed factors are rounded for readability. Do not use these shortened values as new inputs when full precision is available. The Python lab retains its float factors and rounds only its printed output.

## Reciprocal accumulation

Define A(0,n)=(1+i)^n. Then

```math
D(0,n)A(0,n)=1.
```

If a future payment is first discounted and its present equivalent is then accumulated through the same periods at the same rate, the original payment is recovered mathematically. The executable check uses a floating-point tolerance.

The rate i=.04 is not the discount factor. Nor is the factor simply 1−i. For one period, D=1/1.04≈.961538462; 1−.04=.96 is a different number and does not invert multiplication by 1.04. For three periods, use the full compound denominator, not 1−3i.

Similarly, a factor of .90 means that one future currency unit has a present equivalent of .90 under the specified dates and rule. It does not mean an annual effective rate of 10%. Inferring a rate requires the term and a rate convention, developed in Section 9.13.

## Valuation date and payment date

A factor needs two dates. Under this constant-rate model, for payment time t and valuation time s with t≥s,

```math
D(s,t)=(1+i)^{-(t-s)}.
```

For a payment at year 5 valued at year 2, use three elapsed years. At 4%, D(2,5)=1.04^(-3)≈.888996359. Discounting a year-5 payment directly to year 2 therefore uses a different factor from discounting it all the way to year 0.

The common-origin grid yields the same interval factor through a ratio:

```math
D(s,t)=\frac{D(0,t)}{D(0,s)}.
```

The order of that ratio matters. For example,

```math
\frac{D(0,5)}{D(0,2)}=1.04^{-3},
```

whereas reversing the ratio produces the accumulation factor over those three periods.

For dates 0≤s≤t, consistent factors satisfy

```math
D(0,s)D(s,t)=D(0,t).
```

In a constant-rate model, this also gives D(0,m+n)=D(0,m)D(0,n). This last duration-only identity relies on using the same rate for both intervals. Do not extend it to arbitrary independently quoted maturity rates without deriving consistent interval factors. The lab implements the constant-rate case only; it does not construct a yield curve or infer future borrowing terms.

## Factors below, equal to, or above one

The supported domain is i>−1 and nonnegative whole periods.

| Assumptions | Factor behavior |
|---|---|
| Zero elapsed periods | D(s,s)=1 |
| Zero rate | D(s,t)=1 |
| Positive rate and positive elapsed term | 0<D(s,t)<1 |
| −1<i<0 and positive elapsed term | D(s,t)>1 |

For a fixed positive rate, later payment dates have smaller factors when the valuation date is fixed. For a fixed positive term, increasing the rate decreases the factor throughout i>−1. At a negative rate, extending the term increases the factor instead. The reference's positive-rate presentation should therefore not be read as an unconditional less-than-one statement.

A factor above one under a negative rate is not a computational error. At −2% for one period, a future unit discounts to 1/.98≈1.020408163. The factor still remains positive. Rates at or below −100% are excluded from this positive-accumulation model.

## A factor is not a probability

Discount factors do not describe mutually exclusive outcomes, and a grid of them is not expected to sum to one. The four factors in the 4% table sum to approximately 3.775091033, which has no probability-normalization role. A negative-rate factor can also exceed one, reinforcing the distinction.

Do not normalize the grid to make it sum to one: that would change the valuation multipliers. Likewise, plotting or tabulating a factor curve does not establish payment certainty or market calibration. Currency, valuation date, payment date, rate convention, and payment assumptions remain essential metadata.

Rate and period units must match. An effective annual rate is paired with years; an effective monthly rate with months. Nominal quotes, fractional periods, day counts, changing rates, and market term structures require explicit extensions. Financial discounting also differs from an inflation adjustment, which changes purchasing-power units rather than applying this valuation rule.

`discount_factor` computes a common-origin factor; `discount_between` makes both period coordinates explicit; `discounted_payment` applies the factor to a signed amount. The helpers reject booleans, nonfinite numeric inputs, rates at or below −1, negative/noninteger periods, and reversed valuation/payment dates. Date coordinates are integer period labels, not parsed calendar dates.

The direct reciprocal calculation is intentionally simple. It detects unsupported intermediate overflow, complete underflow, nonfinite factors, and nonfinite or completely underflowed monetary outputs. Factor-first arithmetic can reject extreme inputs that another arrangement might support. Very small rates can disappear in 1+i, nonzero subnormal values can lose relative accuracy, and these checks do not guarantee arbitrary-range accuracy. Exact settlement and contractual rounding require a separate policy.

```python
"""Discount factors under one constant effective periodic rate."""
import math
from numbers import Real
import unittest


def finite_real(value,name):
    if isinstance(value,bool) or not isinstance(value,Real):
        raise TypeError(f'{name} must be real, not bool')
    try:
        value=float(value)
    except OverflowError as exc:
        raise ValueError(f'{name} outside float range') from exc
    if not math.isfinite(value):
        raise ValueError(f'{name} must be finite')
    return value


def whole_period(value,name):
    if isinstance(value,bool) or not isinstance(value,int):
        raise TypeError(f'{name} must be an integer, not bool')
    if value < 0:
        raise ValueError(f'{name} must be nonnegative')
    return value


def discount_factor(rate,periods):
    r=finite_real(rate,'rate'); n=whole_period(periods,'periods')
    if r <= -1:
        raise ValueError('rate must exceed -1')
    if r == 0 or n == 0:
        return 1.0
    try:
        accumulation=(1+r)**n
        if accumulation <= 0 or not math.isfinite(accumulation):
            raise ValueError('accumulation outside supported float range')
        factor=1/accumulation
    except OverflowError as exc:
        raise ValueError('factor outside supported float range') from exc
    if factor <= 0 or not math.isfinite(factor):
        raise ValueError('factor outside supported float range')
    return factor


def discount_between(rate,*,valuation,payment):
    s=whole_period(valuation,'valuation'); t=whole_period(payment,'payment')
    if t < s:
        raise ValueError('payment must be at or after valuation')
    return discount_factor(rate,t-s)


def discounted_payment(amount,rate,periods):
    amount=finite_real(amount,'amount')
    value=amount*discount_factor(rate,periods)
    if not math.isfinite(value) or (amount != 0 and value == 0):
        raise ValueError('value outside supported float range')
    return value


class DiscountFactorTests(unittest.TestCase):
    def test_01_reference_grid(self):
        expected=[1,.9615384615384615,.9245562130177514,.8889963586709148]
        for n,target in enumerate(expected):
            self.assertAlmostEqual(discount_factor(.04,n),target)

    def test_02_payment_scaling_and_sign(self):
        self.assertAlmostEqual(discounted_payment(5000,.04,3),4444.981793354574)
        self.assertAlmostEqual(discounted_payment(-5000,.04,3),-4444.981793354574)
        self.assertEqual(discounted_payment(0,.04,3),0)

    def test_03_reciprocal(self):
        for rate in [.04,0,-.02]:
            self.assertAlmostEqual(discount_factor(rate,5)*(1+rate)**5,1)

    def test_04_composition_and_ratio(self):
        direct=discount_between(.04,valuation=2,payment=5)
        self.assertAlmostEqual(direct,discount_factor(.04,5)/discount_factor(.04,2))
        self.assertAlmostEqual(discount_factor(.04,2)*direct,discount_factor(.04,5))

    def test_05_zero_and_negative_rate_boundaries(self):
        self.assertEqual(discount_factor(.04,0),1)
        self.assertEqual(discount_factor(0,100),1)
        self.assertGreater(discount_factor(-.02,3),1)
        self.assertEqual(discount_between(.04,valuation=3,payment=3),1)

    def test_06_positive_rate_ordering_not_probability(self):
        factors=[discount_factor(.04,n) for n in range(4)]
        self.assertTrue(all(a>b for a,b in zip(factors,factors[1:])))
        self.assertGreater(discount_factor(.02,3),discount_factor(.06,3))
        self.assertNotAlmostEqual(sum(factors),1)

    def test_07_invalid_inputs(self):
        for args in [(True,3),('0.04',3),(.04,True),(.04,3.5)]:
            with self.assertRaises(TypeError): discount_factor(*args)
        for args in [(float('nan'),3),(float('inf'),3),(-1,3),(-1.2,3),(.04,-1)]:
            with self.assertRaises(ValueError): discount_factor(*args)
        with self.assertRaises(ValueError): discount_between(.04,valuation=3,payment=2)
        with self.assertRaises(TypeError): discount_between(.04,valuation=True,payment=2)
        with self.assertRaises(ValueError): discounted_payment(float('nan'),.04,3)

    def test_08_numerical_limits(self):
        for args in [(1,2000),(-.5,2000),(10**1000,3)]:
            with self.assertRaises(ValueError): discount_factor(*args)
        with self.assertRaises(ValueError): discounted_payment(5e-324,1,1)
        with self.assertRaises(ValueError): discounted_payment(1e308,-.5,2)


if __name__=='__main__':
    print('Periods | Factor at 4% | PV of 5000 (illustrative USD)')
    for n in range(4):
        print(f'{n} | {discount_factor(.04,n):.9f} | {discounted_payment(5000,.04,n):.2f}')
    unittest.main(verbosity=2)
```

## Practice with answers

1. Calculate D(0,2) at 10% effective annually and apply it to a payment of 1,210 at year 2. **Answer:** factor 1/1.21≈.826446281; present equivalent 1,000.
2. Discount a year-4 payment to year 1 at 5% effective annually. **Answer:** D(1,4)=1.05^(-3)≈.863837599. There are three elapsed years.
3. What is the factor for an immediate payment at a valid nonzero rate? **Answer:** one; no time elapses.
4. Should a factor grid be divided by its own sum before multiplying cash flows? **Answer:** no. It is a set of date-specific valuation multipliers, not a probability distribution.
5. A future obligation is −2,000 and the applicable factor is .95. What is its signed equivalent? **Answer:** −1,900. No purchase transaction or compensating receipt is automatically created.
