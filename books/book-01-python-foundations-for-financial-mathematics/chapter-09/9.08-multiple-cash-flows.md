## Translate each amount before summing

A schedule contains amounts C_j occurring at times t_j. The index j identifies the event; t_j identifies its date. They need not be equal, and distinct events can share a date.

For a constant effective rate i and valuation date 0, each event contributes

```math
PV_j=\frac{C_j}{(1+i)^{t_j}}.
```

The schedule's equivalent at time 0 is

```math
V_0=\sum_j C_jD(0,t_j)
=\sum_j\frac{C_j}{(1+i)^{t_j}}.
```

Each term is now expressed in the same currency at the same valuation date. This provides the reason for summing the translated amounts rather than summing the original differently dated payments first. The California State Board of Equalization's present-worth lesson includes applying present-value calculations to multiple payments [1].

Use one declared currency, a single viewpoint, deterministic amounts, nonnegative whole-period dates, and a constant effective rate greater than −1. Rate and period units must match. Fees are included only when explicitly supplied as events. Taxes, payment uncertainty, changing rates, calendar conventions, and currency conversion are outside this model.

## An original signed schedule

Consider the following synthetic investor receipts and fee, denominated in illustrative USD. One period is one year. The schedule deliberately contains two separate events at year 3 and no time-0 acquisition price.

| Event ID | Year | Signed amount, USD | Meaning |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| income1 | 1 | +300 | Income receipt |
| fee2 | 2 | −40 | Fee payment |
| income3 | 3 | +120 | Later income receipt |
| principal3 | 3 | +900 | Principal receipt |

At an assumed 5% effective annual rate,

```math
V_0=\frac{300}{1.05}-\frac{40}{1.05^2}
+\frac{120}{1.05^3}+\frac{900}{1.05^3}.
```

| Event | Factor to time 0, displayed | Equivalent at time 0, USD, displayed |
|---|---:|---:|
| income1 | .952380952 | +285.71 |
| fee2 | .907029478 | −36.28 |
| income3 | .863837599 | +103.66 |
| principal3 | .863837599 | +777.45 |

Using unrounded terms, the total is approximately **1,130.55 USD at time 0**. The original signed amounts sum to 1,280 USD. That undiscounted sum does not have the same valuation meaning. Also, adding individually rounded display values can differ from rounding the unrounded total; calculate with full precision and format afterward.

The time-0 equivalent is a valuation result. It is not an additional cash receipt today, and the schedule does not automatically imply an investment purchase at that amount. Section 9.9 will introduce net present value with explicit investment costs and interpretation.

## Same-date netting and event retention

The two year-3 payments can be combined mathematically because they share the same factor:

```math
120D(0,3)+900D(0,3)=1020D(0,3).
```

This is linearity at a common date, not permission to combine the year-1 and year-3 amounts before discounting. Retain separate event IDs and descriptions in the source ledger even when a period-total view nets them. A receipt and payment can cancel in the total while remaining economically distinct events.

The lab sorts validated events by period and ID for reproducible presentation. Reversing input order leaves the result unchanged on the checked fixture. Sorting within a period by ID does not infer an intrayear transaction sequence; all events with that label are modeled as simultaneous.

## A common future or intermediate date

At a target date T, translate every payment using its own elapsed interval:

```math
V_T=\sum_j C_j(1+i)^{T-t_j}.
```

If t_j<T, the term accumulates forward. If t_j>T, it discounts backward. If t_j=T, its factor is one. This unified equation follows by multiplying V_0 by the common accumulation factor:

```math
V_T=V_0(1+i)^T.
```

For the example at year 3,

```math
V_3=300(1.05)^2-40(1.05)+120+900=1308.75.
```

The year-1 receipt has two years of accumulation, the year-2 fee has one, and the year-3 receipts have none. Multiplying each event by the same three-year factor would incorrectly ignore its original date.

At year 2, the year-1 receipt accumulates one period, the fee stays at its own date, and both year-3 receipts discount one period. The lab verifies that this intermediate equivalent also agrees with V_0(1.05)^2.

These are common-date equivalents under the same constant rate. They are not automatically actual account balances. Interpreting a terminal equivalent as funds available from reinvested receipts and financed payments requires the assumed accumulation/financing treatment to be achievable. The code neither establishes market availability nor models separate lending and borrowing rates.

## Completeness, zero, and signs

The supplied event schedule is assumed complete for its stated scope. An omitted date means no event only under that explicit completeness assumption. A file missing a promised payment, an unknown amount, or an unresolved date must be corrected or represented in a richer uncertainty model before valuation. Successful execution cannot prove that the author supplied every event.

An explicit zero amount is supported. An unknown amount such as None or NaN is rejected. An empty schedule returns zero as the value of an explicitly empty schedule, not as evidence of no activity in an unexamined external account.

Choose a viewpoint once: positive receipts and negative payments. Reversing every sign reverses V_T. Do not apply absolute values before summation. For mixed-sign schedules, increasing the discount rate need not decrease the total: individual positive and negative contributions move in opposite signed directions. A monotonic statement about one positive payment does not automatically apply to their net sum.

All records must be in the same declared currency and period unit. The simple Flow record does not carry or validate currency metadata, so the author must normalize and verify denomination before constructing records. A production ingestion layer needs explicit source metadata rather than relying on this input contract alone.

The function returns an audit row per event: ID, period, signed source amount, factor to the requested target, and equivalent amount. It validates records explicitly; dataclass annotations alone do not enforce incoming field types. Duplicate event IDs are rejected, while different IDs at one period are valid. The function materializes and sorts its input, so time and memory increase with the number of events.

`math.fsum` reduces summation error relative to a naive running float sum [2]. It does not undo errors in computed factors or guarantee exact monetary settlement. Individual factors and equivalents are checked for selected numerical range failures; factor-first arithmetic can reject extreme cases that another arrangement might support. Very small rates can disappear in 1+i, subnormal outputs can lose relative accuracy, and cancellation can leave a total with large relative error. A zero total can legitimately arise from signed cancellation. Specify appropriate precision and contractual rounding for broader requirements.

```python
"""Audit each signed event at a common date under one constant rate."""
from dataclasses import dataclass
import math
from numbers import Real
import unittest


@dataclass(frozen=True)
class Flow:
    event_id: str
    period: int
    amount: float


def real(value,name):
    if isinstance(value,bool) or not isinstance(value,Real):
        raise TypeError(f'{name} must be real, not bool')
    try:
        value=float(value)
    except OverflowError as exc:
        raise ValueError(f'{name} outside float range') from exc
    if not math.isfinite(value):
        raise ValueError(f'{name} must be finite')
    return value


def whole(value,name):
    if isinstance(value,bool) or not isinstance(value,int):
        raise TypeError(f'{name} must be an integer, not bool')
    if value < 0:
        raise ValueError(f'{name} must be nonnegative')
    return value


def value_flows(flows,rate,*,target=0):
    """Return (event audit rows, total equivalent); one declared currency."""
    r=real(rate,'rate'); target=whole(target,'target')
    if r <= -1:
        raise ValueError('rate must exceed -1')
    seen=set(); checked=[]
    for flow in flows:
        if not isinstance(flow,Flow):
            raise TypeError('records must be Flow objects')
        if not isinstance(flow.event_id,str) or not flow.event_id.strip():
            raise ValueError('event ID must be nonblank text')
        if flow.event_id in seen:
            raise ValueError('duplicate event ID')
        seen.add(flow.event_id)
        t=whole(flow.period,'period'); amount=real(flow.amount,'amount')
        checked.append((flow.event_id,t,amount))
    rows=[]
    for event_id,t,amount in sorted(checked,key=lambda x:(x[1],x[0])):
        delta=target-t
        try:
            accumulation=(1+r)**abs(delta)
            if not math.isfinite(accumulation) or accumulation <= 0:
                raise ValueError('accumulation outside supported float range')
            factor=accumulation if delta >= 0 else 1/accumulation
            equivalent=amount*factor
        except OverflowError as exc:
            raise ValueError('valuation outside supported float range') from exc
        if not math.isfinite(factor) or factor <= 0 or not math.isfinite(equivalent):
            raise ValueError('valuation outside supported float range')
        if amount != 0 and equivalent == 0:
            raise ValueError('nonzero event underflowed to zero')
        rows.append((event_id,t,amount,factor,equivalent))
    try:
        total=math.fsum(row[4] for row in rows)
    except OverflowError as exc:
        raise ValueError('total outside supported float range') from exc
    if not math.isfinite(total):
        raise ValueError('total must be finite')
    return rows,total


def fixture():
    return [Flow('income1',1,300),Flow('fee2',2,-40),
            Flow('principal3',3,900),Flow('income3',3,120)]


class MultipleFlowTests(unittest.TestCase):
    def test_01_manual_pv(self):
        rows,total=value_flows(fixture(),.05)
        self.assertEqual(len(rows),4)
        self.assertAlmostEqual(total,300/1.05-40/1.05**2+1020/1.05**3)

    def test_02_common_target_identity(self):
        _,pv=value_flows(fixture(),.05)
        _,fv=value_flows(fixture(),.05,target=3)
        self.assertAlmostEqual(fv,300*1.05**2-40*1.05+1020)
        self.assertAlmostEqual(fv,pv*1.05**3)
        _,middle=value_flows(fixture(),.05,target=2)
        self.assertAlmostEqual(middle,pv*1.05**2)

    def test_03_order_and_same_date_netting(self):
        rows,a=value_flows(fixture(),.05)
        reverse,b=value_flows(reversed(fixture()),.05)
        _,net=value_flows([Flow('a',1,300),Flow('b',2,-40),Flow('c',3,1020)],.05)
        self.assertEqual(rows,reverse)
        self.assertAlmostEqual(a,b); self.assertAlmostEqual(a,net)
        self.assertEqual(sum(row[1]==3 for row in rows),2)

    def test_04_time_zero_and_empty_schedule(self):
        self.assertEqual(value_flows([],.05),([],0))
        rows,total=value_flows([Flow('now',0,-1000)],.05)
        self.assertEqual(rows[0][3],1); self.assertEqual(total,-1000)

    def test_05_zero_and_negative_rates(self):
        self.assertEqual(value_flows(fixture(),0)[1],1280)
        self.assertGreater(value_flows([Flow('x',2,100)],-.02)[1],100)
        self.assertEqual(value_flows([Flow('zero',2,0)],.05)[1],0)

    def test_06_perspective_and_cancellation(self):
        opposite=[Flow(f.event_id,f.period,-f.amount) for f in fixture()]
        self.assertAlmostEqual(value_flows(opposite,.05)[1],-value_flows(fixture(),.05)[1])
        rows,total=value_flows([Flow('a',2,10),Flow('b',2,-10)],.05)
        self.assertEqual(len(rows),2); self.assertEqual(total,0)

    def test_07_invalid_and_missing_inputs(self):
        for bad in [True,'0.05']:
            with self.assertRaises(TypeError): value_flows([],bad)
        for bad in [-1,float('nan')]:
            with self.assertRaises(ValueError): value_flows([],bad)
        with self.assertRaises(ValueError): value_flows([fixture()[0]]*2,.05)
        with self.assertRaises(TypeError): value_flows([Flow('x',1,None)],.05)
        with self.assertRaises(ValueError): value_flows([Flow('x',1,float('nan'))],.05)
        with self.assertRaises(TypeError): value_flows([Flow('x',True,1)],.05)
        with self.assertRaises(ValueError): value_flows([Flow('x',-1,1)],.05)
        with self.assertRaises(TypeError): value_flows([],.05,target=True)
        with self.assertRaises(TypeError): value_flows([{}],.05)

    def test_08_range_failures_and_careful_sum(self):
        for flows,rate,target in [([Flow('x',2000,1)],1,0),([Flow('x',2000,1)],-.5,0),
                                  ([Flow('x',0,1e308)],1,2),([Flow('x',1,5e-324)],1,0)]:
            with self.assertRaises(ValueError): value_flows(flows,rate,target=target)
        _,total=value_flows([Flow('a',0,1e16),Flow('b',0,1),Flow('c',0,-1e16)],0)
        self.assertEqual(total,1)


if __name__=='__main__':
    rows,total=value_flows(fixture(),.05)
    print('Event | Period | Amount | Factor to t=0 | Equivalent (USD)')
    for event_id,t,amount,factor,equivalent in rows:
        print(f'{event_id} | {t} | {amount:+.2f} | {factor:.9f} | {equivalent:+.2f}')
    print(f'Total at t=0: {total:.2f} USD')
    print(f'Total at t=3: {value_flows(fixture(),.05,target=3)[1]:.2f} USD')
    unittest.main(verbosity=2)
```

## Practice with answers

1. Receive 110 at year 1 and 121 at year 2. Value both at time 0 using 10% effective annually. **Answer:** 100+100=200.
2. Value the same schedule at year 2. **Answer:** 110(1.1)+121=242; also 200(1.1)^2=242.
3. At year 2, receive 150 and pay 50 under the same currency and factor assumptions. **Answer:** the net is 100 at year 2, but retain both source events for audit.
4. A year-1 receipt and a year-3 payment have equal nominal amounts. Can they be deleted as a cancelling pair? **Answer:** no. Their valuation factors differ unless the chosen rule happens to make them equal; retain both dates and signs.
5. Why does the example's 1,130.55 present equivalent not by itself establish a profitable investment? **Answer:** the acquisition price and complete investment costs have not been supplied, and the valuation rate is an assumption.
