## Learning objectives

Calculate covariance and Pearson correlation from aligned returns; construct covariance matrices from volatilities and correlations; quantify correlation's effect on portfolio risk; distinguish correlation from independence and causation; and evaluate correlation instability, positive semidefiniteness, and diversification under stress.

## 1. Why correlation matters

Diversification is determined not only by individual asset volatility but also by how asset returns move together. Correlation summarizes the strength and direction of **linear** co-movement.

For random returns X and Y with finite, strictly positive variances:

**ρ(X,Y)=Cov(X,Y)/(σ_Xσ_Y).**

Pearson correlation lies between −1 and +1. It is undefined when either variable has zero variance.

Correlation is dimensionless, unlike covariance, and permits comparison across return series with different scales.

## 2. Covariance versus correlation

Covariance is:

**Cov(X,Y)=E[(X−E[X])(Y−E[Y])].**

Correlation normalizes covariance:

**ρ=Cov(X,Y)/(σ_Xσ_Y).**

Equivalently:

**Cov(X,Y)=ρσ_Xσ_Y.**

Example: if σ_A=.20, σ_B=.30, and ρ=.50, covariance is:

**Cov(A,B)=.5(.2)(.3)=.03.**

This is covariance in squared decimal-return units for the chosen horizon.

## 3. Interpreting correlation

| Correlation | Interpretation |
|---|---|
| +1 | Perfect positive linear relationship |
| +0.5 | Positive linear co-movement |
| 0 | No linear correlation |
| −0.5 | Negative linear co-movement |
| −1 | Perfect negative linear relationship |

These descriptions are not statements about causation. **Zero correlation does not imply independence**, except under additional distributional assumptions, such as jointly Gaussian variables.

Correlation can also miss nonlinear relationships and tail dependence.

## 4. Sample covariance and sample correlation

Given T aligned observations of X and Y, with T≥2:

**s_XY=Σₜ(Xₜ−X̄)(Yₜ−Ȳ)/(T−1).**

Sample variances are calculated with the same denominator convention. Then:

**r_XY=s_XY/(s_Xs_Y).**

Use the same observation dates and return convention for both series. Correlating raw prices rather than returns can introduce misleading trends.

## 5. Worked sample calculation

Let:

```text
X = [1, 2, 3]
Y = [2, 4, 6]
```

The sample means are 2 and 4. The centered values are [−1,0,1] and [−2,0,2].

The sample covariance is:

**s_XY=[(−1)(−2)+0+(1)(2)]/(3−1)=2.**

The sample variances are **s_X²=1** and **s_Y²=4**, so:

**r_XY=2/(1×2)=1.**

The series exhibit perfect positive sample linear correlation.

## 6. Correlation and two-asset portfolio variance

For weights w_A and w_B:

**σₚ²=w_A²σ_A²+w_B²σ_B²+2w_Aw_Bσ_Aσ_Bρ_AB.**

With equal 50/50 weights and σ_A=σ_B=.2:

**σₚ²=.02+.02ρ_AB.**

| Correlation | Variance | Volatility |
|---|---:|---:|
| +1 | .04 | 20% |
| 0 | .02 | 14.14% |
| −1 | 0 | 0% |

For positive weights, lower correlation reduces portfolio variance when all other inputs are fixed. Short positions can reverse this sensitivity.

## 7. Correlation sensitivity

Differentiate the two-asset variance with respect to correlation:

**∂σₚ²/∂ρ_AB=2w_Aw_Bσ_Aσ_B.**

With w_A=w_B=.5, σ_A=.2, and σ_B=.3:

**∂σₚ²/∂ρ=.03.**

An increase in correlation of .1 increases portfolio variance by .003, holding weights and standalone volatilities fixed.

## 8. Correlation matrices

For n assets, the correlation matrix **C** contains entries:

**Cᵢⱼ=ρ(Rᵢ,Rⱼ).**

A valid correlation matrix is symmetric, has ones on its diagonal for nondegenerate return series, and is positive semidefinite.

Let **D=diag(σ₁,…,σₙ)**. The covariance matrix is:

**Σ=DCD.**

This transformation separates standalone volatility from the correlation structure.

## 9. Why positive semidefiniteness matters

A covariance matrix must satisfy:

**aᵀΣa≥0** for every vector a.

Similarly, a valid correlation matrix must be positive semidefinite. A collection of pairwise correlations individually between −1 and +1 is **not sufficient** to ensure a valid joint correlation matrix.

For example, three assets cannot all have common pairwise correlation −.75. The equicorrelation lower bound for three assets is −.5.

Invalid correlation matrices can yield impossible negative portfolio variances.

## 10. Equal-correlation model

Suppose N assets have identical volatility σ, identical pairwise correlation ρ, and equal weights.

Then:

**σₚ²=σ²[ρ+(1−ρ)/N].**

For N=5, σ=.2, and ρ=.25:

**σₚ²=.04[.25+.75/5]=.016.**

**σₚ≈12.65%.**

As N grows, the idiosyncratic component declines, while the shared correlation component remains.

For ρ≥0:

**lim₍N→∞₎σₚ²=σ²ρ.**

With σ=.2 and ρ=.25, the limiting volatility is 10%.

## 11. Feasible equicorrelation bounds

For N≥2, the equal-correlation matrix is positive semidefinite exactly when:

**−1/(N−1)≤ρ≤1.**

For N=3, the lower bound is −.5. For N=4, it is −1/3.

The lower bound approaches zero from below as N increases. It is impossible for arbitrarily many assets to maintain a fixed, strictly negative common pairwise correlation.

## 12. Correlation versus diversification

Low correlation can improve volatility diversification, but it is not the same as low total portfolio risk.

A low-correlation asset with extremely high standalone volatility can still increase risk, depending on its weight. A portfolio can also remain exposed to common economic factors even when pairwise sample correlations look moderate.

Assess diversification through covariance, weights, factor exposures, and stress outcomes—not correlation alone.

## 13. Rolling correlations and regime changes

Correlation estimates depend on the observation window. A 60-day rolling estimate may differ substantially from a 252-day estimate.

During market stress, correlations among risky assets may increase. Historical diversification may therefore be weaker precisely when it is most needed.

Useful monitoring practices include rolling correlation matrices, correlation heatmaps, regime comparisons, and stress scenarios that raise selected correlations toward one while preserving a valid covariance structure.

## 14. Missing data and estimation artifacts

Correlation estimation requires synchronized return observations. Pairwise deletion of missing observations can result in each correlation being calculated from a different sample.

The resulting matrix may fail to be positive semidefinite. A common aligned sample avoids some inconsistencies but can reduce the number of usable observations.

Document missing-data rules, observation counts, and covariance estimation methods. For production systems, consider PSD-aware covariance estimators or carefully justified matrix repair.

## 15. Outliers, nonlinear dependence, and tail risk

Pearson correlation is sensitive to extreme observations and measures only linear dependence. Two assets can have near-zero Pearson correlation but still be strongly dependent through nonlinear relationships.

Correlation also does not directly describe joint extreme losses. Stress testing and tail-dependence analysis can complement ordinary covariance-based portfolio risk.

## 16. Currency, horizon, and return definitions

Asset returns must be aligned in currency, frequency, and valuation timestamps. A foreign asset's base-currency return incorporates both the local asset return and the exchange-rate movement.

Daily correlation need not equal monthly correlation. Simple and log returns can also differ materially for large moves.

Consistency of inputs is essential before interpreting diversification results.

It includes **12 reference unit tests**.

```python
import math
import unittest
from fractions import Fraction
from decimal import Decimal, localcontext

def exact(x):
    if type(x) not in (int,float):
        raise ValueError("finite int/float required; booleans excluded")
    try:
        if not math.isfinite(x):
            raise ValueError("finite int/float required")
    except OverflowError as exc:
        raise ValueError("input outside finite float range") from exc
    return Fraction(x) if type(x) is int else Fraction(str(x))

def report(x):
    try:
        value=float(x)
    except (OverflowError,ValueError) as exc:
        raise ValueError("unreportable numeric result") from exc
    if not math.isfinite(value) or (x != 0 and value == 0):
        raise ValueError("unreportable numeric result")
    return value

def root(x):
    with localcontext() as ctx:
        ctx.prec=80
        return (Decimal(x.numerator)/Decimal(x.denominator)).sqrt()

def psd(a):
    # Exact Schur complements; a zero pivot must have an all-zero row.
    a=[row[:] for row in a]
    for k in range(len(a)):
        pivot=a[k][k]
        if pivot<0:
            return False
        if pivot==0:
            if any(a[k][j]!=0 for j in range(k+1,len(a))):
                return False
            continue
        for i in range(k+1,len(a)):
            for j in range(i,len(a)):
                a[i][j]-=a[i][k]*a[k][j]/pivot
                a[j][i]=a[i][j]
    return True

def _covariance(cov,tolerance=1e-10):
    relative=exact(tolerance)
    if relative<0 or relative>Fraction(1,10**6):
        raise ValueError("relative tolerance must be in [0,1e-6]")
    n=None
    if not isinstance(cov,(list,tuple)) or not cov:
        raise ValueError("nonempty square covariance required")
    if n is None:
        n=len(cov)
    if len(cov)!=n or any(not isinstance(row,(list,tuple)) or len(row)!=n for row in cov):
        raise ValueError("square aligned covariance required")
    a=[[exact(x) for x in row] for row in cov]
    if any(a[i][i]<0 for i in range(n)):
        raise ValueError("negative asset variance")
    scale=max(a[i][i] for i in range(n))
    tolerance=scale*relative
    for i in range(n):
        for j in range(i+1,n):
            if abs(a[i][j]-a[j][i])>max(abs(a[i][j]),abs(a[j][i]))*relative+tolerance:
                raise ValueError("covariance must be symmetric within stated tolerance")
            a[i][j]=a[j][i]=(a[i][j]+a[j][i])/2
    if any(a[i][i]==0 and any(a[i][j]!=0 for j in range(n)) for i in range(n)):
        raise ValueError("zero variance requires zero covariance row")
    shifted=[[a[i][j]+(tolerance if i==j else 0) for j in range(n)] for i in range(n)]
    if not psd(shifted):
        raise ValueError("covariance is not PSD within scale-relative tolerance")
    return a,tolerance

def _numbers(xs,label):
    if not isinstance(xs,(list,tuple)) or not xs:
        raise ValueError(label+" must be a nonempty list/tuple")
    return [exact(x) for x in xs]

def _samples(x,y):
    x=_numbers(x,"x");y=_numbers(y,"y")
    if len(x)!=len(y) or len(x)<2:
        raise ValueError("aligned samples with at least two observations required")
    return x,y

def _centered_covariance(x,y):
    mx=sum(x)/len(x);my=sum(y)/len(y)
    return sum((a-mx)*(b-my) for a,b in zip(x,y))/(len(x)-1)

def sample_covariance(x,y):
    return report(_centered_covariance(*_samples(x,y)))

def _correlation_from_series(x,y):
    vx=_centered_covariance(x,x);vy=_centered_covariance(y,y)
    if vx==0 or vy==0:
        raise ValueError("correlation undefined for constant series")
    cross=_centered_covariance(x,y)
    magnitude=report(root(cross*cross/(vx*vy)))
    return -magnitude if cross<0 else magnitude

def sample_correlation(x,y):
    return _correlation_from_series(*_samples(x,y))

def correlation_matrix(series):
    if not isinstance(series,(list,tuple)) or not series:
        raise ValueError("asset-major return series required")
    data=[_numbers(x,"asset returns") for x in series]
    if len(set(map(len,data)))!=1 or len(data[0])<2:
        raise ValueError("common aligned sample required")
    return [[_correlation_from_series(x,y) for y in data] for x in data]

def _correlation_input(correlations,n):
    if not isinstance(correlations,(list,tuple)) or len(correlations)!=n:
        raise ValueError("aligned square correlation required")
    rows=[_numbers(x,"correlation row") for x in correlations]
    if any(len(row)!=n for row in rows):
        raise ValueError("square correlation required")
    tolerance=Fraction(1,10**10)
    for i in range(n):
        if abs(rows[i][i]-1)>tolerance:
            raise ValueError("unit correlation diagonal required")
        rows[i][i]=Fraction(1)
        for j in range(i+1,n):
            a,b=rows[i][j],rows[j][i]
            if abs(a)>1+tolerance or abs(b)>1+tolerance:
                raise ValueError("correlation outside [-1,1]")
            if abs(a-b)>max(abs(a),abs(b))*tolerance+Fraction(1,10**12):
                raise ValueError("symmetric correlation required")
            rows[i][j]=rows[j][i]=min(Fraction(1),max(Fraction(-1),(a+b)/2))
    if not psd([[rows[i][j]+(tolerance if i==j else 0) for j in range(n)] for i in range(n)]):
        raise ValueError("full correlation matrix not PSD within tolerance")
    return rows

def covariance_from_volatility(volatilities,correlations):
    sd=_numbers(volatilities,"volatilities")
    if any(x<0 for x in sd):
        raise ValueError("nonnegative volatility required")
    rows=_correlation_input(correlations,len(sd))
    return [[report(sd[i]*rows[i][j]*sd[j]) for j in range(len(sd))] for i in range(len(sd))]

def portfolio_volatility(weights,covariance):
    w=_numbers(weights,"weights")
    budget=sum(w)
    if any(x<0 for x in w) or abs(budget-1)>Fraction(1,10**10):
        raise ValueError("long-only unit budget required")
    w=[x/budget for x in w]
    rows,_=_covariance(covariance)
    if len(rows)!=len(w):
        raise ValueError("weights and covariance must align")
    variance=sum(w[i]*rows[i][j]*w[j] for i in range(len(w)) for j in range(len(w)))
    if variance<0:
        raise ValueError("negative exact requested quadratic form")
    return report(root(variance))

def equicorrelation(n,rho):
    if type(n) is not int or n<2:
        raise ValueError("integer n >= 2 required")
    rho=exact(rho);lower=Fraction(-1,n-1);tolerance=Fraction(1,10**12)
    if rho<lower-tolerance or rho>1+tolerance:
        raise ValueError("equicorrelation violates PSD bound")
    if abs(rho-lower)<=tolerance:
        rho=lower
    elif abs(rho-1)<=tolerance:
        rho=Fraction(1)
    off=report(rho)
    while exact(off)<lower:
        off=math.nextafter(off,0.0)
    return [[1.0 if i==j else off for j in range(n)] for i in range(n)]

class CorrelationTests(unittest.TestCase):
    def test_01_positive(self): self.assertAlmostEqual(sample_correlation([1,2,3],[2,4,6]),1)
    def test_02_negative(self): self.assertAlmostEqual(sample_correlation([1,2,3],[6,4,2]),-1)
    def test_03_zero(self): self.assertAlmostEqual(sample_correlation([-1,0,1],[1,-2,1]),0)
    def test_04_covariance(self): self.assertAlmostEqual(sample_covariance([1,2,3],[2,4,6]),2)
    def test_05_matrix(self):
        c=correlation_matrix([[1,2,3],[3,2,1]])
        self.assertAlmostEqual(c[0][1],-1)
    def test_06_cov_from_corr(self):
        c=covariance_from_volatility([.2,.3],[[1,.5],[.5,1]])
        self.assertAlmostEqual(c[0][1],.03)
    def test_07_risk_zero(self):
        c=covariance_from_volatility([.2,.2],[[1,0],[0,1]])
        self.assertAlmostEqual(portfolio_volatility([.5,.5],c),math.sqrt(.02))
    def test_08_risk_positive(self):
        c=covariance_from_volatility([.2,.2],[[1,1],[1,1]])
        self.assertAlmostEqual(portfolio_volatility([.5,.5],c),.2)
    def test_09_risk_negative(self):
        c=covariance_from_volatility([.2,.2],[[1,-1],[-1,1]])
        self.assertAlmostEqual(portfolio_volatility([.5,.5],c),0)
    def test_10_equicorrelation(self):
        self.assertAlmostEqual(equicorrelation(3,-.5)[0][1],-.5)
    def test_11_constant(self):
        with self.assertRaises(ValueError):sample_correlation([1,1,1],[1,2,3])
    def test_12_invalid(self):
        for fn in (lambda:sample_covariance([1,2],[1]),
                   lambda:equicorrelation(3,-.75),
                   lambda:covariance_from_volatility([.2,.3],[[1,2],[2,1]]),
                   lambda:portfolio_volatility([.6,.3],[[.04,0],[0,.09]])):
            with self.assertRaises(ValueError):fn()

if __name__=="__main__":
    r=equicorrelation(3,.25)
    c=covariance_from_volatility([.2,.2,.2],r)
    print("Three-asset volatility:",portfolio_volatility([1/3]*3,c))
    unittest.main(verbosity=2)
```

**Validation boundary:** Integer/displayed-decimal rational moments precede 80-digit Decimal roots and final finite/nonzero-preserving float outputs. `sample_correlation` uses internal exact moments, so it can remain reportable even when a standalone covariance output would underflow or overflow. Series are asset-major complete common samples; the API cannot infer timestamps or missing-data alignment.

For supplied correlation matrices, diagonals accepted within `1e-10` of one are set to one; within-bound-tolerance off-diagonals are symmetrized and clipped to [-1,1]. A full rational Schur-complement screen requires `C+1e-10*I` to be PSD. Arbitrary covariance inputs receive the Section 16.7 scale-relative full screen with `delta=1e-10*max(diagonal)`; negative diagonals and nonzero zero-variance rows reject. Requested exact negative variance rejects, not clips. This numerical screening is not exact PSD/model certification. Covariance conversion may round near-singular outputs; it does not promise exact rank preservation. Supplied zero SDs are allowed under a model convention, while Pearson correlation estimated from a constant series remains undefined.

Long-only weight budgets accepted within `1e-10` of one are explicitly normalized. Portfolio SD is rooted before float conversion of variance. Generated equicorrelation rho within `1e-12` of an endpoint snaps to that endpoint; negative reported endpoints are rounded inward if necessary. This is a declared boundary convention, not permission for materially invalid correlations.

## 20. Troubleshooting

| Symptom | Likely cause | Corrective action |
|---|---|---|
| Correlation exceeds ±1 | Numerical error or mismatched formulas | Reconcile covariance and variance estimators |
| Correlation undefined | Constant return series | Exclude or handle zero-variance assets explicitly |
| Correlation matrix not PSD | Inconsistent pairwise samples | Use aligned observations or PSD-aware estimation |
| Portfolio variance negative | Invalid covariance matrix | Check PSD and data alignment |
| Diversification disappears in stress | Correlation regime change | Apply correlation stress tests |
| Correlation differs across systems | Different sample windows or missing-data rules | Standardize estimation policy |
| Correlation appears implausibly high | Correlated price trends | Use returns, not price levels |
| Risk estimate misses joint crashes | Linear correlation limitation | Add tail and scenario analysis |
| Currency changes results | Inconsistent FX basis | Convert returns to a common currency |
