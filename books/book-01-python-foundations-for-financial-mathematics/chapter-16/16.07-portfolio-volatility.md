## Learning objectives

Calculate portfolio volatility from a covariance matrix; distinguish variance from standard deviation; interpret diversification, correlation, and volatility contributions; calculate marginal and percentage risk contributions; and assess annualization assumptions, model risk, and volatility targeting.

## 1. What is portfolio volatility?

**Portfolio volatility** is the standard deviation of portfolio returns:

**σₚ=√Var(Rₚ).**

It describes the dispersion of returns around their expectation, measured in the same units as the return itself. Unlike variance, which uses squared return units, volatility is readily interpreted as a percentage of portfolio value for the chosen return horizon.

Volatility is **not** the maximum loss, expected shortfall, drawdown, or a guarantee of a particular future outcome. It summarizes one dimension of risk under a statistical model.

## 2. From covariance to volatility

For a portfolio with fixed starting weights **w** and asset-return covariance matrix **Σ**:

**Var(Rₚ)=wᵀΣw.**

Therefore:

**σₚ=√(wᵀΣw).**

For two assets:

**σₚ=√[w₁²σ₁²+w₂²σ₂²+2w₁w₂σ₁σ₂ρ₁₂].**

This formula makes clear that portfolio volatility depends on **individual volatility**, **weights**, and **correlation**.

## 3. Worked portfolio volatility example

Suppose:

```text
w = [0.60, 0.40]
Σ = [[0.04, 0.01],
     [0.01, 0.09]]
```

From Section 16.6:

**σₚ²=.6²(.04)+2(.6)(.4)(.01)+.4²(.09)=.0336.**

Thus:

**σₚ=√.0336≈.183303=18.33%.**

The individual volatilities are √.04=20% and √.09=30%, but the portfolio volatility is lower than either because of the chosen weights and imperfect co-movement.

This example assumes a covariance matrix estimated for the same horizon as the desired portfolio volatility.

## 4. Diversification and correlation

Consider two assets, each with 20% volatility and equal 50% weights.

| Correlation | Portfolio variance | Portfolio volatility |
|---|---:|---:|
| +1 | .04 | 20% |
| 0 | .02 | 14.14% |
| −1 | 0 | 0% |

The results follow from:

**σₚ²=.25(.04)+.25(.04)+2(.5)(.5)(.04)ρ=.02+.02ρ.**

Lower correlation can reduce risk for fixed long-only weights. Historical correlation, however, may rise in stressed markets, so diversification is not a permanent guarantee.

## 5. Historical realized volatility

Given T aligned portfolio returns R₁,…,R_T, the sample standard deviation is:

**sₚ=√{Σₜ(Rₜ−R̄)²/(T−1)}**, for T≥2.

This is a descriptive or estimation statistic computed from observed returns. A forecast of future volatility requires assumptions about how past return behavior relates to the future.

For returns [+10%,−10%], the sample mean is 0 and sample volatility is **√.02≈14.14%**. The population standard deviation of just those two values is 10%; the denominator convention matters.

## 6. Annualization and square-root-of-time scaling

A common convention for daily returns is:

**σ_annual≈σ_daily√252.**

For a hypothetical daily volatility of 1%:

**σ_annual≈.01√252≈.158745=15.87%.**

This approximation relies on conditions such as stable variance and negligible serial dependence. For monthly volatility, the analogous convention is **σ_annual≈σ_monthly√12**.

If returns exhibit autocorrelation, the variance of a sum contains cross-period covariance terms:

**Var(ΣₜRₜ)=ΣₜVar(Rₜ)+2Σₛ₍ₜ>s₎Cov(Rₛ,Rₜ).**

Consequently, naive √T scaling can be misleading for autocorrelated returns, volatility clustering, or changing market regimes.

## 7. Marginal portfolio volatility

For positive portfolio volatility, the gradient of **σₚ=√(wᵀΣw)** is:

**∇₍w₎σₚ=Σw/σₚ.**

For asset i:

**MRCᵢ=∂σₚ/∂wᵢ=(Σw)ᵢ/σₚ.**

The acronym MRC denotes **marginal risk contribution**, here measured in units of volatility per unit of portfolio weight.

For the worked example:

**Σw=[.028,.042]** and **σₚ≈.183303**.

Thus:

**MRC_A=.028/.183303≈.152753.**

**MRC_B=.042/.183303≈.229129.**

These are unconstrained derivatives. In a fully invested portfolio, changing one weight requires offsetting changes elsewhere.

## 8. Component volatility contributions

Define each asset's component contribution to portfolio volatility:

**RCᵢ=wᵢ(Σw)ᵢ/σₚ.**

Euler's theorem for a positively homogeneous function gives:

**ΣᵢRCᵢ=σₚ**, when σₚ>0.

For the example, the component **variance** contributions are both .0168. Divide each by portfolio volatility:

**RC_A=.0168/√.0336≈.091652.**

**RC_B=.0168/√.0336≈.091652.**

Their sum is approximately .183303. Despite weights of 60% and 40%, both assets contribute **50% of portfolio volatility** under this particular covariance matrix.

## 9. Percentage risk contributions

Define:

**PRCᵢ=RCᵢ/σₚ=wᵢ(Σw)ᵢ/σₚ².**

These shares sum to one for σₚ>0. They need not all be positive in a portfolio containing hedges or short exposures.

Percentage risk contributions can help distinguish **capital allocation** from **risk allocation**. A 10% capital weight in a volatile, highly correlated asset can account for a much larger share of portfolio risk.

## 10. Zero-volatility portfolios

A perfectly hedged portfolio can have **σₚ=0**. The volatility gradient formula divides by σₚ, so marginal and component volatility contributions are generally undefined at that point.

The reference code raises a clear error for zero-volatility risk decomposition rather than reporting misleading zero contributions.

A portfolio with exactly zero volatility under a simplified covariance model may still face liquidity, counterparty, financing, model, and operational risks.

## 11. Volatility targeting

A simple heuristic scales portfolio exposure to a volatility target:

**Scale factor=σ_target/σ_current.**

For current volatility 12% and target volatility 9%:

**Scale=.09/.12=.75.**

Scaling all economic exposures by .75 would reduce linearized volatility by approximately 25% if covariance remained unchanged. In a fully invested portfolio, the remaining allocation may move to cash, which has its own return and risk properties.

Production volatility targeting must consider leverage limits, cash, transaction costs, turnover, estimation error, and the possibility that volatility changes immediately after rebalancing.

## 12. Diversification ratio

A commonly used measure is:

**DR=(Σᵢwᵢσᵢ)/σₚ**, for long-only weights and σₚ>0.

It compares the weighted average standalone volatility with actual portfolio volatility. For numerator .22 and denominator .18, DR≈1.2222.

This measure depends on the portfolio and asset definitions and does not capture all forms of diversification. For short or leveraged positions, the convention requires explicit modification.

## 13. Forecast versus realized volatility

**Realized volatility** describes observed returns over a historical window. **Forecast volatility** uses a model to estimate future dispersion.

Potential approaches include rolling sample covariance, exponentially weighted estimates, factor models, and volatility models such as GARCH. Forecasts are sensitive to estimation windows, market regimes, and data quality.

Do not treat a historical volatility estimate as a guarantee of future volatility.

## 14. Limits of volatility as a risk metric

Volatility treats upside and downside deviations symmetrically. Investors may care more about losses than gains. It can also miss nonlinear exposures and tail dependence.

Options and other derivatives can have nonlinear payoffs. A fixed-weight covariance approximation may not adequately capture gamma, jumps, margin calls, liquidity spirals, or extreme events.

Complement volatility with stress tests, drawdown analysis, tail-risk metrics, and operational constraints.

## 15. Python reference implementation

Save as `portfolio_volatility.py`. This standard-library reference calculates portfolio variance, portfolio volatility, sample realized volatility, marginal and component volatility contributions, percentage risk contributions, and square-root-of-time scaling. It includes **12 reference unit tests**.

```python
import math
import unittest
from fractions import Fraction
from decimal import Decimal, localcontext

def exact(x):
    if type(x) not in (int,float):
        raise ValueError("finite int/float required; booleans excluded")
    try:
        if not math.isfinite(x):
            raise ValueError("finite int/float required")
    except OverflowError as exc:
        raise ValueError("input outside finite float range") from exc
    return Fraction(x) if type(x) is int else Fraction(str(x))

def report(x):
    try:
        value=float(x)
    except (OverflowError,ValueError) as exc:
        raise ValueError("unreportable numeric result") from exc
    if not math.isfinite(value) or (x != 0 and value == 0):
        raise ValueError("unreportable numeric result")
    return value

def root(x):
    with localcontext() as ctx:
        ctx.prec=80
        return (Decimal(x.numerator)/Decimal(x.denominator)).sqrt()

def psd(a):
    # Exact Schur complements; a zero pivot must have an all-zero row.
    a=[row[:] for row in a]
    for k in range(len(a)):
        pivot=a[k][k]
        if pivot<0:
            return False
        if pivot==0:
            if any(a[k][j]!=0 for j in range(k+1,len(a))):
                return False
            continue
        for i in range(k+1,len(a)):
            for j in range(i,len(a)):
                a[i][j]-=a[i][k]*a[k][j]/pivot
                a[j][i]=a[i][j]
    return True

def _covariance(cov,tolerance=1e-10):
    relative=exact(tolerance)
    if relative<0 or relative>Fraction(1,10**6):
        raise ValueError("relative tolerance must be in [0,1e-6]")
    n=None
    if not isinstance(cov,(list,tuple)) or not cov:
        raise ValueError("nonempty square covariance required")
    if n is None:
        n=len(cov)
    if len(cov)!=n or any(not isinstance(row,(list,tuple)) or len(row)!=n for row in cov):
        raise ValueError("square aligned covariance required")
    a=[[exact(x) for x in row] for row in cov]
    if any(a[i][i]<0 for i in range(n)):
        raise ValueError("negative asset variance")
    scale=max(a[i][i] for i in range(n))
    tolerance=scale*relative
    for i in range(n):
        for j in range(i+1,n):
            if abs(a[i][j]-a[j][i])>max(abs(a[i][j]),abs(a[j][i]))*relative+tolerance:
                raise ValueError("covariance must be symmetric within stated tolerance")
            a[i][j]=a[j][i]=(a[i][j]+a[j][i])/2
    if any(a[i][i]==0 and any(a[i][j]!=0 for j in range(n)) for i in range(n)):
        raise ValueError("zero variance requires zero covariance row")
    shifted=[[a[i][j]+(tolerance if i==j else 0) for j in range(n)] for i in range(n)]
    if not psd(shifted):
        raise ValueError("covariance is not PSD within scale-relative tolerance")
    return a,tolerance

def check_covariance(matrix,tolerance=1e-10):
    a,_=_covariance(matrix,tolerance)
    return [[report(x) for x in row] for row in a]

def _weights(weights,n,allow_short):
    if type(allow_short) is not bool:
        raise ValueError("allow_short must be boolean")
    if not isinstance(weights,(list,tuple)) or len(weights)!=n:
        raise ValueError("one weight per asset required")
    w=[exact(x) for x in weights]
    if not allow_short and any(x<0 for x in w):
        raise ValueError("negative weight requires short permission")
    budget=sum(w)
    if abs(budget-1)>Fraction(1,10**10):
        raise ValueError("fully invested weights must sum to one")
    return [x/budget for x in w]

def check_weights(weights,n,allow_short=False):
    return [report(x) for x in _weights(weights,n,allow_short)]

def _inputs(weights,covariance,allow_short):
    a,_=_covariance(covariance)
    w=_weights(weights,len(a),allow_short)
    weighted=[sum(a[i][j]*w[j] for j in range(len(w))) for i in range(len(w))]
    q=sum(w[i]*weighted[i] for i in range(len(w)))
    if q<0:
        raise ValueError("negative exact requested quadratic form; review tolerated covariance")
    return a,w,weighted,q

def portfolio_variance(weights,covariance,allow_short=False):
    _,_,_,q=_inputs(weights,covariance,allow_short)
    return report(q)

def portfolio_volatility(weights,covariance,allow_short=False):
    _,_,_,q=_inputs(weights,covariance,allow_short)
    return report(root(q))

def reconcile(values,total):
    try:
        summed=math.fsum(values)
    except OverflowError as exc:
        raise ValueError("reported attribution cannot reconcile") from exc
    if not math.isclose(summed,total,rel_tol=1e-10,abs_tol=0):
        raise ValueError("reported attribution loses reconciliation; rescale inputs")

def signed_ratio_root(numerator,denominator):
    magnitude=report(root(numerator*numerator/denominator))
    return -magnitude if numerator<0 else magnitude

def component_volatility(weights,covariance,allow_short=False):
    _,w,weighted,q=_inputs(weights,covariance,allow_short)
    if q==0:
        raise ValueError("volatility contributions undefined at zero volatility")
    total=report(root(q))
    marginal=[signed_ratio_root(x,q) for x in weighted]
    component=[signed_ratio_root(w[i]*weighted[i],q) for i in range(len(w))]
    percent=[report(w[i]*weighted[i]/q) for i in range(len(w))]
    reconcile(component,total)
    reconcile(percent,1.0)
    return {"marginal":marginal,"component":component,"percent":percent,"total":total}

def scale_volatility(period_volatility,periods,independent=True):
    value=exact(period_volatility);count=exact(periods)
    if value<0 or count<=0:
        raise ValueError("nonnegative volatility and positive period count required")
    if type(independent) is not bool or not independent:
        raise ValueError("boolean true requires stated stable independent-period assumptions")
    return report(root(value*value*count))

def realized_sample_volatility(returns):
    if not isinstance(returns,(list,tuple)) or len(returns)<2:
        raise ValueError("at least two aligned finite returns required")
    data=[exact(x) for x in returns]
    mean=sum(data)/len(data)
    variance=sum((x-mean)**2 for x in data)/(len(data)-1)
    return report(root(variance))

class PortfolioVolatilityTests(unittest.TestCase):
    C=[[.04,.01],[.01,.09]]
    def test_01_variance(self): self.assertAlmostEqual(portfolio_variance([.6,.4],self.C),.0336)
    def test_02_volatility(self): self.assertAlmostEqual(portfolio_volatility([.6,.4],self.C),math.sqrt(.0336))
    def test_03_single(self): self.assertAlmostEqual(portfolio_volatility([1,0],self.C),.2)
    def test_04_zero_correlation(self): self.assertAlmostEqual(portfolio_volatility([.5,.5],[[.04,0],[0,.04]]),math.sqrt(.02))
    def test_05_perfect_hedge(self): self.assertAlmostEqual(portfolio_volatility([.5,.5],[[1,-1],[-1,1]]),0)
    def test_06_component_sum(self):
        x=component_volatility([.6,.4],self.C)
        self.assertAlmostEqual(math.fsum(x["component"]),x["total"])
    def test_07_percent_sum(self):
        self.assertAlmostEqual(math.fsum(component_volatility([.6,.4],self.C)["percent"]),1)
    def test_08_equal_components(self):
        c=component_volatility([.6,.4],self.C)["component"]
        self.assertAlmostEqual(c[0],c[1])
    def test_09_scaling(self): self.assertAlmostEqual(scale_volatility(.01,252),.01*math.sqrt(252))
    def test_10_sample(self): self.assertAlmostEqual(realized_sample_volatility([.1,-.1]),math.sqrt(.02))
    def test_11_short(self): self.assertAlmostEqual(portfolio_volatility([1.2,-.2],self.C,True),math.sqrt(.0564))
    def test_12_invalid(self):
        for f in (lambda:portfolio_volatility([.6,.3],self.C),
                  lambda:portfolio_volatility([.5,.5],[[1,2],[0,1]]),
                  lambda:component_volatility([.5,.5],[[1,-1],[-1,1]]),
                  lambda:scale_volatility(.1,0),
                  lambda:realized_sample_volatility([.1])):
            with self.assertRaises(ValueError):f()

if __name__=="__main__":
    w=[.6,.4]
    c=[[.04,.01],[.01,.09]]
    print("Volatility:",portfolio_volatility(w,c))
    print("Contributions:",component_volatility(w,c))
    unittest.main(verbosity=2)
```

**Validation boundary:** The corrected listing screens the full matrix using rational Schur complements on `covariance + delta*I`, with `delta=1e-10*max(diagonal)`, rather than checking only a supplied direction. Symmetry uses pair-relative plus diagonal-scale tolerance and averages accepted pairs. Negative diagonals and nonzero zero-variance covariance rows are rejected. The requested exact quadratic form must be nonnegative; it is not clipped. This numerical PSD screen does not certify an exact covariance model or empirical accuracy.

Integer/displayed-decimal rational intermediates retain centered differences, normalized budgets, cancellations and tiny squared values until the final output. Decimal roots use 80-digit precision. Budgets accepted within `1e-10` of one are explicitly normalized; short and scaling-assumption controls must be booleans. Marginals are unconstrained derivatives evaluated at normalized weights, not derivatives through the normalization adapter. Source APIs cannot infer actual asset order, dates, units, currency, financing or common horizon.

Each required float output must be finite and preserve nonzero exact values. Otherwise `ValueError` is deliberate. SD may be reportable when variance is not; roots are computed before float conversion. All attribution fields must be reportable, and reported components/shares must reconcile with total/one within relative `1e-10` (no absolute floor); destructive cancellation in displayed floats raises `ValueError` even if exact rational identities hold. Zero-risk attribution remains undefined. Scaling assumes stable independent equal-horizon increments; passing a boolean does not validate the economic assumption.

## 18. Troubleshooting

| Symptom | Likely cause | Corrective action |
|---|---|---|
| Volatility differs by factor of 100 | Decimal versus percentage returns | Standardize units |
| Portfolio volatility exceeds expectation | Correlations or weights changed | Recompute covariance and exposure |
| Risk contributions do not sum to total | Formula or denominator error | Use wᵢ(Σw)ᵢ/σₚ |
| Zero-volatility attribution crashes | Division by zero | Report attribution as undefined |
| Annual volatility overstated | Invalid √T assumptions | Evaluate serial dependence |
| Short portfolio risk seems negative | Confusion between signed contribution and total risk | Reconcile component and total |
| Risk changes despite unchanged weights | Covariance regime change | Refresh risk estimates |
| Volatility target overshoots | Forecast error or execution costs | Add limits and monitoring |
| Derivative portfolio appears low-risk | Nonlinear payoffs omitted | Use scenario/full-revaluation analysis |
