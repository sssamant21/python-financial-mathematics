## Learning objectives

Calculate portfolio weights from position market values, distinguish long-only and signed weights, quantify net and gross exposure, measure concentration, calculate drifted allocations, and derive basic target rebalancing trades. Recognize valuation, cash, financing, and execution assumptions.

## 1. Portfolio weights as a valuation ratio

Let qᵢ be the quantity of asset i, Pᵢ its consistently timed and currency-converted price, and Vᵢ=qᵢPᵢ its market value. Let total net asset value be V=ΣVᵢ, assuming all relevant positions and cash are represented and V>0.

The **portfolio value weight** is:

**wᵢ=Vᵢ/V.**

For long-only assets worth $600 and $400, V=$1,000 and weights are **[.60,.40]**.

This is a *point-in-time* measure. Weights change with prices, deposits, withdrawals, and trades.

## 2. Fully invested weights and cash

If every asset, cash balance, and liability relevant to the chosen portfolio definition is included:

**Σᵢwᵢ=1.**

A portfolio with risky-asset weights [.6,.3] and cash .1 is fully represented as [.6,.3,.1]. Calling [.6,.3] a fully invested portfolio without recording cash would omit an exposure.

A residual weight can be calculated as **w_cash=1−Σw_risky**, but only when the denominator, leverage, and balance-sheet treatment are clear.

## 3. From shares and prices to weights

Suppose a portfolio holds:

| Asset | Quantity | Unit price | Market value |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| A | 2 | $100 | $200 |
| B | 3 | $50 | $150 |
| **Total** | | | **$350** |

Weights are **200/350=4/7≈57.14%** and **150/350=3/7≈42.86%**.

For securities in different currencies, convert market values into one base currency at a documented valuation timestamp before calculating weights.

## 4. Long-only and signed weights

A **long-only** portfolio has nonnegative weights. A short position may produce a negative value weight, but signed weights alone do not model collateral, margin, financing, and derivative notional conventions.

For signed weights **[1.2,−.2]**:

- Net exposure: 1.2−.2=**1.0**.
- Long exposure: **1.2**.
- Signed short exposure: **−.2**.
- Gross exposure: |1.2|+|−.2|=**1.4**.

Gross exposure above 1 indicates leveraged gross positions relative to the chosen NAV denominator. Net exposure of 1 does not imply unleveraged risk.

## 5. Weight normalization and validation

When position values are nonnegative and their total is positive, weights follow by dividing each value by the total.

For signed values, NAV normalization is meaningful only when net asset value is positive and the accounting treatment is defined.

Practical checks include:

1. Numeric, finite position values and prices.
2. Correct currency conversion and valuation timestamps.
3. Positive net asset value.
4. Explicit treatment of cash, borrowing, liabilities, and shorts.
5. A declared floating-point tolerance for Σw=1.
6. Clear constraints on negative weights and leverage.

Do not silently renormalize an invalid portfolio when the discrepancy could represent omitted cash or a data-quality error.

## 6. Concentration measures

A simple concentration metric for **long-only, nonnegative weights** is the Herfindahl–Hirschman Index:

**HHI=Σᵢwᵢ².**

For equal weights [.5,.5], HHI=.25+.25=.5. For [1,0], HHI=1.

The **concentration-equivalent number of holdings** is:

**N_eff=1/HHI**.

For two equal holdings, N_eff=2. For four equal holdings, HHI=.25 and N_eff=4.

HHI is not a covariance-aware risk measure: two equally weighted assets can still be highly correlated. Signed leveraged portfolios require more carefully defined concentration metrics.

## 7. Portfolio return and starting weights

If wᵢ,0 are **beginning-of-period** weights and Rᵢ are consistent one-period asset returns, then, before external cash flows and costs:

**Rₚ=Σᵢwᵢ,0Rᵢ.**

For weights [.6,.4] and returns [.10,−.05], portfolio return=.06−.02=.04, or **4%**.

Using end-of-period weights in this formula generally creates an error. The distinction becomes important in rebalancing and performance attribution.

## 8. Weight drift after market returns

For a long-only portfolio with no interim trades or external cash flows:

**wᵢ,1=wᵢ,0(1+Rᵢ)/Σⱼwⱼ,0(1+Rⱼ).**

For equal starting weights and returns [.10,0]:

**w_A,1=.55/1.05=1.1/2.1≈.52381**.

**w_B,1=.50/1.05=1/2.1≈.47619**.

The denominator is **1+Rₚ**. This example assumes no complete loss of a position; the reference helper requires individual returns greater than −100%.

## 9. Rebalancing to target weights

Given current position values Vᵢ and a target allocation wᵢ*, a simple **pre-cost, fully invested** dollar trade is:

**Tradeᵢ=wᵢ*V−Vᵢ.**

For current values [$600,$400], total V=$1,000, and target [.5,.5]:

**Trade_A=$500−$600=−$100** (sell).

**Trade_B=$500−$400=+$100** (buy).

The trades sum to zero before costs. Real implementations must account for transaction costs, whole shares, cash reserves, tax lots, settlement, and liquidity constraints.

A common **one-way turnover** convention is half the sum of absolute trade values divided by NAV:

**Turnover=(Σ|Tradeᵢ|)/(2V)**.

Here turnover=$200/(2×$1,000)=**10%**. Alternative turnover conventions exist and should be declared.

## 10. Practical constraints

Examples include maximum issuer weight, sector exposure limits, minimum cash, gross leverage cap, short-sale restrictions, and lot-size constraints.

A 30% maximum issuer weight rejects a 40% allocation even when all weights sum to one. Constraints may conflict, so feasibility must be checked before generating orders.

## 11. Numerical considerations

Weights often contain floating-point approximations. A strict test of `sum(weights) == 1.0` can fail for harmless rounding errors. Use a documented absolute tolerance such as `1e-10` for educational examples, but do not use tolerance to conceal material missing exposure.

For very large or offsetting signed positions, numerical precision and denominator stability need additional review.

## 12. Python reference implementation

Save as `portfolio_weights.py`. The reference implementation calculates position values, normalized weights, exposure, long-only HHI, post-return drift, and pre-cost target trades. It includes **12 reference unit tests**.

```python
import math
import unittest
from fractions import Fraction

def finite_number(x):
    if type(x) not in (int,float):
        return False
    try:
        return math.isfinite(x)
    except OverflowError:
        return False

def exact(x):
    return Fraction(x) if type(x) is int else Fraction(str(x))

def report(x):
    try:
        value=float(x)
    except (OverflowError,ValueError) as exc:
        raise ValueError("result outside float range") from exc
    if not math.isfinite(value) or (x and value==0):
        raise ValueError("result outside float range")
    return value

def finite(values):
    if not isinstance(values,(list,tuple)) or not values:
        raise ValueError("nonempty sequence required")
    if any(not finite_number(v) for v in values):
        raise ValueError("finite numeric values required")
    return list(values)

def market_values(quantities,prices):
    q,p=finite(quantities),finite(prices)
    if len(q)!=len(p) or any(v<=0 for v in p):
        raise ValueError("aligned quantities and positive prices required")
    return [report(exact(a)*exact(b)) for a,b in zip(q,p)]

def weights_from_values(values,long_only=True):
    if type(long_only) is not bool:
        raise ValueError("boolean long_only required")
    v=finite(values)
    if long_only and any(x<0 for x in v):
        raise ValueError("long-only values must be nonnegative")
    total=sum(map(exact,v))
    if total<=0:
        raise ValueError("positive net asset value required")
    result=[report(exact(x)/total) for x in v]
    # Large offsetting signed ratios may lose the net budget on reporting.
    validate_weights(result,long_only=long_only)
    return result

def validate_weights(weights,long_only=True,tol=1e-10):
    if type(long_only) is not bool or not finite_number(tol) or not 0<=tol<1:
        raise ValueError("boolean policy and finite tolerance in [0,1) required")
    w=finite(weights)
    if long_only and any(x<0 for x in w):
        raise ValueError("negative weight in long-only portfolio")
    if abs(sum(map(exact,w))-1)>exact(tol):
        raise ValueError("weights must sum to one within tolerance")
    return w

def exposures(weights):
    w=list(map(exact,validate_weights(weights,long_only=False)))
    return {"net":report(sum(w)),"gross":report(sum(abs(x) for x in w)),
            "long":report(sum(max(x,0) for x in w)),"short":report(sum(min(x,0) for x in w))}

def concentration_hhi(weights):
    w=validate_weights(weights,long_only=True)
    return report(sum(exact(x)**2 for x in w))

def drifted_weights(weights,returns):
    w=validate_weights(weights,long_only=True);r=finite(returns)
    if len(w)!=len(r) or any(x<=-1 for x in r):
        raise ValueError("aligned returns greater than -100% required")
    end_values=[exact(a)*(1+exact(x)) for a,x in zip(w,r)]
    total=sum(end_values)
    if total<=0:
        raise ValueError("nonpositive ending value")
    result=[report(v/total) for v in end_values]
    validate_weights(result)
    return result

def target_trades(current_values,target_weights):
    v=finite(current_values)
    if any(x<0 for x in v):
        raise ValueError("long-only current values required")
    w=validate_weights(target_weights,long_only=True)
    if len(v)!=len(w):
        raise ValueError("aligned current values and target weights required")
    total=sum(map(exact,v))
    if total<=0:
        raise ValueError("positive portfolio value required")
    # Explicitly normalize only targets already accepted within tolerance,
    # so a tiny weight residual cannot create a material dollar cash flow.
    budget=sum(map(exact,w))
    return [report(total*exact(target)/budget-exact(current)) for target,current in zip(w,v)]

class PortfolioWeightTests(unittest.TestCase):
    def test_01_values(self): self.assertEqual(market_values([2,3],[100,50]),[200,150])
    def test_02_weights(self): self.assertEqual(weights_from_values([600,400]),[.6,.4])
    def test_03_cash(self): self.assertEqual(validate_weights([.6,.3,.1]),[.6,.3,.1])
    def test_04_short(self): self.assertEqual(weights_from_values([120,-20],False),[1.2,-.2])
    def test_05_gross(self): self.assertAlmostEqual(exposures([1.2,-.2])["gross"],1.4)
    def test_06_long(self): self.assertAlmostEqual(exposures([1.2,-.2])["long"],1.2)
    def test_07_short_exposure(self): self.assertAlmostEqual(exposures([1.2,-.2])["short"],-.2)
    def test_08_hhi(self): self.assertAlmostEqual(concentration_hhi([.5,.5]),.5)
    def test_09_drift(self):
        self.assertAlmostEqual(drifted_weights([.5,.5],[.1,0])[0],1.1/2.1)
    def test_10_trades(self): self.assertEqual(target_trades([600,400],[.5,.5]),[-100,100])
    def test_11_zero_weight(self): self.assertEqual(weights_from_values([100,0]),[1,0])
    def test_12_invalid(self):
        for fn in (lambda:validate_weights([.6,.3]),
                   lambda:validate_weights([1.2,-.2]),
                   lambda:weights_from_values([0,0]),
                   lambda:market_values([1],[0]),
                   lambda:drifted_weights([.5,.5],[-1,0]),
                   lambda:target_trades([1,2],[1])):
            with self.assertRaises(ValueError):fn()

if __name__=="__main__":
    print("Weights:",weights_from_values([600,400]))
    print("Rebalance trades:",target_trades([600,400],[.5,.5]))
    unittest.main(verbosity=2)
```

Integer inputs retain their precision; rational intermediate arithmetic interprets floats by displayed decimal values. Every reported numeric field must be finite and nonzero values may not silently underflow. Normalized output weights must still satisfy the declared budget tolerance; otherwise reporting is rejected. Each market-value field must be representable, while a trade calculation can use an oversized internal NAV if final trades are representable. Rounded exposure components can lose a small net when recombined; net exposure is computed from the original inputs before rounding.

Weight validation requires boolean policy and finite absolute tolerance in [0,1). It does not silently normalize general portfolios. `target_trades` explicitly normalizes only target vectors already accepted within tolerance, preserving the pre-cost dollar budget rather than amplifying tiny weight residuals at large NAV. Invalid budgets remain rejected. Budget conservation holds in rational intermediates; reported floats can have rounding residuals and are not lot-rounded executable orders. These educational conventions do not imply binary-float compatibility, margin-model acceptance or performance suitability.

## 13. Hands-on lab — Portfolio allocation and rebalancing

**Scenario:** A fictional investor has positions worth [$600,$400] and wants to study a 50/50 target allocation.

1. Calculate initial weights.
2. Verify full investment.
3. Compute long-only concentration HHI.
4. Model returns [.10,−.05] and calculate the portfolio return.
5. Compute end-of-period weights without trading.
6. Distinguish stages: an independent initial 50/50 rebalance of [600,400] trades [−100,+100]. After the modeled returns, current values are [660,380], NAV=1040 and the main post-return 50/50 rebalance trades [−140,+140] before costs.
7. Initial-stage gross traded value is 200 and one-way turnover=.10; post-return gross traded value is 280 and turnover=140/1040=7/52. Do not mix the two valuation times.
8. At the post-return stage, hypothetical cost .001 times gross traded value is .28. Fund it from wealth: post-cost NAV=1039.72, equal target values=519.86 and trades=[−140.14,+139.86], summing to −.28. This supplemental cost design is separate from the source pre-cost helper; the initial-stage cost quote remains .20.
9. Post-return prices [110,47.5] imply fractional target shares, so exact whole-share implementation may be infeasible. A 5% cash minimum requires an explicit cash slot, for example [.475,.475,.05]; the separate pre-cost target for current [660,380,0] trades [−166,+114,+52]. This is not a constrained order optimizer.

## 15. Troubleshooting

| Symptom | Likely cause | Recommended action |
|---|---|---|
| Weights sum to .9 | Cash or position omitted | Reconcile all assets and liabilities |
| Gross exposure unexpectedly high | Shorting or leverage | Report net and gross separately |
| Negative weights rejected | Long-only policy | Explicitly permit and govern shorts |
| Weights drift without trades | Unequal market returns | Recompute post-period values |
| Target trades do not balance | Cash or costs excluded | Reconcile portfolio accounting |
| Incorrect concentration | Signed HHI used naively | Use a metric appropriate to exposures |
| Different weights across systems | Currency/time mismatch | Standardize price snapshots |
| Tiny sum-to-one mismatch | Floating-point rounding | Apply documented tolerance |
