## Learning objectives

Translate investment mandates into mathematical constraints; distinguish portfolio feasibility from optimality; validate budgets, position bounds, group exposure, turnover, liquidity, and leverage; identify contradictory constraints; and implement transparent Python feasibility checks.

## 1. Why portfolio constraints matter

The unconstrained mathematical optimum may require excessive leverage, concentrated positions, illiquid assets, or exposures prohibited by the investor's mandate.

Constraints make the portfolio selection problem realistic. They define the **feasible set** before any optimization takes place.

Let **w=(w₁,…,wₙ)** be asset weights. Expected return is **μₚ=wᵀμ**, and variance is **σₚ²=wᵀΣw**.

The optimizer chooses among weight vectors that satisfy every applicable requirement. A portfolio with excellent expected risk-adjusted return is unusable if it violates the mandate.

## 2. Fully invested budget constraint

For a fully invested portfolio:

**Σᵢwᵢ=1.**

A cash asset can be included as an additional weight. For a portfolio with uninvested cash outside the modeled asset set, the budget equation must explicitly account for that cash.

For weights [.5,.3,.2], the sum is one, so the portfolio satisfies the budget constraint.

The budget constraint alone does not prohibit short positions.

## 3. Long-only constraints

For a long-only portfolio:

**wᵢ≥0** for every asset.

Combined with the budget:

**wᵢ≥0, Σᵢwᵢ=1.**

The feasible set is a simplex in n-dimensional weight space.

This constraint is common in retirement funds, mutual funds, and portfolios without short-selling authorization.

## 4. Individual position bounds

An asset may have lower and upper allocation limits:

**lᵢ≤wᵢ≤uᵢ.**

Example:

**0≤w_A≤.6**, **0≤w_B≤.6**, **0≤w_C≤.4**.

Weights [.5,.3,.2] satisfy these limits. Weights [.7,.2,.1] violate A's maximum by .1.

Position bounds control concentration, but do not automatically prevent correlated exposures across multiple securities.

## 5. Basic budget feasibility checks

A necessary and sufficient condition for a fully invested long-only portfolio to satisfy **only independent position bounds** is:

**Σᵢlᵢ≤1≤Σᵢuᵢ**,

provided **0≤lᵢ≤uᵢ≤1** for every i.

If lower bounds are [.6,.6], their sum is 1.2 and the problem is infeasible.

If upper bounds are [.4,.4], their sum is .8 and the problem is infeasible.

Passing this test does **not** guarantee feasibility once group limits, turnover, or other constraints are added.

## 6. Sector and group exposure constraints

Let **G** be a group of assets. Its weight is:

**W_G=Σᵢ∈G wᵢ.**

A group limit can require:

**L_G≤W_G≤U_G.**

For weights [.5,.3,.2], if the first two assets belong to the same sector, the sector exposure is .8.

A sector cap of .7 is violated by .1. A cap of .85 is satisfied.

Assets may belong to overlapping groups, such as an industry group and a country group. Group exposures should be calculated independently; summing exposures across overlapping groups can double-count assets.

## 7. Issuer, country, and currency limits

An investor may impose limits on exposures to a single issuer, region, or currency.

Look-through exposures matter. Two funds with different names may hold overlapping underlying securities, and a domestic-listed fund may still carry foreign currency or country risk.

Define whether limits apply to security domicile, economic revenue, issuer risk, settlement currency, or another classification. Different definitions yield different exposures.

## 8. Cash and liquidity constraints

A portfolio may require a minimum cash allocation:

**w_cash≥c_min.**

A portfolio may also cap illiquid assets:

**Σᵢ∈Illiquid wᵢ≤L_max.**

Example: cash weight 2% violates a 5% minimum cash requirement by 3 percentage points.

Liquidity is state-dependent. A position that is liquid in normal markets may be difficult to exit during stress.

## 9. Leverage and gross exposure

Net exposure is:

**Net=Σᵢwᵢ**.

Gross exposure is:

**Gross=Σᵢ|wᵢ|**.

For weights [1.2,−.2]:

**Net=1**, but **Gross=1.4**.

A gross-exposure limit of 1.2 is violated by .2.

Gross exposure is not the same as margin requirement or economic risk; derivative notionals and risk-equivalent exposures may need separate treatment.

## 10. Turnover constraints

For old weights **w_old** and new weights **w_new**, a common one-way turnover convention is:

**T=.5Σᵢ|w_new,ᵢ−w_old,ᵢ|**.

For old weights [.4,.4,.2] and new weights [.5,.3,.2]:

**T=.5(.1+.1+0)=.1**.

The portfolio trades 10% of NAV under this convention, assuming self-financing rebalancing with no external flows.

Some institutions report total buys plus sells without the one-half factor. Always state the convention.

## 11. Transaction costs and turnover budgets

A simple linear transaction-cost approximation is:

**Cost≈Σᵢcᵢ|Δwᵢ|**,

where cᵢ is a per-dollar traded cost rate. Under a common symmetric cost rate c, this becomes **2cT** for the one-way turnover definition above.

If a cost rate is instead quoted per unit of one-way turnover, the model must explicitly reflect that different convention.

Real costs can be nonlinear and include spreads, market impact, commissions, taxes, and borrowing fees.

## 12. Expected-return constraints

A mandate may require:

**wᵀμ≥μ_min**.

For weights [.5,.3,.2] and expected returns [.08,.05,.02]:

**μₚ=.5(.08)+.3(.05)+.2(.02)=.059**.

A 6% minimum expected-return constraint is violated by .1 percentage point.

Expected returns are estimates, so a strict return floor can encourage fragile allocations if forecast uncertainty is ignored.

## 13. Risk constraints

Common risk constraints include:

**wᵀΣw≤V_max**,

or equivalently:

**σₚ≤σ_max**.

Other mandates may limit tracking error, factor exposures, duration, credit spread sensitivity, downside loss, or stress-scenario drawdowns.

Some risk constraints are convex under a valid positive-semidefinite covariance model. Others, especially nonlinear or path-dependent measures, can be harder to optimize reliably.

## 14. Tracking-error constraints

For portfolio weights w and benchmark weights b, define active weights:

**a=w−b**.

Under a consistent covariance model, active return variance is:

**TE²=aᵀΣa**.

A tracking-error limit may require:

**√(aᵀΣa)≤TE_max**.

Benchmark constituents and currency treatment must be aligned with the portfolio data. Tracking error measures active volatility, not absolute portfolio volatility.

## 15. Factor and duration constraints

A linear factor exposure can be represented as:

**fₚ=Σᵢwᵢfᵢ**.

A mandate may require:

**f_min≤fₚ≤f_max**.

Examples include equity beta, duration, or a modeled sector factor.

For weights [.5,.5] and simplified bond durations [2,8], weighted duration is approximately 5 years under consistent small-yield-change assumptions.

For more complex bonds and derivatives, duration aggregation requires careful definitions and risk sensitivities.

## 16. Cardinality, lot size, and integer constraints

A cardinality limit restricts the number of nonzero positions:

**#\{i:wᵢ≠0\}≤K**.

Whole-share, minimum-lot, and minimum-trade-size requirements introduce discrete decisions.

Such constraints can make an otherwise convex portfolio problem nonconvex or mixed-integer, substantially increasing computational complexity.

A simple continuous optimizer does not automatically enforce integer trading requirements.

## 17. Feasibility versus optimization

A **feasibility check** answers whether a proposed portfolio satisfies the rules.

An **optimization problem** searches for the best portfolio within those rules.

Example:

**minimize wᵀΣw**

subject to:

**Σᵢwᵢ=1**, **0≤wᵢ≤uᵢ**, **Σᵢ∈Sector wᵢ≤s_max**, and **T≤T_max**.

Before trusting an optimized result, verify every constraint independently. A solver success flag is not a substitute for post-trade feasibility checks.

## 18. Constraint conflicts and infeasibility

Contradictory rules can make the feasible set empty.

For example, a minimum 70% allocation to one group and a maximum 50% allocation to that same group cannot both be satisfied.

Even if individual position bounds pass their basic budget test, group constraints and turnover limits can make the overall system infeasible.

A useful troubleshooting workflow is to isolate the violated rule set, report the smallest meaningful conflict, and obtain approval before changing any mandate.

## 19. Soft constraints and penalties

Some preferences can be expressed as penalties in the objective rather than hard limits.

For example:

**minimize wᵀΣw+λΣᵢ|wᵢ−w_old,ᵢ|**.

The second term discourages turnover. The parameter λ expresses the tradeoff between variance and trading.

A penalty is not equivalent to a hard turnover cap. A sufficiently strong improvement in the primary objective can still justify a large trade under a soft penalty.

## 20. Governance and operational controls

A production constraint framework should maintain:

- Versioned investment mandate and effective dates.
- Named ownership and approval for every limit.
- Security classification and group membership provenance.
- Clear definitions of NAV, cash, gross exposure, and turnover.
- Independent pre-trade and post-trade checks.
- Exception approval and audit records.
- Monitoring of drift and breaches.
- Reconciliation between optimizer weights and actual holdings.

Rules should be tested using both ordinary portfolios and deliberate violations.

## 21. Python reference implementation

Save as `portfolio_constraints.py`. This standard-library reference validates fully invested long-only weights, individual position bounds, group exposure limits, one-way turnover, and basic budget-bound feasibility. It also computes gross exposure and expected return. It includes **12 reference unit tests**.

```python
"""Portfolio constraints: transparent long-only feasibility checks."""
import math
import unittest

def finite_vector(values,name):
    if not isinstance(values,(list,tuple)) or not values:
        raise ValueError(name+" must be a nonempty sequence")
    if any(type(x) not in (int,float) or not math.isfinite(x) for x in values):
        raise ValueError(name+" must contain finite numbers")
    return [float(x) for x in values]

def check_constraints(weights,lower,upper,group_memberships=None,group_limits=None,
                      reference=None,max_turnover=None):
    w=finite_vector(weights,"weights")
    lo=finite_vector(lower,"lower")
    hi=finite_vector(upper,"upper")
    n=len(w)
    if len(lo)!=n or len(hi)!=n:
        raise ValueError("bound dimensions must match")
    if any(lo[i]<0 or hi[i]>1 or lo[i]>hi[i] for i in range(n)):
        raise ValueError("long-only bounds must satisfy 0 <= lower <= upper <= 1")
    if group_memberships is None:group_memberships={}
    if group_limits is None:group_limits={}
    if set(group_memberships)!=set(group_limits):
        raise ValueError("group definitions and limits must match")
    if not isinstance(group_memberships,dict) or not isinstance(group_limits,dict):
        raise ValueError("group inputs must be dictionaries")
    if not math.isclose(math.fsum(w),1,rel_tol=0,abs_tol=1e-10):
        raise ValueError("weights must sum to one")
    violations=[]
    for i in range(n):
        if w[i]<lo[i]-1e-10 or w[i]>hi[i]+1e-10:
            violations.append("asset "+str(i)+" outside bounds")
    groups={}
    for name,indices in group_memberships.items():
        if not isinstance(indices,(list,tuple,set)) or any(type(i) is not int or not 0<=i<n for i in indices):
            raise ValueError("invalid group indices")
        if len(indices)!=len(set(indices)):
            raise ValueError("duplicate group member")
        bounds=group_limits[name]
        if not isinstance(bounds,(list,tuple)) or len(bounds)!=2:
            raise ValueError("group limit pair required")
        gl,gu=bounds
        if any(type(x) not in (int,float) or not math.isfinite(x) for x in (gl,gu)) or not 0<=gl<=gu<=1:
            raise ValueError("invalid group bounds")
        exposure=math.fsum(w[i] for i in indices)
        groups[name]=exposure
        if exposure<gl-1e-10 or exposure>gu+1e-10:
            violations.append("group "+name+" outside bounds")
    turnover=None
    if (reference is None)!=(max_turnover is None):
        raise ValueError("reference and turnover limit required together")
    if reference is not None:
        ref=finite_vector(reference,"reference")
        if len(ref)!=n or any(x<0 for x in ref) or not math.isclose(math.fsum(ref),1,rel_tol=0,abs_tol=1e-10):
            raise ValueError("reference must be aligned long-only weights")
        if type(max_turnover) not in (int,float) or not math.isfinite(max_turnover) or max_turnover<0:
            raise ValueError("invalid turnover limit")
        turnover=.5*math.fsum(abs(w[i]-ref[i]) for i in range(n))
        if turnover>max_turnover+1e-10:
            violations.append("one-way turnover exceeds limit")
    return {"feasible":not violations,"violations":violations,
            "group_exposures":groups,"one_way_turnover":turnover}

def necessary_budget_bounds(lower,upper):
    lo=finite_vector(lower,"lower")
    hi=finite_vector(upper,"upper")
    if len(lo)!=len(hi) or any(not 0<=lo[i]<=hi[i]<=1 for i in range(len(lo))):
        raise ValueError("invalid long-only bounds")
    return math.fsum(lo)<=1+1e-10 and math.fsum(hi)>=1-1e-10

def gross_exposure(weights):
    w=finite_vector(weights,"weights")
    return math.fsum(abs(x) for x in w)

def expected_return(weights,means):
    w=finite_vector(weights,"weights")
    mu=finite_vector(means,"means")
    if len(w)!=len(mu):raise ValueError("dimensions must align")
    return math.fsum(a*b for a,b in zip(w,mu))

class ConstraintTests(unittest.TestCase):
    W=[.5,.3,.2]
    LO=[0,0,0]
    HI=[.6,.6,.4]
    def test_01_feasible(self):self.assertTrue(check_constraints(self.W,self.LO,self.HI)["feasible"])
    def test_02_weight_violation(self):
        r=check_constraints([.7,.2,.1],self.LO,self.HI)
        self.assertFalse(r["feasible"])
    def test_03_group(self):
        r=check_constraints(self.W,self.LO,self.HI,{"risk":[0,1]},{"risk":(.5,.85)})
        self.assertAlmostEqual(r["group_exposures"]["risk"],.8)
    def test_04_group_violation(self):
        r=check_constraints(self.W,self.LO,self.HI,{"risk":[0,1]},{"risk":(0,.7)})
        self.assertFalse(r["feasible"])
    def test_05_turnover(self):
        r=check_constraints(self.W,self.LO,self.HI,reference=[.4,.4,.2],max_turnover=.1)
        self.assertAlmostEqual(r["one_way_turnover"],.1)
    def test_06_turnover_violation(self):
        r=check_constraints(self.W,self.LO,self.HI,reference=[.4,.4,.2],max_turnover=.05)
        self.assertFalse(r["feasible"])
    def test_07_budget_bounds(self):self.assertTrue(necessary_budget_bounds([0,0],[.6,.6]))
    def test_08_infeasible_bounds(self):self.assertFalse(necessary_budget_bounds([.6,.6],[.8,.8]))
    def test_09_gross(self):self.assertAlmostEqual(gross_exposure([1.2,-.2]),1.4)
    def test_10_return(self):self.assertAlmostEqual(expected_return(self.W,[.08,.05,.02]),.059)
    def test_11_empty(self):
        with self.assertRaises(ValueError):check_constraints([],[],[])
    def test_12_invalid(self):
        for fn in (lambda:check_constraints([.5,.4],[0,0],[1,1]),
                   lambda:check_constraints(self.W,self.LO,self.HI,{"g":[4]},{"g":(0,1)}),
                   lambda:check_constraints(self.W,self.LO,self.HI,reference=self.W),
                   lambda:necessary_budget_bounds([.5],[.4])):
            with self.assertRaises(ValueError):fn()

if __name__=="__main__":
    print(check_constraints([.5,.3,.2],[0,0,0],[.6,.6,.4],
          {"equity":[0,1]},{"equity":(.5,.85)},
          reference=[.4,.4,.2],max_turnover=.1))
    unittest.main(verbosity=2)
```

## 24. Troubleshooting

| Symptom | Likely cause | Corrective action |
|---|---|---|
| Weights fail budget | Missing cash or stale NAV | Reconcile weights and cash |
| Portfolio violates issuer cap | Multiple overlapping holdings | Use look-through exposure |
| Constraint system infeasible | Conflicting floors and ceilings | Diagnose conflict and seek mandate clarification |
| Turnover differs across reports | Different one-way/two-way convention | Standardize calculation |
| Leverage seems low | Net rather than gross exposure used | Calculate both |
| Optimizer succeeds but trade breaches | Missing post-trade check | Revalidate final rounded positions |
| Sector exposure double-counted | Overlapping group memberships | Interpret each group separately |
| Integer lots violate target weights | Discrete execution constraints | Use integer-aware execution logic |
| Breach appears after market movement | Weight drift | Monitor and rebalance under approved rules |
