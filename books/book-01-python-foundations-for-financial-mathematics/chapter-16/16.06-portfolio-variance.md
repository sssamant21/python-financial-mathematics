## Learning objectives

Derive portfolio variance from asset returns; calculate two-asset and multi-asset variance; interpret covariance cross terms and diversification; distinguish variance from volatility; compute marginal and component variance contributions; and validate financial covariance inputs.

## 1. What portfolio variance measures

**Portfolio variance** measures the expected squared deviation of a portfolio's return from its expected return. For a random portfolio return Rₚ with finite second moment:

**Var(Rₚ)=E[(Rₚ−E[Rₚ])²].**

Variance is expressed in **squared return units**. Portfolio volatility is the square root of variance and is expressed in the original return units. Neither statistic captures all possible risks, such as illiquidity, tail losses, or changing correlations.

## 2. Derivation for two assets

For fixed starting weights w₁ and w₂, with w₁+w₂=1, define:

**Rₚ=w₁R₁+w₂R₂.**

Let μᵢ=E[Rᵢ]. Then:

**Rₚ−E[Rₚ]=w₁(R₁−μ₁)+w₂(R₂−μ₂).**

Squaring and taking expectations yields:

**Var(Rₚ)=w₁²Var(R₁)+w₂²Var(R₂)+2w₁w₂Cov(R₁,R₂).**

Writing σᵢ²=Var(Rᵢ):

**σₚ²=w₁²σ₁²+w₂²σ₂²+2w₁w₂σ₁σ₂ρ₁₂.**

The **cross term** captures co-movement. A portfolio's variance cannot generally be found by weighting asset variances alone.

## 3. Worked two-asset example

Consider a hypothetical portfolio with:

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---:|
| Asset A weight | 60% |
| Asset B weight | 40% |
| Asset A variance | 0.04 |
| Asset B variance | 0.09 |
| Covariance A,B | 0.01 |

The covariance matrix and weights are:

```text
Σ = [[0.04, 0.01],
     [0.01, 0.09]]
w = [0.60, 0.40]
```

Compute:

**σₚ²=(.6)²(.04)+(.4)²(.09)+2(.6)(.4)(.01)**

**=.0144+.0144+.0048=.0336.**

The portfolio volatility is:

**σₚ=√.0336≈.183303=18.33%.**

The time horizon of this result is the same as the return horizon used to estimate the covariance matrix.

## 4. Matrix representation

For n assets, let **w** be the n×1 vector of fixed weights and **Σ** the n×n covariance matrix.

**Var(Rₚ)=wᵀΣw.**

Expanding:

**wᵀΣw=Σᵢwᵢ²Σᵢᵢ+2Σᵢ₍ⱼ>ᵢ₎wᵢwⱼΣᵢⱼ.**

The first sum contains diagonal variance terms. The second contains pairwise covariance terms. This identity works for long-only and signed exposures when the return and NAV definitions are consistent.

## 5. Diagonal versus cross-term decomposition

For the example above:

- Diagonal contribution: .0144+.0144=**.0288**.
- Cross-term contribution: 2(.6)(.4)(.01)=**.0048**.
- Total: .0288+.0048=**.0336**.

The diagonal/cross decomposition is useful for understanding the mathematical effect of covariance. It is **not** an additive allocation of risk to individual assets because cross terms involve pairs of assets.

## 6. Diversification and correlation

Consider two assets, each with volatility 20%, held at 50/50 weights.

With zero correlation:

**σₚ²=.25(.04)+.25(.04)=.02**, so σₚ≈14.14%.

With perfect positive correlation:

**σₚ²=.02+2(.5)(.5)(.04)=.04**, so σₚ=20%.

With perfect negative correlation:

**σₚ²=.02−.02=0**.

These are theoretical examples. Perfect negative correlation is rare and may not persist. Diversification depends on the covariance structure, not simply the number of holdings.

## 7. Why variance cannot be negative

A valid covariance matrix is symmetric and **positive semidefinite (PSD)**. Therefore:

**wᵀΣw≥0 for every real vector w.**

A materially negative computed variance indicates that the matrix is invalid or the implementation is wrong. Tiny negative values can arise from floating-point error, but clipping should only occur within an explicitly justified tolerance and after validation.

A matrix can be PSD yet singular, particularly when assets have perfectly dependent returns or the sample contains too few observations.

## 8. Variance contributions by asset

Let **m=Σw**, where mᵢ is the ith component of the covariance-weighted exposure vector.

Define the **component variance contribution** of asset i:

**VCᵢ=wᵢ(Σw)ᵢ.**

These contributions satisfy:

**ΣᵢVCᵢ=wᵀΣw=σₚ².**

For the worked example:

**Σw=[.04(.6)+.01(.4), .01(.6)+.09(.4)]=[.028,.042].**

Then:

**VC_A=.6(.028)=.0168.**

**VC_B=.4(.042)=.0168.**

The contributions sum to .0336. They are equal even though the portfolio weights and asset volatilities differ.

Individual contributions may be negative for hedging positions; this does not imply negative total variance.

## 9. Marginal variance

For a symmetric covariance matrix:

**∇₍w₎(wᵀΣw)=2Σw.**

Thus the marginal change in variance per infinitesimal change in weight i, holding the other coordinates fixed, is:

**∂σₚ²/∂wᵢ=2(Σw)ᵢ.**

For the example:

**∂σₚ²/∂w_A=.056**, and **∂σₚ²/∂w_B=.084**.

These are **unconstrained partial derivatives**. Changing one weight alone generally violates the fully invested condition. A feasible rebalance changes at least one other weight; constrained optimization uses appropriate directional derivatives or Lagrange multipliers.

## 10. Sensitivity to covariance

For two assets with fixed weights:

**∂σₚ²/∂Cov(R₁,R₂)=2w₁w₂.**

With weights [.6,.4], this derivative is .48. Increasing covariance by .005 increases variance by:

**Δσₚ²=.48(.005)=.0024.**

The sign reverses for a portfolio with one positive and one negative weight. This is why hedging and shorting can produce different covariance sensitivities.

## 11. Time horizon and annualization

Under assumptions such as independent, identically distributed daily simple returns and stable covariance:

**Σ_annual≈252Σ_daily.**

Then:

**σ²ₚ,annual≈252σ²ₚ,daily** and **σₚ,annual≈√252σₚ,daily**.

These are approximations. Serial correlation, nonstationary volatility, overlapping returns, irregular markets, and compounding can invalidate naive square-root-of-time scaling.

## 12. Data quality and estimation

Portfolio variance estimates inherit all limitations of the covariance matrix:

- Incorrect asset ordering between weights and matrix.
- Misaligned dates or stale observations.
- Mixed currencies or return conventions.
- Different missing-data samples for each covariance entry.
- Percent-versus-decimal scaling errors.
- Insufficient observations relative to assets.
- Regime shifts and stress correlations.
- Non-PSD matrices or unstable estimation.

A robust implementation records data windows, estimator definitions, valuation times, and assumptions. Sections 16.7–16.11 extend these ideas to volatility and diversification.

## 13. Python reference implementation

Save as `portfolio_variance.py`. This standard-library reference provides a two-dimensional or multi-asset quadratic-form calculation, diagonal/cross decomposition, marginal variance, component variance contributions, and portfolio volatility. It includes **12 reference unit tests**.

```python
import math
import unittest
from fractions import Fraction
from decimal import Decimal, localcontext

def exact(x):
    if type(x) not in (int,float):
        raise ValueError("finite int/float required; booleans excluded")
    try:
        if not math.isfinite(x):
            raise ValueError("finite int/float required")
    except OverflowError as exc:
        raise ValueError("input outside finite float range") from exc
    return Fraction(x) if type(x) is int else Fraction(str(x))

def report(x):
    try:
        value=float(x)
    except (OverflowError,ValueError) as exc:
        raise ValueError("unreportable numeric result") from exc
    if not math.isfinite(value) or (x != 0 and value == 0):
        raise ValueError("unreportable numeric result")
    return value

def root(x):
    with localcontext() as ctx:
        ctx.prec=80
        return (Decimal(x.numerator)/Decimal(x.denominator)).sqrt()

def psd(a):
    # Exact Schur complements; a zero pivot must have an all-zero row.
    a=[row[:] for row in a]
    for k in range(len(a)):
        pivot=a[k][k]
        if pivot<0:
            return False
        if pivot==0:
            if any(a[k][j]!=0 for j in range(k+1,len(a))):
                return False
            continue
        for i in range(k+1,len(a)):
            for j in range(i,len(a)):
                a[i][j]-=a[i][k]*a[k][j]/pivot
                a[j][i]=a[i][j]
    return True

def _covariance(cov,tolerance=1e-10):
    relative=exact(tolerance)
    if relative<0 or relative>Fraction(1,10**6):
        raise ValueError("relative tolerance must be in [0,1e-6]")
    n=None
    if not isinstance(cov,(list,tuple)) or not cov:
        raise ValueError("nonempty square covariance required")
    if n is None:
        n=len(cov)
    if len(cov)!=n or any(not isinstance(row,(list,tuple)) or len(row)!=n for row in cov):
        raise ValueError("square aligned covariance required")
    a=[[exact(x) for x in row] for row in cov]
    if any(a[i][i]<0 for i in range(n)):
        raise ValueError("negative asset variance")
    scale=max(a[i][i] for i in range(n))
    tolerance=scale*relative
    for i in range(n):
        for j in range(i+1,n):
            if abs(a[i][j]-a[j][i])>max(abs(a[i][j]),abs(a[j][i]))*relative+tolerance:
                raise ValueError("covariance must be symmetric within stated tolerance")
            a[i][j]=a[j][i]=(a[i][j]+a[j][i])/2
    if any(a[i][i]==0 and any(a[i][j]!=0 for j in range(n)) for i in range(n)):
        raise ValueError("zero variance requires zero covariance row")
    shifted=[[a[i][j]+(tolerance if i==j else 0) for j in range(n)] for i in range(n)]
    if not psd(shifted):
        raise ValueError("covariance is not PSD within scale-relative tolerance")
    return a,tolerance

def validate_covariance(matrix,tolerance=1e-10):
    a,_=_covariance(matrix,tolerance)
    return [[report(x) for x in row] for row in a]

def _weights(weights,n,allow_short):
    if type(allow_short) is not bool:
        raise ValueError("allow_short must be boolean")
    if not isinstance(weights,(list,tuple)) or len(weights)!=n:
        raise ValueError("one weight per asset required")
    w=[exact(x) for x in weights]
    if not allow_short and any(x<0 for x in w):
        raise ValueError("negative weight requires short permission")
    budget=sum(w)
    if abs(budget-1)>Fraction(1,10**10):
        raise ValueError("fully invested weights must sum to one")
    return [x/budget for x in w]

def validate_weights(weights,n,allow_short=False):
    return [report(x) for x in _weights(weights,n,allow_short)]

def _inputs(weights,covariance,allow_short):
    a,_=_covariance(covariance)
    w=_weights(weights,len(a),allow_short)
    weighted=[sum(a[i][j]*w[j] for j in range(len(w))) for i in range(len(w))]
    q=sum(w[i]*weighted[i] for i in range(len(w)))
    if q<0:
        raise ValueError("negative exact requested quadratic form; review tolerated covariance")
    return a,w,weighted,q

def portfolio_variance(weights,covariance,allow_short=False):
    _,_,_,q=_inputs(weights,covariance,allow_short)
    return report(q)

def reconcile(values,total):
    try:
        summed=math.fsum(values)
    except OverflowError as exc:
        raise ValueError("reported attribution cannot reconcile") from exc
    if not math.isclose(summed,total,rel_tol=1e-10,abs_tol=0):
        raise ValueError("reported attribution loses reconciliation; rescale inputs")

def variance_decomposition(weights,covariance,allow_short=False):
    a,w,_,q=_inputs(weights,covariance,allow_short)
    diagonal=sum(w[i]**2*a[i][i] for i in range(len(w)))
    cross=2*sum(w[i]*w[j]*a[i][j] for i in range(len(w)) for j in range(i+1,len(w)))
    values=[report(diagonal),report(cross)]
    total=report(q)
    reconcile(values,total)
    return {"diagonal":values[0],"cross":values[1],"total":total}

def marginal_variance(weights,covariance,allow_short=False):
    _,_,weighted,_=_inputs(weights,covariance,allow_short)
    return [report(2*x) for x in weighted]

def asset_variance_contributions(weights,covariance,allow_short=False):
    _,w,weighted,_=_inputs(weights,covariance,allow_short)
    values=[report(w[i]*weighted[i]) for i in range(len(w))]
    reconcile(values,report(sum(w[i]*weighted[i] for i in range(len(w)))))
    return values

def volatility(weights,covariance,allow_short=False):
    _,_,_,q=_inputs(weights,covariance,allow_short)
    return report(root(q))

class PortfolioVarianceTests(unittest.TestCase):
    C = [[0.04, 0.01], [0.01, 0.09]]
    def test_01_two_asset(self):
        self.assertAlmostEqual(portfolio_variance([.6,.4],self.C), .0336)
    def test_02_volatility(self):
        self.assertAlmostEqual(volatility([.6,.4],self.C), math.sqrt(.0336))
    def test_03_diagonal(self):
        self.assertAlmostEqual(variance_decomposition([.6,.4],self.C)["diagonal"], .0288)
    def test_04_cross(self):
        self.assertAlmostEqual(variance_decomposition([.6,.4],self.C)["cross"], .0048)
    def test_05_single_asset(self):
        self.assertAlmostEqual(portfolio_variance([1,0],self.C), .04)
    def test_06_uncorrelated(self):
        self.assertAlmostEqual(portfolio_variance([.5,.5],[[.04,0],[0,.09]]),.0325)
    def test_07_perfect_hedge(self):
        self.assertAlmostEqual(portfolio_variance([.5,.5],[[1,-1],[-1,1]]),0)
    def test_08_contributions(self):
        self.assertAlmostEqual(math.fsum(asset_variance_contributions([.6,.4],self.C)),.0336)
    def test_09_marginal(self):
        self.assertAlmostEqual(marginal_variance([.6,.4],self.C)[0], .056)
    def test_10_short(self):
        self.assertAlmostEqual(portfolio_variance([1.2,-.2],self.C,True),.0564)
    def test_11_bad_matrix(self):
        for m in ([[1,2],[0,1]], [[-1]], [[float("nan")]]):
            with self.assertRaises(ValueError):portfolio_variance([1] if len(m)==1 else [.5,.5],m)
    def test_12_bad_weights(self):
        for w in ([.6,.3],[1.2,-.2],[float("inf"),0]):
            with self.assertRaises(ValueError):portfolio_variance(w,self.C)

if __name__=="__main__":
    weights=[.6,.4]
    covariance=[[.04,.01],[.01,.09]]
    print("Variance:",portfolio_variance(weights,covariance))
    print("Volatility:",volatility(weights,covariance))
    print("Components:",variance_decomposition(weights,covariance))
    unittest.main(verbosity=2)
```

**Validation boundary:** This corrected educational listing screens the full covariance matrix, then checks the requested exact quadratic form. Integer/displayed-decimal rational arithmetic avoids intermediate float cancellation and overflow. Matrix symmetry uses pair-relative plus maximum-diagonal-scale tolerance; accepted asymmetric pairs are averaged. The full PSD screen applies rational Schur complements to the matrix plus `delta*I`, where `delta=tolerance*max(diagonal)` (default tolerance `1e-10`, allowed range `[0,1e-6]`). Negative diagonal entries and nonzero covariance rows for zero-variance assets are rejected. This is numerical screening, not exact PSD certification or market-model acceptance.

Fully invested weights within `1e-10` of unit mass are explicitly normalized before calculation; signed positions require boolean `allow_short=True`. The source exposes normalized float validation helpers for learners, but calculation routines retain exact intermediate values. Financing, asset identifiers and NAV conventions are external prerequisites. Marginals are unconstrained partial derivatives **at the validated normalized weight vector**; they are not derivatives through the normalization adapter.

Every required float field must be finite and must preserve an exact nonzero value; otherwise `ValueError` is deliberate. Each API validates its own requested outputs. Volatility takes an 80-digit Decimal root before float reporting, so it can be reportable when variance is too small or large for a float. Decomposition fields or contributions can be unreportable even when total variance is finite. Reported diagonal/cross and asset-contribution vectors must also sum to the reported total within relative `1e-10` (no absolute floor); cancellation that destroys float-output reconciliation raises `ValueError` even when exact identities hold. No negative requested exact form is clipped: under rational arithmetic it is negative in the accepted input matrix, including a matrix admitted by the PSD tolerance, and is rejected. This preserves variance/contribution reconciliation instead of hiding negative risk under an absolute floor.

## 16. Troubleshooting

| Symptom | Likely cause | Recommended action |
|---|---|---|
| Portfolio variance is negative | Non-PSD covariance or code error | Investigate matrix and calculation |
| Variance differs across systems | Weight order or covariance convention | Reconcile metadata and units |
| Volatility is 100× too large | Percent/decimal mismatch | Normalize return scale |
| Risk contributions do not sum | Incorrect decomposition | Recompute wᵢ(Σw)ᵢ |
| Marginal risk seems inconsistent | Confused with constrained derivative | Account for budget constraint |
| Variance falls despite risky assets | Negative covariance | Inspect diversification assumptions |
| Matrix is singular | Collinear assets or too few observations | Assess rank and estimation |
| Annual risk differs | Different day count or serial dependence | State annualization assumptions |
| Backtest risk is too low | Look-ahead, survivorship, or missing costs | Enforce point-in-time validation |
