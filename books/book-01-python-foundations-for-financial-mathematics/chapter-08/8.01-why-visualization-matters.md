## 1. Purpose

A correct endpoint calculation can conceal an important feature of the journey. Two price series can each finish 20% above their starting point while one falls sharply along the way. A third can have the same endpoint change but incomplete observations. A table reports exact values; a chart helps the reader inspect their order, spacing, scale, and coverage.

Visualization is part of the analytical process: formulate a question, validate the data, choose a transformation, plot it, investigate what becomes visible, and reconcile the result with numerical checks. Attractive output alone does not establish financial correctness.

This section introduces that purpose through a complete, small plotting lab. Section 8.2 develops Matplotlib's Figure and Axes interface systematically. Later sections expand the chart families introduced here.

## 3. A Financial Question Before a Chart

Begin with a sentence that can be answered from the available data. For example: “How did these supplied price observations evolve relative to their initial values, and which adjacent intervals have calculable returns?” This question defines both a transformation and a missing-data policy.

Avoid allowing a chart type to determine the question. A histogram describes the distribution of supplied observations but loses their time order. A line chart retains order but does not directly summarize the frequency of outcomes. Neither automatically measures investment suitability or predicts subsequent outcomes.

| Question | Useful starting chart | Context that must accompany it |
|---|---|---|
| How did an observed series evolve? | Time-series line with observation markers | Time convention, units, missingness |
| How do price changes compare from a common start? | Indexed price paths | Base date, adjustment policy, common coverage |
| Which supplied intervals gained or lost? | Return markers or bars | Interval definition, percentage convention |
| How are observed returns distributed? | Histogram | Sample size, horizon, bin choices |
| How do two measured variables vary together? | Scatter plot | Aligned observations, units; no causal inference |
| What is the composition of this portfolio? | Bar chart | Valuation date, denominator, short-position policy |
| How does a model respond to a changed input? | Scenario or sensitivity curve | Held-fixed assumptions, parameter range |

These are starting choices, not universal rules. The remaining Chapter 8 sections develop their interpretation and implementation.

## 4. Worked Data: Same Endpoint, Different Evidence

Use synthetic USD price quotes for three fictional series. Elapsed days are measured from the first observation. The shared grid is deliberately irregular; it is not an exchange calendar.

| Elapsed calendar day | CALM price | DIP price | GAP price |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 0 | 100 | 200 | 50 |
| 1 | 105 | 160 | 49 |
| 4 | 110 | 180 | Missing |
| 5 | 115 | 210 | 56 |
| 8 | 120 | 240 | 60 |

All three endpoint changes are:

$$R_{0,T}=\frac{P_T}{P_0}-1=0.20=20\%.$$

That equality does not make the observed paths equivalent. DIP falls from 200 to 160 before recovering. CALM increases at every supplied observation. GAP contains an unobserved point, so the table cannot establish its movement over every adjacent grid interval.

The names describe the fixture, not statistically established risk characteristics. Five observations cannot support a general risk ranking. Unobserved intrainterval movements are unknown even for CALM and DIP.

## 5. Make Comparisons in Explicit Units

DIP starts at 200 and GAP at 50. Overlaying their raw prices answers a price-level question; it does not make their relative changes easy to compare. Index each column to a common starting value:

$$I_t=100\frac{P_t}{P_0}.$$

DIP becomes `[100, 80, 90, 105, 120]`. CALM becomes `[100, 105, 110, 115, 120]`. GAP becomes `[100, 98, NaN, 112, 120]`. The vertical axis is now a dimensionless price index, not USD and not return in percent. An index value of 120 represents 20% above its own starting price, rather than a 120% gain.

For each pair of adjacent supplied grid points, calculate:

$$r_i=\frac{P_i}{P_{i-1}}-1.$$

Store returns as decimal fractions and multiply by 100 only for the plotted percentage values. DIP's first interval is `-0.20`, displayed as `-20%`. The lower panel uses markers at interval endpoints. These returns span 1, 3, 1, and 3 calendar days respectively. Their magnitudes are not directly comparable as equal-horizon daily risk observations, and the lab does not annualize them.

The data contain prices only: no dividends, coupons, fees, taxes, corporate-action adjustments, or currency conversion. Calling the indexed series a total-return wealth curve would require additional assumptions and inputs. Indexing also does not make different currencies economically comparable.

## 6. Missingness Must Survive the Picture

GAP has no price at day 4. Keep `NaN` in the price array. Both returns that depend on that price are unknown: day 1→4 and day 4→5. The independently observed endpoint ratio remains calculable.

Dropping the missing row from GAP alone creates a different interval: day 1→5. Its price change would be `56 / 49 - 1`, about 14.29%, over four calendar days. It cannot stand in for either of the two unknown common-grid returns. Filling day 4 with 49 would invent a zero return followed by a larger positive return.

Matplotlib can break a plotted line at a NaN and omit that observation's marker [2]. Deleting the point instead allows a line segment to connect the surrounding observations. This is why missing-data treatment belongs to the analytical contract, rather than merely to chart cleanup.

A visible gap establishes missing evidence at a supplied grid point. It does not explain why the value is absent or verify that the rest of a real data feed is complete.

## 7. Interpretation Choices That Change the Message

**Time spacing.** Plot the actual elapsed offsets `[0, 1, 4, 5, 8]`, not equally spaced category positions. Otherwise a three-day interval appears as short as a one-day interval. Connecting observed points is a visual guide; it does not establish a linear intervening market path.

**Vertical range.** Line charts may use a focused vertical range when its labels and purpose make that choice clear. A zero baseline is usually essential when bar length encodes magnitude. A tightly cropped price axis can emphasize small variations; changing limits must not be presented as changing the underlying return. The lab uses autoscaled line axes with visible tick labels and reference lines.

**Transformation.** Linear and logarithmic scales emphasize different relationships. A log price axis displays equal proportional changes with equal distances, and needs strictly positive prices. It is not the same object as a sequence of log returns. This lab uses linear axes throughout.

**Coverage and selection.** Start date, end date, included assets, and excluded observations influence the story. A polished panel cannot justify a selected window. State the window and explain exclusions before interpreting a pattern.

**Accessibility.** Use labels, distinct markers, and line styles alongside color. Ensure the exported figure remains readable at its intended size. Overlapping points can hide one another; the accompanying data table remains necessary for exact values.

**Association.** A visual relationship suggests a question to investigate. It does not establish causality, statistical significance, or forecasting ability. A smooth fitted line or shaded region requires a documented model and uncertainty interpretation.

## 9. Implementation — `visualization_purpose.py`

```python
"""Synthetic price paths: endpoint summaries, path shape, and missingness."""
from pathlib import Path

import matplotlib
matplotlib.use("Agg")  # Set before importing pyplot; no desktop required.
import matplotlib.pyplot as plt
import numpy as np


def fixture():
    return (
        np.array([0, 1, 4, 5, 8], dtype=np.int64),
        np.array([[100., 200., 50.], [105., 160., 49.],
                  [110., 180., np.nan], [115., 210., 56.],
                  [120., 240., 60.]]),
        ("CALM", "DIP", "GAP"),
    )


def prepare(days, prices, names):
    """Validate a small common grid; preserve NaNs without filling them."""
    if not isinstance(days, np.ndarray) or days.ndim != 1:
        raise ValueError("days must be a one-dimensional NumPy array")
    if days.dtype.kind not in "iu" or not 2 <= len(days) <= 10_000:
        raise ValueError("days must contain 2..10000 integer offsets")
    if days[0] != 0 or np.any(days[1:] <= days[:-1]):
        raise ValueError("days must start at zero and strictly increase")
    if not isinstance(prices, np.ndarray) or prices.ndim != 2:
        raise ValueError("prices must be a two-dimensional NumPy array")
    if prices.dtype.kind not in "iuf" or prices.shape[0] != len(days):
        raise ValueError("prices must be real numeric data on the supplied grid")
    if not 1 <= prices.shape[1] <= 10:
        raise ValueError("supply 1..10 price columns")
    if (not isinstance(names, tuple) or len(names) != prices.shape[1]
            or any(not isinstance(n, str) or not n.strip() for n in names)
            or len(set(names)) != len(names)):
        raise ValueError("names must be unique nonblank strings in a tuple")
    p = prices.astype(np.float64, copy=True)
    if np.isinf(p).any() or np.any(p[np.isfinite(p)] <= 0):
        raise ValueError("observed prices must be finite and positive")
    if not np.isfinite(p[[0, -1]]).all():
        raise ValueError("every series needs an observed start and endpoint")
    with np.errstate(over="raise", invalid="raise", divide="raise"):
        try:
            indexed = 100.0 * (p / p[0])
            returns = p[1:] / p[:-1] - 1.0
        except FloatingPointError as exc:
            raise ValueError("price ratios exceed supported floating-point range") from exc
    if np.isinf(indexed).any() or np.isinf(returns).any():
        raise ValueError("derived values must not be infinite")
    return days.copy(), indexed, returns


def build_figure(days, prices, names):
    x, indexed, returns = prepare(days, prices, names)
    fig, axes = plt.subplots(2, 1, figsize=(9, 7), sharex=True,
                             layout="constrained")
    styles = (("#0072B2", "o", "-"), ("#D55E00", "s", "--"),
              ("#009E73", "^", ":"))
    for j, name in enumerate(names):
        color, marker, line = styles[j % len(styles)]
        axes[0].plot(x, indexed[:, j], color=color, marker=marker,
                     linestyle=line, label=name, alpha=0.85)
        axes[1].plot(x[1:], 100.0 * returns[:, j], color=color,
                     marker=marker, linestyle="none", label=name,
                     markersize=7, alpha=0.75)
    axes[0].set_title("Same +20% endpoint; different observed paths (demo fixture)")
    axes[0].set_ylabel("Price index (first observation = 100)")
    axes[0].axhline(100, color="gray", linewidth=0.8)
    axes[0].legend(loc="upper left", ncols=3)
    axes[1].set_title("Adjacent-observation returns; interval lengths vary")
    axes[1].set_ylabel("Price return (%)")
    axes[1].set_xlabel("Elapsed calendar days from first observation")
    axes[1].axhline(0, color="gray", linewidth=0.8)
    axes[1].set_xticks(x)
    for ax in axes:
        ax.grid(alpha=0.2)
    fig.suptitle("Synthetic prices | USD quotes | no distributions, fees, or FX")
    fig.supxlabel("NaN breaks price lines and omits return markers; no filling. "
                  "Segments do not establish the intervening price path.", fontsize=9)
    return fig, axes


def main():
    days, prices, names = fixture()
    _, indexed, returns = prepare(days, prices, names)
    for j, name in enumerate(names):
        endpoint = indexed[-1, j] / 100 - 1
        known = np.isfinite(returns[:, j]).sum()
        print(f"{name}: endpoint={endpoint:.2%}; known intervals={known}/4")
    fig, _ = build_figure(days, prices, names)
    try:
        output = Path("output")
        output.mkdir(exist_ok=True)
        fig.savefig(output / "why_visualization.png", dpi=150)
    finally:
        plt.close(fig)
    print("Saved output/why_visualization.png")


if __name__ == "__main__":
    main()
```

`prepare` separates numerical transformation from presentation. The plot receives the resulting values without changing the source arrays. `build_figure` returns the Figure and Axes so tests can inspect the plotted data. The caller closes the figure after saving; this avoids accumulating figures during repeated runs. Matplotlib's object-oriented interface associates drawing and configuration with explicit Axes objects [1].

The script selects the noninteractive Agg backend before importing pyplot. This produces a PNG without requiring a graphical desktop. It does not open a plot window. A later section discusses interactive workflows and backend choices.

## 10. Reference Tests — `test_visualization_purpose.py`

```python
import tempfile
import unittest
from pathlib import Path

import numpy as np
from numpy.testing import assert_allclose, assert_array_equal

from visualization_purpose import build_figure, fixture, prepare, plt


class VisualizationTests(unittest.TestCase):
    def test_indexed_paths_and_endpoint(self):
        d, p, n = fixture()
        _, indexed, _ = prepare(d, p, n)
        assert_allclose(indexed[:, 1], [100, 80, 90, 105, 120])
        assert_allclose(indexed[[0, -1]], [[100]*3, [120]*3])

    def test_scale_invariance(self):
        d, p, n = fixture()
        a = prepare(d, p, n)
        b = prepare(d, p * [3, 0.5, 10], n)
        assert_allclose(a[1], b[1], equal_nan=True)
        assert_allclose(a[2], b[2], equal_nan=True)

    def test_gap_does_not_become_zero_or_bridge_return(self):
        d, p, n = fixture()
        _, indexed, r = prepare(d, p, n)
        self.assertTrue(np.isnan(indexed[2, 2]))
        assert_array_equal(np.isfinite(r[:, 2]), [True, False, False, True])
        self.assertAlmostEqual(r[0, 2], -0.02)
        self.assertAlmostEqual(r[3, 2], 60 / 56 - 1)
        self.assertAlmostEqual(np.prod(1 + r[:, 1]) - 1, 0.2)

    def test_bad_prices(self):
        d, p, n = fixture()
        for value in [0, -1, np.inf, -np.inf]:
            bad = p.copy(); bad[1, 0] = value
            with self.subTest(value=value), self.assertRaises(ValueError):
                prepare(d, bad, n)
        for row in [0, -1]:
            bad = p.copy(); bad[row, 0] = np.nan
            with self.assertRaises(ValueError): prepare(d, bad, n)
        with self.assertRaises(ValueError): prepare(d, p.astype(str), n)
        with self.assertRaises(ValueError): prepare(d, p[:, 0], n)

    def test_bad_time_grid(self):
        d, p, n = fixture()
        for bad in [np.array([0, 1, 1, 5, 8]), np.array([0, 4, 1, 5, 8]),
                    d + 1, d.astype(float), d[:-1], d.reshape(1, -1)]:
            with self.subTest(grid=str(bad)), self.assertRaises(ValueError):
                prepare(bad, p, n)

    def test_bad_names(self):
        d, p, n = fixture()
        for names in [("A", "A", "B"), ("A", " ", "B"), ("A",), list(n)]:
            with self.assertRaises(ValueError): prepare(d, p, names)

    def test_inputs_unchanged_and_output_independent(self):
        d, p, n = fixture(); original = p.copy(); original_days = d.copy()
        x, indexed, r = prepare(d, p, n)
        x[0] = 99; indexed[0, 0] = 99; r[0, 0] = 99
        assert_array_equal(d, original_days)
        assert_allclose(p, original, equal_nan=True)

    def test_artists_and_png(self):
        d, p, n = fixture(); fig, axes = build_figure(d, p, n)
        try:
            self.assertEqual(len(axes), 2)
            self.assertIn("100", axes[0].get_ylabel())
            self.assertIn("%", axes[1].get_ylabel())
            self.assertTrue(np.isnan(axes[0].lines[2].get_ydata()[2]))
            assert_array_equal(axes[0].lines[0].get_xdata(), d)
            self.assertEqual(axes[1].lines[0].get_linestyle(), "None")
            with tempfile.TemporaryDirectory() as folder:
                path = Path(folder) / "chart.png"
                fig.savefig(path, dpi=100)
                self.assertEqual(path.read_bytes()[:8], b"\x89PNG\r\n\x1a\n")
        finally:
            plt.close(fig)


if __name__ == "__main__":
    unittest.main()
```

The eight tests check known numerical values, invariance under a constant positive rescaling of each price column, missing interval propagation, rejection of invalid prices/time grids/names, input preservation, and the plotted artist data plus PNG signature. Compound checks belong to the same scenario; the suite contains eight test methods.

These tests do not compare byte-identical images. Fonts, rendering libraries, and versions can change raster output without changing its analytical meaning. PNG creation and numerical checks also cannot establish that text is legible or that the chosen chart answers the reader's question. Inspect the exported picture as a separate step.

## 13. Exercises and Answer Guidance

1. **Endpoint versus path:** Why do the three +20% endpoint changes fail to describe the same experience?  
   **Guidance:** DIP has a supplied 20% decline before recovery; CALM is increasing at supplied points; GAP has incomplete intermediate evidence.
2. **Index arithmetic:** A price rises from 80 to 92. Find the index and endpoint return.  
   **Guidance:** Index 115; return 15%. State the base observation.
3. **Price scale:** Multiply every DIP quote by 7. What changes?  
   **Guidance:** Raw prices change; indexed values and adjacent returns do not, within numerical tolerance.
4. **Missing observation:** If GAP's day-4 row is dropped, what interval is created?  
   **Guidance:** Day 1→5, a four-day interval; it does not recover the two missing grid returns.
5. **Time spacing:** What information is lost by replacing elapsed days with equally spaced categories?  
   **Guidance:** The different calendar durations between observations disappear.
6. **Choose a chart:** You want to inspect how often supplied returns lie below zero. Is a price line sufficient?  
   **Guidance:** A return distribution or count summary is more direct; disclose interval definitions and sample size. Do not pool these unequal horizons without a justified policy.
7. **Scope:** Can these plots establish total investment return or annualized volatility?  
   **Guidance:** No. Price-only inputs omit distributions and other effects; the tiny irregular sample and missingness require further methodology for volatility.
8. **Validate presentation:** Name an automated check and a visual check that address different failures.  
   **Guidance:** Assert the -20% DIP return numerically; inspect whether labels, gaps, and legend remain legible in the exported PNG.
