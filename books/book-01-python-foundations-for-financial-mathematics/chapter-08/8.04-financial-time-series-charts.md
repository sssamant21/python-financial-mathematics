## 1. Purpose

A financial time-series chart needs more than a line and some date labels. Each value must belong to a defined instant or reporting period, the horizontal spacing must reflect the intended time convention, and missing expected observations must remain visible.

This section combines Chapter 7's time-index and missing-data policies with Chapter 8's plotting objects. The complete lab uses an explicitly supplied hourly grid, aligns two synthetic USD price series, and displays them in New York local time across a repeated clock hour. It pairs the price chart with a coverage diagnostic so that missing values cannot disappear behind a polished date axis.

The example is synthetic and deliberately runs on a Sunday. It is not an equity exchange session, a real quote feed, or an exchange-calendar demonstration.

## 2. Learning Objectives

After completing this section, you should be able to:

1. Distinguish an instant from its displayed local clock label.
2. Normalize timezone-aware indexes to UTC without changing the represented instants.
3. Align source observations to a declared expected grid.
4. Distinguish an absent source row from a missing price within a supplied row.
5. Configure a Matplotlib date formatter with an explicit display timezone.
6. Preserve line-breaking NaNs and verify elapsed-time spacing.
7. Explain why market sessions, aggregation, adjustment policies, and data coverage remain separate analytical responsibilities.

Prerequisites: Section 7.9, missing-data and multi-asset policies from Chapter 7, and Sections 8.1–8.3.

## 3. Define the Time Meaning First

A timestamp can describe an observation time, a transaction time, a publication time, or the end of an aggregation interval. Those meanings are not interchangeable. A daily price labeled with a date also needs a definition: which market, session, closing event, currency, and adjustment policy does it represent?

For this lab, every timestamp means a supplied point observation. The caller separately provides six expected hourly instants. The program does not infer expectations from the rows that happened to arrive.

| Decision | Lab policy |
|---|---|
| Timestamp meaning | Point observation at the supplied instant |
| Internal alignment | Timezone-aware UTC instants |
| Display timezone | `America/New_York` |
| Expected observations | Six caller-supplied hourly grid points |
| Missing data | Preserve NaN; no filling or interpolation |
| Price basis | Synthetic USD quotes, without adjustment or distributions |
| Vertical comparison | Each price series indexed to 100 at the first expected instant |
| Horizontal spacing | Actual elapsed instants, not equal-spaced text categories |

A real application should write down equivalent decisions before producing a chart. Valid datetime objects alone do not establish correct observation semantics.

## 4. UTC Instants and Local Clock Labels

Timezone-aware timestamps identify instants. Converting an aware index with `tz_convert` changes its timezone representation while preserving those instants [2]. Assigning a timezone to a naive clock value is a different operation: localization requires knowing where that clock value belongs.

The lab rejects naive indexes rather than guessing their timezone. In particular, interpreting a naive local value as UTC merely to make it timezone-aware can shift the intended observation by several hours. Resolve that issue in ingestion using the source's documented time convention.

New York's autumn clock change provides a useful synthetic fixture. Under the timezone rules used for validation, two different UTC instants display as 01:30 on November 1, 2026:

| UTC instant | New York display label |
|---|---|
| 2026-11-01 05:30 UTC | 2026-11-01 01:30 EDT -0400 |
| 2026-11-01 06:30 UTC | 2026-11-01 01:30 EST -0500 |

The instants are one hour apart. The local clock text repeats, but the numeric offsets distinguish them. Removing timezone information would destroy that distinction. For the opposite seasonal transition, some local clock times do not occur. Neither case should be resolved by silently inventing an observation.

Python's `zoneinfo` supplies IANA timezone behavior using available system data or the `tzdata` package [4]. Timezone rules are external data and can change. The example checks the displayed offsets under the runtime used for publication; it is not a claim that every jurisdiction's future rules are immutable.

## 5. The Worked Fixture

The expected grid begins at 04:30 UTC and ends at 09:30 UTC on November 1, 2026, at one-hour intervals. The source omits the entire 06:30 UTC row and contains a missing BETA price at 07:30 UTC.

| UTC time | New York clock label | ALPHA, USD | BETA, USD | Source row present? |
|---|---|---:|---:|---|
| 04:30 | 00:30 EDT -0400 | 100 | 200 | Yes |
| 05:30 | 01:30 EDT -0400 | 101 | 202 | Yes |
| 06:30 | 01:30 EST -0500 | Missing | Missing | No |
| 07:30 | 02:30 EST -0500 | 103 | Missing | Yes |
| 08:30 | 03:30 EST -0500 | 102 | 204 | Yes |
| 09:30 | 04:30 EST -0500 | 104 | 206 | Yes |

There are five supplied source rows, six expected instants, and nine observed price cells out of twelve expected cells. The absent source row accounts for two unknown prices. The explicit BETA NaN accounts for the third unknown price.

“Observed” here means a nonmissing supplied numeric cell that passes the lab's range checks. It does not establish feed authenticity, quote freshness, or economic accuracy.

## 6. Align to Expectations Without Filling

The helper copies the source, converts its index to UTC, checks that every source instant belongs to the expected grid, then applies:

```python
aligned = frame.reindex(grid)
```

Reindexing to new labels introduces missing values where source observations are absent [3]. The lab does not pass a fill method. It also preserves existing NaNs in supplied rows. Out-of-grid observations are rejected before reindexing so that they cannot be silently discarded.

Maintain row provenance separately:

```python
present = pd.Series(grid.isin(index), index=grid, name="source_row_present")
```

A row can be present while one or both of its prices are missing. Conversely, a row introduced by reindexing can contain NaN in both columns because no source row existed. The coverage panel reports nonmissing price cells per expected instant and adds a black cross for an absent source row.

The source index and expected index must each be unique and chronologically ordered. Duplicate observations require a documented upstream resolution rule; plotting is not an appropriate place to choose the last row silently. An expected grid cannot be recovered reliably from a source that may already be incomplete.

## 7. Compare Indexed Prices with a Common Base Instant

The price panel uses:

$$I_{t,j}=100\frac{P_{t,j}}{P_{t_0,j}},$$

where $t_0$ is the first expected instant and $j$ identifies a column. Both starting prices must be observed; the program does not choose a later start independently for each series.

ALPHA's final index is 104 and BETA's final index is 103. Their raw prices, USD 104 and USD 206, differ in scale, but the index makes their price changes from the common baseline directly visible. The axis states that the plotted unit is an index, not USD and not return in percent.

The code does not calculate a complete interval-return path. Endpoint price ratios can remain observable despite intermediate gaps. Such ratios do not recover missing adjacent-period returns or establish total investment returns. Dividends, coupons, corporate actions, costs, taxes, and FX would need separate policies and inputs.

NaNs remain in the indexed data. ALPHA's line breaks at 06:30 UTC. BETA has breaks at both 06:30 and 07:30 UTC. Later supplied values remain visible. No prior value is carried forward to manufacture a flat path.

## 8. Date Coordinates, Locators, and Formatters

Matplotlib represents dates numerically for plotting and provides date-aware locators and formatters [1]. The lab makes the conversion explicit:

```python
x = mdates.date2num(aligned.index.to_pydatetime())
```

The aware instants produce ordered date coordinates. Consecutive grid points remain one hour apart in elapsed time, including the two repeated local 01:30 labels. Do not pass formatted clock strings as x-values: categorical string positions can conceal the distinction between repeated labels and separate instants.

A locator determines where ticks appear; a formatter determines their displayed text. For this six-point bounded illustration, the code selects grid tick positions explicitly and applies:

```python
mdates.DateFormatter("%H:%M\n%Z %z", tz=ZoneInfo("America/New_York"))
```

The first line shows the clock time, and the second includes the timezone abbreviation and numeric offset. The title states the complete UTC reporting window. For a reused multi-date figure, add the local date to tick labels or provide unambiguous date context for every panel. The helper permits up to 48 hours but this clock-only label design is intended for the demonstrated window.

For a longer or interactively zoomed series, automatic ticks are often preferable. This illustrative configuration uses Matplotlib's date locator and concise formatter [1]:

```python
zone = ZoneInfo("America/New_York")
locator = mdates.AutoDateLocator(tz=zone)
formatter = mdates.ConciseDateFormatter(locator, tz=zone)
ax.xaxis.set_major_locator(locator)
ax.xaxis.set_major_formatter(formatter)
```

Here `ax` is an existing Axes. This alternative is not the lab's tick configuration. Inspect automatic labels around repeated hours; use explicit offsets when needed to distinguish otherwise identical labels. Formatting choices must not hide the identity of the underlying instants.

## 9. Financial Calendars and Aggregation Boundaries

An elapsed-time axis leaves time between observations proportional to its duration. An alternative session axis compresses market closures and spaces selected sessions equally. Both can be useful, but they answer different questions. A compressed axis should be labeled as session order, rather than presented as continuous elapsed time.

A generic weekday sequence is not an exchange calendar. Holidays, shortened sessions, overnight sessions, venue timezone rules, and instrument-specific conventions require an authoritative schedule. The synthetic Sunday window here avoids making any claim about such a schedule.

Aggregation also changes time meaning. A resampled price might mean the final observation in a bin; volume might mean a sum over that bin. A bin label can denote its start or end. Review closure and labeling conventions from Section 7.13 before plotting those outputs. Merely formatting a daily tick does not aggregate intraday observations.

When aligning different instruments, confirm common observation semantics before comparing their paths. A calendar date in two venues does not imply simultaneous closing events. The lab accepts two columns on a common explicitly supplied instant grid and does not solve asynchronous-market alignment.

### `time_series_charts.py`

```python
"""A UTC-aligned hourly grid displayed across a repeated local clock hour."""
from pathlib import Path
from zoneinfo import ZoneInfo
import matplotlib
matplotlib.use('Agg')
import matplotlib.pyplot as plt
import matplotlib.dates as mdates
import numpy as np
import pandas as pd

COLUMNS = ('ALPHA', 'BETA')


def fixture():
    expected = pd.date_range('2026-11-01T04:30:00Z', periods=6, freq='h')
    source = pd.DataFrame({'ALPHA': [100., 101., 103., 102., 104.],
                           'BETA': [200., 202., np.nan, 204., 206.]},
                          index=expected.delete(2))
    return source, expected


def utc_index(index, name):
    if (not isinstance(index, pd.DatetimeIndex) or index.tz is None
            or index.hasnans or not index.is_unique
            or not index.is_monotonic_increasing):
        raise ValueError(f'{name} must be a sorted, unique, aware DatetimeIndex')
    result = index.tz_convert('UTC').copy()
    if len(result) and (result[0].year < 2000 or result[-1].year > 2100):
        raise ValueError('supported years are 2000..2100')
    return result


def prepare(source, expected):
    grid = utc_index(expected, 'expected')
    if not 2 <= len(grid) <= 48 or not (grid[1:] - grid[:-1] == pd.Timedelta(hours=1)).all():
        raise ValueError('expected must contain 2..48 consecutive hourly instants')
    if (not isinstance(source, pd.DataFrame) or tuple(source.columns) != COLUMNS
            or not 1 <= len(source) <= len(grid)
            or any(dtype != np.dtype('float64') for dtype in source.dtypes)):
        raise ValueError('source needs ALPHA/BETA float64 columns and 1..grid rows')
    index = utc_index(source.index, 'source')
    if not index.isin(grid).all():
        raise ValueError('source includes instants outside the expected grid')
    frame = source.copy(deep=True)
    frame.index = index
    values = frame.to_numpy()
    observed = values[np.isfinite(values)]
    if (np.isinf(values).any() or (observed < 1e-12).any()
            or (observed > 1e12).any()):
        raise ValueError('observed prices must be in [1e-12, 1e12]')
    aligned = frame.reindex(grid)
    if aligned.iloc[0].isna().any():
        raise ValueError('both starting prices must be observed')
    indexed = 100.0 * aligned.div(aligned.iloc[0])
    present = pd.Series(grid.isin(index), index=grid, name='source_row_present')
    return aligned, indexed, present


def build_figure(source, expected, display_zone='America/New_York'):
    aligned, indexed, present = prepare(source, expected)
    zone = ZoneInfo(display_zone)
    # Explicit date numbers preserve elapsed UTC spacing; timezone controls labels.
    x = mdates.date2num(aligned.index.to_pydatetime())
    fig, axes = plt.subplots(2, 1, figsize=(10, 7), sharex=True,
                             layout='constrained', height_ratios=[2, 1])
    for column, color, marker, style in [
            ('ALPHA', '#0072B2', 'o', '-'), ('BETA', '#D55E00', 's', '--')]:
        axes[0].plot(x, indexed[column].to_numpy(), label=column,
                     color=color, marker=marker, linestyle=style)
    axes[0].set(title='Hourly supplied grid | no filling or interpolation',
                ylabel='Price index (first expected instant = 100)')
    axes[0].legend(loc='upper left')
    counts = aligned.notna().sum(axis=1).to_numpy()
    axes[1].plot(x, counts, linestyle='none', marker='o', color='#0072B2',
                 label='Observed price cells (out of two)')
    missing_x = x[~present.to_numpy()]
    axes[1].plot(missing_x, np.zeros(len(missing_x)), linestyle='none',
                 marker='x', markersize=11, color='black', label='Absent source row')
    axes[1].set(ylabel='Observed cells', ylim=(-0.3, 2.5), yticks=[0, 1, 2],
                title='Coverage diagnostic on the expected grid')
    axes[1].legend(loc='lower right', fontsize=8)
    # Fixed grid ticks are appropriate for this bounded six-point demonstration.
    tick_ids = np.unique(np.linspace(0, len(x)-1, min(6, len(x)), dtype=int))
    axes[1].set_xticks(x[tick_ids])
    axes[1].xaxis.set_major_formatter(mdates.DateFormatter('%H:%M\n%Z %z', tz=zone))
    axes[1].set_xlabel(f'Local clock labels: {display_zone} | elapsed spacing in UTC')
    for ax in axes:
        ax.grid(alpha=0.2)
    start = aligned.index[0].strftime('%Y-%m-%d %H:%M UTC')
    end = aligned.index[-1].strftime('%Y-%m-%d %H:%M UTC')
    fig.suptitle(f'Synthetic USD price quotes | {start} to {end}', fontsize=12)
    fig.supxlabel('Expected grid is supplied by the caller; it is not an exchange calendar.',
                  fontsize=9)
    return fig, axes


def main():
    source, expected = fixture()
    aligned, indexed, present = prepare(source, expected)
    print(f'Expected instants: {len(expected)}; source rows: {len(source)}')
    print(f'Absent rows: {(~present).sum()}; observed price cells: {aligned.notna().sum().sum()}/12')
    for stamp in expected[1:3]:
        print(stamp.tz_convert('America/New_York').strftime('%Y-%m-%d %H:%M %Z %z'))
    fig, _ = build_figure(source, expected)
    try:
        Path('output').mkdir(exist_ok=True)
        fig.savefig('output/financial_time_series.png', dpi=150)
    finally:
        plt.close(fig)
    print('Saved output/financial_time_series.png')


if __name__ == '__main__':
    main()
```

The aligned prices, indexed prices, and source-row-presence vector remain separate outputs. The source is copied before index normalization and transformation. Plotting uses the declared grid rather than filtering the data down to complete rows. The caller saves and closes its specific Figure in a `finally` block. Re-running the demonstration replaces the PNG.

## 11. Reference Tests

### `test_time_series_charts.py`

```python
import tempfile
import unittest
from pathlib import Path
from zoneinfo import ZoneInfo
import matplotlib.dates as mdates
import numpy as np
import pandas as pd
from numpy.testing import assert_allclose, assert_array_equal
from pandas.testing import assert_frame_equal
from time_series_charts import fixture, prepare, build_figure, plt


class TimeSeriesTests(unittest.TestCase):
    def test_alignment_coverage_and_index_values(self):
        source, grid = fixture(); aligned, indexed, present = prepare(source, grid)
        self.assertTrue(aligned.iloc[2].isna().all())
        self.assertTrue(np.isnan(aligned.iloc[3, 1]))
        assert_array_equal(present.to_numpy(), [True, True, False, True, True, True])
        assert_array_equal(aligned.notna().sum(axis=1), [2, 2, 0, 1, 2, 2])
        assert_allclose(indexed.iloc[-1], [104, 103])

    def test_zone_conversion_preserves_instants(self):
        source, grid = fixture(); local = source.copy()
        local.index = local.index.tz_convert('America/New_York')
        assert_frame_equal(prepare(source, grid)[0],
                           prepare(local, grid.tz_convert('Asia/Kolkata'))[0])

    def test_repeated_clock_hour_and_elapsed_spacing(self):
        _, grid = fixture(); x = mdates.date2num(grid.to_pydatetime())
        assert_allclose(np.diff(x) * 24, np.ones(5), atol=1e-9)
        fmt = mdates.DateFormatter('%H:%M %Z %z', tz=ZoneInfo('America/New_York'))
        self.assertEqual(fmt(x[1]), '01:30 EDT -0400')
        self.assertEqual(fmt(x[2]), '01:30 EST -0500')

    def test_invalid_time_indexes(self):
        source, grid = fixture()
        for bad in [grid.tz_localize(None), grid[::-1], grid.insert(1, grid[1]),
                    pd.DatetimeIndex([grid[0], pd.NaT]), grid.delete(2)]:
            with self.subTest(grid=str(bad)), self.assertRaises(ValueError):
                prepare(source, bad)
        bad = source.copy(); bad.index = bad.index.tz_localize(None)
        with self.assertRaises(ValueError): prepare(bad, grid)
        bad = source.copy(); bad.index = bad.index + pd.Timedelta(minutes=1)
        with self.assertRaises(ValueError): prepare(bad, grid)

    def test_invalid_prices_schema_and_baseline(self):
        source, grid = fixture()
        for value in [0, -1, np.inf, 1e13]:
            bad = source.copy(); bad.iloc[1, 0] = value
            with self.assertRaises(ValueError): prepare(bad, grid)
        bad = source.copy(); bad.iloc[0, 0] = np.nan
        with self.assertRaises(ValueError): prepare(bad, grid)
        with self.assertRaises(ValueError): prepare(source[['BETA', 'ALPHA']], grid)
        with self.assertRaises(ValueError): prepare(source.astype(str), grid)

    def test_source_preserved(self):
        source, grid = fixture(); before = source.copy(deep=True)
        aligned, indexed, present = prepare(source, grid)
        aligned.iloc[0, 0] = 99; indexed.iloc[0, 0] = 99; present.iloc[0] = False
        assert_frame_equal(source, before)

    def test_artist_gaps_and_formatter(self):
        source, grid = fixture(); fig, axes = build_figure(source, grid)
        try:
            a, b = axes[0].lines
            self.assertTrue(np.isnan(a.get_ydata()[2]))
            self.assertTrue(np.isnan(b.get_ydata()[2:4]).all())
            x = a.get_xdata()
            assert_allclose(np.diff(x) * 24, np.ones(5), atol=1e-9)
            fmt = axes[1].xaxis.get_major_formatter()
            self.assertIn('EST -0500', fmt(x[2]))
            assert_array_equal(axes[1].lines[1].get_xdata(), [x[2]])
        finally:
            plt.close(fig)

    def test_png_and_cleanup(self):
        source, grid = fixture(); before = set(plt.get_fignums())
        fig, _ = build_figure(source, grid)
        try:
            with tempfile.TemporaryDirectory() as folder:
                path = Path(folder) / 'chart.png'; fig.savefig(path)
                self.assertEqual(path.read_bytes()[:8], b'\x89PNG\r\n\x1a\n')
        finally:
            plt.close(fig)
        self.assertEqual(set(plt.get_fignums()), before)


if __name__ == '__main__':
    unittest.main()
```

The eight methods verify coverage and endpoint index values, equivalence under aware timezone conversion, repeated local-hour labels and actual elapsed spacing, invalid indexes, invalid prices/schema/baseline, source preservation, line Artist gaps/formatter configuration, and PNG export with figure cleanup.

The one-hour spacing check allows floating-point tolerance because date coordinates are represented numerically. It does not claim nanosecond plotting precision. The known-label assertions check the timezone data behavior relevant to this fixture. PNG-signature and Artist tests complement, rather than replace, visual inspection.

## 14. Common Errors and Corrections

| Error | Result | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Labeling naive local times as UTC | Observations can be shifted to incorrect instants | Resolve source timezone semantics before conversion |
| Removing offsets before deduplication | Distinct repeated-hour observations may collapse | Compare aware instants |
| Plotting clock strings as categories | Clock labels replace elapsed-time geometry | Use datetime/date coordinates |
| Deriving expectations from received rows | A completely absent row may remain invisible | Supply the expected schedule independently |
| Reindexing without checking extra source times | Off-grid data may be silently excluded | Reject or explicitly reconcile extra instants first |
| Dropping incomplete rows before plotting | Missing observations disappear and lines bridge gaps | Preserve the declared grid and NaNs |
| Forward-filling to make the path continuous | Invented flat prices look observed | Require a documented imputation policy, then disclose it |
| Treating weekdays as the full trading calendar | Holiday and session semantics may be wrong | Obtain the instrument/venue schedule |
| Changing tick frequency to daily | Gives an appearance of daily data without aggregation | Apply and validate a separate aggregation policy |

## 15. Exercises and Answer Guidance

1. **Repeated hour:** Why are the two 01:30 observations not duplicates?  
   **Guidance:** Their offsets differ; they identify 05:30 and 06:30 UTC, one hour apart.
2. **Timezone conversion:** Convert the source index to another aware timezone before calling `prepare`. Should the aligned prices change?  
   **Guidance:** No. The instants and associated prices remain the same; the tests verify this.
3. **Coverage arithmetic:** Explain the nine observed cells out of twelve.  
   **Guidance:** The absent expected row removes two cells and the supplied BETA NaN removes one more.
4. **Baseline:** Why reject a missing first expected price instead of selecting each asset's first available quote?  
   **Guidance:** Independent choices would change the common-base comparison. A different baseline needs an explicit policy.
5. **Index units:** What does BETA's final index of 103 mean?  
   **Guidance:** Its supplied final price is 3% above its supplied price at the common base instant. The index itself is not a USD amount or a total-return measure.
6. **Calendar:** Does the hourly expected grid establish that a real exchange should have produced quotes on this Sunday?  
   **Guidance:** No. It is a synthetic caller-defined schedule, not an exchange calendar.
7. **Missing rows versus cells:** Could a source row be present with zero observed prices?  
   **Guidance:** Yes, outside the required complete baseline. The row-presence flag distinguishes it from an absent source row, while both have zero observed price cells.
8. **Extend the window:** What label change is appropriate when a report spans several local dates?  
   **Guidance:** Add local-date context or use a suitable date locator/formatter; preserve offsets when repeated clock times need disambiguation.
