## 1. Purpose

A payoff diagram maps a hypothetical underlying price at a specified future event to a contract's terminal payoff. Unlike a time-series chart, its horizontal axis is a scenario variable, not time. Unlike a histogram, it assigns no frequency or probability to those scenarios.

This section uses simple call and put examples to distinguish terminal payoff from a premium-adjusted profit calculation. The complete lab checks the strike kink, break-even prices, contract scaling, and plotted curves. It introduces diagram construction rather than option valuation or trading strategy selection.

The terminal formulas are specified for a simplified European-style exercise-at-expiration model. No early exercise, financing, transaction costs, taxes, settlement frictions, or pre-expiration option-pricing model is included.

## 2. Learning Objectives

2. Distinguish a terminal option payoff from profit after the initial premium.

Prerequisites: piecewise functions, NumPy arrays, and Sections 8.1–8.8. Broader option valuation belongs to later books in the series.

## 3. Define the Scenario and Contract Units

Let $S_T$ be the underlying price at expiration, $K$ the strike, and $c$ or $p$ the initial call or put premium per underlying unit. All are expressed in consistent USD units in this fixture. The underlying scenario domain is nonnegative.

Define the positive part as $x^+=\max(x,0)$. For a long option on one underlying unit:

$$H_c(S_T)=(S_T-K)^+,\qquad H_p(S_T)=(K-S_T)^+.$$

These are economic terminal exercise values in the stated model. They do not describe how a physically settled contract is processed, whether an exercise instruction is submitted, or which position an account holds afterward. Contract-specific settlement and exercise procedures require their own specifications.

The Options Industry Council's long-call and long-put educational references support the expiration-value and break-even conventions used here [1,2]. The numerical fixture and charts are independently developed.

## 4. Payoff and Simplified Profit Are Different Curves

A long option's terminal payoff excludes the premium paid when the position was entered. Define the simplified undiscounted premium-adjusted results as:

$$\Pi_c(S_T)=H_c(S_T)-c,\qquad
\Pi_p(S_T)=H_p(S_T)-p.$$

The premium was paid earlier than expiration. Subtracting it directly is an explicit **no-carry simplification**, not a claim that cash flows at different dates are economically equivalent. A financing-aware terminal profit calculation would require a convention for carrying the initial premium forward, plus any other applicable costs or cash flows. Present-value comparisons would require another stated convention.

For this lab, the profit curve is the payoff curve shifted downward by the initial premium amount. A positive terminal payoff can still accompany negative simplified profit. For example, a call with strike 100 and premium 6 has payoff 3 at a terminal price of 103, but simplified profit -3 per underlying unit.

Do not label both curves simply “payoff.” The figure names the solid line **Terminal payoff** and the dashed line **Payoff minus initial premium (no carry)**. They use the same units, allowing the premium gap to be inspected directly.

## 5. Contract Count, Multiplier, and Sign

Let $n$ be signed contract count and $m$ the number of underlying units represented per contract. Define:

$$Q=nm,\qquad H_{\mathrm{position}}=QH,\qquad
\Pi_{\mathrm{position}}=Q(H-\text{premium}).$$

The fixture uses one long contract and a multiplier of 100. That multiplier is an explicit example input, not an assumption that every option contract has the same deliverable. Adjusted contracts and other products need their actual specifications.

For positive $n$, the premium term is an initial outflow. For negative $n$, the model reverses the position's terminal payoff and premium cash-flow signs. The resulting short-position curve is the negative of the corresponding long-position curve under identical assumed premium and costs. This is an algebraic comparison, not a model of margin, borrowing, liquidation, or assignment exposure.

Multiplying a per-unit premium of 6 by 100 gives an initial premium amount of USD 600 for one fixture call contract. Mixing a per-unit payoff with a per-contract premium would produce an incorrect vertical shift.

## 6. Worked Call and Put Examples

Use strike $K=100$, call premium $c=6$, put premium $p=4$, one long contract, and multiplier 100.

| Terminal underlying price, USD | Call payoff, USD/position | Call simplified profit, USD/position | Put payoff, USD/position | Put simplified profit, USD/position |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 0 | 0 | -600 | 10,000 | 9,600 |
| 96 | 0 | -600 | 400 | 0 |
| 100 | 0 | -600 | 0 | -400 |
| 106 | 600 | 0 | 0 | -400 |
| 120 | 2,000 | 1,400 | 0 | -400 |

At the strike, both long terminal payoffs are zero, while the simplified profit values remain negative because of the premiums. The call's payoff rises above the strike; the put's payoff rises as the terminal price falls below the strike.

For the positive premiums in this fixture, the simplified expiration break-even coordinates are:

$$S_{T,c}^{\mathrm{BE}}=K+c=106,\qquad
S_{T,p}^{\mathrm{BE}}=K-p=96.$$

Those formulas are specific to the stated no-carry, no-cost convention [1,2]. The call's maximum loss under that convention is the premium amount, USD 600; the put's is USD 400. With $S_T\geq0$, the fixture put's maximum payoff is USD 10,000 at zero underlying price. A finite displayed call-price range is not an upper bound on its mathematical payoff.

The reusable helper permits other nonnegative premiums. If a put premium exceeds its strike, the formal `K - premium` crossing lies outside the nonnegative underlying domain. With a zero premium, an entire zero-payoff region also has zero simplified profit, so the usual crossing is not necessarily the only break-even scenario. The plotted fixture avoids these edge cases and marks only its stated positive-premium break-even points.

## 7. Build a Scenario Grid That Retains Kinks

The chart uses terminal underlying prices from 0 through 180 and explicitly inserts the strike and both break-even prices. A coarse grid that omits the strike can draw a straight segment across the change in slope, rounding away the contract's piecewise shape.

For example, connecting call payoffs only at prices 90 and 110 would draw a positive interpolated payoff at 100, even though the call payoff there is exactly zero. Include the kink itself before drawing line segments.

NumPy's elementwise `maximum` implements the positive-part calculation [3]:

```python
call_payoff = np.maximum(terminal_prices - strike, 0.0)
put_payoff = np.maximum(strike - terminal_prices, 0.0)
```

These are illustrative per-unit formulas for validated inputs. The complete helper also checks bounds and applies the position units. Once all relevant kinks are included, straight segments describe the specified piecewise-linear terminal formulas accurately between scenario points. This is different from claiming that observed market prices move linearly between dates.

The horizontal range is a visualization choice. It is not a confidence interval, forecast range, or statement about possible future prices. No probability distribution is attached to the grid.

## 8. Diagram Labels and Interpretation

Each panel identifies long call or long put, strike 100, and its per-unit premium. The horizontal axis says **underlying price at expiration**, and the vertical axis says **USD per position (1 contract, multiplier 100)**.

A horizontal zero line separates positive and negative simplified outcomes. A vertical dotted line marks the strike. A black marker and annotation identify the simplified-profit break-even coordinate. That coordinate is where the profit curve crosses zero, not where the payoff curve first becomes positive.

Both panels use a common vertical scale for comparison. This makes the put's large payoff at a zero underlying price visible alongside the finite displayed call range. Different vertical scaling could emphasize the premium region but would require clear labeling; it would not change the numerical results.

The diagram is not an option-price curve before expiration. Before expiration, market value can depend on additional inputs and contract terms. Replacing a pre-expiration quote with intrinsic value would answer a different question. No volatility, discount curve, dividend model, Greeks, or probability estimate is calculated here.

## 9. Algebraic Checks Before Visual Inspection

Under the same strike and terminal scenario, the long per-unit terminal payoffs satisfy:

$$H_c(S_T)-H_p(S_T)=S_T-K.$$

The tests verify this identity at a dense set of terminal scenarios. It is an identity between the defined terminal positive-part functions, **not** a present-value put-call parity pricing assertion. No option premiums, discount factors, or dividends are implied by it.

Other checks include:

- Long call payoff is nondecreasing in $S_T$.
- Long put payoff is nonincreasing in $S_T$.
- Both long payoffs are zero at $S_T=K$.
- Payoff minus simplified profit equals the signed initial premium amount at every scenario.
- Reversing contract sign negates the model outputs; doubling contract count doubles them.

These financial-shape and arithmetic checks can detect errors that a visually smooth chart would conceal. A correctly drawn line alone does not validate its units or premium convention.

Signed contract count is an integer from -100 through 100, and multiplier is an integer from 1 through 10,000; Booleans are excluded. A zero position is permitted and produces zero amounts.

These are teaching bounds, not market contract specifications. The helper calculates the supplied scenarios and does not insert kinks automatically. The fixed figure builder inserts the fixture's strike and break-even coordinates explicitly. Change its labels, range, and critical coordinates together when adapting the illustration.

### `payoff_diagrams.py`

```python
"""Terminal option payoff and simplified undiscounted profit, not option pricing."""
from numbers import Real
from pathlib import Path
import matplotlib
matplotlib.use('Agg')
import matplotlib.pyplot as plt
import numpy as np


def option_result(terminal_prices, strike, premium, kind, contracts=1, multiplier=100):
    for name, value in [('strike', strike), ('premium', premium)]:
        if isinstance(value, (bool, np.bool_)) or not isinstance(value, Real):
            raise ValueError(f'{name} must be a real scalar, not Boolean')
    if not 0 < strike <= 1e6 or not np.isfinite(strike):
        raise ValueError('strike must be finite and in (0,1e6]')
    if not 0 <= premium <= 1e6 or not np.isfinite(premium):
        raise ValueError('premium must be finite and in [0,1e6]')
    for name, value, lower, upper in [('contracts', contracts, -100, 100),
                                       ('multiplier', multiplier, 1, 10000)]:
        if (isinstance(value, (bool, np.bool_)) or not isinstance(value, (int, np.integer))
                or not lower <= value <= upper):
            raise ValueError(f'{name} must be an integer in [{lower},{upper}]')
    if kind not in ('call', 'put'):
        raise ValueError('kind must be call or put')
    if (not isinstance(terminal_prices, np.ndarray) or terminal_prices.ndim != 1
            or terminal_prices.dtype.kind not in 'iuf'
            or not 2 <= len(terminal_prices) <= 10001):
        raise ValueError('terminal_prices must be a 1-D real numeric array with 2..10001 values')
    s = terminal_prices.astype(float, copy=True)
    if (not np.isfinite(s).all() or (s < 0).any() or (s > 1e6).any()
            or not np.all(s[1:] > s[:-1])):
        raise ValueError('terminal prices must strictly increase within [0,1e6]')
    intrinsic = np.maximum(s-strike, 0.) if kind == 'call' else np.maximum(strike-s, 0.)
    units = int(contracts)*int(multiplier)
    payoff = units*intrinsic
    profit = payoff-units*float(premium)
    return s, payoff, profit


def build_figure():
    strike, call_premium, put_premium = 100., 6., 4.
    # Include the kink and break-even prices explicitly, not by interpolation.
    grid = np.unique(np.r_[np.linspace(0., 180., 181), strike,
                            strike+call_premium, strike-put_premium])
    fig, axes = plt.subplots(1, 2, figsize=(10, 5), sharex=True, sharey=True,
                             layout='constrained')
    for ax, kind, premium, breakeven in [
            (axes[0], 'call', call_premium, strike+call_premium),
            (axes[1], 'put', put_premium, strike-put_premium)]:
        s, payoff, profit = option_result(grid, strike, premium, kind)
        ax.plot(s, payoff, color='#0072B2', linewidth=2, label='Terminal payoff')
        ax.plot(s, profit, color='#D55E00', linestyle='--', linewidth=2,
                 label='Payoff minus initial premium (no carry)')
        ax.axhline(0, color='gray', linewidth=.8)
        ax.axvline(strike, color='gray', linestyle=':', label='Strike 100')
        ax.scatter([breakeven], [0], color='black', s=25, zorder=4)
        ax.annotate(f'Break-even {breakeven:.0f}', xy=(breakeven, 0),
                     xytext=((-40, 55) if kind == 'call' else (40, 55)),
                     textcoords='offset points', ha='center', fontsize=8,
                     arrowprops={'arrowstyle': '->', 'color': 'gray'})
        ax.set(title=f'Long {kind} | strike 100 | premium {premium:.0f} per unit',
                xlabel='Underlying price at expiration (USD per unit)')
        ax.legend(loc='upper right', fontsize=7)
        ax.grid(alpha=.2)
    axes[0].set_ylabel('USD per position (1 contract, multiplier 100)')
    fig.suptitle('Synthetic expiration diagrams | payoff and simplified profit are different')
    fig.supxlabel('No probabilities, early exercise, fees, taxes, financing, or pre-expiration option valuation', fontsize=9)
    return fig, axes


def main():
    prices = np.array([0., 96., 100., 106., 120.])
    for kind, premium in [('call', 6.), ('put', 4.)]:
        _, payoff, profit = option_result(prices, 100., premium, kind)
        print(f'{kind}: terminal prices {prices.tolist()}')
        print(f'  payoff USD: {payoff.tolist()}')
        print(f'  simplified profit USD: {profit.tolist()}')
    fig, _ = build_figure()
    try:
        Path('output').mkdir(exist_ok=True)
        fig.savefig('output/payoff_diagrams.png', dpi=150)
    finally:
        plt.close(fig)
    print('Saved output/payoff_diagrams.png')


if __name__ == '__main__':
    main()
```

The calculation helper returns an independent copy of the terminal-price grid plus payoff and simplified-profit arrays. It does not mutate the caller's scenarios. The figure builder uses the same helper as the demonstration table and tests.

Agg writes a PNG without a desktop window. The caller saves and closes the Figure in a `finally` block. Re-running the demonstration replaces that output.

## 11. Reference Tests

### `test_payoff_diagrams.py`

```python
import tempfile
import unittest
from pathlib import Path
import numpy as np
from numpy.testing import assert_allclose, assert_array_equal
from payoff_diagrams import option_result, build_figure, plt


class PayoffTests(unittest.TestCase):
    def test_known_call_and_put_values(self):
        s = np.array([0., 96., 100., 106., 120.])
        _, payoff, profit = option_result(s, 100, 6, 'call')
        assert_allclose(payoff, [0, 0, 0, 600, 2000])
        assert_allclose(profit, [-600, -600, -600, 0, 1400])
        _, payoff, profit = option_result(s, 100, 4, 'put')
        assert_allclose(payoff, [10000, 400, 0, 0, 0])
        assert_allclose(profit, [9600, 0, -400, -400, -400])

    def test_kinks_breakeven_and_premium_gap(self):
        s = np.array([96., 100., 106.])
        _, call, cp = option_result(s, 100, 6, 'call')
        _, put, pp = option_result(s, 100, 4, 'put')
        self.assertEqual(call[1], 0); self.assertEqual(put[1], 0)
        self.assertEqual(cp[-1], 0); self.assertEqual(pp[0], 0)
        assert_allclose(call-cp, 600); assert_allclose(put-pp, 400)

    def test_short_mirror_and_position_scaling(self):
        s = np.array([0., 100., 120.])
        _, payoff, profit = option_result(s, 100, 6, 'call')
        _, shortpay, shortprofit = option_result(s, 100, 6, 'call', contracts=-1)
        assert_allclose(shortpay, -payoff); assert_allclose(shortprofit, -profit)
        _, doubled, dp = option_result(s, 100, 6, 'call', contracts=2)
        assert_allclose(doubled, 2*payoff); assert_allclose(dp, 2*profit)

    def test_terminal_identity_and_monotonicity(self):
        s = np.linspace(0, 200, 201)
        _, call, _ = option_result(s, 100, 6, 'call', multiplier=1)
        _, put, _ = option_result(s, 100, 4, 'put', multiplier=1)
        assert_allclose(call-put, s-100)
        self.assertTrue((np.diff(call) >= 0).all())
        self.assertTrue((np.diff(put) <= 0).all())

    def test_invalid_scalars(self):
        s = np.array([0., 100.])
        for strike in [True, 0, -1, np.nan, np.inf, '100']:
            with self.assertRaises(ValueError): option_result(s, strike, 6, 'call')
        for premium in [False, -1, np.nan, np.inf, '6']:
            with self.assertRaises(ValueError): option_result(s, 100, premium, 'call')
        for kwargs in [{'kind': 'other'}, {'kind': 'call', 'contracts': True},
                       {'kind': 'call', 'contracts': 1.5}, {'kind': 'call', 'multiplier': 0}]:
            with self.assertRaises(ValueError): option_result(s, 100, 6, **kwargs)

    def test_invalid_terminal_grid(self):
        for s in [np.array([0.]), np.array([0., np.nan]), np.array([-1., 1.]),
                  np.array([100., 0.]), np.array([0., 0.]), np.array(['0', '100']),
                  np.array([[0., 100.]])]:
            with self.assertRaises(ValueError): option_result(s, 100, 6, 'call')

    def test_source_and_outputs_independent(self):
        s = np.array([0., 100., 120.]); original = s.copy()
        x, payoff, profit = option_result(s, 100, 6, 'call')
        x[0] = 99; payoff[-1] = 99
        assert_array_equal(s, original); self.assertEqual(profit[-1], 1400)

    def test_artists_png_and_cleanup(self):
        before = set(plt.get_fignums()); fig, axes = build_figure()
        try:
            for ax, gap, kink, be in [(axes[0], 600, 100, 106), (axes[1], 400, 100, 96)]:
                x = ax.lines[0].get_xdata()
                self.assertIn(kink, x); self.assertIn(be, x)
                assert_allclose(ax.lines[0].get_ydata()-ax.lines[1].get_ydata(), gap)
            with tempfile.TemporaryDirectory() as folder:
                path = Path(folder)/'payoff.png'; fig.savefig(path)
                self.assertEqual(path.read_bytes()[:8], b'\x89PNG\r\n\x1a\n')
        finally:
            plt.close(fig)
        self.assertEqual(set(plt.get_fignums()), before)


if __name__ == '__main__':
    unittest.main()
```

The eight methods check known call/put table values, strike and break-even behavior, the premium gap, signed-position symmetry and scaling, the terminal payoff identity and monotonicity, invalid scalars/grids, source independence, and the plotted curve data plus PNG export/cleanup.

The identity and shape tests supplement the known-value examples rather than merely checking that a function called another function. ## 14. Common Errors and Extensions

| Error | Consequence | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Labeling intrinsic payoff as profit | Premium cost disappears | Plot or state the premium-adjusted result separately |
| Mixing per-unit and per-contract amounts | Vertical shifts and break-even interpretation become inconsistent | Apply one explicit multiplier and position convention |
| Omitting the strike from a coarse scenario grid | The kink can be connected incorrectly | Insert critical coordinates explicitly |
| Calling the horizontal axis time | Scenario ordering looks like a market path | Label terminal underlying price |
| Treating a scenario range as a forecast interval | Adds unsupported probability meaning | Keep scenario support separate from a probability model |
| Using the expiration curve as a pre-expiration option quote | Omits time and pricing inputs | Use a separately specified valuation model |
| Subtracting a premium without naming timing assumptions | Profit convention is ambiguous | State no-carry simplification or model the cash-flow timing |
| Interpreting short symmetry as an operational risk model | Omits margin, assignment, and financing mechanics | Keep the algebraic scope explicit |

For a multi-leg terminal position, add compatible leg payoff arrays on a common scenario grid and expiration basis. Insert every leg's strike and apply each signed quantity/multiplier separately. Premiums and costs must also be combined consistently. Different expirations, path-dependent contracts, barriers, and early exercise require additional modeling; a single terminal-price axis may not contain enough information.

The present lab deliberately stops at two simple terminal contracts and their signed algebra. It is an original visualization exercise, not a trading recommendation.
