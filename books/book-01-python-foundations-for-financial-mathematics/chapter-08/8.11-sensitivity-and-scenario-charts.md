## 1. Purpose

A sensitivity chart asks how a model output changes when a specified input changes while the other inputs stay fixed. A scenario chart evaluates explicitly defined combinations of assumptions. Both are conditional calculations; neither automatically assigns probabilities or forecasts outcomes.

This section uses an annual-compound balance model from Section 8.10 to build three complementary views: a one-factor rate curve, named joint scenarios, and a two-factor rate/horizon grid. Their different axes and baselines are labeled so that a reader can distinguish the questions they answer.

The complete lab verifies the matrix orientation, reference case, scenario differences, and a local derivative against an independent formula. It does not choose investment assumptions for a real account.

## 3. Model, Reference Case, and Units

For principal $P$, effective annual rate $r$, and a whole-year horizon $n$, define the deterministic terminal balance:

$$F(P,r,n)=P(1+r)^n.$$

The reference inputs are $P_0=1{,}000$ USD, $r_0=0.05$, and $n_0=10$ years. Its output is:

$$F_0=1{,}000(1.05)^{10}\approx1{,}628.894627\text{ USD}.$$

All credited amounts remain invested. There are no contributions, withdrawals, fees, taxes, defaults, or inflation adjustments. Rate is held constant over each evaluated horizon. This is a mathematical illustration, not an estimated return distribution or a market quote.

| View | Varying inputs | Fixed inputs | Plotted output |
|---|---|---|---|
| One-factor curve | Effective annual rate from 0% to 10% | USD 1,000 principal; 10 years | Terminal balance, USD |
| Named scenarios | Both rate and horizon | USD 1,000 principal | USD difference from reference terminal balance |
| Two-factor grid | Rates 1%, 3%, 5%, 7%, 9%; horizons 5, 10, 15, 20 years | USD 1,000 principal | Terminal balance, USD at each sampled pair |

The reference is an input definition, not a claim that it is the most likely outcome. Named scenarios below use their actual assumptions rather than labels such as “expected” or “95% case.”

## 4. One-Factor Sensitivity

The left panel evaluates $F(1{,}000,r,10)$ while changing only $r$. A marker identifies the reference rate of 5% and balance near USD 1,628.89.

Every plotted x-value is an assumed effective annual rate, not an observation date. The line is a sampled model-response curve. It does not represent a sequence of annual returns or gradual changes in a market forecast.

Holding inputs fixed is part of the question, not evidence that those inputs are independent in the real world. A real change in rates might coincide with changes in cash flows, prices, or other assumptions. One-factor analysis intentionally isolates a model relationship; a jointly specified scenario asks a different question.

The curve rises with rate for this positive-principal, positive-horizon fixture. The implemented helper also supports bounded negative rates, but the positive-rate figure should not be interpreted as covering all possible return behavior.

## 5. Shock Size and Local Sensitivity

A move from 5% to 6% is one percentage point, or 100 basis points, represented by a decimal change of `0.01`. It is a 20% relative increase in the numerical rate, not a 1% relative increase. A 1% relative increase from 5% would instead give 5.05%.

For this model, the partial derivative with respect to the decimal rate is:

$$\frac{\partial F}{\partial r}=Pn(1+r)^{n-1}.$$

At the reference case it is approximately USD 15,513.28 per unit decimal rate. Multiplying by a small decimal rate shock gives a local linear approximation. A one-percentage-point shock therefore has a first-order approximation of about USD 155.13, not USD 15,513.28.

The central finite-difference approximation is:

$$\frac{F(P_0,r_0+h,n_0)-F(P_0,r_0-h,n_0)}{2h}.$$

The tests use $h=10^{-5}$ and reconcile the result with the analytic derivative. This numerical agreement checks the implementation near the reference point. It does not establish that a derivative remains accurate for large shocks.

Full revaluation computes $F$ at the shocked inputs without replacing it by a local straight-line approximation. Because the rate response is curved, positive and negative shocks of equal size can produce different absolute changes. Do not force symmetric bars unless the calculated model outputs or explicitly chosen approximation justify them.

## 6. Joint Scenarios and Reference Differences

The middle panel evaluates three independently specified input combinations:

| Scenario definition | Effective annual rate | Horizon | Terminal balance, USD | Difference from reference, USD |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 3% rate, 8 years | 3% | 8 years | 1,266.77 | -362.12 |
| Reference: 5%, 10 years | 5% | 10 years | 1,628.89 | 0.00 |
| 7% rate, 12 years | 7% | 12 years | 2,252.19 | +623.30 |

The plotted difference is $\Delta F=F(P_0,r,n)-F_0$. It is an absolute USD amount. It is not a percentage change, profit, investment return, or loss relative to principal.

Rate and horizon change together. The bars therefore cannot be interpreted as the isolated effect of the rate change. In particular, the cases have different terminal dates in model time, so the dollar comparisons are not same-date economic valuations. They are explicit examples of conditional outputs with differing inputs. A common-date comparison would require fixing the horizon or introducing a documented time-value conversion.

The zero reference case receives a visible marker because its zero-width bar alone could be overlooked. The ordering is the declared scenario ordering; it is not a ranking by probability or suitability.

## 7. Two-Factor Grid and Matrix Orientation

The right panel evaluates every pair of selected rates and horizons. Its matrix uses **rates as rows** and **horizons as columns**. NumPy's explicit mesh indexing makes that choice reviewable [1]:

```python
rr, nn = np.meshgrid(rates, years, indexing="ij")
balances = principal * np.exp(nn * np.log1p(rr))
```

Here the inputs stand for validated vectors. With five rates and four horizons, the output shape is `(5,4)`. Entry `[2,1]` is rate 5% and horizon 10, the reference balance. Transposing the matrix without updating the coordinates would mislabel the financial scenarios even if the color pattern looked plausible.

The grid reports all 20 combinations under the model. It does not claim that every combination is equally likely, economically feasible for a particular product, or jointly independent. Input-grid coverage and probability coverage are different concepts.

Each cell's number is the terminal balance rounded to whole USD for display. Its color encodes the same unrounded balance. Rows of fixed positive rate rise with horizon; columns of fixed positive horizon rise with rate in this fixture. The tests verify both directions before plotting.

## 8. Color Mapping Without Invented Probability Meaning

Matplotlib's `pcolormesh` draws a coordinate-aligned colored grid [2]. For `shading="flat"`, the figure supplies explicit cell edges around the sampled input centers. These cell boundaries are a rendering layout, not additional evaluated scenarios and not rate/horizon uncertainty bands.

For example, the cell centered at rate 5% and horizon 10 contains the balance calculated at exactly those inputs. Its rectangular extent does not assert that the same output applies to every input inside the rectangle. No interpolation or interval bound is calculated by coloring that cell.

A labeled colorbar identifies the mapping from color to **terminal balance in USD** [3]. The figure uses one sequential colormap for positive balances, not a red/green judgment about whether a scenario is acceptable. Numeric cell labels provide an additional reading route.

If comparing multiple grids, preserve a common color normalization when the goal is cross-panel magnitude comparison. Independent color scales can make very different values look equally intense. Difference grids may warrant a diverging map centered on a declared zero, but that is another output convention and must be labeled accordingly.

## 9. Scenarios, Stress Tests, and Uncertainty Are Different Products

A set of named assumptions is not a probability distribution. A shaded area between selected scenario curves is not automatically a confidence interval. A confidence or prediction band requires a probability model, estimation procedure, and a precise statement of what coverage means.

Likewise, a stress case requires a rationale for the shock or combination being examined. Calling a case “severe” does not establish historical plausibility, regulatory relevance, or a quantified tail probability. The present cases are purely synthetic teaching definitions.

A tornado chart can compare one-factor output changes, but its ranking depends on the chosen baseline, shock sizes, and units. Changing one rate by 100 basis points and another parameter by 1% relative is not a neutral comparison of their importance. Disclose the experiment before interpreting the bar order.

Interactions also matter. When multiple inputs change, adding their separate one-factor effects may fail to reproduce joint revaluation in a nonlinear model. The lab calculates joint cases directly rather than adding isolated changes. It does not decompose the joint result into uniquely attributed contributions.

### `scenario_charts.py`

```python
"""Conditional annual-compound sensitivities and explicit joint scenarios."""
from numbers import Real
from pathlib import Path
import matplotlib
matplotlib.use('Agg')
import matplotlib.pyplot as plt
from matplotlib.ticker import PercentFormatter
import numpy as np


def balance_grid(principal, rates, years):
    if (isinstance(principal, (bool, np.bool_)) or not isinstance(principal, Real)
            or not 0 < principal <= 1e9 or not np.isfinite(principal)):
        raise ValueError('principal must be a finite real scalar in (0,1e9]')
    for name, values in [('rates', rates), ('years', years)]:
        if (not isinstance(values, np.ndarray) or values.ndim != 1
                or values.dtype.kind not in 'iuf' or not 1 <= len(values) <= 101):
            raise ValueError(f'{name} must be a 1-D real numeric array with 1..101 entries')
    r = rates.astype(float, copy=True); n = years.astype(float, copy=True)
    if (not np.isfinite(r).all() or (r < -.5).any() or (r > 1).any()
            or (len(r)>1 and not np.all(r[1:] > r[:-1]))):
        raise ValueError('rates must increase strictly within [-0.5,1]')
    if (not np.isfinite(n).all() or (n < 0).any() or (n > 50).any()
            or not np.array_equal(n, np.rint(n))
            or (len(n)>1 and not np.all(n[1:] > n[:-1]))):
        raise ValueError('years must be distinct increasing whole years in 0..50')
    # Rows are rates, columns are horizons: explicit matrix indexing.
    rr, nn = np.meshgrid(r, n, indexing='ij')
    balances = float(principal)*np.exp(nn*np.log1p(rr))
    return r, n, balances


def scenario_table():
    definitions = [('3% rate, 8 years', .03, 8),
                   ('Reference: 5%, 10 years', .05, 10),
                   ('7% rate, 12 years', .07, 12)]
    baseline = float(balance_grid(1000, np.array([.05]), np.array([10]))[2][0, 0])
    rows = []
    for label, rate, horizon in definitions:
        value = float(balance_grid(1000, np.array([rate]), np.array([horizon]))[2][0, 0])
        rows.append((label, rate, horizon, value, value-baseline))
    return rows, baseline


def build_figure():
    fig, axes = plt.subplots(1, 3, figsize=(15, 5), layout='constrained')
    r, _, values = balance_grid(1000, np.linspace(0, .10, 101), np.array([10]))
    axes[0].plot(r, values[:, 0], color='#0072B2')
    _, baseline = scenario_table()
    axes[0].scatter([.05], [baseline], color='black', s=25, label='Reference 5%, 10 years')
    axes[0].set(title='One-factor sensitivity\nPrincipal 1,000; horizon 10 years fixed',
                xlabel='Assumed effective annual rate', ylabel='Terminal balance (USD)')
    axes[0].xaxis.set_major_formatter(PercentFormatter(xmax=1., decimals=0))
    axes[0].legend(fontsize=7, loc='upper left'); axes[0].grid(alpha=.2)
    rows, _ = scenario_table()
    deltas = [row[4] for row in rows]
    axes[1].barh(np.arange(3), deltas, color=['#D55E00', '#777777', '#009E73'])
    axes[1].plot([0], [1], marker='o', color='black', linestyle='none')
    axes[1].set_yticks(np.arange(3), [row[0] for row in rows])
    axes[1].invert_yaxis(); axes[1].axvline(0, color='black', linewidth=.8)
    axes[1].set(title='Joint input scenarios\nRate and horizon change together',
                xlabel='USD difference from reference balance')
    axes[1].grid(axis='x', alpha=.2)
    rates = np.array([.01, .03, .05, .07, .09]); years = np.array([5, 10, 15, 20])
    _, _, grid = balance_grid(1000, rates, years)
    mesh = axes[2].pcolormesh(np.array([2.5, 7.5, 12.5, 17.5, 22.5]),
                              np.array([0, .02, .04, .06, .08, .10]), grid,
                              shading='flat', cmap='viridis')
    for i, rate in enumerate(rates):
        for j, year in enumerate(years):
            axes[2].text(year, rate, f'{grid[i,j]:,.0f}', ha='center', va='center',
                          color='white' if grid[i,j] < 3500 else 'black', fontsize=7)
    axes[2].set(title='Two-factor grid\nCell labels: USD at sampled inputs',
                xlabel='Horizon (whole years)', ylabel='Effective annual rate',
                xticks=years, yticks=rates)
    axes[2].yaxis.set_major_formatter(PercentFormatter(xmax=1., decimals=0))
    fig.colorbar(mesh, ax=axes[2], label='Terminal balance (USD)', shrink=.85)
    fig.suptitle('Synthetic annual compounding | principal USD 1,000 | deterministic conditional outputs', fontsize=13)
    fig.supxlabel('No scenario probabilities or confidence bands. No flows, fees, taxes, inflation adjustment, or forecast.', fontsize=10)
    return fig, axes


def main():
    rows, baseline = scenario_table()
    print(f'Reference terminal balance: USD {baseline:.2f}')
    for label, rate, horizon, value, delta in rows:
        print(f'{label}: balance USD {value:.2f}; difference USD {delta:+.2f}')
    fig, _ = build_figure()
    try:
        Path('output').mkdir(exist_ok=True)
        fig.savefig('output/scenario_charts.png', dpi=150)
    finally:
        plt.close(fig)
    print('Saved output/scenario_charts.png')


if __name__ == '__main__':
    main()
```

The figure's three views all call the same validated numerical helper. The named-case table stores the explicit rate/horizon inputs alongside the full revalued output and its reference difference. The colorbar is attached to the actual mesh returned by the grid drawing call.

Agg produces a file without a desktop window. The caller saves and closes its Figure in `finally`; re-running the demonstration replaces the PNG.

## 11. Reference Tests

### `test_scenario_charts.py`

```python
import tempfile
import unittest
from pathlib import Path
import numpy as np
from numpy.testing import assert_allclose, assert_array_equal
from scenario_charts import balance_grid, scenario_table, build_figure, plt


class ScenarioTests(unittest.TestCase):
    def test_known_values_and_matrix_orientation(self):
        _, _, grid = balance_grid(1000, np.array([0., .05]), np.array([0, 10, 20]))
        self.assertEqual(grid.shape, (2, 3))
        assert_allclose(grid[0], 1000)
        assert_allclose(grid[1], [1000, 1000*1.05**10, 1000*1.05**20])

    def test_monotonicity_for_positive_fixture(self):
        _, _, grid = balance_grid(1000, np.array([.01, .05, .09]), np.array([5, 10, 20]))
        self.assertTrue((np.diff(grid, axis=0)>0).all())
        self.assertTrue((np.diff(grid, axis=1)>0).all())

    def test_local_derivative_independent_formula(self):
        h = 1e-5
        _, _, values = balance_grid(1000, np.array([.05-h, .05+h]), np.array([10]))
        difference = (values[1,0]-values[0,0])/(2*h)
        derivative = 1000*10*1.05**9
        self.assertAlmostEqual(difference/derivative, 1., places=7)

    def test_named_scenarios_full_revaluation(self):
        rows, baseline = scenario_table()
        self.assertAlmostEqual(baseline, 1000*1.05**10)
        for label, rate, horizon, value, delta in rows:
            self.assertAlmostEqual(value, 1000*(1+rate)**horizon)
            self.assertAlmostEqual(delta, value-baseline)
        self.assertEqual(rows[1][-1], 0)

    def test_invalid_principal_and_rates(self):
        for principal in [True, 0, -1, np.inf, '1000']:
            with self.assertRaises(ValueError): balance_grid(principal, np.array([.05]), np.array([10]))
        for rates in [np.array([np.nan]), np.array([1.1]), np.array([.05, .03]),
                      np.array([.05, .05]), np.array(['.05']), np.array([[.05]])]:
            with self.assertRaises(ValueError): balance_grid(1000, rates, np.array([10]))

    def test_invalid_horizons(self):
        for years in [np.array([]), np.array([np.nan]), np.array([-1]), np.array([51]),
                      np.array([1.5]), np.array([10, 5]), np.array([10, 10])]:
            with self.assertRaises(ValueError): balance_grid(1000, np.array([.05]), years)

    def test_source_copies_scaling_and_negative_rates(self):
        rates = np.array([-.1, 0., .05]); years = np.array([0, 10])
        r, n, a = balance_grid(1000, rates, years)
        _, _, b = balance_grid(2000, rates, years)
        assert_allclose(b, 2*a); self.assertLess(a[0,-1], 1000)
        r[0]=99; n[0]=99
        assert_array_equal(rates, [-.1, 0., .05]); assert_array_equal(years, [0, 10])

    def test_artists_heatmap_export_and_cleanup(self):
        before=set(plt.get_fignums()); fig, axes=build_figure()
        try:
            assert_allclose(axes[0].lines[0].get_ydata()[50], 1000*1.05**10)
            assert_allclose([bar.get_width() for bar in axes[1].patches],
                            [row[-1] for row in scenario_table()[0]])
            grid=axes[2].collections[0].get_array()
            self.assertEqual(grid.shape, (5,4))
            self.assertAlmostEqual(grid[2,1], 1000*1.05**10)
            self.assertEqual(len(fig.axes), 4)  # Three data panels and a colorbar.
            with tempfile.TemporaryDirectory() as folder:
                path=Path(folder)/'scenario.png'; fig.savefig(path)
                self.assertEqual(path.read_bytes()[:8], b'\x89PNG\r\n\x1a\n')
        finally:
            plt.close(fig)
        self.assertEqual(set(plt.get_fignums()), before)


if __name__ == '__main__':
    unittest.main()
```

The eight methods cover known values and matrix orientation, fixture monotonicity, a central finite-difference check against an independent derivative, named-case full revaluation, invalid principal/rates/horizons, source-copy and scaling behavior, negative-rate support, plotted curve/bar/mesh values, and PNG export with cleanup.

The matrix-orientation checks use asymmetric dimensions so an unintended transpose cannot pass solely because a square matrix has the expected shape. The named-case test independently evaluates `(1 + rate)**horizon`. The derivative check tests a different local property rather than simply duplicating the implementation expression.

## 14. Common Errors and Corrections

| Error | Consequence | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Changing several inputs but labeling the view one-factor sensitivity | Attributes a joint effect to one variable | State all changing and fixed assumptions |
| Confusing a percentage-point shock with a relative-percent shock | Evaluates the wrong rate change | Convert and label shocks explicitly |
| Plotting levels but labeling reference differences | Changes the result's meaning | Compute and label the chosen output convention |
| Transposing the grid without matching coordinates | Rate/horizon scenarios are mislabeled | Test asymmetric shapes and known cells |
| Using a heatmap without a labeled colorbar | Color has no readable numerical meaning | Attach the colorbar to the actual mesh and name units |
| Treating cell extent as an input interval bound | Implies uncomputed range coverage | Explain sampled centers and rendering edges |
| Calling scenario envelopes confidence bands | Adds unsupported probability claims | Define a probability model or retain scenario language |
| Adding isolated changes to approximate every joint case | Misses nonlinear interactions | Revalue the full joint inputs or state an approximation |
| Comparing different horizons as same-date value | Mixes timing bases | Fix a common horizon or model the time conversion |

## 15. Exercises and Answer Guidance

1. **Reference:** What inputs define the reference balance?  
   **Guidance:** USD 1,000 principal, 5% effective annual rate, 10 whole years, no flows/costs/inflation adjustment.
2. **Shock units:** What decimal rate represents a 100-basis-point increase from 5%?  
   **Guidance:** 0.06; the decimal change is 0.01.
3. **Local units:** Why is the derivative near USD 15,513 not the estimated effect of a one-percentage-point change?  
   **Guidance:** It is per unit decimal rate; multiply by 0.01 for that first-order shock estimate.
4. **Joint case:** May the +USD 623.30 bar be attributed only to the 7% rate?  
   **Guidance:** No. Its horizon also changes from 10 to 12 years.
5. **Orientation:** Which matrix element represents 5% and 10 years in the plotted grid?  
   **Guidance:** `[2,1]` with zero-based indexing: rates are rows, horizons columns.
6. **Color meaning:** Does a bright cell mean a more probable scenario?  
   **Guidance:** No. It represents a larger modeled terminal USD balance on this color scale.
7. **Scenario coverage:** Do the colored cell edges bound every possible value within each rectangle?  
   **Guidance:** No. Each cell displays the calculation at its labeled center inputs; no within-cell range analysis is performed.
8. **Probability:** What would be needed to label a band as a 95% prediction interval?  
   **Guidance:** A defined probabilistic model, prediction target, estimation/validation procedure, and an explicit coverage interpretation. The current scenarios supply none of those.
