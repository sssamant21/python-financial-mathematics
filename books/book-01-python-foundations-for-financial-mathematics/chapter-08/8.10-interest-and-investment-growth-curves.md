## 1. Purpose

A growth curve maps elapsed time to a modeled balance. Its shape depends on the rate definition, compounding convention, crediting times, cash flows, and displayed scale. A smooth upward line alone does not establish which of those assumptions were used.

This section compares simple interest, annual compounding, monthly compounding, and continuous compounding under explicit deterministic assumptions. It also plots the same balance arrays on linear and logarithmic axes. The purpose is to make the model visible and testable, not to predict an investment outcome.

Chapter 9 develops time-value-of-money mathematics systematically. Here the formulas are introduced only as needed to construct and validate the charts. Section 8.11 extends the visualization work to sensitivity and scenario comparisons.

## 4. Simple Interest and Compound Growth

For principal $P$, simple-interest rate $r_s$, and elapsed years $t$, define:

$$B_s(t)=P(1+r_s t).$$

Simple interest grows by an amount proportional to the original principal. Under this model, interest does not itself become a new interest-bearing base.

For nominal annual rate $j_m$ compounded $m$ times per model year, at a completed credit period $k=mt$, define:

$$B_m(t)=P\left(1+\frac{j_m}{m}\right)^{mt},\qquad mt\in\{0,1,2,\ldots\}.$$

The recurrence at successive credits is:

$$B_{k+1}=B_k\left(1+\frac{j_m}{m}\right).$$

The credited balance becomes the next period's base. The lab evaluates periodic balances only at whole credit periods. It does not use a fractional power to infer an account balance partway through a credit period.

For continuously compounded rate, or force, $\delta$, define:

$$B_c(t)=P e^{\delta t}.$$

The continuous model is evaluated on the monthly-spaced plotting grid and connected by line segments. Those are samples of the specified smooth mathematical model; they are not observed investment balances.

All three definitions return $P$ at time zero. With a zero rate, all remain at $P$. A supported negative rate produces declining values, subject to the helper's positive-balance contract.

## 5. Worked Endpoint Values

The fixture sets the numerical rate in each convention to `0.05`. It does not equate their effective annual growth.

| Model year | Simple interest, USD | Annual compound, USD | Monthly compound, USD | Continuous compound, USD |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 0 | 1,000.00 | 1,000.00 | 1,000.00 | 1,000.00 |
| 1 | 1,050.00 | 1,050.00 | 1,051.16 | 1,051.27 |
| 10 | 1,500.00 | 1,628.89 | 1,647.01 | 1,648.72 |
| 20 | 2,000.00 | 2,653.30 | 2,712.64 | 2,718.28 |

At the same positive numeric rate in this fixture, monthly compounding has a slightly larger one-year factor than annual compounding, and the continuous convention is slightly larger again. This comparison is conditional on the stated rate inputs. It does not establish that a real monthly-paying product offers a better economic return than an annually paying one.

The simple-interest curve is linear in elapsed time. Positive-rate compound growth becomes increasingly separated from it over the horizon. The table rounds only for display; calculations and tests use unrounded floating-point balances.

## 6. Equal Numeric Rates Versus Equivalent Annual Rates

For periodic nominal rate $j_m$, the effective one-year rate is:

$$i=\left(1+\frac{j_m}{m}\right)^m-1.$$

The fixture's 5% nominal rate with monthly compounding implies an effective annual rate of approximately **5.116190%**. The 5% continuous force implies $e^{0.05}-1$, approximately **5.127110%**. Neither is the same one-year factor as 5% annual compounding.

To express a common effective annual rate $i$ in different conventions, use:

$$j_m=m\left((1+i)^{1/m}-1\right),\qquad
\delta=\ln(1+i).$$

For $i=5\%$, the equivalent nominal monthly rate is approximately **4.888949%** per model year and the equivalent continuous force approximately **4.879016%** per model year. At common completed-period observation times, those rates produce the same compound balances as annual 5% in the specified model.

The tests compare all three conventions at integer years through year 20. That equality concerns the chosen observation times and growth factors. It does not make their intermediate credit schedules or real contract withdrawal rights identical.

Simple interest at 5% matches the annual compound model at year one but generally does not match its multi-year balances. A single constant simple-interest rate cannot represent the same exponential growth for every horizon unless the growth is zero.

## 7. Credited Balances, Accrual, and Model Time

The annual compound series contains years `0,1,...,20`; the monthly compound series contains `0,1/12,...,20`. Each periodic series is drawn as a post-step state: the balance credited at a timestamp persists visually until the next modeled credit.

This is an explicit presentation contract. It shows credited balance, not all potentially accrued-but-uncredited interest, liquidation value, or a contractual payoff between credits. A real product may accrue daily, credit monthly, impose withdrawal penalties, or use another basis. Those features need separate terms and inputs.

“Monthly” here means twelve equal model periods per year. Fractions such as `1/12` are not actual calendar timestamps. The code implements no calendar-month lengths, holidays, day-count basis, business-day adjustment, or leap-year rule.

A periodic input grid must land on whole credit periods. For annual frequency, year 0.5 is rejected; for monthly frequency, year 0.5 represents six completed model periods and is accepted. The helper tolerates small floating-point representation error when checking these integers and does not silently turn an arbitrary fractional period into a completed one.

The final step ends at the requested horizon. Its drawing does not assert a further credit or continuation beyond year 20.

## 8. Linear and Logarithmic Balance Axes

The two figure panels use identical input balance arrays. The left panel uses an ordinary linear y-axis. Equal vertical distances correspond to equal dollar differences.

The right panel changes the y-scale with `set_yscale("log")` [4]. Equal vertical distances correspond to equal balance ratios. The values remain USD balances; they are not replaced by log returns. Tick labels may appear in scientific notation, such as $10^3$ for USD 1,000.

For a continuously compounded balance,

$$\ln B_c(t)=\ln P+\delta t.$$

That relationship is linear in elapsed time, so the smooth continuous growth model appears straight on a log-balance plot. The periodic credited series remain steps because their displayed state is still discrete. Simple-interest growth has a different logarithmic shape.

A logarithmic balance axis requires strictly positive values. This lab enforces positive balances before charting rather than dropping nonpositive points to make the plot render. The linear panel also uses the validated positive values; the rule belongs to the shared output contract.

Scale choice affects visual emphasis. It does not change the final balances, their ratios, the credited times, or the underlying model. Inspect the numerical table before interpreting apparent steepness.

## 9. Growth Assumptions Are Not Investment Forecasts

These curves are deterministic outputs conditional on fixed rates and reinvestment conventions. They contain no uncertain return paths, losses from default, estimation error, or probability bands. A line labeled with an assumed rate should not be presented as a guaranteed outcome for an investment whose returns fluctuate.

Cash contributions would introduce a different model. Its recurrence must specify whether each contribution occurs before or after the period's return. Comparing a contributed balance with a no-contribution curve without disclosing the flows can falsely attribute added capital to investment growth.

Similarly, a real purchasing-power curve needs an inflation convention, and after-fee or after-tax growth needs applicable charges and timing. Those cannot be inferred from the nominal USD balance. This section excludes them explicitly and does not choose personal savings or investment assumptions.

A scenario range is also not a confidence interval unless a justified probability model has been specified. The next section develops visual scenario and sensitivity comparisons while retaining that distinction.

Principal must be a finite real scalar in `(0,1e9]`; rate must be finite and in `[-0.5,1]`, excluding Booleans. The rate's interpretation depends on `convention`: simple per-year rate, periodic nominal annual rate, or continuous force. Supported conventions are `simple`, `periodic`, and `continuous`.

Frequency must be an integer in `1..365`, excluding Booleans. It applies only to the periodic convention; other conventions require the default value one. The time grid must be a one-dimensional real numeric NumPy array containing 2–10,001 finite values, strictly increasing from zero through at most 50 years.

Periodic grids must land on completed credit periods. Every calculated balance must remain finite and strictly positive. In particular, simple interest with a negative rate can eventually reach zero or become negative; such a requested path is rejected rather than partially plotted. The input and output ranges are teaching/numerical bounds, not product restrictions or suitability limits.

### `growth_curves.py`

```python
"""Deterministic growth conventions with explicit periodic credit instants."""
from numbers import Real
from pathlib import Path
import matplotlib
matplotlib.use('Agg')
import matplotlib.pyplot as plt
import numpy as np


def growth(principal, rate, years, convention, frequency=1):
    for name, value in [('principal', principal), ('rate', rate)]:
        if isinstance(value, (bool, np.bool_)) or not isinstance(value, Real):
            raise ValueError(f'{name} must be a real scalar, not Boolean')
    if not 0 < principal <= 1e9 or not np.isfinite(principal):
        raise ValueError('principal must be finite and in (0,1e9]')
    if not -.5 <= rate <= 1 or not np.isfinite(rate):
        raise ValueError('rate must be finite and in [-0.5,1]')
    if (isinstance(frequency, (bool, np.bool_)) or not isinstance(frequency, (int, np.integer))
            or not 1 <= frequency <= 365):
        raise ValueError('frequency must be an integer in 1..365')
    if convention not in ('simple', 'periodic', 'continuous'):
        raise ValueError('convention must be simple, periodic, or continuous')
    if convention != 'periodic' and frequency != 1:
        raise ValueError('frequency applies only to periodic compounding')
    if (not isinstance(years, np.ndarray) or years.ndim != 1
            or years.dtype.kind not in 'iuf' or not 2 <= len(years) <= 10001):
        raise ValueError('years must be a 1-D real numeric array with 2..10001 points')
    t = years.astype(float, copy=True)
    if (not np.isfinite(t).all() or t[0] != 0 or t[-1] > 50
            or not np.all(t[1:] > t[:-1])):
        raise ValueError('years must increase strictly from zero through at most 50')
    if convention == 'simple':
        balance = float(principal)*(1 + float(rate)*t)
    elif convention == 'continuous':
        balance = float(principal)*np.exp(float(rate)*t)
    else:
        periods = np.rint(frequency*t)
        if not np.allclose(frequency*t, periods, rtol=0, atol=1e-9):
            raise ValueError('periodic outputs must be at whole credit periods')
        balance = float(principal)*np.exp(periods*np.log1p(float(rate)/frequency))
    if not np.isfinite(balance).all() or not (balance > 0).all():
        raise ValueError('the entire balance path must be finite and strictly positive')
    return t, balance


def fixture():
    annual = np.arange(21, dtype=float)
    monthly = np.arange(241, dtype=float)/12
    curves = []
    for label, grid, convention, frequency, color, style in [
            ('Simple 5% per year', monthly, 'simple', 1, '#777777', ':'),
            ('Annual 5% nominal', annual, 'periodic', 1, '#0072B2', '-'),
            ('Monthly 5% nominal', monthly, 'periodic', 12, '#D55E00', '--'),
            ('Continuous 5% force', monthly, 'continuous', 1, '#009E73', '-.')]:
        t, balances = growth(1000., .05, grid, convention, frequency)
        curves.append((label, t, balances, convention, color, style))
    return curves


def build_figure():
    curves = fixture()
    fig, axes = plt.subplots(1, 2, figsize=(11, 5), layout='constrained', sharex=True)
    for ax in axes:
        for label, t, balances, convention, color, style in curves:
            if convention == 'periodic':
                ax.step(t, balances, where='post', color=color, linestyle=style,
                         linewidth=1.6, label=label)
            else:
                ax.plot(t, balances, color=color, linestyle=style,
                         linewidth=1.6, label=label)
        ax.set(xlabel='Elapsed model years', ylabel='Modeled USD balance')
        ax.grid(alpha=.2)
    axes[0].set_title('Linear balance scale')
    axes[1].set_yscale('log')
    axes[1].set_title('Log balance scale | same balance arrays')
    axes[0].legend(loc='upper left', fontsize=8)
    fig.suptitle('Synthetic principal USD 1,000 | equal numeric rates, different conventions', fontsize=12)
    fig.supxlabel('Periodic lines show credited states (post-step). No flows, fees, taxes, inflation adjustment, or forecast.', fontsize=9)
    return fig, axes


def main():
    for label, t, balances, convention, color, style in fixture():
        print(f'{label}: year {t[-1]:.0f} balance USD {balances[-1]:.2f}')
    effective = (1+.05/12)**12-1
    print(f'Effective annual rate from monthly 5% nominal: {effective:.6%}')
    fig, _ = build_figure()
    try:
        Path('output').mkdir(exist_ok=True)
        fig.savefig('output/growth_curves.png', dpi=150)
    finally:
        plt.close(fig)
    print('Saved output/growth_curves.png')


if __name__ == '__main__':
    main()
```

The periodic implementation evaluates `exp(periods * log1p(rate/frequency))`. NumPy's `log1p` computes the natural logarithm of one plus its argument and retains useful precision for small increments [2]; `exp` supplies the exponential [3]. This expression does not change the defined compounding convention. The independent recurrence test checks the result against repeated period-by-period multiplication.

The helper returns a copied time grid and a separate balance array. The fixed figure builder plots the same curves on both scales. The caller saves and closes its specific Figure in `finally`. Agg writes a PNG without opening a desktop window; re-running the demonstration replaces that output.

## 11. Reference Tests

### `test_growth_curves.py`

```python
import tempfile
import unittest
from pathlib import Path
import numpy as np
from numpy.testing import assert_allclose, assert_array_equal
from growth_curves import growth, fixture, build_figure, plt


class GrowthTests(unittest.TestCase):
    def test_known_endpoints_and_initial_principal(self):
        curves = fixture()
        assert_allclose([c[2][0] for c in curves], 1000)
        assert_allclose([c[2][-1] for c in curves],
                        [2000, 1000*1.05**20, 1000*(1+.05/12)**240, 1000*np.exp(1)])
        self.assertTrue(np.all(np.diff(curves[1][2]) > 0))

    def test_periodic_recurrence_independent_route(self):
        t = np.arange(13, dtype=float)/12
        _, actual = growth(1000, .05, t, 'periodic', 12)
        expected = [1000.]
        for _ in range(12): expected.append(expected[-1]*(1+.05/12))
        assert_allclose(actual, expected)

    def test_equivalent_annual_rates(self):
        t = np.arange(21, dtype=float)
        _, annual = growth(1000, .05, t, 'periodic')
        equivalent_nominal = 12*np.expm1(np.log1p(.05)/12)
        _, monthly = growth(1000, equivalent_nominal, t, 'periodic', 12)
        _, continuous = growth(1000, np.log1p(.05), t, 'continuous')
        assert_allclose(annual, monthly); assert_allclose(annual, continuous)

    def test_zero_and_negative_rates_and_positive_domain(self):
        t = np.arange(3, dtype=float)
        for convention in ['simple', 'periodic', 'continuous']:
            assert_allclose(growth(1000, 0, t, convention)[1], 1000)
            self.assertLess(growth(1000, -.1, t, convention)[1][-1], 1000)
        with self.assertRaises(ValueError): growth(1000, -.5, t, 'simple')

    def test_invalid_scalars_and_conventions(self):
        t = np.array([0., 1.])
        for principal in [True, 0, -1, np.inf, '1000']:
            with self.assertRaises(ValueError): growth(principal, .05, t, 'periodic')
        for rate in [False, np.nan, -.6, 1.1, '0.05']:
            with self.assertRaises(ValueError): growth(1000, rate, t, 'periodic')
        for convention, freq in [('other', 1), ('periodic', True), ('periodic', 0),
                                  ('periodic', 12.0), ('simple', 12)]:
            with self.assertRaises(ValueError): growth(1000, .05, t, convention, freq)

    def test_grid_and_credit_period_validation(self):
        for t in [np.array([0.]), np.array([0., np.nan]), np.array([1., 2.]),
                  np.array([0., 0.]), np.array([0., 51.]), np.array([0., .5])]:
            with self.assertRaises(ValueError): growth(1000, .05, t, 'periodic')
        t, _ = growth(1000, .05, np.array([0., .5]), 'periodic', 12)
        self.assertEqual(t[-1], .5)

    def test_source_preservation_and_positive_scaling(self):
        years = np.arange(5, dtype=float); original = years.copy()
        t, a = growth(1000, .05, years, 'periodic')
        _, b = growth(2000, .05, years, 'periodic')
        assert_allclose(b, 2*a); t[0] = 99
        assert_array_equal(years, original)

    def test_chart_data_scales_steps_export_and_cleanup(self):
        before = set(plt.get_fignums()); fig, axes = build_figure()
        try:
            self.assertEqual(axes[0].get_yscale(), 'linear')
            self.assertEqual(axes[1].get_yscale(), 'log')
            for left, right in zip(axes[0].lines, axes[1].lines):
                assert_allclose(left.get_ydata(), right.get_ydata())
            self.assertEqual(axes[0].lines[1].get_drawstyle(), 'steps-post')
            with tempfile.TemporaryDirectory() as folder:
                path = Path(folder)/'growth.png'; fig.savefig(path)
                self.assertEqual(path.read_bytes()[:8], b'\x89PNG\r\n\x1a\n')
        finally:
            plt.close(fig)
        self.assertEqual(set(plt.get_fignums()), before)


if __name__ == '__main__':
    unittest.main()
```

The eight methods check the known endpoints and common initial principal, periodic recurrence, equivalent effective-rate conventions, zero/negative rates and the positive-balance rule, invalid inputs, completed-credit-period validation, source preservation and principal scaling, and the plotted arrays/scales/step styles with export cleanup.

The equivalent-rate check is separate from the figure's equal-numeric-rate comparison. It verifies that converting rate conventions produces matching annual observation balances; it does not substitute those converted rates into the publication chart.

Object and file-signature checks establish what was plotted and that a PNG was written. They do not establish readability, so the exported picture also receives a separate visual inspection.

## 14. Common Errors and Corrections

| Error | Consequence | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Treating 5% nominal monthly as 5% effective annual | Misstates comparable growth | Convert the rate convention explicitly |
| Passing the number 5 when the helper expects 0.05 | Uses an incorrect rate scale | Store rates as decimal fractions |
| Applying a full annual rate every month | Greatly overstates growth | Use the nominal rate divided by the stated frequency |
| Drawing annual credited balance as continuously updated | Adds an unstated between-credit convention | Show credit instants and an explicit state/interpolation policy |
| Treating fractional model years as calendar months | Implies a nonexistent calendar/day-count implementation | State equal model periods or supply actual calendar rules |
| Calling a log balance chart a log-return chart | Confuses scale with a different computed quantity | Label the underlying balance and the axis transformation |
| Plotting a nonpositive balance on a log axis | Omits or misrepresents a model result | Enforce a positive domain or choose a different reporting design |
| Adding deposits without separating flows from returns | Misattributes contributed capital to growth | Specify and reconcile the cash-flow recurrence |
| Interpreting assumed constant growth as a forecast | Adds unsupported certainty | Present it as a conditional deterministic model |

## 15. Exercises and Answer Guidance

1. **Simple interest:** What is the simple-interest balance at year 10 for USD 1,000 and 5%?  
   **Guidance:** USD 1,500 under the stated model.
2. **Annual compounding:** What recurrence gives the next annual credited balance?  
   **Guidance:** Multiply the previous credited balance by `1.05`.
3. **Effective rate:** Why does nominal monthly 5% have an effective annual rate above 5%?  
   **Guidance:** Twelve successive factors `1 + 0.05/12` compound within the year.
4. **Equivalent rates:** How can monthly compounding reproduce 5% effective annual growth at common year-end observations?  
   **Guidance:** Use nominal rate `12 * ((1.05)**(1/12) - 1)`, approximately 4.888949%.
5. **Credited state:** Why does the annual helper reject `[0,0.5]` but the monthly helper accept it?  
   **Guidance:** Half a year is not a completed annual period; it is six completed monthly model periods.
6. **Logarithmic scale:** Does setting the y-axis to log alter the final USD balance?  
   **Guidance:** No. It changes the display mapping while retaining the same balances.
7. **Negative rate:** Why can a long simple-interest path with a negative rate be rejected?  
   **Guidance:** `1 + rate * time` can reach zero or become negative, violating the shared positive-balance contract.
8. **Forecast scope:** Which missing input would be required to show uncertainty around actual investment returns?  
   **Guidance:** A separately specified and validated uncertain-return model with appropriate data and assumptions. The four deterministic curves contain no probability distribution.
