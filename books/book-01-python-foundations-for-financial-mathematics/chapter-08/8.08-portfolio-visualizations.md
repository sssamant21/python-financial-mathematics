## 1. Purpose

A portfolio chart turns a set of positions into a visual statement about composition, direction, or change. The denominator and valuation basis determine that statement. A percentage labeled only “weight” can be ambiguous when a portfolio includes cash, short positions, missing prices, or instruments whose exposure differs from their marked value.

This section develops a static snapshot with two deliberately different views: signed weights relative to net marked value, and nonnegative shares of absolute marked values including cash. A short position remains negative in the first view and contributes its magnitude to the second. The complete lab reconciles both views to one validated synthetic holdings table.

The chart describes supplied positions at a stated valuation instant. It does not calculate risk contributions, portfolio optimization, or investment performance. Chapter 16 develops portfolio mathematics in more detail.

## 2. Learning Objectives

After completing this section, you should be able to:

1. State a portfolio chart's valuation instant, currency, numerator, and denominator.
2. Calculate signed marked values and signed weights for a simple cash/spot-position model.
3. Distinguish net marked value from the sum of absolute marked values.
4. Display short positions without converting them silently into positive allocations.
5. Explain when composition shares omit direction and why they are not risk contributions.
6. Block the full snapshot when required valuation inputs are unknown.
7. Verify chart labels, bar widths, ordering, and numerical reconciliation.

Prerequisites: holdings structures from Chapter 4, labeled calculations from Chapter 7, and Matplotlib fundamentals from Sections 8.1–8.7.

## 3. Choose the Portfolio Question

| Question | Useful view | Required context |
|---|---|---|
| Which positions are long or short, and how large relative to net marked value? | Signed horizontal weight bars | Complete valuation, sign convention, net denominator |
| How is the total absolute marked value distributed? | Nonnegative composition bars | Explicit absolute-value denominator and cash policy |
| How are positions grouped by sector or asset class? | Grouped signed bars or a composition table | Reliable classifications and aggregation rules |
| How do current and target weights differ? | Paired bars or a difference chart | Common valuation basis, denominators, target constraints |
| How does composition evolve? | Repeated snapshots or carefully defined stacked areas | Revaluation, cash-flow, trading, and denominator policies |
| Which positions contribute to risk? | A separately calculated risk-contribution view | Risk model, covariance/exposure definitions, horizon |

These views answer different questions. A large bar in a marked-value composition chart does not by itself identify the largest source of portfolio risk. A signed market-value chart is also not a universal exposure chart for derivatives.

## 4. Worked Snapshot and Valuation Assumptions

Assume all rows describe one synthetic USD snapshot at **2026-01-30 21:00 UTC**. For the risky positions, quantity is signed units and price is a positive USD quote per unit. Cash is represented as USD units at a price of one.

| Asset | Kind | Signed quantity | USD price | Signed marked value, USD |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| ALPHA | risky | 6 | 100 | 600 |
| BETA | risky | 4 | 100 | 400 |
| GAMMA | risky | -2 | 100 | -200 |
| CASH | cash | 200 | 1 | 200 |

For this restricted model, define:

$$v_i=q_i p_i,\qquad V=\sum_i v_i,\qquad A=\sum_i|v_i|.$$

Then:

$$V=600+400-200+200=1{,}000,$$

$$A=600+400+200+200=1{,}400.$$

The negative GAMMA quantity denotes a short position. Its negative marked value enters the net sum; its positive magnitude enters the absolute sum.

The supplied cash is an explicit balance. The lab does not infer short-sale proceeds or add them again. It also contains no omitted fees, financing balances, liabilities, accrued income, derivatives, or FX conversion. Therefore call the result **net marked value of the supplied snapshot**. Treating that sum as an account's independently verified NAV would require reconciling the complete account and its valuation conventions.

The timestamp is caller-supplied snapshot metadata. A complete table and a valid timestamp do not prove that the quantities and quotes were actually measured simultaneously or are fresh. That provenance belongs upstream.

## 5. Signed Weights and Absolute Composition Shares

Define two measures:

$$w_i=\frac{v_i}{V},\qquad a_i=\frac{|v_i|}{A}.$$

For positive $V$, signed weights preserve direction. Absolute shares are nonnegative and describe magnitudes under their own denominator.

| Asset | Signed weight, $v_i/V$ | Absolute share, $|v_i|/A$ |
|---|---:|---:|
| ALPHA | 60% | 42.857143…% |
| BETA | 40% | 28.571429…% |
| GAMMA | -20% | 14.285714…% |
| CASH | 20% | 14.285714…% |
| Total | 100% | 100% |

Both columns sum to 100%, but they are not interchangeable. In the signed view, the positive weights total 120% and the negative weights total -20%. The negative portion offsets part of the positive marked values in the net denominator.

In the absolute view, GAMMA and CASH have equal shares because their marked-value magnitudes are both USD 200. That equality does not make them economically equivalent: GAMMA is a short risky position and CASH is a positive cash balance. Direction and instrument meaning remain in the signed view and source table.

A weight above 100% or below zero can be meaningful in a long/short snapshot. Automatically clipping weight bars to `[0,100%]` would hide that information. Conversely, nonnegative absolute shares remain in `[0,100%]`, which is the range used for the second panel.

## 6. Cash and Gross Measures Need Explicit Definitions

The sum of absolute risky marked values in the fixture excludes cash:

$$G_{\mathrm{risky}}=|600|+|400|+|-200|=1{,}200.$$

Its ratio to net marked value is 120%. The absolute total including cash is USD 1,400, or 140% of net marked value. These are different measures because their included rows differ.

The demonstration prints **risky gross / net value (cash excluded)** and labels the second panel **absolute composition including cash**. It does not call both of them simply “gross exposure.” A report must specify the instrument set, valuation basis, cash treatment, and denominator for such a measure.

For derivatives or nonlinear instruments, marked values can be small while their economic exposures are substantial. Notional, delta-adjusted, duration-based, or model-based exposure measures require separate definitions and inputs. This lab's `quantity * price` model is limited to the supplied simple marked-value positions.

Similarly, absolute marked-value shares are not covariance-based risk contributions. A portfolio visualization should identify the quantity it actually computed rather than assigning a broader interpretation to its appearance.

## 7. Why Horizontal Bars Fit This Example

Horizontal bars make asset names readable and provide an explicit zero origin. Matplotlib's `barh` uses bar widths to represent values and supports a chosen horizontal origin [1]. The left panel's negative GAMMA bar extends left of zero; positive bars extend right.

The right panel uses the same asset order but displays nonnegative absolute shares. The two panels retain different x-axis labels because their denominators differ. Percentage formatting changes decimal labels into percentages without changing the underlying calculated widths.

Ordering is deterministic: descending absolute marked value, with asset label ascending for ties. CASH and GAMMA both have magnitude USD 200, so CASH comes first. The same row order is used in both panels. Reordering one panel independently would make side-by-side comparison harder.

Pie charts can be useful for a small, complete, nonnegative part-to-whole composition; their wedges use normalized fractional areas [2]. Signed long/short weights do not fit that interpretation. Taking absolute values merely to obtain a drawable pie changes the question. If using an absolute composition pie, identify that denominator and retain a separate signed view. The lab uses bars for both questions and does not produce a pie chart.

## 8. Completeness Before Normalization

The helper requires every supplied quantity and price to be finite and known. It rejects a missing GAMMA price instead of dropping that row, treating its value as zero, or renormalizing the remaining positions.

If GAMMA's price were unknown, the remaining observed signed values would sum to USD 1,200. That partial sum is not a released full-snapshot denominator. Normalizing only those rows would change every displayed weight and make the missing short position disappear.

Even a zero-quantity row must have a valid price under this lab's strict schema. That is an explicit teaching contract; another system could adopt a different rule for zero positions, but it must define and test the exception rather than obtain it accidentally through missing-value arithmetic.

The helper can validate the rows it receives. It cannot detect an entirely omitted holding without an independently supplied inventory or reconciliation source. Passing its checks therefore means completeness under the provided-table contract, not certified completeness of an external brokerage account.

Currency is restricted to USD. Summing differently denominated marked values without conversion would not form a meaningful common denominator. Real multi-currency reporting needs a reporting currency, FX quotes with timestamps, and a stated conversion policy.

## 9. Net-Value Boundaries and Numerical Policies

Signed weights relative to a zero or negative net denominator need another reporting design. This helper requires net marked value above `1e-6` USD and rejects snapshots outside that condition. It does not silently substitute an absolute denominator while keeping the label “signed weight.”

A very small positive net value relative to offsetting large positions can also produce enormous weights and numerically sensitive sums. The lab caps the ratio of total absolute marked value including cash to net marked value at 1,000. This is a teaching/numerical reporting bound, not a financial suitability threshold or a regulatory limit.

Calculations use floating-point values and compare expected results with tolerances. Formatted title values are display summaries; they are not the calculation inputs or an accounting cent-rounding schedule. In particular, the title's whole-dollar amounts are rounded for readability while the returned table retains calculated values.

Scaling every quantity by the same positive factor doubles both denominators when the factor is two, but leaves both sets of normalized bars unchanged. The tests verify that invariant. It describes this mathematical model and does not include transaction costs or financing changes.

### `portfolio_visualizations.py`

```python
"""Signed portfolio weights versus absolute marked-value composition."""
from pathlib import Path
import matplotlib
matplotlib.use('Agg')
import matplotlib.pyplot as plt
from matplotlib.ticker import PercentFormatter
import numpy as np
import pandas as pd


def fixture():
    holdings = pd.DataFrame({'kind': ['risky', 'risky', 'risky', 'cash'],
                              'quantity': [6., 4., -2., 200.],
                              'price': [100., 100., 100., 1.],
                              'currency': ['USD']*4},
                             index=pd.Index(['ALPHA', 'BETA', 'GAMMA', 'CASH'], name='asset'))
    return holdings, pd.Timestamp('2026-01-30T21:00:00Z')


def summarize(holdings, valuation_time):
    if (not isinstance(valuation_time, pd.Timestamp) or pd.isna(valuation_time)
            or valuation_time.tzinfo is None):
        raise ValueError('valuation_time must be an aware pandas Timestamp')
    instant = valuation_time.tz_convert('UTC')
    if not 2000 <= instant.year <= 2100:
        raise ValueError('supported UTC years are 2000..2100')
    if (not isinstance(holdings, pd.DataFrame)
            or tuple(holdings.columns) != ('kind', 'quantity', 'price', 'currency')
            or not 1 <= len(holdings) <= 30 or not holdings.index.is_unique
            or any(not isinstance(a, str) or not a or a != a.strip() or len(a)>24
                   for a in holdings.index)):
        raise ValueError('provide 1..30 unique asset labels and the exact holdings columns')
    if (holdings['quantity'].dtype != np.dtype('float64')
            or holdings['price'].dtype != np.dtype('float64')
            or not holdings['kind'].isin(['risky', 'cash']).all()
            or not holdings['currency'].eq('USD').all()):
        raise ValueError('quantities/prices must be float64; kinds risky/cash; currency USD')
    q = holdings.quantity.to_numpy(); p = holdings.price.to_numpy()
    if (not np.isfinite(q).all() or not np.isfinite(p).all()
            or (np.abs(q) > 1e6).any() or (p < 1e-6).any() or (p > 1e6).any()):
        raise ValueError('complete finite quantities/prices within lab bounds are required')
    cash = holdings['kind'].eq('cash').to_numpy()
    if cash.sum() > 1 or (q[cash] < 0).any() or not (p[cash] == 1).all():
        raise ValueError('allow at most one nonnegative USD cash balance at price one')
    result = holdings.copy(deep=True)
    result['value'] = q*p
    net = float(result.value.sum())
    absolute_total = float(result.value.abs().sum())
    if net <= 1e-6 or absolute_total <= 1e-6:
        raise ValueError('positive net value above 1e-6 is required for this weight view')
    if absolute_total/net > 1000:
        raise ValueError('absolute marked value / net value exceeds the lab cap of 1000')
    result['weight'] = result.value/net
    result['absolute_share'] = result.value.abs()/absolute_total
    # Deterministic row order: absolute value descending, asset ascending.
    result['asset_sort'] = result.index
    result['absolute_value'] = result.value.abs()
    result = result.sort_values(['absolute_value', 'asset_sort'], ascending=[False, True])
    result = result.drop(columns='asset_sort')
    risky_gross = float(result.loc[result.kind.eq('risky'), 'absolute_value'].sum())
    report = {'valuation_time': instant, 'net_value': net,
              'absolute_value_including_cash': absolute_total,
              'risky_gross_over_net': risky_gross/net,
              'cash_value': float(result.loc[result.kind.eq('cash'), 'value'].sum())}
    return result, report


def build_figure(holdings, valuation_time):
    data, report = summarize(holdings, valuation_time)
    positions = np.arange(len(data))
    fig, axes = plt.subplots(1, 2, figsize=(10, 5), sharey=True, layout='constrained')
    colors = np.where(data.weight.to_numpy() < 0, '#D55E00', '#0072B2')
    axes[0].barh(positions, data.weight.to_numpy(), color=colors)
    axes[0].axvline(0, color='black', linewidth=.8)
    axes[0].set(title='Signed weights\nDenominator: net marked value', xlabel='Signed marked value / net value')
    axes[1].barh(positions, data.absolute_share.to_numpy(), color='#009E73')
    axes[1].set(title='Absolute composition\nDenominator: absolute values including cash', xlabel='Absolute marked value / sum of absolute values', xlim=(0, 1))
    axes[0].set_yticks(positions, data.index.tolist())
    axes[0].invert_yaxis()
    for ax in axes:
        ax.xaxis.set_major_formatter(PercentFormatter(xmax=1., decimals=0))
        ax.grid(axis='x', alpha=.2)
    stamp = report['valuation_time'].strftime('%Y-%m-%d %H:%M UTC')
    fig.suptitle(f'Synthetic USD snapshot | {stamp}\n'
                 f'Net USD {report["net_value"]:,.0f}; absolute marked value incl. cash '
                 f'USD {report["absolute_value_including_cash"]:,.0f}', fontsize=12)
    fig.supxlabel('Negative weights remain visible. Absolute shares do not encode direction or risk contribution.', fontsize=9)
    return fig, axes


def main():
    holdings, instant = fixture(); data, report = summarize(holdings, instant)
    print(f'Net marked value: USD {report["net_value"]:.2f}')
    print(f'Absolute marked value including cash: USD {report["absolute_value_including_cash"]:.2f}')
    print(f'Signed weight sum: {data.weight.sum():.6f}')
    print(f'Absolute share sum: {data.absolute_share.sum():.6f}')
    print(f'Risky gross / net value (cash excluded): {report["risky_gross_over_net"]:.2%}')
    fig, _ = build_figure(holdings, instant)
    try:
        Path('output').mkdir(exist_ok=True)
        fig.savefig('output/portfolio_visualizations.png', dpi=150)
    finally:
        plt.close(fig)
    print('Saved output/portfolio_visualizations.png')


if __name__ == '__main__':
    main()
```

The summary helper computes the data and report before creating a Figure. It returns a copy, with signed marked value, signed weight, absolute marked value, and absolute share attached. Sorting uses explicit keys [3], and the plot receives the resulting order without recomputing denominators.

The caller saves and closes the specific Figure in `finally`. Agg writes a PNG without opening a desktop window. Re-running the demonstration replaces that PNG.

## 11. Reference Tests

### `test_portfolio_visualizations.py`

```python
import tempfile
import unittest
from pathlib import Path
import numpy as np
import pandas as pd
from numpy.testing import assert_allclose
from pandas.testing import assert_frame_equal
from portfolio_visualizations import fixture, summarize, build_figure, plt


class PortfolioTests(unittest.TestCase):
    def test_known_values_and_two_denominators(self):
        h, t = fixture(); data, report = summarize(h, t)
        self.assertEqual(report['net_value'], 1000)
        self.assertEqual(report['absolute_value_including_cash'], 1400)
        self.assertAlmostEqual(report['risky_gross_over_net'], 1.2)
        assert_allclose(data.loc[['ALPHA', 'BETA', 'GAMMA', 'CASH'], 'weight'], [.6, .4, -.2, .2])
        self.assertAlmostEqual(data.weight.sum(), 1)
        self.assertAlmostEqual(data.absolute_share.sum(), 1)
        self.assertAlmostEqual(data.loc['GAMMA', 'absolute_share'], 1/7)

    def test_row_order_and_positive_scaling(self):
        h, t = fixture(); a, report = summarize(h, t)
        b, _ = summarize(h.iloc[::-1], t)
        assert_frame_equal(a, b)
        scaled = h.copy(); scaled['quantity'] *= 2
        c, report2 = summarize(scaled, t)
        assert_allclose(a.weight, c.weight)
        assert_allclose(a.absolute_share, c.absolute_share)
        self.assertEqual(report2['net_value'], 2*report['net_value'])

    def test_missing_invalid_and_schema(self):
        h, t = fixture()
        for value in [np.nan, np.inf, 0, -1, 1e7]:
            bad = h.copy(); bad.loc['GAMMA', 'price'] = value
            with self.assertRaises(ValueError): summarize(bad, t)
        bad = h.copy(); bad.loc['ALPHA', 'currency'] = 'EUR'
        with self.assertRaises(ValueError): summarize(bad, t)
        with self.assertRaises(ValueError): summarize(h[['price', 'quantity', 'kind', 'currency']], t)
        bad = h.copy(); bad.index = ['A', 'A', 'B', 'C']
        with self.assertRaises(ValueError): summarize(bad, t)

    def test_cash_contract_and_nonpositive_net(self):
        h, t = fixture()
        for quantity, price in [(-100, 1), (200, 2)]:
            bad = h.copy(); bad.loc['CASH', ['quantity', 'price']] = [quantity, price]
            with self.assertRaises(ValueError): summarize(bad, t)
        for quantity in [-12, -11.999999]:
            bad = h.copy(); bad.loc['GAMMA', 'quantity'] = quantity
            with self.assertRaises(ValueError): summarize(bad, t)

    def test_valuation_time(self):
        h, t = fixture()
        for bad in [t.tz_localize(None), pd.NaT, '2026-01-30']:
            with self.assertRaises(ValueError): summarize(h, bad)
        self.assertEqual(summarize(h, t.tz_convert('America/New_York'))[1]['valuation_time'], t)

    def test_zero_position_and_long_only_case(self):
        h, t = fixture(); h.loc['GAMMA', 'quantity'] = 0
        data, report = summarize(h, t)
        self.assertEqual(data.loc['GAMMA', 'weight'], 0)
        self.assertEqual(data.loc['GAMMA', 'absolute_share'], 0)
        assert_allclose(data.weight, data.absolute_share)

    def test_source_preservation(self):
        h, t = fixture(); before = h.copy(deep=True)
        data, _ = summarize(h, t); data.iloc[0, 1] = 999
        assert_frame_equal(h, before)

    def test_bar_widths_labels_png_and_cleanup(self):
        h, t = fixture(); data, _ = summarize(h, t)
        before = set(plt.get_fignums()); fig, axes = build_figure(h, t)
        try:
            assert_allclose([p.get_width() for p in axes[0].patches], data.weight)
            assert_allclose([p.get_width() for p in axes[1].patches], data.absolute_share)
            self.assertEqual([v.get_text() for v in axes[0].get_yticklabels()], data.index.tolist())
            with tempfile.TemporaryDirectory() as folder:
                path = Path(folder)/'portfolio.png'; fig.savefig(path)
                self.assertEqual(path.read_bytes()[:8], b'\x89PNG\r\n\x1a\n')
        finally:
            plt.close(fig)
        self.assertEqual(set(plt.get_fignums()), before)


if __name__ == '__main__':
    unittest.main()
```

The eight methods verify the known marked values and both denominators, signed/absolute weight sums, row-order invariance and positive scaling, missing/invalid/schema rejection, cash and net-value boundaries, timestamp behavior, zero positions and a long-only variant, source preservation, and the plotted bar widths/labels with export cleanup.

The long-only test sets GAMMA's quantity to zero only in its separate fixture. When every marked value is nonnegative, the two denominators coincide and the signed weights equal the absolute shares. This does not alter the published long/short example.

## 14. Extensions and Common Mistakes

Grouping by sector requires reliable classifications and a stated sign policy. Summing signed values within a sector can hide offsetting long and short positions. Summing absolute values answers a magnitude question instead; the two aggregations should be labeled separately.

A target/current comparison requires the same denominator convention and asset universe. A missing target is not automatically zero, and a proposed target chart is not a completed trade. A history of weights also reflects price changes, trades, flows, and denominator changes; it is not automatically a performance-attribution chart.

| Error | Consequence | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Using absolute values while labeling bars signed weights | Short direction disappears | Preserve sign or rename the computed product |
| Dropping a holding with an unknown price | Partial valuation appears complete | Block or explicitly scope a partial report |
| Summing mixed currencies | Denominator has incompatible units | Convert under a documented FX policy |
| Calling every absolute sum gross exposure | Cash/instrument conventions remain hidden | Name included rows, valuation basis, and denominator |
| Calling marked-value shares risk contributions | Overstates the calculation | Obtain and label a separate risk model |
| Pie-charting signed weights | Negative positions cannot be represented as ordinary wedges | Use a signed view; disclose any absolute composition separately |
| Clipping signed axes to 0–100% | Shorts or leveraged weights disappear | Show the computed signed range |
| Comparing independently sorted panels | Asset correspondence becomes hard to follow | Reuse one deterministic order |
| Treating a caller timestamp as proof of freshness | Metadata replaces market-data validation | Reconcile source timestamps and valuation provenance upstream |

7. **Ties:** Why is CASH above GAMMA in the chart?
