## Learning objectives

Value finite cash-flow streams at a common date, reconcile present and future value, distinguish annuity timing conventions, combine positive and negative payments, and build a reusable Python valuation function for irregular integer-period schedules.

## 1. A unified cash-flow model

Let a stream consist of signed payments **C(k)** occurring at periods **t(k)**, with periodic effective interest rate **i > -1**. The dates are measured in the same time unit as the rate. A payment can occur at time zero, at a repeated period, or at a later period.

```text
Time          0         1         2         3         4
              |---------|---------|---------|---------|
Cash flow    -500      +200                +400
```

The example represents an initial $500 outflow followed by $200 at period 1 and $400 at period 3. The signed convention is essential: payments received are positive, and payments made are negative, from the selected perspective.

## 2. Present value at time zero

The present value of a finite stream is the sum of individually discounted cash flows:

**PV = sum over k of C(k)/(1+i)^(t(k)).**

For the example at 10% periodic interest:

PV = -500 + 200/1.1 + 400/1.1³.

A payment at time zero is not discounted. Cash flows sharing a date can be combined, but keeping them separate can improve auditability.

## 3. Future value at a selected horizon

Choose a valuation horizon **T** in the same period units. Then:

**V(T) = sum over k of C(k) × (1+i)^(T-t(k)).**

If T is at or after the last cash-flow date, this is commonly called the **future value**. The expression also works for an earlier valuation date, in which case later cash flows are discounted to T.

The central reconciliation identity is:

**V(T) = PV × (1+i)^T.**

It holds for every finite stream under a single constant periodic rate, regardless of cash-flow signs or payment timing.

## 4. Recovering annuity formulas from the general model

The timeline method includes earlier special cases:

| Stream | Payment dates | Amounts |
|---|---|---|
| Ordinary level annuity | 1 through n | Constant C |
| Annuity due | 0 through n-1 | Constant C |
| Deferred ordinary annuity | d+1 through d+n | Constant C |
| Growing ordinary annuity | 1 through n | C(1+g)^(k-1) |
| Irregular stream | Arbitrary discrete dates | Arbitrary signed amounts |

For an ordinary level annuity with three $100 payments at 10%, the general PV formula gives the same result as the closed-form annuity factor. Accumulating those three payments to period 3 gives **$331**.

## 5. Worked example: mixed cash flows

Consider the stream [(-$500, time 0), (+$200, time 1), (+$400, time 3)] and i = 10%.

PV = -500 + 200/1.1 + 400/1.1³, approximately **-$17.58**.

The value at period 3 is:

V(3) = -500 × 1.1³ + 200 × 1.1² + 400.

Both calculations must satisfy V(3) = PV × 1.1³. A negative present value indicates the discounted outflows exceed the discounted inflows under the selected assumptions; it does not alone determine whether the project should proceed.

## 6. Numerical and modeling safeguards

- Match the interest-rate period to the payment-date unit. Monthly dates require a monthly effective rate.
- Explicitly document the valuation horizon and whether it precedes or follows the final cash flow.
- Permit repeated dates and signed payments; sort cash flows for presentation, not because summation mathematically requires sorting.
- Reject nonfinite amounts, invalid periods, and rates at or below -100%.
- Avoid rounding intermediate discounted values. Financial systems may require decimal arithmetic and contract-specific rounding.
- A constant-rate integer-period model is not a substitute for dated cash flows, day-count conventions, variable forward rates, or credit-risk modeling.

```python
"""Payment-stream valuation with explicit integer-period dates."""
import math
import unittest

def _rate(rate):
    if isinstance(rate, bool) or not isinstance(rate, (int, float)) or not math.isfinite(rate):
        raise ValueError("rate must be finite and numeric")
    if rate <= -1:
        raise ValueError("rate must exceed -1")

def _flows(flows):
    checked = []
    for period, amount in flows:
        if isinstance(period, bool) or not isinstance(period, int) or period < 0:
            raise ValueError("payment period must be a nonnegative integer")
        if isinstance(amount, bool) or not isinstance(amount, (int, float)) or not math.isfinite(amount):
            raise ValueError("payment must be finite")
        checked.append((period, float(amount)))
    return checked

def value_at(flows, rate, valuation_period):
    _rate(rate)
    if isinstance(valuation_period, bool) or not isinstance(valuation_period, int) or valuation_period < 0:
        raise ValueError("valuation period must be nonnegative integer")
    data = _flows(flows)
    log_factor = math.log1p(rate)
    try:
        terms = [amount * math.exp((valuation_period - period) * log_factor)
                 for period, amount in data]
        value = math.fsum(terms)
    except OverflowError as exc:
        raise ValueError("valuation overflow") from exc
    if not math.isfinite(value):
        raise ValueError("valuation overflow")
    return value

def pv(flows, rate):
    return value_at(flows, rate, 0)

def fv(flows, rate, horizon):
    return value_at(flows, rate, horizon)

def level_stream(payment, first_period, count):
    if isinstance(first_period, bool) or not isinstance(first_period, int) or first_period < 0:
        raise ValueError("first_period must be nonnegative integer")
    if isinstance(count, bool) or not isinstance(count, int) or count < 0:
        raise ValueError("count must be nonnegative integer")
    if isinstance(payment, bool) or not isinstance(payment, (int, float)) or not math.isfinite(payment):
        raise ValueError("payment must be finite")
    return [(first_period+k, payment) for k in range(count)]

class Tests(unittest.TestCase):
    def test_01_ordinary_pv(self):
        self.assertAlmostEqual(pv(level_stream(100,1,3),.1),
                               sum(100/1.1**k for k in (1,2,3)))
    def test_02_ordinary_fv(self):
        self.assertAlmostEqual(fv(level_stream(100,1,3),.1,3),331)
    def test_03_due(self):
        self.assertAlmostEqual(pv(level_stream(100,0,3),.1),
                               100+100/1.1+100/1.1**2)
    def test_04_deferred(self):
        self.assertAlmostEqual(pv(level_stream(100,3,3),.1),
                               sum(100/1.1**k for k in (3,4,5)))
    def test_05_irregular(self):
        self.assertAlmostEqual(pv([(0,-500),(1,200),(3,400)],.1),
                               -500+200/1.1+400/1.1**3)
    def test_06_repeated_dates(self):
        self.assertAlmostEqual(pv([(1,50),(1,25)],.1),75/1.1)
    def test_07_empty(self):
        self.assertEqual(pv([],.05),0)
    def test_08_zero_rate(self):
        self.assertEqual(pv([(1,100),(4,200)],0),300)
    def test_09_negative_rate(self):
        self.assertAlmostEqual(pv([(1,100)],-.1),100/.9)
    def test_10_consistency(self):
        flows=[(0,-500),(2,200),(5,600)]
        self.assertAlmostEqual(fv(flows,.07,5),pv(flows,.07)*1.07**5)
    def test_11_prior_horizon(self):
        self.assertAlmostEqual(value_at([(5,100)],.1,3),100/1.1**2)
    def test_12_invalid(self):
        with self.assertRaises(ValueError): pv([(1,100)],-1)
        with self.assertRaises(ValueError): pv([(-1,100)],.05)
        with self.assertRaises(ValueError): fv([(1,100)],.05,2.5)
        with self.assertRaises(ValueError): level_stream(100,1,-1)

if __name__ == "__main__":
    print(f"PV = {pv([(0,-500),(1,200),(3,400)],.1):.2f}")
    unittest.main(verbosity=2)
```

## 8. Exercises and worked answers

1. **General PV:** How do you value a payment at period t? **Answer:** Divide its signed amount by (1+i)^t.
2. **General FV:** How do you value a payment at horizon T? **Answer:** Multiply by (1+i)^(T-t).
3. **Time zero:** What is the PV of a $500 outflow at time zero? **Answer:** -$500.
4. **Ordinary annuity:** What are the dates of three ordinary payments? **Answer:** 1, 2, and 3.
5. **Annuity due:** What are the dates of three due payments? **Answer:** 0, 1, and 2.
6. **Deferred annuity:** For d = 2 and n = 3, what are the dates? **Answer:** 3, 4, and 5.
7. **Repeated date:** What is the PV of $50 and $25 both at period 1, with i = 10%? **Answer:** 75/1.1, approximately $68.18.
8. **Zero rate:** What is the PV of $100 at period 1 and $200 at period 4 with i = 0? **Answer:** $300.
9. **Negative rate:** What is the PV of $100 at period 1 with i = -10%? **Answer:** 100/0.9, approximately $111.11.
10. **Mixed stream:** Write PV of -$500 at time 0, +$200 at time 1, and +$400 at time 3, with i = 10%. **Answer:** -500 + 200/1.1 + 400/1.1³.
11. **Reconciliation:** How do you compare PV and value at T? **Answer:** Check V(T) = PV × (1+i)^T.
12. **Audit:** How do you test a closed-form annuity factor? **Answer:** Generate the corresponding individual payments and compare the discounted sum.
