## Part A — Foundations and cash-flow timelines

### Exercise 1 — Timeline construction

An investor pays $2,000 at time 0 and receives $700, $800, and $900 at the ends of years 1, 2, and 3. Write the signed timeline and its present-value equation at 8%.

**Worked answer:** The timeline is C0 = -2000, C1 = +700, C2 = +800, C3 = +900. Therefore PV = -2000 + 700/1.08 + 800/1.08² + 900/1.08³. This is the investment's NPV at the selected discount rate.

### Exercise 2 — Valuation-date movement

A $500 receipt occurs at time 2. Find its value at time 0 and at time 5 using 6% per period.

**Worked answer:** PV(0) = 500/1.06² ≈ $445.00. Value at time 5 = 500 × 1.06³ ≈ $595.51. Verify that value at time 5 also equals PV(0) × 1.06⁵.

### Exercise 3 — Mixed signs

A project has -$100 at time 0, +$40 at time 1, and -$20 at time 2. Write the NPV at 10%.

**Worked answer:** NPV = -100 + 40/1.1 - 20/1.1². Do not treat the final expenditure as a positive receipt.

## Part B — Annuities

### Exercise 4 — Ordinary annuity present value

Compute the present value of five annual $1,000 receipts at 7%, with the first receipt at year 1.

**Worked answer:** PV = 1000 × [1 - (1.07)^(-5)]/0.07 ≈ $4,100.20. A direct sum of 1000/(1.07)^t for t = 1, ..., 5 gives the same result.

### Exercise 5 — Ordinary annuity future value

Accumulate four end-of-year $250 deposits to year 4 at 5%.

**Worked answer:** FV = 250 × [(1.05)^4 - 1]/0.05 = $1,077.53. The last payment at time 4 earns no additional interest.

### Exercise 6 — Annuity due

Find the present value of four $500 payments made at times 0, 1, 2, and 3, at 8%.

**Worked answer:** PV_due = 500 × [1 - 1.08^(-4)]/0.08 × 1.08 ≈ $1,788.55. The extra factor 1.08 reflects the one-period shift from an ordinary annuity.

### Exercise 7 — Deferred annuity

A stream pays $300 at times 4, 5, and 6. Find its present value at 6%.

**Worked answer:** PV = 300/1.06⁴ + 300/1.06⁵ + 300/1.06⁶ ≈ $673.29. Equivalently, PV = 300 × [1 - 1.06^(-3)]/0.06 × 1.06^(-3). The first payment is at time 4, not time 3.

### Exercise 8 — Growing annuity

Three receipts begin with $100 at time 1 and grow by 5% annually. Discount at 10%.

**Worked answer:** Cash flows are 100, 105, and 110.25. PV = 100/1.1 + 105/1.1² + 110.25/1.1³ ≈ $260.52. The first payment is not increased by the growth rate.

## Part C — Perpetuities

### Exercise 9 — Level perpetuity

A perpetual end-of-year payment is $80 and the discount rate is 8%.

**Worked answer:** PV = 80/0.08 = $1,000, provided the rate is positive and payments continue indefinitely.

### Exercise 10 — Growing perpetuity

The first payment next year is $100 and subsequent payments grow at 3% annually. The discount rate is 9%.

**Worked answer:** PV = 100/(0.09 - 0.03) = $1,666.67. This requires r > g.

### Exercise 11 — Convergence diagnosis

Can the standard growing-perpetuity formula be used with r = 4% and g = 5% for positive payments?

**Worked answer:** No. The infinite discounted sum does not converge because r ≤ g. A negative denominator does not represent a valid positive-payment present value.

## Part D — Payment streams and investment decisions

### Exercise 12 — Present/future value reconciliation

Consider -$100 at time 0 and +$60 at times 1 and 2. At 10%, compute PV and FV at time 2.

**Worked answer:** PV = -100 + 60/1.1 + 60/1.1² ≈ +$4.13. FV(2) = -100(1.1²) + 60(1.1) + 60 = $5.00. The identity FV(2) = PV × 1.1² holds.

### Exercise 13 — Irregular dates

A $365 receipt is due 182 actual days after a valuation date. Write its present value using a 10% effective annual rate and Actual/365 Fixed.

**Worked answer:** PV = 365/(1.1)^(182/365). The fractional exponent is a year fraction, not a count of equal annual periods.

### Exercise 14 — NPV decision

An investment costs $500 today and returns $300 at the end of each of the next two years. Required return: 10%.

**Worked answer:** NPV = -500 + 300/1.1 + 300/1.1² ≈ +$20.66. Under the modeled assumptions, the investment passes the positive-NPV criterion.

### Exercise 15 — One-period IRR

A project costs $400 now and returns $460 in one year.

**Worked answer:** 0 = -400 + 460/(1+r). Thus r = 460/400 - 1 = 15%.

### Exercise 16 — Multiple IRRs

For the stream [-100, +230, -132] at periods 0, 1, and 2, identify possible IRRs.

**Worked answer:** Set -100 + 230/(1+r) - 132/(1+r)² = 0. Multiplying by (1+r)² gives a quadratic whose roots correspond to r = 10% and r = 20%. A single reported IRR would be incomplete.

## Part E — Applied case studies

### Exercise 17 — Equipment replacement

Machine A costs $4,000 today and yields annual net savings of $1,200 for four years. Machine B costs $5,000 and yields $1,550 annually for four years. Use 8%.

**Worked answer:** NPV_A = -4000 + 1200 × [1 - 1.08^(-4)]/0.08. NPV_B = -5000 + 1550 × [1 - 1.08^(-4)]/0.08. NPV_A ≈ -$25.45 and NPV_B ≈ $133.80. B has the higher NPV and passes the positive-NPV criterion. Compare signed NPVs, not their magnitudes, and confirm comparable service and risk assumptions.

### Exercise 18 — Healthcare reimbursement schedule

A provider incurs $2,000 at service date (time 0), receives $1,300 after three months, and $900 after nine months. The effective annual discount rate is 8%.

**Worked answer:** Under a simplified monthly model, the effective monthly rate is (1.08)^(1/12) - 1. NPV = -2000 + 1300/(1+r_m)^3 + 900/(1+r_m)^9 ≈ $124.75. For exact settlement dates, instead use an explicitly chosen calendar day-count convention.

### Exercise 19 — Sensitivity grid

For the stream [-1000, +400, +450, +500] at annual periods 0–3, evaluate NPV at 5%, 10%, and 15%.

**Worked answer:** For each rate r, compute NPV(r) = -1000 + 400/(1+r) + 450/(1+r)² + 500/(1+r)³. With conventional positive future receipts, NPV decreases as r increases. NPVs at 5%, 10%, and 15% are $221.03, $111.19, and $16.85 respectively; all three pass the positive-NPV criterion under the modeled assumptions.

### Exercise 20 — Solver design review

Explain why an IRR solver should not silently return a root for any cash-flow stream.

**Worked answer:** A stream may have no economically relevant root, multiple roots, or a root outside the searched bracket. A reliable solver validates inputs, documents the rate domain, checks the residual, and reports bracket failures rather than guessing.

The standalone lab implements periodic PV, FV, annuity, growing-annuity, and bracketed IRR reference calculations. The lab accepts (period, amount) pairs with nonnegative integer periods and finite numeric amounts. Bisection materializes its input once, uses a finite rate half-width tolerance, and requires a sign change or computed endpoint zero. A zero NPV polynomial has no unique IRR. Check residuals separately. Nonzero terms outside the supported float range are rejected; computed zeros and extreme cancellation remain numerical limitations.

Its **12 unit tests** check timing, formulas, convergence examples, NPV, and multiple-root cases. These are educational reference tests, not evidence of a completed production validation.

```python
"""Chapter 10 acceptance exercises: independent periodic valuation checks."""
import math
from fractions import Fraction
import unittest

def _finite_number(value):
    return isinstance(value, (int, float)) and not isinstance(value, bool) and math.isfinite(value)

def _count(value, label):
    if isinstance(value, bool) or not isinstance(value, int) or value < 0:
        raise ValueError(f"{label} must be a nonnegative integer")

def _amount(value):
    if not _finite_number(value):
        raise ValueError("payment must be finite numeric")

def _rate(value):
    if not _finite_number(value) or value <= -1:
        raise ValueError("invalid rate")

def _pairs(flows):
    data = []
    for period, amount in flows:
        _count(period, "period")
        _amount(amount)
        data.append((period, amount))
    return data

def _value(data, r, horizon):
    try:
        if r == 0:
            return math.fsum(amount for _, amount in data)
        log_rate = math.log1p(r)
        terms = []
        for period, amount in data:
            if amount == 0:
                continue
            value = amount * math.exp((horizon-period)*log_rate)
            if not math.isfinite(value) or value == 0:
                raise ValueError("nonzero cash-flow value outside supported range")
            terms.append(value)
        return math.fsum(terms)
    except OverflowError as exc:
        raise ValueError("valuation overflow") from exc

def pv(flows, r):
    _rate(r)
    return _value(_pairs(flows), r, 0)

def fv(flows, r, horizon):
    _rate(r)
    _count(horizon, "horizon")
    return _value(_pairs(flows), r, horizon)

def annuity(payment, n, r, first=1):
    _amount(payment)
    _count(n, "count")
    _count(first, "first period")
    return pv(((first+k,payment) for k in range(n)),r)

def growing(payment, g, n, r, first=1):
    _amount(payment)
    _count(n, "count")
    _count(first, "first period")
    _rate(g)
    _rate(r)
    data = []
    amount, factor = payment, 1.0 + g
    for k in range(n):
        if not math.isfinite(amount) or (payment != 0 and amount == 0):
            raise ValueError("growing payment outside supported range")
        data.append((first+k, amount))
        if k+1 < n:
            amount *= factor
    return _value(data, r, 0)

def bisect_irr(flows, lo, hi, tol=1e-11):
    data = _pairs(flows)
    if not any(c > 0 for _, c in data) or not any(c < 0 for _, c in data):
        raise ValueError("IRR requires both cash-flow signs")
    if (not all(_finite_number(x) for x in (lo, hi, tol))
            or not (-1 < lo < hi) or tol <= 0):
        raise ValueError("invalid root bracket or solver settings")
    # Detect a zero polynomial exactly for the represented input amounts.
    net = {}
    for period, amount in data:
        net[period] = net.get(period, 0) + Fraction(amount)
    if not any(net.values()):
        raise ValueError("IRR indeterminate: net payments are zero at every period")
    a, b = lo, hi
    fa, fb = _value(data,a,0), _value(data,b,0)
    if fa == 0: return a
    if fb == 0: return b
    if (fa > 0) == (fb > 0):
        raise ValueError("no sign change within bracket")
    for _ in range(200):
        half_width = (b-a)/2
        mid = a + half_width
        fm = _value(data,mid,0)
        if fm == 0 or half_width <= tol:
            return mid
        if mid == a or mid == b:
            raise RuntimeError("floating-point bracket cannot narrow further")
        if (fa > 0) != (fm > 0):
            b = mid
        else:
            a, fa = mid, fm
    raise RuntimeError("IRR did not converge")

class Chapter10Acceptance(unittest.TestCase):
    def test_01_timeline(self):
        self.assertAlmostEqual(pv([(0,-100),(1,110)],.1),0)
    def test_02_ordinary_annuity(self):
        self.assertAlmostEqual(annuity(100,3,.1),100*(1-1.1**-3)/.1)
    def test_03_due(self):
        self.assertAlmostEqual(annuity(100,3,.1,0),annuity(100,3,.1)*1.1)
    def test_04_deferred(self):
        self.assertAlmostEqual(annuity(100,3,.1,3),annuity(100,3,.1)/1.1**2)
    def test_05_growing(self):
        self.assertAlmostEqual(growing(100,.05,3,.1),sum(100*1.05**k/1.1**(k+1) for k in range(3)))
    def test_06_level_perpetuity(self):
        self.assertAlmostEqual(100/.08,1250)
    def test_07_growing_perpetuity(self):
        self.assertAlmostEqual(100/(.09-.03),100/.06)
    def test_08_stream_fv(self):
        a=[(0,-100),(1,60),(2,60)]
        self.assertAlmostEqual(fv(a,.1,2),pv(a,.1)*1.1**2)
    def test_09_irregular_periods(self):
        self.assertAlmostEqual(pv([(0,-100),(1,30),(3,100)],.1),-100+30/1.1+100/1.1**3)
    def test_10_npv(self):
        self.assertAlmostEqual(pv([(0,-100),(1,60),(2,60)],.1),4.13223140495867)
    def test_11_irr(self):
        self.assertAlmostEqual(bisect_irr([(0,-100),(1,110)],0,.5),.1,places=8)
    def test_12_multiple_irr(self):
        a=[(0,-100),(1,230),(2,-132)]
        self.assertAlmostEqual(bisect_irr(a,.05,.15),.1,places=8)
        self.assertAlmostEqual(bisect_irr(a,.15,.3),.2,places=8)
        self.assertAlmostEqual(bisect_irr(iter([(0,-100),(1,110)]),0,1),.1)
        self.assertAlmostEqual(bisect_irr([(0,-1e-200),(1,1.1e-200)],0,1),.1)
        huge = bisect_irr([(0,-7e-309),(1,1)],1e308,1.7e308,1e296)
        self.assertAlmostEqual(huge/(1/7e-309),1)
        self.assertEqual(fv([(0,0)],.1,10000),0)
        self.assertAlmostEqual(growing(1e-308,1e308,3,0)/1e308,1)
        for call in (lambda: pv([(1,5e-324)],1),
                     lambda: growing(5e-324,-.5,2,0),
                     lambda: bisect_irr([(0,-1e-200),(1,5e-201)],0,1),
                     lambda: bisect_irr([(0,-100),(1,110)],0,1,float("inf")),
                     lambda: bisect_irr([(0,-100),(0,100)],0,1),
                     lambda: pv([(0,True)],0),
                     lambda: annuity(float("nan"),0,.1),
                     lambda: pv([(-1,100)],.1),
                     lambda: growing(100,-1,3,.1)):
            with self.assertRaises(ValueError):
                call()

if __name__ == "__main__":
    unittest.main(verbosity=2)
```
