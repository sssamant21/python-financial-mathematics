## 1. What NPV measures

**Net present value (NPV)** is the sum of all signed project cash flows expressed at a common valuation date. Under a constant periodic discount rate r and end-of-period future cash flows:

**NPV(r) = C0 + C1/(1+r) + C2/(1+r)^2 + ... + Cn/(1+r)^n.**

Here C0 is the cash flow at time zero, typically a negative initial investment. The future Ck values are net cash flows after relevant operating costs and other project cash movements. NPV is a currency amount, not a percentage.

```text
Period         0            1            2
               |------------|------------|
Cash flow     -100         +60          +60
```

A positive NPV indicates that the modeled cash flows exceed the required return represented by the discount rate. A negative NPV indicates the opposite. This decision interpretation depends on accurate cash-flow estimates, appropriate discounting, and consideration of constraints and risks not captured in the model.

## 2. Derivation from present-value mathematics

Each payment at period t is discounted by (1+r)^t. Adding the discounted payments produces NPV. If the project has an initial investment I > 0 and future inflows F1 through Fn, then:

**NPV = -I + sum from t=1 to n of Ft/(1+r)^t.**

This is a special case of the payment-stream valuation model developed in Section 10.10. For irregular calendar dates, use a dated convention such as the Actual/365 Fixed model in Section 10.11 rather than pretending every interval is identical.

## 3. Worked investment example

An investment costs $100 today and returns $60 at the ends of years 1 and 2. At a 10% effective annual discount rate:

NPV = -100 + 60/1.10 + 60/1.10².

The discounted receipts are approximately $54.55 and $49.59, producing **NPV ≈ +$4.13**. Under the modeled assumptions, the project earns more than the 10% required return.

If the receipts were $40 and $40 instead, the NPV would be negative. An initial outflow is not discounted because it already occurs at the valuation date.

## 4. NPV decision rules

For an independent investment with no binding constraints, the conventional financial rule is to **accept if NPV > 0**, reject if NPV < 0, and recognize indifference at NPV = 0, subject to strategic, regulatory, liquidity, and risk considerations.

For **mutually exclusive** alternatives that provide comparable services and have consistent cash-flow and risk assumptions, compare their NPVs and generally prefer the higher positive value. Projects with unequal lives, differing risk, constrained capital, or different timing may need additional analysis. A high NPV does not guarantee project success.

## 5. Discount-rate sensitivity

The chosen rate reflects the opportunity cost of capital and the risk appropriate to the cash flows. For a conventional project with an initial outflow and positive future receipts, increasing the discount rate reduces NPV. With later negative cash flows, this monotonic behavior need not hold.

A useful sensitivity study evaluates the same cash-flow forecast at multiple plausible discount rates and documents how the accept/reject conclusion changes. NPV at one rate must not be confused with an internal rate of return.

## 6. Profitability index and its limitations

For a conventional project with one initial outflow and only nonnegative subsequent receipts, a simple profitability index is:

**PI = PV(future inflows)/initial investment.**

Then PI > 1 corresponds to positive NPV. This ratio can assist capital-rationing discussions but may rank projects differently from NPV because it measures value per unit of initial investment rather than total value created. The reference implementation rejects later outflows in this simplified PI function.

## 7. Practical modeling controls

Use consistent cash-flow units and discount periods. Model incremental after-tax cash flows where appropriate, include terminal or disposal cash flows when justified, and avoid double-counting financing costs when the discount rate already reflects capital costs. Keep signed cash flows explicit. Validate input completeness and reject nonfinite amounts. A constant-rate model does not replace a term structure, scenario forecasts, or an audit trail of assumptions.

```python
"""NPV of periodic cash flows, with scenario analysis."""
import math
import unittest

def npv(rate, cash_flows):
    if isinstance(rate, bool) or not isinstance(rate,(int,float)) or not math.isfinite(rate) or rate <= -1:
        raise ValueError("rate must be finite and greater than -1")
    flows=list(cash_flows)
    if any(isinstance(x,bool) or not isinstance(x,(int,float)) or not math.isfinite(x) for x in flows):
        raise ValueError("cash flows must be finite numbers")
    try:
        result=math.fsum(float(x)*math.exp(-t*math.log1p(rate))
                         for t,x in enumerate(flows))
    except OverflowError as exc:
        raise ValueError("valuation overflow") from exc
    if not math.isfinite(result):
        raise ValueError("valuation overflow")
    return result

def discounted_components(rate, cash_flows):
    if not isinstance(cash_flows,(list,tuple)):
        cash_flows=list(cash_flows)
    npv(rate,cash_flows)
    return [(t,float(x)/(1+rate)**t) for t,x in enumerate(cash_flows)]

def profitability_index(rate, cash_flows):
    flows=list(cash_flows)
    if not flows or flows[0]>=0:
        raise ValueError("initial investment must be negative")
    if any(x<0 for x in flows[1:]):
        raise ValueError("simple PI requires no subsequent outflows")
    return npv(rate,[0]+flows[1:])/-flows[0]

class Tests(unittest.TestCase):
    def test_01_positive(self):
        self.assertAlmostEqual(npv(.1,[-100,60,60]),-100+60/1.1+60/1.1**2)
    def test_02_negative(self):
        self.assertLess(npv(.1,[-100,40,40]),0)
    def test_03_zero(self):
        self.assertAlmostEqual(npv(.1,[-100,110]),0)
    def test_04_initial_not_discounted(self):
        self.assertEqual(npv(.2,[-500]),-500)
    def test_05_zero_rate(self):
        self.assertEqual(npv(0,[-100,50,75]),25)
    def test_06_negative_rate(self):
        self.assertAlmostEqual(npv(-.1,[-100,90]),0)
    def test_07_empty(self):
        self.assertEqual(npv(.1,[]),0)
    def test_08_components(self):
        flows=[-100,60,60]
        self.assertAlmostEqual(sum(v for _,v in discounted_components(.1,flows)),npv(.1,flows))
    def test_09_rate_sensitivity(self):
        flows=[-100,60,60]
        self.assertGreater(npv(.05,flows),npv(.15,flows))
    def test_10_profitability_index(self):
        self.assertAlmostEqual(profitability_index(.1,[-100,60,60]),
                               (60/1.1+60/1.1**2)/100)
    def test_11_nonconventional(self):
        self.assertAlmostEqual(npv(.1,[-100,230,-132]),
                               -100+230/1.1-132/1.1**2)
    def test_12_invalid(self):
        with self.assertRaises(ValueError): npv(-1,[-100,110])
        with self.assertRaises(ValueError): npv(.1,[-100,float("nan")])
        with self.assertRaises(ValueError): profitability_index(.1,[-100,50,-20])
        with self.assertRaises(ValueError): profitability_index(.1,[100,20])

if __name__=="__main__":
    print("Example NPV:",round(npv(.1,[-100,60,60]),2))
    unittest.main(verbosity=2)
```

## 9. Exercises and worked answers

1. **Definition:** What does NPV measure? **Answer:** The total value at one date of all signed project cash flows.
2. **Initial cost:** How is a $100 investment today recorded? **Answer:** C0 = -100.
3. **PV:** What is the present value of $110 received after one year at 10%? **Answer:** $100.
4. **Break-even NPV:** What is NPV of -$100 today and +$110 after one year at 10%? **Answer:** $0.
5. **Two receipts:** Find NPV of -$100, +$60, +$60 at 10%. **Answer:** -100 + 60/1.1 + 60/1.1², approximately +$4.13.
6. **Negative NPV:** Find NPV of -$100, +$40, +$40 at 10%. **Answer:** Approximately -$30.58.
7. **Zero discount:** Find NPV of -$100, +$50, +$75 at 0%. **Answer:** +$25.
8. **Independent project:** What does a positive NPV generally indicate? **Answer:** The project exceeds the modeled required return, subject to other constraints.
9. **Mutually exclusive projects:** Which is preferred under comparable assumptions? **Answer:** The one with the higher NPV, if positive.
10. **Rate sensitivity:** For an initial outflow followed only by inflows, what usually happens when r increases? **Answer:** NPV decreases.
11. **Profitability index:** What does PI > 1 imply under the simplified conventional definition? **Answer:** Positive NPV.
12. **Irregular dates:** Should a fixed-period NPV function be used directly for arbitrary calendar dates? **Answer:** No; use a documented dated discounting convention.
