## Learning objectives

Define covariance and Pearson correlation; compute population and sample statistics; interpret positive, negative, and zero linear association; apply covariance to portfolio variance; understand independence and dependence; and implement a reproducible Python risk-analysis lab.

## 1. Why co-movement matters

An investment portfolio contains multiple uncertain returns. Individual asset volatility does not determine total portfolio volatility: how returns move **together** matters.

Covariance measures the direction and scale of linear co-movement. Correlation standardizes covariance into a unitless measure between −1 and +1.

These concepts also apply to correlated credit defaults, interest-rate exposures, claims, and operational failures.

## 2. Population covariance

For random variables X and Y with finite second moments:

**Cov(X,Y)=E[(X−E[X])(Y−E[Y])].**

An equivalent identity is:

**Cov(X,Y)=E[XY]−E[X]E[Y].**

Positive covariance means above-average values tend to coincide with above-average values, and below-average values with below-average values, in the linear-average sense. Negative covariance means they tend to move oppositely.

Covariance is sensitive to scale and has the product of the variables' units.

## 3. Worked example — Two related returns

Suppose three equally likely paired scenarios produce:

| Scenario | X | Y |
|---|---:|---:|
| Bear | 1 | 2 |
| Base | 2 | 4 |
| Bull | 3 | 6 |

**E[X]=2** and **E[Y]=4**.

Compute products of deviations:

| X−2 | Y−4 | Product |
|---:|---:|---:|
| −1 | −2 | 2 |
| 0 | 0 | 0 |
| 1 | 2 | 2 |

**Cov(X,Y)=(2+0+2)/3=4/3.**

Because Y=2X exactly, the Pearson correlation is +1.

## 4. Pearson correlation

If both standard deviations are strictly positive:

**ρ_XY=Cov(X,Y)/(σ_Xσ_Y).**

The Cauchy–Schwarz inequality guarantees **−1≤ρ≤1**.

Interpretation:

- **+1:** perfect positive linear relationship.
- **0:** no linear correlation; nonlinear dependence may remain.
- **−1:** perfect negative linear relationship.

Correlation is undefined if either variable has zero variance.

### Worked example A — Zero correlation without independence

Let X be equally likely to equal −1, 0, or 1, and let Y=X².

Then E[X]=0, E[XY]=E[X³]=0, so **Cov(X,Y)=0**.

Yet Y is determined by X. Therefore zero correlation does **not** imply independence.

## 5. Independence and covariance

If X and Y are independent and have finite second moments:

**E[XY]=E[X]E[Y]**, so **Cov(X,Y)=0**.

The reverse implication does not generally hold.

In financial data, dependence can appear during market stress even when a historical Pearson correlation estimate is small.

## 6. Covariance properties

For constants a,b,c,d:

**Cov(aX+b,cY+d)=ac Cov(X,Y).**

Shifting either variable does not change covariance. Scaling changes covariance by the product of the scale factors.

**Cov(X,Y)=Cov(Y,X)**.

**Cov(X,X)=Var(X)**.

The variance of a sum is:

**Var(X+Y)=Var(X)+Var(Y)+2Cov(X,Y).**

The variance of a difference is:

**Var(X−Y)=Var(X)+Var(Y)−2Cov(X,Y).**

## 7. Portfolio variance

For two asset returns R₁ and R₂ and fixed weights w₁,w₂:

**R_p=w₁R₁+w₂R₂.**

Then:

**Var(R_p)=w₁²σ₁²+w₂²σ₂²+2w₁w₂Cov(R₁,R₂).**

Using correlation:

**Var(R_p)=w₁²σ₁²+w₂²σ₂²+2w₁w₂ρσ₁σ₂.**

### Worked example B — Two-asset portfolio

Assume:

- Weight 1 = 60%, weight 2 = 40%.
- Variance 1 = .04; variance 2 = .09.
- Covariance = .012, corresponding to σ₁=.2, σ₂=.3, ρ=.2.

Then:

**Var(R_p)=(.6²)(.04)+(.4²)(.09)+2(.6)(.4)(.012)**

**=.0144+.0144+.00576=.03456.**

**σ_p=√.03456≈.18590=18.59%.**

The return units here are decimal fractions, so .18590 corresponds to 18.59 percentage points of standard deviation.

## 8. Diversification and correlation

For long-only positive portfolio weights and fixed individual volatilities, lower correlation reduces the covariance contribution to portfolio variance.

Diversification does not eliminate all risk. Market-wide shocks, liquidity constraints, concentration, and tail dependence can still create significant losses.

Correlation estimates can change through time and often behave differently during crises.

## 9. Population versus sample covariance

For N paired observations that constitute the full population:

**Cov_pop(X,Y)=(1/N)Σᵢ(xᵢ−μ_x)(yᵢ−μ_y).**

For n paired sample observations, the conventional unbiased estimator under iid sampling and finite moments is:

**s_xy=(1/(n−1))Σᵢ(xᵢ−x̄)(yᵢ−ȳ)**, n≥2.

For paired values X=[1,2,3], Y=[2,4,6]:

- Population covariance = 4/3.
- Sample covariance = 2.
- Sample and population Pearson correlations both equal +1.

Never combine unaligned timestamps or mismatched sample windows.

## 10. Correlation matrices and feasibility

For several assets, a covariance matrix must be symmetric and positive semidefinite.

Each diagonal entry is a variance. Each off-diagonal entry is a covariance.

A proposed two-asset covariance must satisfy:

**|Cov(X,Y)|≤σ_Xσ_Y.**

For σ₁=.2 and σ₂=.3, the absolute covariance cannot exceed .06. A proposed covariance of .08 is infeasible.

A pairwise-valid set of correlations is not necessarily a valid positive-semidefinite multi-asset matrix. Larger portfolio models should validate matrix feasibility.

## 11. Operational and modeling risks

- Missing or stale market observations distort correlations.
- Non-synchronous prices can create misleading estimates.
- Short historical windows increase estimation uncertainty.
- Structural breaks change relationships.
- Outliers can strongly influence Pearson correlation.
- Zero correlation does not establish independence.
- Tail dependence and nonlinear co-movement require additional analysis.
- Mixing return horizons or currencies produces invalid comparisons.

The Python standard-library implementation calculates population and sample covariance, Pearson correlation, and two-asset portfolio variance with input checks. It includes **12 reference unit tests**. Independent runtime verification and editorial review remain pending.

```python
from math import fsum, isfinite, sqrt
import unittest

def _numbers(values):
    out=list(values)
    if not out or any(isinstance(x,bool) or not isinstance(x,(int,float)) or not isfinite(x) for x in out):
        raise ValueError("finite nonempty numeric series required")
    return out

def paired(x,y,min_count=1):
    a,b=_numbers(x),_numbers(y)
    if len(a)!=len(b) or len(a)<min_count: raise ValueError("paired lengths/count invalid")
    return a,b

def mean(x):
    v=_numbers(x)
    return fsum(v)/len(v)

def population_covariance(x,y):
    a,b=paired(x,y)
    ma,mb=mean(a),mean(b)
    return fsum((u-ma)*(v-mb) for u,v in zip(a,b))/len(a)

def sample_covariance(x,y):
    a,b=paired(x,y,2)
    ma,mb=mean(a),mean(b)
    return fsum((u-ma)*(v-mb) for u,v in zip(a,b))/(len(a)-1)

def population_variance(x):
    return population_covariance(x,x)

def correlation(x,y):
    a,b=paired(x,y,2)
    vx,vy=population_variance(a),population_variance(b)
    if vx<=0 or vy<=0: raise ValueError("correlation undefined for zero variance")
    r=population_covariance(a,b)/sqrt(vx*vy)
    return max(-1.0,min(1.0,r))

def portfolio_variance(weights,variances,covariance):
    if len(weights)!=2 or len(variances)!=2: raise ValueError("two assets required")
    if any(isinstance(z,bool) or not isinstance(z,(int,float)) or not isfinite(z) for z in [*weights,*variances,covariance]):
        raise ValueError("finite inputs required")
    if any(v<0 for v in variances): raise ValueError("negative variance")
    if abs(covariance)>sqrt(variances[0]*variances[1])+1e-12:
        raise ValueError("infeasible covariance")
    w1,w2=weights
    v1,v2=variances
    return w1*w1*v1+w2*w2*v2+2*w1*w2*covariance

class CovarianceTests(unittest.TestCase):
    def setUp(self):
        self.x=[1,2,3]
        self.y=[2,4,6]
    def test_01_mean(self): self.assertEqual(mean(self.x),2)
    def test_02_population_covariance(self): self.assertAlmostEqual(population_covariance(self.x,self.y),4/3)
    def test_03_sample_covariance(self): self.assertAlmostEqual(sample_covariance(self.x,self.y),2)
    def test_04_positive_correlation(self): self.assertAlmostEqual(correlation(self.x,self.y),1)
    def test_05_negative_correlation(self): self.assertAlmostEqual(correlation(self.x,[-2,-4,-6]),-1)
    def test_06_zero_covariance(self): self.assertAlmostEqual(population_covariance([-1,0,1],[1,0,1]),0)
    def test_07_symmetry(self): self.assertAlmostEqual(population_covariance(self.x,self.y),population_covariance(self.y,self.x))
    def test_08_self_covariance(self): self.assertAlmostEqual(population_covariance(self.x,self.x),2/3)
    def test_09_portfolio(self): self.assertAlmostEqual(portfolio_variance([.6,.4],[.04,.09],.012),.03456)
    def test_10_independent_formula(self): self.assertAlmostEqual(portfolio_variance([.5,.5],[.04,.09],0),.0325)
    def test_11_shift_invariance(self): self.assertAlmostEqual(correlation([x+10 for x in self.x],self.y),1)
    def test_12_invalid(self):
        for fn in (lambda:correlation([1,1,1],[1,2,3]),
                   lambda:sample_covariance([1],[2]),
                   lambda:population_covariance([1,2],[1]),
                   lambda:portfolio_variance([.5,.5],[.04,.09],.5)):
            with self.assertRaises(ValueError): fn()

if __name__=="__main__":
    result=unittest.TextTestRunner(verbosity=2).run(
        unittest.defaultTestLoader.loadTestsFromTestCase(CovarianceTests))
    if not result.wasSuccessful(): raise SystemExit(1)
```

## 13. Twenty-four worked exercises

1. **Positive covariance:** X=[1,2,3], Y=[2,4,6]. **Solution:** Population covariance=4/3.

2. **Sample covariance:** Same paired observations. **Solution:** Sample covariance=2.

3. **Positive correlation:** Y=2X, nonconstant X. **Solution:** ρ=+1.

4. **Negative correlation:** Y=−2X. **Solution:** ρ=−1.

5. **Self-covariance:** X=[1,2,3]. **Solution:** Cov_pop(X,X)=2/3.

6. **Covariance symmetry:** Cov(X,Y)=.03. **Solution:** Cov(Y,X)=.03.

7. **Zero covariance example:** X=[−1,0,1],Y=[1,0,1]. **Solution:** Cov_pop(X,Y)=0.

8. **Zero is not independence:** Y=X² for symmetric X taking −1,0,1. **Solution:** Cov(X,Y)=0, yet Y depends on X.

9. **Units:** X in dollars, Y in percent. **Solution:** Covariance units are dollar-percent; correlation is unitless.

10. **Covariance of sum:** Var(X)=4,Var(Y)=9,Cov(X,Y)=2. **Solution:** Var(X+Y)=17.

11. **Covariance of difference:** Same inputs. **Solution:** Var(X−Y)=9.

12. **Portfolio independent case:** Weights .5,.5; variances .04,.09; covariance 0. **Solution:** Portfolio variance=.0325.

13. **Portfolio correlated case:** Weights .6,.4; variances .04,.09; covariance .012. **Solution:** Portfolio variance=.03456.

14. **Portfolio volatility:** Variance=.03456. **Solution:** Volatility≈.185903=18.59%.

15. **Correlation to covariance:** σ₁=.2, σ₂=.3, ρ=.2. **Solution:** Cov=.012.

16. **Perfect positive dependence:** σ₁=.2, σ₂=.3, ρ=1. **Solution:** Cov=.06.

17. **Perfect negative dependence:** σ₁=.2, σ₂=.3, ρ=−1. **Solution:** Cov=−.06.

18. **Bound check:** σ₁=.2,σ₂=.3,Cov=.08. **Solution:** Invalid: |Cov| exceeds .06.

19. **Scaling covariance:** Cov(X,Y)=2. **Solution:** Cov(3X,−2Y)=−12.

20. **Shift invariance:** Cov(X,Y)=2. **Solution:** Cov(X+10,Y−4)=2.

21. **Pearson definition:** Cov=.012,σ₁=.2,σ₂=.3. **Solution:** ρ=.012/(.2×.3)=.2.

22. **Diversification:** Correlation drops while volatilities and weights stay fixed positive. **Solution:** Two-asset portfolio variance generally falls.

23. **Sample pairing:** One asset has missing dates. **Solution:** Align timestamps before computing covariance.

24. **Tail dependence:** Two assets have modest historical Pearson correlation. **Solution:** Joint crash risk may still be significant.

## 15. Troubleshooting and operational controls

| Symptom | Likely cause | Action |
|---|---|---|
| Correlation exceeds one | Incorrect normalization or numeric error | Recheck standard deviations and formulas |
| Correlation is undefined | Constant series | Reject or separately handle zero variance |
| Covariance changes when converting units | Scale sensitivity | Prefer correlation for unitless comparison |
| Portfolio variance is unexpectedly small | Missing covariance cross term | Apply complete portfolio formula |
| Correlation collapses in production | Data alignment or regime change | Inspect timestamps and rolling estimates |
| Risk rises during market stress | Correlation and tail dependence shift | Stress test and diversify sources of risk |
| Covariance matrix is invalid | Not positive semidefinite | Validate eigenvalues or PSD constraints |
