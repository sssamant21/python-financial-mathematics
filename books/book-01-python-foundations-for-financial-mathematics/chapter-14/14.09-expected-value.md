## Learning objectives

Compute expected values from discrete distributions and continuous densities; apply linearity of expectation; evaluate transformed payoffs; calculate expected credit losses and discounted expected cash flows; distinguish expectation from realized outcomes and risk; and validate calculations with Python.

## 1. Why expected value matters in finance

A future financial payoff is uncertain. **Expected value** summarizes its probability-weighted center. It supports credit loss estimates, scenario analysis, insurance pricing, investment decision analysis, and portfolio return forecasts.

An expected value is not a promise, a median, or necessarily an outcome that can actually occur. It also does not capture the full risk of the distribution.

## 2. Definition for discrete random variables

For a discrete random variable X with probability mass function p_X:

**E[X]=Σ_x x p_X(x)**,

provided the absolute-weighted sum is finite, or the expectation is otherwise well-defined in the extended sense.

### Worked example A — Investment payoff

| Scenario | Probability | Net payoff |
|---|---:|---:|
| Bear | 25% | −$20 |
| Base | 50% | +$5 |
| Bull | 25% | +$30 |

**E[X]=.25(−20)+.50(5)+.25(30)=−5+2.5+7.5=$5.**

No scenario produces exactly $5 by necessity; it is a weighted average of uncertain outcomes.

## 3. Expected value for continuous random variables

For a random variable with density f_X and an integrable payoff:

**E[X]=∫₋∞^∞ x f_X(x) dx.**

For an exponential waiting-time variable with rate λ>0:

**E[X]=1/λ.**

If λ=.5 per hour, the expected waiting time is 2 hours.

The continuous integral and discrete weighted sum represent the same underlying idea: averaging numerical outcomes according to their probability distribution.

## 4. Expected value of a transformed payoff

For a measurable transformation g and an integrable g(X):

**E[g(X)]=Σ_x g(x)p_X(x)** for discrete X,

or **E[g(X)]=∫g(x)f_X(x)dx** when X has a density.

This is sometimes called the law of the unconscious statistician.

### Worked example B — Nonlinear payoff

For the three-state investment:

**E[X²]=.25(400)+.50(25)+.25(900)=337.5.**

But:

**(E[X])²=25.**

Therefore **E[X²]≠(E[X])²** in general. This distinction becomes central to variance calculations.

## 5. Linearity of expectation

For integrable random variables X and Y and constants a,b:

**E[aX+bY]=aE[X]+bE[Y].**

**Independence is not required.**

If two investment components have expected payoffs of $4 and $7, the expected combined payoff is $11, even if their outcomes are correlated.

### Worked example C — Portfolio expected return

For portfolio weights w₁=.6 and w₂=.4 and expected asset returns of 5% and 10%:

**E[R_p]=.6(.05)+.4(.10)=.07=7%.**

This is an expected one-period return under the specified weights and return definition, not a guaranteed portfolio performance figure.

## 6. Expected value of indicators

For an event A, define indicator I_A=1 if A occurs and zero otherwise.

Then:

**E[I_A]=P(A).**

If a default event has probability 2%, the expected default indicator is .02.

Indicators are useful for modeling counts and converting event probabilities into expected financial losses.

## 7. Expected credit loss

Under a simplified fixed-exposure, fixed-loss-given-default model:

**Expected loss = EAD × LGD × PD.**

Where:

- **EAD:** exposure at default, expressed in currency.
- **LGD:** loss given default as a fraction.
- **PD:** probability of default over a specified horizon.

For EAD=$10,000, LGD=40%, and PD=2%:

**EL=10,000×.40×.02=$80.**

In real portfolios, EAD, LGD, and default may be random and dependent. In that case the exact expectation is **E[EAD×LGD×I_default]**; multiplying separate averages without justified assumptions can be incorrect.

## 8. Expected insurance claim

Suppose a claim pays $1,000 with probability 5% and $0 otherwise.

**E[claim]=.05($1,000)+.95($0)=$50.**

A premium of $50 is a simplified actuarially fair expected-claim amount before administration, capital, profit, taxes, and other pricing considerations. It is not necessarily a commercially sufficient premium.

## 9. Discounted expected cash flows

For a random cash flow C received in n periods and a **deterministic** discount rate r>−1:

**E[PV(C)]=E[C]/(1+r)^n.**

For a guaranteed $110 cash flow one year from now and r=10%:

**PV=$110/1.10=$100.**

If discount factors are stochastic and correlated with cash flows, **E[DC]** cannot generally be replaced by **E[D]E[C]**.

In asset pricing, the appropriate discounting method depends on risk, cash-flow characteristics, and the valuation framework. A simple expected-cash-flow discount at an arbitrary rate is not automatically a market-consistent price.

## 10. Conditional expected value

Conditional expectation incorporates new information. For a discrete random variable:

**E[X|B]=Σ_x x P(X=x|B)** for P(B)>0.

Suppose a $100 payoff occurs with conditional probability 40% given a recession signal and $0 otherwise:

**E[X|signal]=.40($100)+.60($0)=$40.**

Conditional expectations can be aggregated using the law of total expectation:

**E[X]=Σ_i E[X|B_i]P(B_i)**,

where the events B_i form a partition and integrability conditions hold.

## 11. Expectation and decision risk

Two investments can have equal expected returns but very different probabilities of large losses. A high expected payoff does not automatically mean an investment is suitable.

Expected value should be paired with downside probabilities, dispersion, liquidity needs, capital constraints, and the decision-maker's risk preferences.

For a concave utility function u, Jensen's inequality implies **E[u(X)]≤u(E[X])**, when the expectations exist. This helps explain why a risk-averse investor may prefer a less variable payoff even at the same expected value.

## 12. Common numerical and modeling issues

- Probability weights must be nonnegative and sum to one.
- Payoff units must be consistent; do not mix percentages and dollars without conversion.
- Distinguish gross cash flows from net payoffs.
- Use a common observation horizon for scenarios.
- Do not assume independence to calculate E[X+Y]; it is unnecessary.
- Do not assume E[XY]=E[X]E[Y] without a valid independence or other sufficient condition.
- Validate expected losses against observed outcomes over an appropriate calibration horizon.
- Report the underlying scenario assumptions and uncertainties.

```python
from math import fsum, isfinite
import unittest

def validate(pmf):
    if not pmf: raise ValueError("empty PMF")
    if any(isinstance(x,bool) or not isinstance(x,(int,float)) or not isfinite(x) for x in pmf):
        raise ValueError("invalid outcome")
    if any(isinstance(p,bool) or not isinstance(p,(int,float)) or not isfinite(p) or not 0<=p<=1 for p in pmf.values()):
        raise ValueError("invalid probability")
    if abs(fsum(pmf.values())-1)>1e-12:
        raise ValueError("probabilities must sum to one")
    return dict(pmf)

def expected(pmf):
    d=validate(pmf)
    return fsum(x*p for x,p in d.items())

def transformed_expected(pmf,transform):
    d=validate(pmf)
    values=[transform(x) for x in d]
    if any(isinstance(y,bool) or not isinstance(y,(int,float)) or not isfinite(y) for y in values):
        raise ValueError("invalid transformed payoff")
    return fsum(y*p for y,p in zip(values,d.values()))

def expected_loss(exposure,lgd,pd):
    for v in (exposure,lgd,pd):
        if isinstance(v,bool) or not isinstance(v,(int,float)) or not isfinite(v):
            raise ValueError("inputs must be finite")
    if exposure<0 or not 0<=lgd<=1 or not 0<=pd<=1:
        raise ValueError("invalid loss inputs")
    return exposure*lgd*pd

def discounted_expected(pmf,rate,periods):
    if isinstance(rate,bool) or not isinstance(rate,(int,float)) or not isfinite(rate) or rate<=-1:
        raise ValueError("invalid discount rate")
    if isinstance(periods,bool) or not isinstance(periods,int) or periods<0:
        raise ValueError("invalid periods")
    return expected(pmf)/(1+rate)**periods

def scenario_expected(probs,payoffs):
    if len(probs)!=len(payoffs) or not probs:
        raise ValueError("scenario lengths differ or empty")
    return expected({i:p for i,p in enumerate(probs)}) if False else _scenario_sum(probs,payoffs)

def _scenario_sum(probs,payoffs):
    if any(isinstance(p,bool) or not isinstance(p,(int,float)) or not isfinite(p) or not 0<=p<=1 for p in probs):
        raise ValueError("invalid scenario probability")
    if any(isinstance(x,bool) or not isinstance(x,(int,float)) or not isfinite(x) for x in payoffs):
        raise ValueError("invalid scenario payoff")
    if abs(fsum(probs)-1)>1e-12: raise ValueError("probabilities must sum to one")
    return fsum(p*x for p,x in zip(probs,payoffs))

class ExpectedValueTests(unittest.TestCase):
    def setUp(self): self.d={-20:.25,5:.5,30:.25}
    def test_01_payoff(self): self.assertAlmostEqual(expected(self.d),5)
    def test_02_constant(self): self.assertAlmostEqual(expected({7:1}),7)
    def test_03_bernoulli(self): self.assertAlmostEqual(expected({0:.98,1:.02}),.02)
    def test_04_linear(self): self.assertAlmostEqual(transformed_expected(self.d,lambda x:2*x+1),11)
    def test_05_square(self): self.assertAlmostEqual(transformed_expected(self.d,lambda x:x*x),337.5)
    def test_06_expected_loss(self): self.assertAlmostEqual(expected_loss(10000,.4,.02),80)
    def test_07_zero_default(self): self.assertEqual(expected_loss(10000,.4,0),0)
    def test_08_discount(self): self.assertAlmostEqual(discounted_expected({110:1},.1,1),100)
    def test_09_scenarios(self): self.assertAlmostEqual(scenario_expected([.25,.5,.25],[-20,5,30]),5)
    def test_10_negative_expected(self): self.assertAlmostEqual(expected({-10:.8,10:.2}),-6)
    def test_11_invariance(self): self.assertAlmostEqual(transformed_expected(self.d,lambda x:x),expected(self.d))
    def test_12_invalid(self):
        for fn in (lambda:expected({0:.4,1:.4}),
                   lambda:expected_loss(100,-.1,.2),
                   lambda:discounted_expected(self.d,-1,1),
                   lambda:scenario_expected([.5,.5],[1]),
                   lambda:transformed_expected(self.d,lambda x:float("nan"))):
            with self.assertRaises(ValueError): fn()

if __name__=="__main__":
    result=unittest.TextTestRunner(verbosity=2).run(
        unittest.defaultTestLoader.loadTestsFromTestCase(ExpectedValueTests))
    if not result.wasSuccessful(): raise SystemExit(1)
```

## 14. Twenty-four worked exercises

1. **Weighted payoff:** Payoffs −20,5,30; probabilities .25,.5,.25. **Solution:** E[X]=5.

2. **Certain payoff:** X=7 with probability 1. **Solution:** E[X]=7.

3. **Bernoulli expectation:** Default indicator with p=.02. **Solution:** E[D]=.02.

4. **Expected credit loss:** Exposure $10,000, LGD 40%, PD 2%. **Solution:** EL=$80.

5. **Loss-free loan:** PD=0. **Solution:** EL=0.

6. **Full loss:** Exposure $500, LGD=1, PD=1. **Solution:** EL=$500.

7. **Negative expectation:** −$10 with .8, +$10 with .2. **Solution:** E[X]=−$6.

8. **Linear scaling:** E[X]=5, Y=2X+1. **Solution:** E[Y]=11.

9. **Shift:** E[X]=5, Y=X−3. **Solution:** E[Y]=2.

10. **Squared payoff:** X=−20,5,30 with .25,.5,.25. **Solution:** E[X²]=337.5.

11. **Nonlinear warning:** E[X]=5, E[X²]=337.5. **Solution:** E[X²]≠(E[X])²=25.

12. **Discounting:** Guaranteed $110 in one year, rate 10%. **Solution:** PV=$100.

13. **Expected PV:** Expected cash flow $110 in one year, deterministic rate 10%. **Solution:** Discounted expected amount=$100 under stated assumptions.

14. **Insurance payout:** $1,000 claim with probability .05, otherwise $0. **Solution:** Expected claim=$50.

15. **Fair premium baseline:** Expected claim $50. **Solution:** Actuarially fair premium before expenses and risk loading=$50.

16. **Scenario weighted:** Bear −$100 with .2, base $20 with .5, bull $80 with .3. **Solution:** E[X]=−20+10+24=$14.

17. **Expected return:** Returns −10% and 20% with equal probabilities. **Solution:** E[R]=5%.

18. **Sum of expectations:** E[X]=4, E[Y]=7. **Solution:** E[X+Y]=11 even without independence.

19. **Portfolio expectation:** Weights .6 and .4; expected returns 5% and 10%. **Solution:** Expected portfolio return=7%.

20. **Indicator identity:** P(A)=.3. **Solution:** E[1_A]=.3.

21. **Conditional expectation:** Payoff $0 or $100; conditional success probability .4. **Solution:** E[X|condition]=$40.

22. **Jensen illustration:** X=0 or 2 equally likely. **Solution:** E[X²]=2, while (E[X])²=1.

23. **Expectation versus risk:** Two investments have the same expected payoff. **Solution:** They may have very different downside distributions.

24. **Model validation:** Scenario probabilities do not sum to 1. **Solution:** Do not calculate an authoritative expected value until the inputs are corrected.

## 16. Troubleshooting and operational runbook

| Symptom | Likely cause | Action |
|---|---|---|
| Expected payoff differs from manual total | Incorrect scenario weights | Reconcile weights and payoffs |
| Expected loss unexpectedly high | PD or LGD entered as whole percentage | Normalize percentage conventions |
| Expected present value inconsistent | Wrong horizon or discount rate | Confirm rate compounding and timing |
| Portfolio expectation calculation blocked by correlation | Misunderstood linearity | Independence is not needed for E[X+Y] |
| Expected product is miscalculated | Assumed E[XY]=E[X]E[Y] | Model joint dependence |
| Backtest drift | Scenario probabilities no longer calibrated | Refresh estimates and review model |
| Outputs mix currencies | Missing unit controls | Normalize and label currency |
