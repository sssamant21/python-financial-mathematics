## 1. What is a loan?

A loan transfers funds from a lender to a borrower today in exchange for promised payments later. In this chapter, the **principal** is the amount initially advanced, the **periodic interest rate** measures the contractual charge per payment period, and the **term** is the number of scheduled payment periods. The payment amount is determined by the loan structure.

For a borrower, receiving the initial principal is a positive cash flow, while scheduled repayments are negative. From the lender's perspective, signs are reversed.

```text
Borrower timeline, level-payment example

Period          0          1          2         ...         n
                |----------|----------|--------------------|
Cash flow      +L         -A         -A                   -A
```

Here L is the loan principal and A is the constant end-of-period payment.

## 2. Basic loan assumptions

The initial model assumes a fully amortizing loan with:

- a positive principal L;
- a constant, nonnegative effective periodic rate i;
- n positive integer payment periods;
- equal payments A at the **end** of each period;
- no fees, taxes, balloon payment, missed payments, or prepayment;
- exact arithmetic for the derivation, with floating-point approximation in the lab.

Real loans may include compounding conventions, payment holidays, variable rates, escrow, fees, and contract-specific rounding. Those features must be modeled separately.

## 3. Present-value identity

At origination, the lender advances L and receives a stream of n payments A. Using the ordinary-annuity formula from Chapter 10:

**L = A × [1 - (1+i)^(-n)] / i**, for i > 0.

Rearranging:

**A = L × i / [1 - (1+i)^(-n)].**

When i = 0, no interest accrues and the limiting formula is:

**A = L/n.**

This formula is for end-of-period level payments. An annuity-due structure requires different timing.

## 4. Worked example

A borrower takes a $10,000 loan with a 0.5% effective monthly rate and 24 monthly end-of-period payments.

**A = 10000 × 0.005 / [1 - (1.005)^(-24)]**, approximately **$443.21 per month**.

The rate is monthly; it must not be treated as an annual rate. The first month's interest is $10,000 × 0.005 = $50. The first payment covers that interest, and the remainder reduces principal.

## 5. Interest and principal components

Let B(k-1) be the outstanding balance immediately after payment k-1. At the end of period k:

**Interest(k) = i × B(k-1).**

**Principal repaid(k) = A - Interest(k).**

**B(k) = B(k-1) + Interest(k) - A.**

With B(0) = L, this recurrence tracks the remaining obligation. For a standard positive-rate fully amortizing loan, principal repayment generally grows over time while interest decreases.

## 6. Outstanding balance

Immediately after k scheduled payments, the balance is the present value at time k of the remaining n-k payments:

**B(k) = A × [1 - (1+i)^(-(n-k))] / i**, for i > 0.

For i = 0, **B(k) = L - kA**. At k = n, the fully amortized balance is zero in exact arithmetic. The prospective formula is often numerically convenient because it avoids subtracting two large accumulated quantities.

## 7. Financial interpretation

A payment includes both a financing cost (interest) and a return of borrowed capital (principal). These components are not interchangeable. A borrower may care about affordability and total interest paid; a lender may evaluate present value, credit risk, and cash-flow timing. A nominal quoted rate, APR, and effective periodic rate can differ, so always identify the rate convention.

```python
"""Loan mathematics: payment, outstanding balance and independent tests."""
import math
import unittest

def validate(principal, rate, periods):
    if isinstance(principal, bool) or not isinstance(principal,(int,float)) or not math.isfinite(principal) or principal <= 0:
        raise ValueError("principal must be finite and positive")
    if isinstance(rate, bool) or not isinstance(rate,(int,float)) or not math.isfinite(rate) or rate < 0:
        raise ValueError("periodic rate must be finite and nonnegative")
    if isinstance(periods, bool) or not isinstance(periods,int) or periods <= 0:
        raise ValueError("periods must be a positive integer")

def payment(principal, rate, periods):
    validate(principal,rate,periods)
    if rate == 0:
        return principal/periods
    q = -math.expm1(-periods*math.log1p(rate))
    result = principal*rate/q
    if not math.isfinite(result):
        raise ValueError("payment overflow")
    return result

def balance(principal, rate, periods, completed):
    validate(principal,rate,periods)
    if isinstance(completed,bool) or not isinstance(completed,int) or not 0 <= completed <= periods:
        raise ValueError("completed must be an integer between 0 and periods")
    if completed == periods:
        return 0.0
    p = payment(principal,rate,periods)
    if rate == 0:
        return principal-p*completed
    # Prospective method: PV of remaining payments.
    return p*(-math.expm1(-(periods-completed)*math.log1p(rate)))/rate

def first_period(principal, rate, periods):
    p=payment(principal,rate,periods)
    interest=principal*rate
    return {"payment":p,"interest":interest,"principal":p-interest,
            "balance":principal-(p-interest)}

class Tests(unittest.TestCase):
    def test_01_zero_rate_payment(self):
        self.assertEqual(payment(1200,0,12),100)
    def test_02_one_period(self):
        self.assertAlmostEqual(payment(100,.1,1),110)
    def test_03_payment_formula(self):
        self.assertAlmostEqual(payment(1000,.01,12),1000*.01/(1-1.01**-12))
    def test_04_initial_balance(self):
        self.assertAlmostEqual(balance(1000,.01,12,0),1000)
    def test_05_final_balance(self):
        self.assertEqual(balance(1000,.01,12,12),0)
    def test_06_intermediate_balance(self):
        p=payment(1000,.01,12)
        self.assertAlmostEqual(balance(1000,.01,12,1),1000*1.01-p)
    def test_07_zero_rate_balance(self):
        self.assertAlmostEqual(balance(1200,0,12,3),900)
    def test_08_interest(self):
        self.assertAlmostEqual(first_period(1000,.01,12)["interest"],10)
    def test_09_components(self):
        a=first_period(1000,.01,12)
        self.assertAlmostEqual(a["payment"],a["interest"]+a["principal"])
    def test_10_positive_principal_reduction(self):
        self.assertGreater(first_period(1000,.01,12)["principal"],0)
    def test_11_bad_inputs(self):
        with self.assertRaises(ValueError): payment(0,.01,12)
        with self.assertRaises(ValueError): payment(100,-.01,12)
        with self.assertRaises(ValueError): payment(100,.01,0)
    def test_12_bad_completed(self):
        with self.assertRaises(ValueError): balance(100,.01,12,13)
        with self.assertRaises(ValueError): balance(100,.01,12,1.5)

if __name__ == "__main__":
    p=payment(10000,.005,24)
    print("Payment:",round(p,2))
    print("Balance after 12:",round(balance(10000,.005,24,12),2))
    unittest.main(verbosity=2)
```

## 9. Exercises with worked answers

1. **Borrower sign convention:** How is a $1,000 loan advance recorded? **Answer:** +$1,000 at origination.
2. **Payment sign:** How is a $100 borrower payment recorded? **Answer:** -$100 at the payment date.
3. **Rate unit:** What rate is required for monthly payment periods? **Answer:** An effective monthly rate consistent with the contract.
4. **Zero interest:** What is the payment for $1,200 over 12 periods at 0%? **Answer:** $100.
5. **One-period loan:** What repays $100 after one period at 10%? **Answer:** $110.
6. **Annuity identity:** Write the PV equation for n equal end-of-period payments. **Answer:** L = A × [1-(1+i)^(-n)]/i.
7. **Payment formula:** Solve the annuity identity for A. **Answer:** A = Li/[1-(1+i)^(-n)].
8. **First interest:** What is first-period interest on $10,000 at 0.5%? **Answer:** $50.
9. **Principal component:** If payment is $443.21 and interest is $50, what principal is repaid? **Answer:** Approximately $393.21.
10. **Balance recurrence:** Write the next-balance equation. **Answer:** New balance = old balance + interest - payment.
11. **Remaining balance:** What is the balance immediately after the final scheduled payment? **Answer:** Zero in the ideal fully amortizing model.
12. **Loan review:** Why might actual contractual totals differ from the mathematical model? **Answer:** Fees, variable rates, payment timing, and rounding conventions.
