## 1. One payment, two components

For a fully amortizing loan with level end-of-period payments, each scheduled payment consists of two components:

**Payment = Interest charged + Principal repaid.**

The interest component is the cost of borrowing for that period. The principal component reduces the outstanding debt. Although the total scheduled payment is fixed, these two components change as the outstanding balance falls.

```text
Scheduled payment A
        |
        +-- Interest I(k) = r × B(k-1)
        |
        +-- Principal P(k) = A - I(k)
                                |
                                +-- Reduces outstanding balance
```

## 2. Model and timing assumptions

Assume an original principal L > 0, a constant effective rate r ≥ 0 per payment period, n equal end-of-period payments A, and no fees, missed payments, extra payments, or rate resets. Let B(k) denote the balance **immediately after** payment k, with B(0) = L.

The rate must use the same period as the payment interval. For example, a nominal 6% annual rate compounded monthly corresponds to 0.5% per month.

## 3. Deriving interest and principal

During period k, interest accrues on the balance from the preceding payment:

**I(k) = r × B(k-1).**

Of the fixed payment A, the portion left after paying interest reduces principal:

**P(k) = A - I(k).**

The balance then becomes:

**B(k) = B(k-1) - P(k).**

Substituting the principal component gives the equivalent recurrence:

**B(k) = B(k-1)(1+r) - A.**

This recurrence is the foundation of an amortization schedule.

## 4. Worked example: first three payments

Consider a $10,000 loan at a 0.5% effective monthly rate, amortized through 24 monthly payments. The exact payment is determined by:

**A = 10000 × 0.005/[1-(1.005)^(-24)] ≈ $443.21.**

<text color="secondary" size="sm">Illustrative values below are rounded for display; the internal calculation uses the unrounded payment.</text>

| Period | Interest | Principal | Ending balance |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | $50.00 | ≈ $393.21 | ≈ $9,606.79 |
| 2 | ≈ $48.03 | ≈ $395.18 | ≈ $9,211.61 |
| 3 | ≈ $46.06 | ≈ $397.15 | ≈ $8,814.46 |

The interest portion declines because the balance declines, while the principal portion rises because the payment stays fixed.

## 5. Why the allocation changes

With positive interest and a standard amortizing payment, the principal component follows:

**P(k+1) = (1+r) × P(k).**

To see this, note that the next interest charge is lower by r times the principal repaid in the previous period:

I(k+1) = r[B(k-1)-P(k)] = I(k)-rP(k).

Therefore P(k+1) = A-I(k+1) = P(k)(1+r).

This geometric growth of the principal component explains why early payments are more interest-heavy than later payments.

## 6. Reconciliation identities

A correct schedule must satisfy all of the following under exact arithmetic:

- **Each period:** A = I(k) + P(k).
- **Balance movement:** B(k) = B(k-1) - P(k).
- **Total principal:** sum of P(k) for k = 1 through n equals L.
- **Total interest:** sum of I(k) equals nA-L.
- **Final balance:** B(n) = 0.

Use tolerances in floating-point tests. Real lender schedules may round each payment or interest charge to cents and adjust the final installment.

## 7. Special cases and practical risks

**Zero-rate loans:** Interest is zero for every period, and principal reduction equals the payment.

**Interest-only loans:** Payment may cover interest without reducing principal; this is not the fully amortizing model used here.

**Negative amortization:** If a payment is smaller than accrued interest, principal reduction is negative and the balance increases. This can occur in other loan structures but should not occur in the standard fixed-rate fully amortizing schedule modeled here.

**Extra payments:** An additional principal payment changes future interest and the remaining schedule. That extension is covered in Section 11.9.

```python
"""Loan payment decomposition with independent reconciliation tests."""
import math
import unittest

def payment(principal, rate, periods):
    if isinstance(principal,bool) or not isinstance(principal,(int,float)) or not math.isfinite(principal) or principal<=0:
        raise ValueError("principal must be finite and positive")
    if isinstance(rate,bool) or not isinstance(rate,(int,float)) or not math.isfinite(rate) or rate<0:
        raise ValueError("rate must be finite and nonnegative")
    if isinstance(periods,bool) or not isinstance(periods,int) or periods<1:
        raise ValueError("periods must be a positive integer")
    if rate==0: return principal/periods
    return principal*rate/(-math.expm1(-periods*math.log1p(rate)))

def amortization(principal,rate,periods):
    amount=payment(principal,rate,periods)
    balance=principal
    rows=[]
    for period in range(1,periods+1):
        interest=balance*rate
        principal_part=amount-interest
        balance-=principal_part
        rows.append({"period":period,"payment":amount,"interest":interest,
                     "principal":principal_part,"balance":balance})
    return rows

def summary(principal,rate,periods):
    rows=amortization(principal,rate,periods)
    return {"payment":rows[0]["payment"],
            "interest":math.fsum(x["interest"] for x in rows),
            "principal":math.fsum(x["principal"] for x in rows),
            "final_balance":rows[-1]["balance"]}

class Tests(unittest.TestCase):
    def test_01_zero_rate(self):
        self.assertEqual(payment(1200,0,12),100)
    def test_02_first_interest(self):
        self.assertAlmostEqual(amortization(1000,.01,12)[0]["interest"],10)
    def test_03_first_principal(self):
        row=amortization(1000,.01,12)[0]
        self.assertAlmostEqual(row["principal"],row["payment"]-row["interest"])
    def test_04_balance_update(self):
        row=amortization(1000,.01,12)[0]
        self.assertAlmostEqual(row["balance"],1000-row["principal"])
    def test_05_payment_reconciliation(self):
        for row in amortization(1000,.01,12):
            self.assertAlmostEqual(row["payment"],row["interest"]+row["principal"])
    def test_06_interest_declines(self):
        rows=amortization(1000,.01,12)
        self.assertLess(rows[-1]["interest"],rows[0]["interest"])
    def test_07_principal_grows(self):
        rows=amortization(1000,.01,12)
        self.assertGreater(rows[-1]["principal"],rows[0]["principal"])
    def test_08_principal_total(self):
        self.assertAlmostEqual(summary(1000,.01,12)["principal"],1000)
    def test_09_interest_total(self):
        s=summary(1000,.01,12)
        self.assertAlmostEqual(s["interest"],12*s["payment"]-1000)
    def test_10_final_balance(self):
        self.assertAlmostEqual(summary(1000,.01,12)["final_balance"],0,places=8)
    def test_11_zero_rate_components(self):
        rows=amortization(1200,0,12)
        self.assertTrue(all(x["interest"]==0 for x in rows))
        self.assertAlmostEqual(rows[-1]["balance"],0)
    def test_12_invalid_inputs(self):
        for args in ((0,.01,12),(100,-.01,12),(100,.01,0),(100,float("inf"),12)):
            with self.assertRaises(ValueError): payment(*args)

if __name__=="__main__":
    rows=amortization(10000,.005,24)
    for row in rows[:3]:
        print(row["period"],"payment",round(row["payment"],2),
              "interest",round(row["interest"],2),
              "principal",round(row["principal"],2),
              "balance",round(row["balance"],2))
    unittest.main(verbosity=2)
```

## 9. Exercises with worked answers

1. **Payment identity:** What two components make up a standard loan payment? **Answer:** Interest and principal repayment.
2. **Interest formula:** How is period-k interest calculated? **Answer:** I(k) = rB(k-1).
3. **Principal formula:** How is period-k principal reduction calculated? **Answer:** P(k) = A-I(k).
4. **Balance formula:** State the recurrence. **Answer:** B(k) = B(k-1)(1+r)-A.
5. **First interest:** Find interest on $10,000 at 0.5% for one period. **Answer:** $50.
6. **First principal:** If payment is $443.21 and interest is $50, what is the approximate principal component? **Answer:** $393.21.
7. **First balance:** What is the approximate new balance? **Answer:** $9,606.79.
8. **Next interest:** Estimate the second-period interest using the first balance. **Answer:** 0.005 × 9606.79 ≈ $48.03.
9. **Direction:** What happens to the interest portion over a normal amortizing schedule? **Answer:** It generally decreases.
10. **Total principal:** What should all principal components sum to? **Answer:** Original principal L.
11. **Total interest:** Express the total scheduled interest. **Answer:** nA-L.
12. **Negative amortization:** What happens if a payment is below accrued interest? **Answer:** The unpaid interest increases the balance, subject to the loan contract.
