## Learning objectives

Construct a period-by-period loan amortization schedule; separate opening balance, interest, principal reduction, payment, and closing balance; reconcile total principal and interest; distinguish exact mathematical calculations from contractual cent-rounding; and test an implementation in Python.

## 1. Why build an amortization schedule?

An **amortization schedule** explains how a debt is repaid over time. It is more informative than a single monthly payment because it shows how much of each installment covers interest, how much reduces principal, and what remains owed after each payment.

For a fixed-rate fully amortizing loan with n end-of-period payments, the standard schedule has six columns:

| Column | Interpretation |
|---|---|
| Period | Scheduled installment number |
| Opening balance | Principal outstanding just after the prior payment |
| Payment | Amount due at the end of the period |
| Interest | Interest accrued on opening balance |
| Principal repaid | Payment minus interest |
| Closing balance | Opening balance minus principal repaid |

## 2. Core equations

Let L be original principal, r the effective periodic rate, n the payment count, and A the level installment. Under a constant rate and equal end-of-period payments:

**A = Lr/[1-(1+r)^(-n)]**, for r > 0, and **A = L/n** for r = 0.

For each period k, starting from B(0)=L:

**Interest(k) = r × B(k-1).**

**Principal(k) = A - Interest(k).**

**B(k) = B(k-1) - Principal(k).**

Equivalently, **B(k) = B(k-1)(1+r)-A**.

These formulas assume no fees, payment delays, prepayments, interest-rate resets, or irregular dates.

## 3. Worked example — three opening rows

Consider a $10,000 loan with a 0.5% monthly rate and 24 end-of-month payments.

The exact mathematical payment is **10000 × 0.005/[1-(1.005)^(-24)]**, approximately **$443.21**.

| Period | Opening balance | Interest | Principal | Payment | Closing balance |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | $10,000.00 | $50.00 | ≈ $393.21 | ≈ $443.21 | ≈ $9,606.79 |
| 2 | ≈ $9,606.79 | ≈ $48.03 | ≈ $395.17 | ≈ $443.21 | ≈ $9,211.62 |
| 3 | ≈ $9,211.62 | ≈ $46.06 | ≈ $397.15 | ≈ $443.21 | ≈ $8,814.47 |

Each row uses the preceding **unrounded** closing balance for the mathematical schedule. Displayed numbers are rounded independently and can differ by a cent from sums of displayed cells.

## 4. Algorithm

```text
Set opening balance = original principal.
Calculate the scheduled level payment.
For k from 1 to n:
    interest = opening balance × periodic rate
    principal repaid = payment - interest
    closing balance = opening balance - principal repaid
    record the six schedule fields
    next opening balance = closing balance
Check final balance and total reconciliations.
```

For a standard positive-rate fully amortizing loan, interest declines and principal repayment grows over the term. The installment remains fixed before rounding.

## 5. Essential reconciliations

A trustworthy schedule satisfies:

- **Row identity:** Payment = Interest + Principal repaid.
- **Balance identity:** Opening balance - Principal repaid = Closing balance.
- **Principal total:** Sum of principal repaid = original principal.
- **Interest total:** Sum of interest = sum of payments - original principal.
- **Maturity:** Final closing balance = 0.

These equalities hold exactly in mathematical arithmetic. Floating-point calculations require tolerances; cent-rounded accounting should reconcile exactly in decimal currency units.

## 6. Exact mathematics versus contract rounding

The payment formula generally produces a number with more than two decimal places. A real lender may use a specified rounding convention for each installment and each interest calculation. Those conventions can leave a small residual balance if every scheduled payment is forced to the same cent amount.

This chapter's Python lab intentionally implements **two distinct policies**:

1. **Exact-model schedule:** floating-point calculations retain full precision; round only for presentation.
2. **Illustrative cent-rounded schedule:** book the principal once to cents, then compute the regular payment from that booked opening. Round the payment and each period’s interest half up using exact rational arithmetic and return Decimal amounts. Reject an opening that rounds to zero. Cap each payment at the amount due and settle any residual on the last installment; rows after an early settlement contain zero amounts.

The second policy is an educational example, **not a claim about any lender's contractual rules**. Some lenders use different day-count conventions, rounding modes, payoff calculations, and last-payment adjustments.

## 7. Special situations

**Zero-interest loan:** Every payment is principal; interest is zero.

**Interest-only loan:** The regular payment may not reduce principal, so a balloon or later amortization period is needed.

**Negative amortization:** A payment smaller than accrued interest increases the balance; it is outside the standard fully amortizing model.

**Extra principal payment:** A voluntary additional payment reduces the balance immediately, changing subsequent interest and possibly the payoff date. Section 11.9 develops this case.

**Early payoff:** An official payoff amount may include accrued interest and fees not captured in the principal schedule.

The standard-library lab uses `math.log1p` and `math.expm1` for numerical stability, `fractions.Fraction` for exact cent-rounding decisions and `decimal.Decimal` for the resulting currency amounts, and **12 unit tests** for component identities, totals, maturity, zero rates, rounding reconciliation, and invalid inputs. The exact-model implementation uses the prospective remaining-payment balance and the discounted principal component to avoid subtracting nearly equal floats. The rational cent policy is intended for ordinary teaching-sized schedules; very long terms or rates with long decimal representations can make exact rational powers costly.

```python
"""Amortization schedule with explicit exact-model versus cent-rounded policies."""
import math
import unittest
from decimal import Decimal
from fractions import Fraction

def validate(L,r,n):
    if any(isinstance(v,bool) or not isinstance(v,(int,float)) or not math.isfinite(v) for v in (L,r)):
        raise ValueError("nonfinite loan input")
    if L<=0 or r<0 or isinstance(n,bool) or not isinstance(n,int) or n<=0:
        raise ValueError("invalid principal, rate, or term")

def _positive(value, label):
    if not math.isfinite(value) or value <= 0:
        raise ValueError(f"{label} outside supported range")
    return value

def _loss(log_rate, periods):
    try:
        return _positive(-math.expm1(-periods*log_rate), "discount loss")
    except OverflowError as exc:
        raise ValueError("discount loss outside supported range") from exc

def level_payment(principal,rate,periods):
    L, r, n = principal, rate, periods
    validate(L,r,n)
    if r == 0:
        amount = L/n
    elif n == 1:
        amount = L*(1+r)
    else:
        amount = L*(r/_loss(math.log1p(r),n))
    return _positive(amount,"payment")

def _balance(L,log_rate,n,k,full_loss):
    if k == 0:
        return L
    if k == n:
        return 0.0
    fraction = (n-k)/n if log_rate == 0 else _loss(log_rate,n-k)/full_loss
    return _positive(L*fraction,"balance")

def exact_schedule(principal, rate, periods):
    amount = level_payment(principal, rate, periods)
    log_rate = math.log1p(rate)
    full_loss = _loss(log_rate, periods) if rate > 0 and periods > 1 else None
    balance = principal
    rows = []
    for k in range(1, periods+1):
        opening = balance
        interest = _positive(opening*rate, "interest") if rate > 0 else 0.0
        if k == periods:
            repaid = opening
        elif rate == 0:
            repaid = amount
        else:
            # A - interest equals the installment discounted by the number of payments left.
            exponent = -(periods-k+1)*log_rate
            factor = math.exp(exponent)
            repaid = amount*factor if factor >= 1e-300 else math.exp(math.log(amount)+exponent)
            repaid = _positive(repaid, "principal")
        balance = _balance(principal, log_rate, periods, k, full_loss)
        rows.append(dict(period=k, opening=opening, payment=amount,
                         interest=interest, principal=repaid, closing=balance))
    return rows

def _round_cents(value):
    # Round an amount expressed in cents, breaking ties away from zero.
    sign = -1 if value < 0 else 1
    numerator, denominator = abs(value).as_integer_ratio()
    return sign*((2*numerator+denominator)//(2*denominator))

def _from_cents(cents):
    whole, remainder = divmod(abs(cents), 100)
    sign = "-" if cents < 0 else ""
    return Decimal(f"{sign}{whole}.{remainder:02d}")

def money(value):
    return _from_cents(_round_cents(Fraction(str(value))*100))

def rounded_schedule(principal, rate, periods):
    validate(principal, rate, periods)
    balance = _round_cents(Fraction(str(principal))*100)
    if balance == 0:
        raise ValueError("booked opening must be at least one cent")
    periodic_rate = Fraction(str(rate))
    if periodic_rate == 0:
        regular = _round_cents(Fraction(balance, periods))
    else:
        discount = (1+periodic_rate)**(-periods)
        regular = _round_cents(balance*periodic_rate/(1-discount))
    rows = []
    for k in range(1, periods+1):
        opening = balance
        interest = _round_cents(opening*periodic_rate)
        due = opening+interest
        paid = due if k == periods else min(regular, due)
        if paid < interest:
            raise ValueError("payment would cause negative amortization")
        repaid = paid-interest
        balance = opening-repaid
        rows.append(dict(period=k, opening=_from_cents(opening), payment=_from_cents(paid),
                         interest=_from_cents(interest), principal=_from_cents(repaid),
                         closing=_from_cents(balance)))
    return rows

class Tests(unittest.TestCase):
    def test_01_row_count(self): self.assertEqual(len(exact_schedule(1000,.01,12)),12)
    def test_02_first_interest(self): self.assertAlmostEqual(exact_schedule(1000,.01,12)[0]["interest"],10)
    def test_03_first_balance(self):
        x=exact_schedule(1000,.01,12)[0]
        self.assertAlmostEqual(x["closing"],1000*(1.01)-x["payment"])
    def test_04_component_identity(self):
        for x in exact_schedule(1000,.01,12):
            self.assertAlmostEqual(x["payment"],x["interest"]+x["principal"])
    def test_05_balance_identity(self):
        for x in exact_schedule(1000,.01,12):
            self.assertAlmostEqual(x["opening"]-x["principal"],x["closing"])
    def test_06_total_principal(self):
        self.assertAlmostEqual(sum(x["principal"] for x in exact_schedule(1000,.01,12)),1000)
    def test_07_total_interest(self):
        rows=exact_schedule(1000,.01,12)
        self.assertAlmostEqual(sum(x["interest"] for x in rows),12*rows[0]["payment"]-1000)
    def test_08_final_balance(self): self.assertAlmostEqual(exact_schedule(1000,.01,12)[-1]["closing"],0,places=8)
    def test_09_zero_rate(self):
        rows=exact_schedule(1200,0,12)
        self.assertEqual(rows[0]["payment"],100)
        self.assertEqual(rows[-1]["closing"],0)
    def test_10_rounded_final(self): self.assertEqual(rounded_schedule(1000,.01,12)[-1]["closing"],Decimal("0.00"))
    def test_11_rounded_reconcile(self):
        rows=rounded_schedule(1000,.01,12)
        self.assertEqual(sum((x["principal"] for x in rows),Decimal("0")),Decimal("1000.00"))
        for x in rows: self.assertEqual(x["payment"],x["interest"]+x["principal"])
        self.assertEqual(rounded_schedule(.15,0,6)[0]["payment"],Decimal("0.03"))
        self.assertEqual(rounded_schedule(.005,0,2)[0]["payment"],Decimal("0.01"))
    def test_12_invalid(self):
        for args in ((0,.01,12),(100,-.01,12),(100,.01,0),(100,float("nan"),12)):
            with self.assertRaises(ValueError): exact_schedule(*args)
        with self.assertRaises(ValueError): rounded_schedule(.004,0,2)
        with self.assertRaises(ValueError): level_payment(5e-324,0,2)
        with self.assertRaises(ValueError): level_payment(1e308,1,1)

if __name__=="__main__":
    rows=rounded_schedule(10000,.005,24)
    for x in rows[:3]: print(x)
    print("Final:",rows[-1])
    unittest.main(verbosity=2)
```

## 9. Exercises with worked answers

1. **Columns:** Name the six core fields. **Answer:** Period, opening balance, payment, interest, principal, closing balance.
2. **First interest:** On $10,000 at 0.5% per month, calculate first-period interest. **Answer:** $50.
3. **First principal:** If payment is approximately $443.21, find first principal reduction. **Answer:** Approximately $393.21.
4. **First closing balance:** Find the balance after Exercise 3. **Answer:** Approximately $9,606.79.
5. **Second interest:** Estimate next month's interest. **Answer:** 0.005 × 9606.79 ≈ $48.03.
6. **Payment identity:** Write the row reconciliation. **Answer:** Payment = Interest + Principal.
7. **Balance identity:** Write the closing-balance formula. **Answer:** Closing = Opening - Principal.
8. **Total principal:** What should all principal components sum to? **Answer:** Original loan principal.
9. **Total interest:** Express total interest using payments. **Answer:** Total payments - original principal.
10. **Zero rate:** What does a $1,200 loan over 12 zero-rate periods pay each period? **Answer:** $100, all principal.
11. **Rounding:** Why might the last installment differ from the regular payment? **Answer:** Per-period cent rounding may leave a residual that the final payment settles.
12. **Validation:** What should the final balance be for a fully amortizing loan? **Answer:** Zero, within numerical tolerance or exactly under a reconciled cent-rounded policy.
13. **Timing:** When does period-k interest accrue? **Answer:** Over period k on the opening balance, under the assumed periodic-interest convention.
14. **Scope:** Why might a real payoff quote differ from the schedule balance? **Answer:** Accrued interest, fees, and contractual payoff rules.
