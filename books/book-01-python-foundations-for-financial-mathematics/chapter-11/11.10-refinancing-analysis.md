## 1. What is refinancing?

**Refinancing** replaces an existing loan with a new loan. A borrower may refinance to change the interest rate, scheduled payment, repayment term, or loan structure. The new loan pays off the old obligation, subject to lender and contractual conditions.

A lower monthly payment is not sufficient evidence that refinancing is beneficial. Extending a repayment term may lower monthly payments while increasing total interest, and closing costs may outweigh near-term savings.

## 2. Define both alternatives

A sound comparison begins at the same valuation date:

- **Keep the old loan:** principal balance B, old periodic rate r_old, and m remaining scheduled installments.
- **Refinance:** new periodic rate r_new, new term n, and closing costs C. If eligible costs are financed, they increase the new principal; otherwise, they are paid upfront.

The fixed-payment formula is:

**A(L,r,n) = Lr/[1-(1+r)^(-n)]** for r>0, and **A=L/n** for r=0.

Thus:

**Old payment = A(B,r_old,m).**

**New payment = A(B+C_financed,r_new,n).**

## 3. Worked example: lower rate, same remaining term

Suppose the existing principal balance is $200,000, with 20 years (240 monthly installments) remaining at a nominal annual 6% rate compounded monthly.

A refinance offer has a nominal annual 4.5% rate, the same 240-month amortization term, and $3,000 closing costs paid in cash.

**Old payment = A(200000,0.06/12,240).**

**New payment = A(200000,0.045/12,240).**

**Monthly payment savings = Old payment - New payment.**

The Python lab calculates both exact values and the simple break-even period.

## 4. Simple break-even calculation

If the borrower pays closing costs upfront and saves a constant positive amount S per month:

**Simple break-even months = C/S.**

For whole payment periods, use the ceiling of this ratio. If S≤0 and C>0, there is no positive simple payment-savings break-even.

This is a **cash-flow heuristic**, not a complete economic valuation. It ignores discounting, changes in outstanding principal, tax effects, other charges, and what happens when the property is sold or the loan is repaid early.

If costs are financed, the upfront amount may be zero, but the larger loan principal creates additional repayment obligations. A zero upfront amount does **not** mean refinancing is costless.

## 5. Compare at a common horizon

Suppose the borrower expects to sell the home or repay the loan after H months. Both alternatives must be compared over the same H months, including the outstanding balance at that horizon.

For a loan with principal L, rate r, term n, and payment A, the outstanding balance after H installments is:

**B(H) = A[1-(1+r)^(-(n-H))]/r**, for r>0, 0≤H≤n.

At zero rate, **B(H)=A(n-H)**.

With a monthly discount rate d, define the present value of financing-related cash outflows over horizon H:

**PV_cost = Upfront cost + sum from k=1 to H of A/(1+d)^k + B(H)/(1+d)^H.**

This expression includes the residual principal payoff at the horizon, so it does not unfairly favor a longer-term loan with lower monthly installments but more principal outstanding.

Compare **PV_cost(keep) - PV_cost(refinance)**. A positive value means the refinance has lower modeled present-value cash outflows under the stated assumptions.

For this simplified model, use H no greater than the shorter of the two remaining terms. Other horizons require explicit handling of the loan that matures earlier.

## 6. Cash versus financed closing costs

**Cash closing costs:** pay C at time zero; the new principal is usually the old payoff balance, subject to other adjustments.

**Financed closing costs:** add eligible C to the new principal; the borrower pays more interest on a larger balance and may face lender or LTV restrictions.

Do not compare only the new monthly payments without including the financing of fees.

## 7. Rate, APR, and other considerations

The **interest rate** determines interest charges under the contract. **APR** is a disclosure measure incorporating specified finance charges under applicable rules; it is not simply the periodic rate used in an amortization formula.

Other relevant items can include discount points, origination charges, prepayment penalties, mortgage insurance changes, escrow adjustments, credit requirements, and whether the new loan resets the repayment term.

An expected sale date or early payoff can materially change the result. Tax treatment and opportunity cost of cash are outside the basic loan model.

## 8. Numerical and model safeguards

Use rates consistent with payment frequency. Avoid rounding intermediate balances to cents unless a contractual rounding policy has been defined. Keep the discount rate separate from either loan's contractual rate; it represents the valuation assumption.

A robust comparison should test multiple holding horizons and discount rates rather than rely on a single break-even month.

```python
"""Refinancing comparison with common-horizon cash flows."""
import math
import unittest

def payment(L,r,n):
    if isinstance(L,bool) or not isinstance(L,(int,float)) or not math.isfinite(L) or L<=0: raise ValueError("invalid principal")
    if isinstance(r,bool) or not isinstance(r,(int,float)) or not math.isfinite(r) or r<0: raise ValueError("invalid rate")
    if isinstance(n,bool) or not isinstance(n,int) or n<1: raise ValueError("invalid term")
    return L/n if r==0 else L*r/(-math.expm1(-n*math.log1p(r)))

def balance(L,r,n,k):
    A=payment(L,r,n)
    if isinstance(k,bool) or not isinstance(k,int) or k<0 or k>n: raise ValueError("invalid period")
    if k==n: return 0.0
    if r==0: return A*(n-k)
    return A*(-math.expm1(-(n-k)*math.log1p(r)))/r

def refinancing(old_balance,old_rate,old_remaining,new_rate,new_term,
                closing_cost=0,finance_cost=False):
    if isinstance(closing_cost,bool) or not isinstance(closing_cost,(int,float)) or not math.isfinite(closing_cost) or closing_cost<0:
        raise ValueError("invalid closing cost")
    old=payment(old_balance,old_rate,old_remaining)
    new_principal=old_balance+(closing_cost if finance_cost else 0)
    new=payment(new_principal,new_rate,new_term)
    upfront=0 if finance_cost else closing_cost
    savings=old-new
    breakeven=math.ceil(upfront/savings) if upfront>0 and savings>0 else (0 if upfront==0 else None)
    return dict(old_payment=old,new_payment=new,monthly_savings=savings,
                upfront_cost=upfront,break_even_periods=breakeven,new_principal=new_principal)

def discounted_cost(principal,rate,term,horizon,discount_rate=0,upfront=0):
    if isinstance(horizon,bool) or not isinstance(horizon,int) or horizon<0 or horizon>term:
        raise ValueError("horizon must be in 0..term")
    if isinstance(discount_rate,bool) or not isinstance(discount_rate,(int,float)) or not math.isfinite(discount_rate) or discount_rate<0:
        raise ValueError("invalid discount rate")
    if isinstance(upfront,bool) or not isinstance(upfront,(int,float)) or not math.isfinite(upfront) or upfront<0:
        raise ValueError("invalid upfront cost")
    A=payment(principal,rate,term)
    flows=math.fsum(A/(1+discount_rate)**k for k in range(1,horizon+1))
    residual=balance(principal,rate,term,horizon)
    return upfront+flows+residual/(1+discount_rate)**horizon

class Tests(unittest.TestCase):
    def test_01_old_payment(self):
        x=refinancing(200000,.06/12,240,.045/12,240)
        self.assertAlmostEqual(x["old_payment"],payment(200000,.06/12,240))
    def test_02_lower_new_rate(self):
        x=refinancing(200000,.06/12,240,.045/12,240)
        self.assertGreater(x["monthly_savings"],0)
    def test_03_cash_cost(self):
        x=refinancing(200000,.06/12,240,.045/12,240,3000)
        self.assertEqual(x["upfront_cost"],3000)
    def test_04_financed_cost(self):
        x=refinancing(200000,.06/12,240,.045/12,240,3000,True)
        self.assertEqual(x["upfront_cost"],0)
        self.assertEqual(x["new_principal"],203000)
    def test_05_financing_increases_payment(self):
        a=refinancing(200000,.06/12,240,.045/12,240,3000,False)
        b=refinancing(200000,.06/12,240,.045/12,240,3000,True)
        self.assertGreater(b["new_payment"],a["new_payment"])
    def test_06_break_even(self):
        x=refinancing(200000,.06/12,240,.045/12,240,3000)
        self.assertEqual(x["break_even_periods"],math.ceil(3000/x["monthly_savings"]))
    def test_07_no_savings(self):
        x=refinancing(200000,.04/12,240,.05/12,240,3000)
        self.assertIsNone(x["break_even_periods"])
    def test_08_horizon_zero(self):
        self.assertAlmostEqual(discounted_cost(1000,.01,12,0,0,100),1100)
    def test_09_horizon_end(self):
        A=payment(1000,.01,12)
        self.assertAlmostEqual(discounted_cost(1000,.01,12,12),12*A)
    def test_10_pv_at_contract_rate(self):
        self.assertAlmostEqual(discounted_cost(1000,.01,12,6,.01),1000)
    def test_11_balance_after(self):
        self.assertAlmostEqual(balance(1000,.01,12,1),1010-payment(1000,.01,12))
    def test_12_invalid(self):
        with self.assertRaises(ValueError): refinancing(1000,.01,12,.005,12,-1)
        with self.assertRaises(ValueError): discounted_cost(1000,.01,12,13)
        with self.assertRaises(ValueError): discounted_cost(1000,.01,12,6,-.01)

if __name__=="__main__":
    old=dict(principal=200000,rate=.06/12,term=240)
    new=dict(principal=200000,rate=.045/12,term=240)
    print(refinancing(200000,.06/12,240,.045/12,240,3000))
    for h in (12,36,60):
        keep=discounted_cost(old["principal"],old["rate"],old["term"],h,.003)
        refi=discounted_cost(new["principal"],new["rate"],new["term"],h,.003,3000)
        print("Months",h,"PV savings",round(keep-refi,2))
    unittest.main(verbosity=2)
```

## 10. Exercises with worked answers

1. **Definition:** What is refinancing? **Answer:** Replacing an existing loan with a new loan.
2. **Old payment:** How do you calculate the remaining old loan's installment? **Answer:** Apply A(B,r_old,m) using the current balance and remaining term.
3. **New payment:** How do financed fees affect the new payment calculation? **Answer:** Add eligible financed fees to the new principal.
4. **Monthly savings:** Express payment savings. **Answer:** Old payment minus new payment.
5. **Simple break-even:** If costs are $3,000 and monthly savings $100, how many months? **Answer:** 30 months.
6. **No savings:** What if the new payment is larger and closing costs are positive? **Answer:** No positive simple payment-savings break-even.
7. **Term reset:** Why can a longer new term be misleading? **Answer:** It can lower payments while leaving more principal and increasing total interest.
8. **Common horizon:** What must be included at the end of the holding period? **Answer:** The outstanding loan balance or payoff amount.
9. **PV cost:** List its three modeled components. **Answer:** Upfront costs, discounted installments, and discounted remaining balance.
10. **Financed costs:** Does zero cash at closing imply zero financing cost? **Answer:** No; financed fees increase the amount borrowed.
11. **Discount rate:** Is the discount rate necessarily equal to the mortgage rate? **Answer:** No; it is a separate valuation assumption.
12. **APR:** Is APR identical to the nominal note rate? **Answer:** Not necessarily; APR can reflect specified finance charges.
13. **Holding period:** Why does an early home sale matter? **Answer:** It limits payment savings and triggers payoff of remaining principal.
14. **Decision:** What does positive keep-minus-refinance PV cost indicate? **Answer:** The refinance has lower modeled financing cash outflows under the assumptions.
