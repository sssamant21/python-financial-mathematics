## Learning objectives

## 1. What does outstanding balance mean?

The **outstanding principal balance** is the principal still owed at a particular point in the repayment schedule. It is not the original amount borrowed, the sum of remaining payments, or necessarily the amount required for a legal payoff quote.

For this chapter, B(k) denotes the outstanding balance **immediately after the kth scheduled payment**, with B(0) = L and B(n) = 0 for a fully amortizing loan.

```text
Origination         Payment 1           Payment k             Payment n
    |-------------------|-------------------|--------- ... ---------|
   B(0)=L             B(1)                B(k)                 B(n)=0
                                              ^
                                     valuation immediately
                                     after payment k
```

Interest accrued between payment dates, fees, escrow, and prepayment charges can cause a lender's payoff amount to differ from this modeled principal balance.

## 2. Model assumptions

Assume L > 0, a fixed nonnegative effective periodic interest rate r, n positive integer end-of-period level payments A, and no fees, rate changes, missed payments, or unscheduled principal reductions. These assumptions are important because the closed-form balance formulas depend on the original payment stream remaining unchanged.

## 3. The level-payment equation

The scheduled payment is:

**A = Lr/[1-(1+r)^(-n)]**, for r > 0.

At r = 0, **A = L/n**.

The balance after payment k can be calculated using either future promised payments or past accumulated cash flows.

## 4. Prospective balance method

Immediately after payment k, there are n-k payments remaining. Their present value at time k is:

**B(k) = A × [1-(1+r)^(-(n-k))]/r**, for r > 0.

For zero rate, **B(k) = A(n-k)**.

The prospective method is especially intuitive: the balance is the present value of what remains to be paid. It also avoids subtracting two large accumulated quantities when the loan is close to maturity.

## 5. Retrospective balance method

Start with the original principal accumulated for k periods and subtract the accumulated value of the k payments already made:

**B(k) = L(1+r)^k - A[(1+r)^k-1]/r**, for r > 0.

At zero rate, **B(k) = L-kA**.

This method answers the same question from the opposite direction: how much of the original obligation remains after accounting for all payments to date?

The prospective and retrospective expressions are algebraically equivalent under the stated assumptions.

## 6. Worked example

Consider a $10,000 loan at 0.5% effective monthly interest, repaid over 24 months.

**A = 10000 × 0.005/[1-(1.005)^(-24)] ≈ $443.21.**

Immediately after payment 12, there are 12 payments remaining:

**B(12) = A × [1-(1.005)^(-12)]/0.005.**

An independent calculation using the retrospective expression should give the same balance within floating-point tolerance. The Python lab prints the numeric result using the unrounded payment.

## 7. Connection to the balance recurrence

For each scheduled payment:

**B(k) = B(k-1)(1+r)-A.**

This recurrence provides a third way to validate a balance formula. Starting at B(0) = L, applying it k times should reproduce the closed-form balance.

The interest component for period k is rB(k-1), while the principal component is A-rB(k-1). Both components must sum to A.

## 8. Extra principal payments and payoff estimates

If a borrower makes an extra principal payment E **immediately after** scheduled payment k, the simplified principal balance becomes:

**B_extra(k) = max(0, B(k)-E)**, provided 0 ≤ E ≤ B(k).

This is an instantaneous principal adjustment, not a complete revised amortization schedule. After an extra payment, the lender may preserve the original payment and shorten the term, or recast payments under an agreed process. These choices change future interest and are covered later in the chapter.

A real payoff quote can include accrued interest and contractual charges. Never label this simple principal-balance estimate as an official payoff amount.

## 9. Numerical considerations

For very small rates, direct evaluation of 1-(1+r)^(-m) may lose precision. The Python reference uses `math.log1p` and `math.expm1` to improve stability. The retrospective method can be less stable near maturity because it subtracts large quantities.

Use full precision internally and round only for display. Actual amortization systems require a documented currency rounding policy.

```python
"""Outstanding loan balances: prospective and retrospective valuation."""
import math
import unittest

def inputs(L,r,n,k):
    if isinstance(L,bool) or not isinstance(L,(int,float)) or not math.isfinite(L) or L<=0:
        raise ValueError("principal must be finite positive")
    if isinstance(r,bool) or not isinstance(r,(int,float)) or not math.isfinite(r) or r<0:
        raise ValueError("rate must be finite nonnegative")
    if isinstance(n,bool) or not isinstance(n,int) or n<1:
        raise ValueError("term must be positive integer")
    if isinstance(k,bool) or not isinstance(k,int) or not 0<=k<=n:
        raise ValueError("completed payments must be between 0 and term")

def payment(L,r,n):
    inputs(L,r,n,0)
    if r==0: return L/n
    return L*r/(-math.expm1(-n*math.log1p(r)))

def prospective_balance(L,r,n,k):
    inputs(L,r,n,k)
    if k==n: return 0.0
    A=payment(L,r,n)
    if r==0: return A*(n-k)
    return A*(-math.expm1(-(n-k)*math.log1p(r)))/r

def retrospective_balance(L,r,n,k):
    inputs(L,r,n,k)
    A=payment(L,r,n)
    if r==0: return L-k*A
    growth=math.expm1(k*math.log1p(r))
    return L*(1+growth)-A*growth/r

def balance_after_extra(L,r,n,k,extra):
    inputs(L,r,n,k)
    if isinstance(extra,bool) or not isinstance(extra,(int,float)) or not math.isfinite(extra) or extra<0:
        raise ValueError("extra payment must be finite nonnegative")
    b=prospective_balance(L,r,n,k)
    if extra>b+1e-9:
        raise ValueError("extra payment exceeds balance")
    return max(0.0,b-extra)

class Tests(unittest.TestCase):
    def test_01_start(self): self.assertAlmostEqual(prospective_balance(1000,.01,12,0),1000)
    def test_02_end(self): self.assertEqual(prospective_balance(1000,.01,12,12),0)
    def test_03_first_period(self):
        L,r,n=1000,.01,12
        self.assertAlmostEqual(prospective_balance(L,r,n,1),L*(1+r)-payment(L,r,n))
    def test_04_methods(self):
        for k in (0,1,3,6,11,12):
            self.assertAlmostEqual(prospective_balance(1000,.01,12,k),retrospective_balance(1000,.01,12,k),places=7)
    def test_05_zero_rate(self): self.assertAlmostEqual(prospective_balance(1200,0,12,3),900)
    def test_06_zero_rate_retrospective(self): self.assertAlmostEqual(retrospective_balance(1200,0,12,3),900)
    def test_07_decreasing(self):
        self.assertLess(prospective_balance(1000,.01,12,5),prospective_balance(1000,.01,12,4))
    def test_08_remaining_pv(self):
        A=payment(1000,.01,12)
        self.assertAlmostEqual(prospective_balance(1000,.01,12,5),sum(A/1.01**j for j in range(1,8)))
    def test_09_extra(self):
        self.assertAlmostEqual(balance_after_extra(1000,.01,12,5,100),prospective_balance(1000,.01,12,5)-100)
    def test_10_full_payoff(self):
        b=prospective_balance(1000,.01,12,5)
        self.assertEqual(balance_after_extra(1000,.01,12,5,b),0)
    def test_11_bad_k(self):
        with self.assertRaises(ValueError): prospective_balance(1000,.01,12,13)
        with self.assertRaises(ValueError): prospective_balance(1000,.01,12,1.5)
    def test_12_bad_extra(self):
        with self.assertRaises(ValueError): balance_after_extra(1000,.01,12,5,-1)
        with self.assertRaises(ValueError): balance_after_extra(1000,.01,12,5,2000)

if __name__=="__main__":
    L,r,n=10000,.005,24
    print("Payment:",round(payment(L,r,n),2))
    print("Balance after 12 payments:",round(prospective_balance(L,r,n,12),2))
    unittest.main(verbosity=2)
```

## 11. Exercises with worked answers

1. **Definition:** What is B(k)? **Answer:** Outstanding principal immediately after scheduled payment k.
2. **Origination:** What is B(0)? **Answer:** L.
3. **Maturity:** What is B(n) for a fully amortizing loan? **Answer:** Zero in exact arithmetic.
4. **Prospective method:** Write the formula for B(k). **Answer:** A[1-(1+r)^(-(n-k))]/r for r > 0.
5. **Retrospective method:** Write the alternative formula. **Answer:** L(1+r)^k-A[(1+r)^k-1]/r.
6. **Zero rate:** Find B(3) on a $1,200, 12-period, zero-rate loan. **Answer:** $900.
7. **Recurrence:** State the balance update. **Answer:** B(k)=B(k-1)(1+r)-A.
8. **Remaining payments:** How many payments remain immediately after payment 5 of 24? **Answer:** 19.
9. **Timing:** Is B(5) measured before or after the fifth scheduled payment? **Answer:** After it.
10. **Extra payment:** If B(k)=$5,000 and extra principal E=$400 is paid immediately, what is the simplified new principal balance? **Answer:** $4,600.
11. **Payoff quote:** Why can a lender payoff quote exceed B(k)? **Answer:** Accrued interest and contractual fees or charges.
12. **Validation:** How can you verify the balance formula? **Answer:** Reconcile prospective and retrospective values and the period-by-period recurrence.
