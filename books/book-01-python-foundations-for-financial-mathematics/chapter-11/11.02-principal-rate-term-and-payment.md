## 1. Four loan variables

A fully amortizing level-payment loan is characterized by four core quantities:

| Symbol | Meaning | Typical unit |
|---|---|---|
| L | Initial loan principal | Currency |
| i | Effective interest rate per payment period | Decimal per period |
| n | Number of scheduled payments | Positive integer |
| A | Level payment at the end of each period | Currency per period |

When three variables are specified, the fourth may be determined under the assumed loan model. Real contracts may require additional variables for fees, escrow, variable rates, balloon payments, and rounding.

```text
Time 0        Period 1      Period 2                  Period n
  |--------------|-------------|-------------------------|
 +L             -A            -A                        -A
```

From the borrower's perspective, the initial advance is positive and repayments are negative.

## 2. Loan principal

**Principal** is the amount initially borrowed, not necessarily the purchase price of the asset being financed. A down payment reduces the principal, while financed fees may increase it. For a $300,000 purchase with a $50,000 down payment and no financed fees, principal is $250,000.

The original principal and outstanding balance differ after repayments begin. Chapter 11.6 studies the outstanding balance in detail.

## 3. Interest rate: use matching units

A rate must be expressed for the same period as the payment schedule.

For a quoted nominal annual rate with monthly compounding, the periodic rate is typically:

**i_month = j_nominal / 12.**

For an effective annual rate EAR, the equivalent monthly effective rate is:

**i_month = (1 + EAR)^(1/12) - 1.**

These conversions are not interchangeable. For example, a nominal annual 12% rate compounded monthly implies 1% per month and an effective annual rate of (1.01)^12 - 1, about 12.68%. By contrast, a 12% effective annual rate implies a monthly rate of (1.12)^(1/12) - 1, about 0.949%.

An advertised APR may be a regulated disclosure measure that includes or reflects fees and jurisdiction-specific rules. Do not assume every disclosed APR can simply be divided by 12 without checking the contract and applicable convention.

## 4. Loan term and payment count

The loan term specifies how long repayment lasts. If payments occur monthly for 30 years, the count is:

**n = 30 × 12 = 360 payments.**

If payments occur quarterly for five years, n = 20. The payment count is distinct from the number of calendar years, and a missed or extra payment can change the actual repayment path.

## 5. Derive the payment equation

For n equal end-of-period payments and a constant periodic rate i > 0, the present value of repayments must equal the amount advanced:

**L = A × [1 - (1+i)^(-n)] / i.**

Solving for A gives:

**A = L × i / [1 - (1+i)^(-n)].**

When i = 0, use the continuous limiting case **A = L/n**. For positive i and finite n, A is greater than L/n because the borrower pays interest as well as principal.

## 6. Worked example — mortgage-style loan

Suppose a borrower finances $250,000 for 30 years at a nominal 6% annual rate with monthly compounding and monthly end-of-period payments.

- Principal L = $250,000.
- Periodic rate i = 0.06/12 = 0.005.
- Payment count n = 30 × 12 = 360.

The scheduled principal-and-interest payment is:

**A = 250000 × 0.005 / [1 - (1.005)^(-360)] ≈ $1,498.88.**

This excludes taxes, insurance, fees, and escrow. The model's unrounded total scheduled interest is **360A - 250000**. Actual loan statements may differ because lenders typically round payments and interest to cents under their contractual rules.

## 7. Principal-payment relationship

For fixed i and n, the payment scales linearly with principal. Doubling L doubles A.

Conversely, the affordable principal implied by a known payment A is:

**L = A × [1 - (1+i)^(-n)] / i**, for i > 0.

For i = 0, **L = A × n**.

This inverse calculation estimates the principal supported by a payment limit under the simple model; it is not a credit approval or underwriting decision.

## 8. Sensitivity and trade-offs

Holding principal and rate fixed, increasing the term usually lowers the periodic payment but increases total interest over the full schedule. Holding principal and term fixed, a higher rate raises the payment. A lower monthly payment therefore does not necessarily mean cheaper financing.

A practical comparison should report payment, total scheduled interest, and other financing costs, using consistent rate and timing conventions.

```python
"""Loan inputs and payment: standard-library reference implementation."""
import math
import unittest

def validate(principal, periodic_rate, periods):
    for value in (principal, periodic_rate):
        if isinstance(value, bool) or not isinstance(value, (int, float)) or not math.isfinite(value):
            raise ValueError("principal and rate must be finite numeric")
    if principal <= 0 or periodic_rate < 0:
        raise ValueError("principal must be positive and rate nonnegative")
    if isinstance(periods, bool) or not isinstance(periods, int) or periods <= 0:
        raise ValueError("periods must be a positive integer")

def payment(principal, periodic_rate, periods):
    validate(principal, periodic_rate, periods)
    if periodic_rate == 0:
        return principal / periods
    factor = -math.expm1(-periods * math.log1p(periodic_rate))
    amount = principal * periodic_rate / factor
    if not math.isfinite(amount):
        raise ValueError("payment overflow")
    return amount

def monthly_rate_from_annual_effective(annual_effective):
    if isinstance(annual_effective, bool) or not isinstance(annual_effective, (int, float)) or not math.isfinite(annual_effective) or annual_effective < 0:
        raise ValueError("annual effective rate must be finite and nonnegative")
    return math.expm1(math.log1p(annual_effective) / 12)

def monthly_rate_from_nominal_apr(apr):
    if isinstance(apr, bool) or not isinstance(apr, (int, float)) or not math.isfinite(apr) or apr < 0:
        raise ValueError("APR must be finite and nonnegative")
    return apr / 12

def interest_total(principal, rate, periods):
    return periods * payment(principal, rate, periods) - principal

def principal_from_payment(amount, rate, periods):
    if isinstance(amount, bool) or not isinstance(amount, (int,float)) or not math.isfinite(amount) or amount <= 0:
        raise ValueError("payment must be positive and finite")
    validate(1, rate, periods)
    return amount / payment(1, rate, periods)

class Tests(unittest.TestCase):
    def test_01_zero_rate(self):
        self.assertEqual(payment(1200, 0, 12), 100)
    def test_02_one_period(self):
        self.assertAlmostEqual(payment(100, .1, 1), 110)
    def test_03_level_payment(self):
        self.assertAlmostEqual(payment(10000, .005, 24), 10000*.005/(1-1.005**-24))
    def test_04_payment_increases_with_principal(self):
        self.assertAlmostEqual(payment(2000,.01,12),2*payment(1000,.01,12))
    def test_05_payment_decreases_with_term(self):
        self.assertLess(payment(1000,.01,24),payment(1000,.01,12))
    def test_06_payment_increases_with_rate(self):
        self.assertGreater(payment(1000,.02,12),payment(1000,.01,12))
    def test_07_annual_effective(self):
        m=monthly_rate_from_annual_effective(.12)
        self.assertAlmostEqual((1+m)**12-1,.12)
    def test_08_nominal_apr(self):
        self.assertAlmostEqual(monthly_rate_from_nominal_apr(.12),.01)
    def test_09_rate_conventions_differ(self):
        self.assertNotAlmostEqual(monthly_rate_from_nominal_apr(.12),monthly_rate_from_annual_effective(.12))
    def test_10_interest_total(self):
        self.assertAlmostEqual(interest_total(1200,0,12),0)
        self.assertGreater(interest_total(1000,.01,12),0)
    def test_11_inverse_principal(self):
        p=payment(1000,.01,12)
        self.assertAlmostEqual(principal_from_payment(p,.01,12),1000)
    def test_12_validation(self):
        for args in ((0,.01,12),(100,-.01,12),(100,.01,0),(100,float("nan"),12)):
            with self.assertRaises(ValueError): payment(*args)
        with self.assertRaises(ValueError): monthly_rate_from_nominal_apr(-.1)

if __name__ == "__main__":
    L, apr, n = 250000, .06, 360
    r=monthly_rate_from_nominal_apr(apr)
    print("Monthly payment:",round(payment(L,r,n),2))
    print("Total scheduled interest:",round(interest_total(L,r,n),2))
    unittest.main(verbosity=2)
```

## 10. Exercises with worked answers

1. **Principal:** A $300,000 purchase has a $50,000 down payment and no financed fees. What is L? **Answer:** $250,000.
2. **Monthly count:** How many monthly payments occur in a 15-year loan? **Answer:** 180.
3. **Quarterly count:** How many quarterly payments occur in five years? **Answer:** 20.
4. **Nominal conversion:** What monthly periodic rate follows from a nominal annual 6% rate compounded monthly? **Answer:** 0.5%.
5. **Effective conversion:** What is the monthly effective rate equivalent to 12% EAR? **Answer:** (1.12)^(1/12) - 1, approximately 0.949%.
6. **Zero-rate loan:** What is the payment on $1,200 for 12 periods at 0%? **Answer:** $100.
7. **One-period loan:** What is the payment on $100 at 10% for one period? **Answer:** $110.
8. **Payment equation:** Express A in terms of L, i, and n. **Answer:** A = Li/[1-(1+i)^(-n)].
9. **Principal equation:** Express L in terms of A, i, and n. **Answer:** L = A[1-(1+i)^(-n)]/i.
10. **Principal scaling:** If principal doubles and i and n are unchanged, what happens to A? **Answer:** It doubles.
11. **Term trade-off:** What usually happens when the term increases with L and i fixed? **Answer:** Payment decreases, while total scheduled interest generally increases.
12. **Model limitations:** Why might a real mortgage bill exceed the modeled payment? **Answer:** Taxes, insurance, escrow, fees, and contractual adjustments may be additional.
