## 1. What is a mortgage?

A **mortgage** is a loan secured by real property. The borrower promises scheduled payments, and the property serves as collateral under the loan agreement and applicable law. A mortgage differs from an unsecured loan because collateral, property valuation, insurance, title, and foreclosure rules matter.

This section focuses on the mathematics of a conventional fixed-rate, fully amortizing mortgage. Real mortgage products may use adjustable rates, interest-only periods, balloon payments, lender credits, points, or special repayment rules.

## 2. Purchase price, down payment, and principal

The loan principal is generally not the same as the home's purchase price.

**Loan principal = Purchase price - Down payment + Financed fees.**

For a $300,000 property, a $60,000 down payment, and no financed fees:

**L = 300000 - 60000 = $240,000.**

If $2,000 of eligible fees is financed, the starting principal becomes $242,000, subject to the lender's terms.

The down payment is the borrower's initial equity contribution. Closing costs may be paid separately, financed where permitted, or offset by credits; do not assume they are all part of the principal.

## 3. Loan-to-value ratio

**LTV = Loan amount / Property value.**

Using a $240,000 loan and a $300,000 property valuation:

**LTV = 240000/300000 = 80%.**

LTV helps describe the size of the loan relative to collateral value. Actual underwriting can use a lender-defined valuation and may distinguish original LTV, current LTV, and combined LTV for multiple liens.

A larger down payment generally reduces principal and LTV, all else equal.

## 4. Mortgage payment mathematics

Let L be the mortgage principal, j the nominal annual interest rate compounded monthly, y the loan term in years, r=j/12 the monthly periodic rate, and n=12y the number of payments.

For a fixed-rate, fully amortizing mortgage with end-of-month payments:

**A = Lr/[1-(1+r)^(-n)]**, when r > 0.

At r=0, **A=L/n**.

The scheduled payment A here includes only **principal and interest**. It does not include taxes, homeowners insurance, mortgage insurance, association dues, or escrow adjustments.

## 5. Worked example: 30-year fixed-rate mortgage

Assume a $300,000 purchase, $60,000 down payment, a 30-year term, and a nominal 6% annual rate with monthly compounding.

- Principal L = $240,000.
- Monthly rate r = 0.06/12 = 0.005.
- Payment count n = 30 × 12 = 360.

**A = 240000 × 0.005/[1-(1.005)^(-360)] ≈ $1,438.92 per month.**

The first month's interest is **$1,200.00**; the remainder of the unrounded payment reduces principal. Later installments contain less interest and more principal.

The total scheduled principal-and-interest paid over the term is 360A; scheduled interest is **360A - L**. A mortgage's total financing cost can exceed this amount because fees and other charges may apply.

## 6. Principal and interest versus total housing payment

A household's recurring housing cost can include:

| Component | Included in mathematical A? |
|---|---|
| Mortgage principal | Yes |
| Mortgage interest | Yes |
| Property taxes | No |
| Homeowners insurance | No |
| Mortgage insurance, if applicable | No |
| HOA or condominium dues | No |
| Maintenance and utilities | No |

For an illustrative property with annual property taxes of $3,600 and annual homeowners insurance of $1,200, the monthly tax and insurance allowance is **$400**. Added to the example's approximately $1,438.92 principal-and-interest payment, the estimated monthly amount is **$1,838.92**, excluding other charges.

Escrow amounts can change even when a fixed-rate mortgage's principal-and-interest payment stays constant.

## 7. Fixed-rate and adjustable-rate mortgages

A **fixed-rate mortgage** uses a constant contractual interest rate over the fixed-rate term, so the scheduled principal-and-interest installment is generally level under standard amortization.

An **adjustable-rate mortgage (ARM)** can reset its interest rate at specified dates, subject to contract rules such as indexes, margins, and caps. Its payment calculation must be revisited after resets, and a single fixed-rate annuity formula cannot model every future payment without assumptions about future rates.

This chapter's reference code does not forecast ARM rates.

## 8. Comparing mortgage terms

For the same principal and interest rate, a shorter amortization term normally means a larger monthly payment but less total scheduled interest. A longer term can improve monthly cash flow while increasing lifetime interest.

When comparing actual loan offers, consider rates, APR disclosures, lender fees, points, prepayment terms, and the timing of cash flows. A payment-only comparison can be misleading.

## 9. Mortgage risks and model limitations

The mathematical payment formula assumes timely payments, constant rate, equal intervals, and no additional charges. Real mortgages can include delinquency fees, escrow changes, mortgage insurance, rate resets, special assessments, and prepayment. The lender's official documents govern.

A lower LTV does not guarantee approval or a particular rate. Loan qualification also depends on income, credit, debt obligations, and lender criteria.

The standard-library lab calculates principal from purchase inputs, LTV, fixed-rate mortgage payment, and illustrative monthly housing cost. It includes **12 unit tests** for rate/term sensitivity, payment formulas, principal and financed fees, LTV, cost components, and invalid inputs.

```python
"""Mortgage principal, payment, LTV and housing-cost lab."""
import math
import unittest

def mortgage_payment(principal, annual_nominal_rate, years, frequency=12):
    for v in (principal, annual_nominal_rate, years):
        if isinstance(v,bool) or not isinstance(v,(int,float)) or not math.isfinite(v):
            raise ValueError("finite numeric input required")
    if principal<=0 or annual_nominal_rate<0 or years<=0:
        raise ValueError("principal/years positive, rate nonnegative")
    if isinstance(frequency,bool) or not isinstance(frequency,int) or frequency<1:
        raise ValueError("frequency must be positive integer")
    periods=years*frequency
    if not periods.is_integer():
        raise ValueError("years and frequency must give whole periods")
    n=int(periods)
    r=annual_nominal_rate/frequency
    if r==0: return principal/n
    return principal*r/(-math.expm1(-n*math.log1p(r)))

def loan_to_value(principal, property_value):
    if any(isinstance(v,bool) or not isinstance(v,(int,float)) or not math.isfinite(v) or v<=0 for v in (principal,property_value)):
        raise ValueError("positive finite inputs required")
    return principal/property_value

def loan_from_purchase(price,down_payment,financed_fees=0):
    if any(isinstance(v,bool) or not isinstance(v,(int,float)) or not math.isfinite(v) for v in (price,down_payment,financed_fees)):
        raise ValueError("finite inputs required")
    if price<=0 or down_payment<0 or financed_fees<0 or down_payment>price:
        raise ValueError("invalid purchase inputs")
    loan=price-down_payment+financed_fees
    if loan<=0: raise ValueError("loan principal must be positive")
    return loan

def monthly_housing_cost(pi,annual_tax,annual_insurance,monthly_hoa=0,monthly_mi=0):
    vals=(pi,annual_tax,annual_insurance,monthly_hoa,monthly_mi)
    if any(isinstance(v,bool) or not isinstance(v,(int,float)) or not math.isfinite(v) or v<0 for v in vals):
        raise ValueError("costs must be finite nonnegative")
    return pi+annual_tax/12+annual_insurance/12+monthly_hoa+monthly_mi

class Tests(unittest.TestCase):
    def test_01_zero_rate(self): self.assertEqual(mortgage_payment(1200,0,1),100)
    def test_02_one_year(self):
        self.assertAlmostEqual(mortgage_payment(1200,.12,1),1200*.01/(1-1.01**-12))
    def test_03_thirty_year(self):
        self.assertAlmostEqual(mortgage_payment(250000,.06,30),250000*.005/(1-1.005**-360))
    def test_04_principal(self): self.assertEqual(loan_from_purchase(300000,60000),240000)
    def test_05_fees(self): self.assertEqual(loan_from_purchase(300000,60000,2000),242000)
    def test_06_ltv(self): self.assertAlmostEqual(loan_to_value(240000,300000),.8)
    def test_07_housing_cost(self):
        self.assertEqual(monthly_housing_cost(1500,3600,1200,100,50),2050)
    def test_08_term_sensitivity(self):
        self.assertLess(mortgage_payment(250000,.06,30),mortgage_payment(250000,.06,15))
    def test_09_rate_sensitivity(self):
        self.assertGreater(mortgage_payment(250000,.07,30),mortgage_payment(250000,.06,30))
    def test_10_frequency(self):
        self.assertAlmostEqual(mortgage_payment(1000,.12,1,4),1000*.03/(1-1.03**-4))
    def test_11_zero_down(self): self.assertEqual(loan_from_purchase(100000,0),100000)
    def test_12_invalid(self):
        with self.assertRaises(ValueError): mortgage_payment(0,.06,30)
        with self.assertRaises(ValueError): loan_from_purchase(100000,120000)
        with self.assertRaises(ValueError): loan_to_value(1000,0)
        with self.assertRaises(ValueError): monthly_housing_cost(1000,-100,0)

if __name__=="__main__":
    principal=loan_from_purchase(300000,60000)
    pi=mortgage_payment(principal,.06,30)
    print("Principal:",principal,"LTV:",round(loan_to_value(principal,300000),4))
    print("Principal and interest:",round(pi,2))
    print("Illustrative housing cost:",round(monthly_housing_cost(pi,3600,1200),2))
    unittest.main(verbosity=2)
```

## 11. Exercises with worked answers

1. **Principal:** A $300,000 home has a $60,000 down payment and no financed fees. What is the loan? **Answer:** $240,000.
2. **Financed fees:** Add $2,000 financed fees to Exercise 1. **Answer:** $242,000.
3. **LTV:** Find LTV for $240,000 borrowed against $300,000 property value. **Answer:** 80%.
4. **Payment count:** How many monthly payments are in a 30-year loan? **Answer:** 360.
5. **Periodic rate:** What monthly rate corresponds to nominal annual 6% compounded monthly? **Answer:** 0.5%.
6. **Monthly payment:** Estimate principal-and-interest payment on $240,000 at 6% nominal over 30 years. **Answer:** Approximately $1,438.92.
7. **First interest:** Find the first month's interest on $240,000 at 0.5%. **Answer:** $1,200.
8. **First principal:** Approximate the first month's principal reduction. **Answer:** $1,438.92 - $1,200 ≈ $238.92.
9. **Taxes:** Convert $3,600 annual property taxes to monthly allowance. **Answer:** $300.
10. **Insurance:** Convert $1,200 annual insurance to monthly allowance. **Answer:** $100.
11. **Total housing allowance:** Add Exercises 9 and 10 to the modeled principal-and-interest payment. **Answer:** Approximately $1,838.92 per month, before other costs.
12. **Fixed versus ARM:** Which mortgage can change its interest rate at reset dates? **Answer:** An adjustable-rate mortgage.
13. **Term trade-off:** What generally happens with a longer term at the same principal and rate? **Answer:** Lower monthly payment and higher total scheduled interest.
14. **Scope:** Does the fixed-rate payment formula include escrow or HOA dues? **Answer:** No.
