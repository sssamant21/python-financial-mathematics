## 1. Why prepay a loan?

A borrower may pay more than the required installment to reduce principal faster. Because future interest generally accrues on the remaining principal, an additional principal payment can reduce total interest and shorten the repayment term.

A **scheduled payment** covers the contractually required installment. An **extra principal payment** is an additional amount explicitly applied to the outstanding principal. The allocation matters: an advance payment held for a future installment may not have the same effect as an immediate principal reduction.

## 2. Starting point: a level-payment loan

Let original principal be L, constant effective periodic interest rate r, and original number of end-of-period installments n. The scheduled payment is:

**A = Lr/[1-(1+r)^(-n)]**, for r > 0; **A=L/n** for r=0.

The ordinary balance recurrence without extra payments is:

**B(k) = B(k-1)(1+r) - A.**

For this section, extra payments occur **at the same time as** the scheduled end-of-period installment, after interest for the period is calculated.

## 3. Recurrence with extra principal

Let E(k) be an additional principal payment in period k. Ignoring overpayment, the modified recurrence is:

**B(k) = B(k-1)(1+r) - A(k) - E(k).**

Here A(k) is the actual scheduled installment paid in period k. The final installment may be smaller than A if the remaining amount due is less than the regular installment.

To avoid a negative balance:

**Scheduled(k) = min(A, B(k-1)(1+r)).**

**Extra(k) = min(requested extra, max(0, B(k-1)(1+r)-Scheduled(k))).**

**B(k) = max(0, B(k-1)(1+r)-Scheduled(k)-Extra(k)).**

These equations apply to the educational simulation, which has no fees, penalties, irregular accrual dates, or payment-processing delays.

## 4. Worked example

A borrower takes a $10,000 loan at 0.5% monthly interest for 24 months.

The original scheduled payment is:

**A = 10000 × 0.005/[1-(1.005)^(-24)] ≈ $443.21.**

Without prepayment, the schedule lasts 24 installments. If the borrower instead adds **$100 of principal every month**, the first-period calculation is:

- Opening balance: $10,000.
- Interest: $50.
- Scheduled installment: approximately $443.21.
- Extra principal: $100.
- Total payment: approximately $543.21.
- Closing balance: approximately $9,506.79.

The extra $100 lowers the next month's interest because the balance is $100 lower than under the original schedule. The simulation calculates the shortened payoff date and interest savings without assuming the final payment is a full installment.

## 5. Comparing strategies

Two useful scenarios are:

**Preserve payment, shorten term.** Continue the original scheduled payment after each extra principal contribution. The debt is repaid earlier, and total interest is generally lower.

**Recast or re-amortize.** Subject to lender approval and contract terms, the payment is recalculated over a remaining term after a principal reduction. This can lower future required payments but does not necessarily shorten the contractual maturity.

The reference lab models the first strategy only. It does not automatically recast the loan.

## 6. Prepayment timing matters

An extra principal payment earlier in the schedule typically saves more interest than the same payment made later, all else equal, because the reduced balance affects more future interest-accrual periods.

A one-time prepayment and recurring prepayments are different strategies. The timing, amount, and whether the borrower continues regular payments must be explicit.

## 7. Measuring savings

Run two schedules under identical assumptions:

- **Baseline:** no extra principal.
- **Prepayment:** specified extra principal and start period.

Then compute:

**Interest savings = Baseline interest - Prepayment interest.**

**Periods saved = Baseline payoff period - Prepayment payoff period.**

**Total cash paid = Original principal + Actual interest**, in this no-fee model.

A prepayment is not automatically the best use of cash. Liquidity, emergency reserves, other debt rates, tax considerations, investment risk, and any prepayment penalty may matter. This chapter illustrates the loan mathematics rather than giving personalized financial advice.

## 8. Reconciliation and controls

A correct simulation should verify that:

1. Interest equals the opening balance multiplied by the periodic rate.
2. Actual payment equals scheduled plus extra principal.
3. Closing balance equals opening balance plus interest minus actual payment.
4. Total principal reduction equals original principal at payoff.
5. Final balance is zero within numerical tolerance.
6. No installment exceeds the amount due under the model.

Real loans may have prepayment restrictions, minimum amounts, or different posting rules. Always check contractual terms.

```python
"""Extra-payment amortization: preserve regular payment and shorten term."""
import math
import unittest

def payment(L,r,n):
    if isinstance(L,bool) or not isinstance(L,(int,float)) or not math.isfinite(L) or L<=0: raise ValueError("invalid principal")
    if isinstance(r,bool) or not isinstance(r,(int,float)) or not math.isfinite(r) or r<0: raise ValueError("invalid rate")
    if isinstance(n,bool) or not isinstance(n,int) or n<1: raise ValueError("invalid term")
    return L/n if r==0 else L*r/(-math.expm1(-n*math.log1p(r)))

def simulate(L,r,n,extra=0,first_extra_period=1):
    A=payment(L,r,n)
    if isinstance(extra,bool) or not isinstance(extra,(int,float)) or not math.isfinite(extra) or extra<0:
        raise ValueError("extra must be finite and nonnegative")
    if isinstance(first_extra_period,bool) or not isinstance(first_extra_period,int) or first_extra_period<1:
        raise ValueError("invalid extra start")
    B=L
    rows=[]
    for k in range(1,n+1):
        opening=B
        interest=opening*r
        due=opening+interest
        scheduled=min(A,due)
        additional=min(extra,max(0.0,due-scheduled)) if k>=first_extra_period else 0.0
        paid=scheduled+additional
        B=max(0.0,due-paid)
        rows.append(dict(period=k,opening=opening,interest=interest,
                         scheduled=scheduled,extra=additional,payment=paid,closing=B))
        if B<=1e-9:
            rows[-1]["closing"]=0.0
            break
    return rows

def metrics(L,r,n,extra=0,first_extra_period=1):
    rows=simulate(L,r,n,extra,first_extra_period)
    return dict(periods=len(rows),interest=math.fsum(x["interest"] for x in rows),
                paid=math.fsum(x["payment"] for x in rows),
                final_balance=rows[-1]["closing"])

class Tests(unittest.TestCase):
    def test_01_baseline_periods(self): self.assertEqual(metrics(10000,.005,24)["periods"],24)
    def test_02_baseline_final(self): self.assertEqual(metrics(10000,.005,24)["final_balance"],0)
    def test_03_extra_shortens(self): self.assertLess(metrics(10000,.005,24,100)["periods"],24)
    def test_04_extra_saves_interest(self):
        self.assertLess(metrics(10000,.005,24,100)["interest"],metrics(10000,.005,24)["interest"])
    def test_05_principal_reconciliation(self):
        rows=simulate(10000,.005,24,100)
        self.assertAlmostEqual(sum(x["payment"]-x["interest"] for x in rows),10000)
    def test_06_payment_identity(self):
        for x in simulate(10000,.005,24,100):
            self.assertAlmostEqual(x["payment"],x["scheduled"]+x["extra"])
    def test_07_balance_identity(self):
        for x in simulate(10000,.005,24,100):
            self.assertAlmostEqual(x["closing"],x["opening"]+x["interest"]-x["payment"],places=7)
    def test_08_zero_rate(self):
        self.assertLess(metrics(1200,0,12,25)["periods"],12)
        self.assertEqual(metrics(1200,0,12,25)["interest"],0)
    def test_09_delayed_extra(self):
        self.assertGreaterEqual(metrics(10000,.005,24,100,12)["interest"],metrics(10000,.005,24,100,1)["interest"])
    def test_10_large_extra(self):
        self.assertEqual(metrics(1000,.01,12,2000)["periods"],1)
    def test_11_no_overpayment(self):
        rows=simulate(1000,.01,12,2000)
        self.assertAlmostEqual(rows[-1]["payment"],1010)
    def test_12_validation(self):
        for args in ((1000,.01,12,-1),(1000,.01,12,float("nan"))):
            with self.assertRaises(ValueError): simulate(*args)
        with self.assertRaises(ValueError): simulate(1000,.01,12,10,0)

if __name__=="__main__":
    for extra in (0,50,100):
        print(extra,metrics(10000,.005,24,extra))
    unittest.main(verbosity=2)
```

## 10. Exercises with worked answers

1. **Definition:** What is an extra principal payment? **Answer:** Money applied directly to principal beyond the scheduled installment.
2. **Original payment:** State the formula for A. **Answer:** A=Lr/[1-(1+r)^(-n)] for r>0.
3. **Modified recurrence:** Write the balance update with extra E(k). **Answer:** B(k)=B(k-1)(1+r)-A(k)-E(k).
4. **First interest:** What is first-period interest on $10,000 at 0.5%? **Answer:** $50.
5. **First total payment:** With a $443.21 scheduled payment and $100 extra, what is paid? **Answer:** Approximately $543.21.
6. **First principal:** How much of Exercise 5 reduces principal? **Answer:** Approximately $493.21.
7. **First balance:** Find the approximate new balance. **Answer:** $9,506.79.
8. **Interest effect:** Why does an extra principal payment lower future interest? **Answer:** Future interest is calculated on a smaller balance.
9. **Term effect:** What generally happens when regular installments are preserved? **Answer:** The loan pays off earlier.
10. **Recast:** Does a recast necessarily shorten the term? **Answer:** No; it often recalculates a lower installment over the remaining term.
11. **Savings:** How is interest savings calculated? **Answer:** Baseline total interest minus prepayment-scenario total interest.
12. **Early versus late:** Which identical prepayment generally saves more interest? **Answer:** The earlier payment, all else equal.
13. **Last payment:** Why cap the final payment? **Answer:** To prevent paying more than the remaining principal plus accrued interest.
14. **Contract:** What should a borrower verify before prepaying? **Answer:** Application to principal, timing, fees or penalties, and lender processing rules.
