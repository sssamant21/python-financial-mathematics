## Learning objectives

Distinguish the stated note rate from the economic cost of borrowing; derive the internal-rate-of-return (IRR) equation for a loan with upfront fees; compute the periodic borrowing rate and annualize it correctly; explain APR limitations; and implement a numerically robust Python solver with reconciliations.

## 1. The stated rate is not always the true cost

A loan agreement may quote a nominal interest rate, but the borrower may receive less than the face amount because of upfront origination fees or discount points. The borrower still repays the contractual principal and interest. Consequently, the effective cost of the cash actually received may exceed the stated interest rate.

The key principle is to analyze **borrower cash flows**, not merely the note rate.

```text
Time 0                  Months 1 ... n
Borrower receives       Borrower pays
+ net proceeds          - scheduled installments
```

## 2. Borrower cash-flow convention

Assume a loan face amount L, upfront fees F withheld from proceeds, constant periodic note rate r, and n end-of-period installments A.

**Net cash received at origination = L - F.**

The payment obligation is still computed on the contractual loan principal:

**A = Lr/[1-(1+r)^(-n)]** for r > 0, or **A = L/n** for r = 0.

The cash-flow yield q is the rate that equates net proceeds to the present value of repayments:

**L - F = sum from k=1 to n of A/(1+q)^k.**

For q > 0, equivalently:

**L - F = A[1-(1+q)^(-n)]/q.**

Here q is the **effective periodic borrowing cost** under this cash-flow model. It is not automatically the legally disclosed APR.

## 3. Why upfront fees raise the effective rate

At the note rate r, the present value of the scheduled payments equals L. If F > 0, the borrower receives only L-F, which is less than that present value. To discount the same positive payments to the smaller amount, the implied rate q must be higher than r.

Therefore, under the stated assumptions:

**F > 0 implies q > r.**

If there are no fees and no other cash-flow differences, q=r.

## 4. Worked example

Consider a $10,000, 24-month loan with a monthly note rate of 0.5% and $200 in upfront fees withheld from the proceeds.

- Contractual principal: $10,000.
- Net proceeds: $9,800.
- Scheduled payment: **A = 10000 × 0.005/[1-(1.005)^(-24)] ≈ $443.21**.
- Effective monthly borrowing rate q solves:

**9800 = sum from k=1 to 24 of A/(1+q)^k.**

The Python lab solves q by bisection and verifies that the discounted repayment stream reconstructs $9,800.

A borrower should not simply divide total fees by the number of months and add that number to the note rate. Cash-flow timing matters.

## 5. Annualizing the periodic cost

For m equal compounding/payment periods per year and periodic cost q:

**Nominal annualized rate = m × q.**

**Effective annual rate = (1+q)^m - 1.**

For monthly periods, m=12. These are distinct measures. The effective annual rate reflects compounding and exceeds the nominal annualized rate when q>0 and m>1.

Use the effective annual rate when comparing economically equivalent annual returns or borrowing costs with different compounding frequencies, provided the cash-flow assumptions are consistent.

## 6. APR and disclosure caution

A regulatory **annual percentage rate (APR)** may incorporate specified finance charges and is computed according to applicable disclosure rules, including timing and fee classifications. The simple IRR annualization in this chapter is an **educational economic borrowing-cost measure**, not a substitute for an official APR calculation or a lender disclosure.

For real mortgage comparisons, use the lender's Loan Estimate and applicable APR disclosure, and review which costs are included or excluded.

## 7. Additional costs and complications

Borrowing costs can also involve recurring servicing fees, mortgage insurance, points, financed fees, irregular dates, balloon payments, prepayment penalties, and variable rates.

To model them, place each actual borrower cash flow at its actual date and solve for the rate that sets their net present value to zero. The level-payment formula used here is only valid for equally spaced installments.

**Financed fees** are different from fees withheld from proceeds: financed fees increase the debt principal and therefore alter the scheduled payment and payoff balance. Avoid double-counting them.

## 8. Numerical solution

For fixed positive installments, the present value of payments decreases as the discount rate increases. This makes **bisection** a robust choice when the root is bracketed.

Algorithm:

1. Calculate the contractual payment A.
2. Calculate net proceeds L-F.
3. Define PV(q) as the discounted repayment stream.
4. Start with a lower rate equal to the note rate when F≥0.
5. Increase an upper bound until PV(upper) is below net proceeds.
6. Bisect until the interval is sufficiently small.
7. Reconcile PV(q) against net proceeds.

The implementation uses `math.log1p` and `math.expm1` to improve numerical stability near zero interest.

The standard-library reference code calculates net proceeds, contractual payments, an implied monthly borrowing cost, nominal annualized cost, and effective annual cost. It includes **12 unit tests** covering no-fee equality, fee sensitivity, PV reconciliation, annualization, zero-interest loans, and invalid inputs.

```python
"""Effective borrowing cost from borrower cash flows; no third-party packages."""
import math
import unittest

def payment(principal, rate, periods):
    if isinstance(principal,bool) or not isinstance(principal,(int,float)) or not math.isfinite(principal) or principal<=0:
        raise ValueError("positive finite principal required")
    if isinstance(rate,bool) or not isinstance(rate,(int,float)) or not math.isfinite(rate) or rate<0:
        raise ValueError("nonnegative finite rate required")
    if isinstance(periods,bool) or not isinstance(periods,int) or periods<1:
        raise ValueError("positive integer periods required")
    return principal/periods if rate==0 else principal*rate/(-math.expm1(-periods*math.log1p(rate)))

def net_proceeds(principal, prepaid_fees):
    if isinstance(prepaid_fees,bool) or not isinstance(prepaid_fees,(int,float)) or not math.isfinite(prepaid_fees) or prepaid_fees<0 or prepaid_fees>=principal:
        raise ValueError("fees must be finite, nonnegative, and less than principal")
    payment(principal,0,1)
    return principal-prepaid_fees

def pv_level(amount, rate, periods):
    if rate==0: return amount*periods
    return amount*(-math.expm1(-periods*math.log1p(rate)))/rate

def periodic_irr(principal, note_rate, periods, prepaid_fees=0):
    proceeds=net_proceeds(principal,prepaid_fees)
    amount=payment(principal,note_rate,periods)
    if prepaid_fees==0: return note_rate
    lo=note_rate
    hi=max(.01,2*note_rate+.01)
    while pv_level(amount,hi,periods)>proceeds:
        hi=2*hi+0.01
        if not math.isfinite(hi): raise ValueError("could not bracket IRR")
    for _ in range(180):
        mid=(lo+hi)/2
        if pv_level(amount,mid,periods)>proceeds: lo=mid
        else: hi=mid
    return (lo+hi)/2

def annual_rates(periodic_rate, periods_per_year=12):
    if isinstance(periods_per_year,bool) or not isinstance(periods_per_year,int) or periods_per_year<1:
        raise ValueError("positive integer frequency required")
    if not math.isfinite(periodic_rate) or periodic_rate<0: raise ValueError("invalid rate")
    return (periodic_rate*periods_per_year,
            math.expm1(periods_per_year*math.log1p(periodic_rate)))

class Tests(unittest.TestCase):
    def test_01_zero_fee(self): self.assertAlmostEqual(periodic_irr(10000,.005,24),.005)
    def test_02_fee_increases_cost(self): self.assertGreater(periodic_irr(10000,.005,24,200),.005)
    def test_03_more_fee(self): self.assertGreater(periodic_irr(10000,.005,24,300),periodic_irr(10000,.005,24,100))
    def test_04_pv_reconciles(self):
        r=periodic_irr(10000,.005,24,200)
        self.assertAlmostEqual(pv_level(payment(10000,.005,24),r,24),9800,places=7)
    def test_05_proceeds(self): self.assertEqual(net_proceeds(10000,200),9800)
    def test_06_nominal(self): self.assertAlmostEqual(annual_rates(.01)[0],.12)
    def test_07_effective(self): self.assertAlmostEqual(annual_rates(.01)[1],1.01**12-1)
    def test_08_effective_gt_nominal(self):
        a,b=annual_rates(.01)
        self.assertGreater(b,a)
    def test_09_zero_rate_with_fee(self): self.assertGreater(periodic_irr(1200,0,12,50),0)
    def test_10_zero_rate_no_fee(self): self.assertEqual(periodic_irr(1200,0,12),0)
    def test_11_zero_rate_pv(self): self.assertEqual(pv_level(100,0,12),1200)
    def test_12_invalid(self):
        for fees in (-1,1000,float("nan")):
            with self.assertRaises(ValueError): periodic_irr(1000,.01,12,fees)
        with self.assertRaises(ValueError): annual_rates(.01,0)

if __name__=="__main__":
    r=periodic_irr(10000,.005,24,200)
    nominal,effective=annual_rates(r)
    print("Net proceeds:",net_proceeds(10000,200))
    print("Periodic cost:",round(r,8))
    print("Annualized nominal:",round(nominal,6))
    print("Effective annual:",round(effective,6))
    unittest.main(verbosity=2)
```

## 10. Exercises with worked answers

1. **Net proceeds:** What does a borrower receive on a $10,000 loan with $200 withheld upfront? **Answer:** $9,800.
2. **Payment basis:** Is the scheduled installment calculated on $9,800 or $10,000 in this model? **Answer:** $10,000.
3. **Rate equation:** State the effective borrowing-cost equation. **Answer:** L-F = sum of A/(1+q)^k over all installments.
4. **Zero fees:** What is q if F=0 and all other assumptions hold? **Answer:** q=r.
5. **Fee effect:** If F increases while the contractual payment stays fixed, what happens to q? **Answer:** It increases.
6. **Nominal annualization:** Convert 1% monthly to nominal annualized rate. **Answer:** 12%.
7. **Effective annualization:** Convert 1% monthly to effective annual rate. **Answer:** (1.01)^12-1 ≈ 12.6825%.
8. **Comparison:** Which rate is larger in Exercise 7? **Answer:** The effective annual rate.
9. **Zero note rate:** Can a zero-interest loan have positive effective borrowing cost? **Answer:** Yes, if positive fees reduce net proceeds.
10. **Numerical solver:** Why is bisection appropriate? **Answer:** Discounted PV decreases monotonically with the rate for positive fixed payments.
11. **Validation:** What identity should the solved q satisfy? **Answer:** Discounted scheduled payments equal net proceeds.
12. **APR:** Is the educational IRR necessarily the legally disclosed APR? **Answer:** No; disclosure rules can differ.
13. **Financed fees:** What happens if fees are added to loan principal? **Answer:** The contractual debt and typically the installment increase.
14. **Irregular dates:** Can a single equal-period annuity formula handle irregular cash-flow dates exactly? **Answer:** No; date-specific discounting is required.
