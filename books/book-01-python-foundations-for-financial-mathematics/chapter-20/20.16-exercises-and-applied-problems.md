## How to use this section

Work each problem before reading its solution. Examples are synthetic. Amounts share a currency and units; rates are effective per period unless stated otherwise. Exact mathematical results and floating-point approximations are distinguished. The examples are educational, not financial advice or evidence of production readiness.

## Part A — Tests and financial benchmarks

### 1. Known value

**Problem:** A payment of 110 is due one period from now. Find its present value at 10% and propose a test.

**Solution:** \(110/1.1=100\) in exact arithmetic. Compare the computed result with 100 using an appropriate absolute tolerance, such as `1e-10` for this small illustrative example.

### 2. Time zero

**Problem:** What is the NPV of `[-100, 110]` at 10% when the first element is at time zero?

**Solution:** \(-100+110/1.1=0\) in exact arithmetic. An implementation that discounts both entries has used a different timeline.

### 3. Two periods

**Problem:** Find the NPV of `[-100, 60, 60]` at 10%.

**Solution:** \(-100+60/1.1+60/1.1^2\approx4.132231405\). A benchmark should be independently derived and recorded with its convention.

### 4. Zero rate

**Problem:** What should periodic NPV equal when the rate is zero?

**Solution:** The sum of the cash flows in exact arithmetic. Floating-point summation may differ slightly because of rounding.

### 5. Negative rates

**Problem:** Find the present value of 95 due in one period at −5%.

**Solution:** \(95/0.95=100\) in exact arithmetic. A negative rate above −1 is valid for this periodic formula.

### 6. Boundary

**Problem:** Why reject a periodic rate of −1?

**Solution:** The discount denominator is zero. Rates below −1 are outside the stated model domain as well.

### 7. Independent expected value

**Problem:** Why is using the function under test to generate its own expected answer weak?

**Solution:** The same defect can affect both the result and expected value. Use independently derived values, a separately reviewed benchmark or relevant mathematical properties.

### 8. Assertion behavior

**Problem:** Is `assert` a suitable sole mechanism for validating public inputs?

**Solution:** No. Assertions can be disabled with Python optimization. Explicit checks and documented exceptions are appropriate for required input validation.

### 9. Unit test

**Problem:** What would a unit test for one valuation function isolate?

**Solution:** Its specified behavior for selected inputs, including valid values and failures, without requiring unrelated services.

### 10. Regression test

**Problem:** A defect discounts the initial cash flow. What test prevents its return?

**Solution:** Add a case with a nonzero initial flow and an independently derived expected NPV, such as exercise 2. Document the original defect.

## Part B — Numerics, invariants and contracts

### 11. Tolerance around zero

**Problem:** Why compare a near-zero NPV with an absolute tolerance?

**Solution:** Relative tolerance alone does not allow a nonzero difference from zero. Choose an absolute tolerance in output units appropriate to the use.

### 12. Tolerance choice

**Problem:** Does `abs_tol=1e-10` suit every currency amount and application?

**Solution:** No. Consider units, scale, algorithms, input uncertainty and materiality. A numerical tolerance is not automatically a business acceptance threshold.

### 13. Floating-point equality

**Problem:** Why can `0.1 + 0.2 == 0.3` be false?

**Solution:** Those decimal fractions generally do not have exact finite binary floating-point representations; intermediate and stored values are rounded.

### 14. Decimal construction

**Problem:** Why prefer `Decimal("0.1")` to `Decimal(0.1)` when the intended input is exactly one tenth?

**Solution:** The string represents the intended decimal value. Constructing from a float captures the float's existing binary approximation.

### 15. Rounding policy

**Problem:** Two amounts of `Decimal("0.005")` are each rounded to cents with `ROUND_HALF_UP`. Compare rounding each first with rounding the sum once.

**Solution:** Each rounds to `0.01`, totaling `0.02`; the exact sum is `0.010`, which rounds to `0.01`. The required stage and rounding mode must be specified.

### 16. Scaling

**Problem:** At a fixed rate, what is the relationship between the NPV of twice each cash flow and the original NPV?

**Solution:** It doubles in exact arithmetic. Allow for floating-point rounding and ensure the scaled values remain within the supported domain.

### 17. Additivity

**Problem:** At the same rate and timeline, how does the NPV of the elementwise sum of two schedules relate to their individual NPVs?

**Solution:** It equals their sum in exact arithmetic. Align time periods and units first; floating-point results may differ slightly.

### 18. Monotonicity

**Problem:** Does a positive future payment's present value fall when the rate increases above −1?

**Solution:** Yes, for a fixed positive payment and positive number of periods. Mixed-sign schedules need not have monotonic total NPV.

### 19. Invalid numbers

**Problem:** Why reject `NaN` and infinities for this library's inputs?

**Solution:** They violate its finite-input contract and can propagate into misleading output or comparisons. Failure should be explicit.

### 20. Type boundary

**Problem:** Section 20.15 accepts only built-in floats. Are `True` and `100` accepted?

**Solution:** No. This is a deliberate narrow runtime contract. Type annotations alone do not enforce it.

## Part C — Errors, logs and reproducibility

### 21. Empty schedule

**Problem:** What should the project function do for an empty list?

**Solution:** Raise the documented `ValueError`. Returning zero would violate its nonempty-input contract.

### 22. Exception handling

**Problem:** Why is `except Exception: return 0.0` dangerous around valuation?

**Solution:** It hides the cause and makes a failure indistinguishable from a legitimate zero valuation. Catch expected errors at an appropriate boundary and report failure.

### 23. Exception test

**Problem:** How can a unit test check that a rate of −1 raises `ValueError`?

**Solution:** Use `with self.assertRaises(ValueError):` around the call, and ensure the tested input otherwise satisfies the contract.

### 24. Overflow

**Problem:** Can finite inputs produce a nonfinite intermediate?

**Solution:** Yes. For instance, `1e308 / 0.5` exceeds the usual finite float range. Section 20.15 rejects a nonfinite intermediate.

### 25. Underflow

**Problem:** Does a finite output mean every small cash-flow contribution was represented accurately?

**Solution:** No. Underflow, rounding and cancellation can lose contributions; numerical review must assess the intended range.

### 26. Logging

**Problem:** Should a reusable financial library configure the application's root logger?

**Solution:** Generally leave configuration to the application. Log only appropriate metadata, avoid secrets and sensitive cash flows, and follow organizational policy.

### 27. Reproducibility

**Problem:** What evidence should accompany a test run?

**Solution:** Record the source revision, command, actual output, Python and dependency versions, platform and relevant configuration. Store evidence securely.

### 28. Randomness

**Problem:** Does recording a seed alone guarantee the same simulation results on every platform and software version?

**Solution:** No. Record the generator, implementation, versions and environment as well; reproducibility depends on their behavior.

### 29. Documentation

**Problem:** What minimum financial information should an NPV docstring convey?

**Solution:** Timing, rate convention, units, input domain, return value, exceptions and important numerical or model limitations.

### 30. Input ownership

**Problem:** Why test that an input list remains unchanged?

**Solution:** A caller may reuse it. Unexpected mutation can contaminate later calculations and break reproducibility.

## Part D — Model review and applied problems

### 31. Software versus model

**Problem:** If a function correctly discounts a promised payment, is it necessarily suitable for valuing a risky bond?

**Solution:** No. Credit, recovery, liquidity, taxes and other assumptions may matter. Software verification and model suitability require distinct evidence.

### 32. Coverage

**Problem:** Does 100% line coverage prove financial correctness?

**Solution:** No. Coverage shows which lines executed, not whether assertions are meaningful or all financial cases and assumptions are correct.

### 33. Review independence

**Problem:** Why independently review benchmarks and assumptions?

**Solution:** A separate reviewer can challenge choices and detect shared implementation or specification errors. Appropriate independence depends on the context and risk.

### 34. Period conversion

**Problem:** Convert an effective annual rate of 12% to an equivalent effective monthly rate.

**Solution:** \((1.12)^{1/12}-1\approx0.00948879\), or about 0.948879% per month. Dividing 12% by 12 is not the same effective-rate conversion.

### 35. Sign and timeline

**Problem:** An investment pays 121 at the end of year two after an initial outflow of 100. NPV at 10% annually?

**Solution:** \(-100+121/1.1^2=0\) in exact arithmetic. The time-one cash flow is zero: `[-100.0, 0.0, 121.0]`.

### 36. Sensitivity

**Problem:** A positive payment of 100 is due in one period. Compare values at 5% and 10%.

**Solution:** Approximately 95.238095 and 90.909091, respectively. The higher rate gives the lower present value.

### 37. Benchmark discrepancy

**Problem:** A reference system gives a different NPV. What should be checked before increasing tolerance?

**Solution:** Reconcile time-zero treatment, timing, rate frequency and compounding, signs, currency, rounding, input data and model scope. Investigate genuine differences.

### 38. Change control

**Problem:** A release changes rounding or the interpretation of the first cash flow. What is needed?

**Solution:** Assess compatibility and financial impact, update specification and versioning, add independent tests, document migration, and obtain reviews required by policy.

### 39. Production release

**Problem:** Do these exercises and reference tests certify a production library?

**Solution:** No. Readiness requires documented requirements, independent review, appropriate testing, controlled packaging, operational ownership and applicable approvals.

## Twelve Python reference checks

Save as `chapter20_reference_checks.py`. These self-contained checks use standard-library `unittest` and a small one-period teaching function; they do not test the Section 20.15 package. Execute both suites during deferred QA. Tolerances below are illustrative, and no test definition certifies the model.

```python
import math
import unittest
from decimal import Decimal, ROUND_HALF_UP


def pv_one(payment: float, rate: float) -> float:
    """Discount one finite float payment one period; require rate > -1."""
    if type(payment) is not float or not math.isfinite(payment):
        raise ValueError("payment must be a finite float")
    if type(rate) is not float or not math.isfinite(rate) or rate <= -1.0:
        raise ValueError("rate must be a finite float greater than -1")
    result = payment / (1.0 + rate)
    if not math.isfinite(result):
        raise ValueError("nonfinite result")
    return result


class Chapter20Checks(unittest.TestCase):
    def test_known_value(self):
        self.assertTrue(math.isclose(pv_one(110.0, 0.1), 100.0,
                                     rel_tol=0.0, abs_tol=1e-10))

    def test_zero_rate(self):
        self.assertEqual(pv_one(100.0, 0.0), 100.0)

    def test_negative_rate(self):
        self.assertTrue(math.isclose(pv_one(95.0, -0.05), 100.0,
                                     rel_tol=0.0, abs_tol=1e-10))

    def test_zero_payment(self):
        self.assertEqual(pv_one(0.0, 0.1), 0.0)

    def test_negative_payment(self):
        self.assertTrue(math.isclose(pv_one(-110.0, 0.1), -100.0,
                                     rel_tol=0.0, abs_tol=1e-10))

    def test_scaling(self):
        self.assertTrue(math.isclose(pv_one(200.0, 0.1), 2 * pv_one(100.0, 0.1),
                                     rel_tol=1e-12, abs_tol=1e-10))

    def test_positive_payment_rate_monotonicity(self):
        self.assertGreater(pv_one(100.0, 0.05), pv_one(100.0, 0.10))

    def test_rate_boundary(self):
        with self.assertRaises(ValueError):
            pv_one(100.0, -1.0)

    def test_nonfinite_amount(self):
        with self.assertRaises(ValueError):
            pv_one(float("nan"), 0.1)

    def test_nonfinite_rate(self):
        with self.assertRaises(ValueError):
            pv_one(100.0, float("inf"))

    def test_boolean_rejected(self):
        with self.assertRaises(ValueError):
            pv_one(100.0, True)

    def test_decimal_rounding_stage(self):
        cent = Decimal("0.01")
        x = Decimal("0.005")
        individually = sum((x.quantize(cent, rounding=ROUND_HALF_UP) for _ in range(2)),
                           Decimal("0"))
        combined = (x + x).quantize(cent, rounding=ROUND_HALF_UP)
        self.assertEqual(individually, Decimal("0.02"))
        self.assertEqual(combined, Decimal("0.01"))


if __name__ == "__main__":
    unittest.main(verbosity=2)
```

## Troubleshooting

| Symptom | Possible cause | Next step |
|---|---|---|
| Benchmark differs | Rate, timing or sign conventions differ | Reconcile the specification and independent calculation |
| Near-zero check fails | Inappropriate tolerance or arithmetic error | Investigate the discrepancy before revising tolerance |
| `ValueError` | Input violates the function contract | Inspect type, finiteness and rate domain |
| Decimal result differs | Rounding mode or stage differs | Specify context and policy and repeat the calculation |
| Checks pass but model is unsuitable | Simplifying assumptions omit material risks | Review intended use and limitations independently |
