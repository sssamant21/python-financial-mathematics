## Learning objectives

Distinguish price return from total return, calculate cash-dividend yield, reconcile cash and reinvested dividends, account for transaction costs and stock splits, construct linked total-return series, and validate Python implementations.

## 1. Price return is not total return

An equity investor can earn wealth through **price appreciation** and **distributions**. Price-only performance ignores cash dividends and can understate investment performance.

For beginning share price P0 > 0 and ending share price P1 ≥ 0:

**Price return = (P1 − P0)/P0.**

For a single holding period with a dividend D received as cash, and no external capital flows or dividend reinvestment:

**Gross-of-cost total return = (P1 − P0 + D)/P0.**

If investor-borne costs C are not already included in the values:

**Net total return = (P1 − P0 + D − C)/P0.**

The phrase **gross-of-cost** describes the fee basis; it must not be confused with the **gross return factor** 1+R.

### Worked example A — Price and total return

A share costs $100, finishes at $105, and pays a $3 dividend.

- Dollar price gain: $5.
- Price return: 5/100 = **5%**.
- Dividend contribution: 3/100 = **3%**.
- Total return: (5+3)/100 = **8%**.

If a $1 fee is separately paid and not already reflected in the reported ending value, net return is **7%**.

## 2. Dividend yield conventions

For a dividend D received during the period, the **realized dividend contribution relative to beginning price** is:

**y_D = D/P0**.

For a $3 dividend on a $100 beginning price, y_D=3%.

This is distinct from a *forward indicated annual dividend yield*, which estimates future annual dividends relative to a current market price, or a *trailing dividend yield*, which uses historical distributions over a defined lookback. The numerator's timing and the denominator's price date must be disclosed.

For a period with no reinvestment or external cash flows:

**Total simple return = Price simple return + Dividend contribution − Cost contribution.**

## 3. Ex-dividend price behavior

On an ex-dividend date, a share begins trading without entitlement to the announced dividend. Other things equal, its price may decline by roughly the dividend amount. A raw price series can therefore show an apparent loss even though the investor has received a corresponding cash distribution.

### Worked example B — Dividend-adjusted wealth

A share is worth $100 before a $2 ex-dividend adjustment and $98 afterward, ignoring other market effects.

**Raw price return = −2%.**

**Total return = (98−100+2)/100 = 0%.**

The dividend is not free additional wealth; part of the shareholder's investment value has shifted from the share price to cash.

Actual ex-dividend market prices also reflect other trading forces, taxes, and market conditions; an exact $2 adjustment is an illustration, not a prediction.

## 4. Cash dividends versus reinvestment

If dividends remain as **cash**, end-of-period wealth is the ending share value plus cash received, subject to any cash interest, taxes, or costs.

If dividends are **reinvested**, the investor acquires additional shares at the reinvestment price. Later price changes affect those additional shares.

Suppose an investor owns 10 shares, each initially costing $50. A $2 dividend per share is reinvested when the share price is $50.

**Dividend cash = 10 × $2 = $20.**

**New shares = $20/$50 = 0.4 shares.**

**Total shares = 10.4.**

If the final price becomes $55:

**Final wealth = 10.4 × $55 = $572.**

**Initial wealth = 10 × $50 = $500.**

**Reinvested total return = 572/500−1 = 14.4%.**

By contrast, if the investor retained the $20 as idle cash:

**Final wealth = 10×$55+$20 = $570**, or **14%**.

The 0.4 percentage-point difference comes from the later price appreciation on the newly purchased shares. No dividend should be added again after it has already been reinvested into share count.

## 5. Multiple dividends and total-return indices

When distributions occur during a multi-period horizon, a robust approach is to compute each subperiod's total return with an explicit distribution/reinvestment convention and then link gross factors:

**G_total = ∏(1+R_t)**.

**R_total = G_total−1.**

A **total-return index** is often initialized at 100:

**TRI_0=100**, and **TRI_t=TRI_(t−1)(1+R_total,t)**.

For total returns +8% and −2%:

**TRI: 100 → 108 → 105.84**.

**Cumulative total return = 5.84%.**

The return index assumes the same economic exposure is carried forward under the defined reinvestment convention. For multiple distributions and dates, construct subperiods carefully rather than adding all dividends to a single price ratio without addressing timing.

## 6. Corporate actions: stock splits

A stock split changes share count and quoted price without necessarily changing shareholder wealth.

In a **2-for-1 split**, one share becomes two shares. If the pre-split price is $100 and the post-split price is $50, the investor's economic position remains $100.

**Naive raw price return = 50/100−1 = −50%.**

**Split-adjusted wealth return = (2×50)/100−1 = 0%.**

If the post-split price rises to $55, the adjusted return is:

**(2×55)/100−1 = 10%.**

Corporate-action adjustment must also consider rights, spin-offs, special dividends, and other events. Price adjustments and dividend adjustments must not be applied twice.

## 7. Bond coupons, fund distributions, and total return

The same principle applies beyond common shares:

- **Bonds:** coupon income and price movement both affect total return; accrued interest and clean/dirty pricing require consistent treatment.
- **Mutual funds and ETFs:** NAV or market price may drop when distributions are paid; a distribution-adjusted series can capture total wealth changes.
- **REITs:** cash distributions may include economically different components; reporting conventions should be explicit.
- **Foreign assets:** convert both market value and distributions into the investor's reporting currency using the relevant FX conventions.

The precise calculation depends on cash-flow timing, valuation, withholding taxes, and the definition of net versus gross performance.

## 8. Taxes, fees, and withholding

A performance report must identify whether returns are:

- Before or after investment management fees.
- Before or after transaction costs.
- Before or after dividend withholding taxes.
- Before or after investor-specific income or capital-gains taxes.

A cash dividend of $3 with $0.45 withholding delivers $2.55 of net cash to the investor. If the price rises from $100 to $105, and there are no other costs:

**After-withholding total return = (105−100+2.55)/100 = 7.55%.**

Never subtract a withholding tax again if the dividend input is already net of withholding.

## 9. External contributions and withdrawals

Investor contributions are **not dividends**. A $5,000 new deposit increases portfolio assets but does not constitute an earned investment return.

For portfolios with external cash flows, use properly segmented **time-weighted return** calculations or an appropriate **money-weighted return** measure. These are developed in Sections 13.9 and 13.10.

A simple beginning-to-ending value formula that ignores contributions and withdrawals can produce severely misleading performance figures.

The lab calculates price and total returns, cash-dividend yield, share reinvestment, split-adjusted returns, and multi-period linking. It includes **12 reference unit tests**. Independent runtime verification remains pending.

```python
import math
import unittest

def finite(x, name):
    if isinstance(x, bool) or not isinstance(x, (int, float)) or not math.isfinite(x):
        raise ValueError(f"{name} must be finite numeric")

def holding_period_total_return(start, end, dividends=0.0, costs=0.0):
    for n, x in (("start", start), ("end", end), ("dividends", dividends), ("costs", costs)):
        finite(x, n)
    if start <= 0 or min(end, dividends, costs) < 0 or end + dividends - costs < 0:
        raise ValueError("invalid long-only terminal wealth")
    return (end + dividends - costs) / start - 1

def price_return(start, end):
    return holding_period_total_return(start, end)

def cash_dividend_yield(start, dividends):
    finite(start, "start"); finite(dividends, "dividends")
    if start <= 0 or dividends < 0: raise ValueError("invalid price or dividend")
    return dividends / start

def reinvested_shares(shares, dividend_per_share, reinvestment_price):
    for n, x in (("shares", shares), ("dividend", dividend_per_share), ("price", reinvestment_price)):
        finite(x, n)
    if shares < 0 or dividend_per_share < 0 or reinvestment_price <= 0:
        raise ValueError("invalid reinvestment inputs")
    return shares * (1 + dividend_per_share / reinvestment_price)

def reinvested_total_return(initial_shares, initial_price, final_shares, final_price):
    for n, x in (("initial_shares", initial_shares), ("initial_price", initial_price),
                 ("final_shares", final_shares), ("final_price", final_price)):
        finite(x, n)
    if initial_shares <= 0 or initial_price <= 0 or final_shares < 0 or final_price < 0:
        raise ValueError("invalid holding or price")
    return final_shares * final_price / (initial_shares * initial_price) - 1

def split_adjusted_price_return(start_price, end_price, new_shares_per_old_share):
    for n, x in (("start", start_price), ("end", end_price), ("split_ratio", new_shares_per_old_share)):
        finite(x, n)
    if start_price <= 0 or end_price < 0 or new_shares_per_old_share <= 0:
        raise ValueError("invalid split inputs")
    return end_price * new_shares_per_old_share / start_price - 1

def link_total_returns(returns):
    if not returns: raise ValueError("nonempty returns required")
    factor = 1.0
    for r in returns:
        finite(r, "return")
        if r < -1: raise ValueError("return below -100%")
        factor *= 1 + r
    return factor - 1

class DividendReturnTests(unittest.TestCase):
    def test_01_price_only(self): self.assertAlmostEqual(price_return(100, 105), .05)
    def test_02_dividend(self): self.assertAlmostEqual(holding_period_total_return(100, 105, 3), .08)
    def test_03_cost(self): self.assertAlmostEqual(holding_period_total_return(100, 105, 3, 1), .07)
    def test_04_yield(self): self.assertAlmostEqual(cash_dividend_yield(100, 3), .03)
    def test_05_flat_price(self): self.assertAlmostEqual(holding_period_total_return(100, 100, 4), .04)
    def test_06_reinvest_shares(self): self.assertAlmostEqual(reinvested_shares(10, 2, 50), 10.4)
    def test_07_reinvest_return(self): self.assertAlmostEqual(reinvested_total_return(10, 50, 10.4, 55), .144)
    def test_08_split(self): self.assertAlmostEqual(split_adjusted_price_return(100, 50, 2), 0)
    def test_09_split_gain(self): self.assertAlmostEqual(split_adjusted_price_return(100, 55, 2), .10)
    def test_10_link(self): self.assertAlmostEqual(link_total_returns([.08, -.02]), .0584)
    def test_11_total_loss(self): self.assertEqual(holding_period_total_return(100, 0), -1)
    def test_12_invalid(self):
        cases = [lambda: holding_period_total_return(0, 100),
                 lambda: holding_period_total_return(100, 0, 0, 101),
                 lambda: cash_dividend_yield(0, 3),
                 lambda: reinvested_shares(10, 2, 0),
                 lambda: split_adjusted_price_return(100, 50, 0),
                 lambda: link_total_returns([]),
                 lambda: link_total_returns([-1.01])]
        for fn in cases:
            with self.assertRaises(ValueError): fn()

if __name__ == "__main__":
    print("Price return:", f"{price_return(100,105):.2%}")
    print("Total return:", f"{holding_period_total_return(100,105,3):.2%}")
    print("Net return:", f"{holding_period_total_return(100,105,3,1):.2%}")
    unittest.main(verbosity=2)
```

## 11. Exercises with worked solutions

1. **Price gain:** $100 to $105. **Answer:** +5%.
2. **Cash dividend:** Same share pays $3. **Answer:** 3% beginning-price dividend contribution.
3. **Total return:** Same values. **Answer:** (105−100+3)/100=8%.
4. **Costs:** Add a separately paid $1 fee. **Answer:** 7% net return.
5. **Ex-dividend price:** $100 to $98 with $2 dividend. **Answer:** 0% total return.
6. **Cash dividend yield:** $4 on a $200 beginning price. **Answer:** 2%.
7. **Price decline and dividend:** $100 to $95, $7 dividend. **Answer:** +2% total return.
8. **Dividend withholding:** $3 dividend, 15% withheld. **Answer:** Net cash dividend $2.55.
9. **After-withholding return:** $100 to $105 plus $2.55 net dividend. **Answer:** 7.55%.
10. **Reinvestment cash:** 10 shares paying $2 each. **Answer:** $20 available to reinvest.
11. **Reinvestment shares:** Reinvest $20 at $50. **Answer:** 0.4 new shares.
12. **Final reinvested wealth:** 10.4 shares at $55. **Answer:** $572.
13. **Reinvested return:** Initial wealth $500, final $572. **Answer:** 14.4%.
14. **Unreinvested return:** 10 shares at $55 plus $20 cash. **Answer:** 14%.
15. **2-for-1 split:** $100 before, $50 after. **Answer:** 0% split-adjusted return.
16. **Split appreciation:** $100 before, $55 after 2-for-1. **Answer:** +10% adjusted return.
17. **Linked total returns:** +8%, then −2%. **Answer:** 1.08×0.98−1=5.84%.
18. **Cash-flow classification:** A new investor deposit of $1,000 arrives. **Answer:** It is external capital, not investment income or return.

## 12. Applied mini-project — Dividend-aware total-return ledger

An investor starts with **20 shares at $50 each**. Over two reporting intervals:

| Event | Price | Cash distribution per share | Action |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Initial purchase | $50 | — | Buy 20 shares |
| Dividend date | $48 | $2 | Reinvest at $48 |
| Final valuation | $54 | — | Mark all shares to market |

Assume no commissions, taxes, external contributions, or fractional-share restrictions.

**Tasks:**

1. Calculate initial invested wealth.
2. Calculate dividend cash earned on the initial 20 shares.
3. Determine the additional fractional shares acquired.
4. Calculate ending share count and market value.
5. Compute the reinvested total return.
6. Compute the alternative return if the $40 dividend had been held as idle cash.
7. Explain why adding the $40 dividend to the final value of the reinvested shares would double-count it.
8. Provide an audit ledger with date/event, share count, cash movement, reinvestment price, and market value.

**Worked checks:** Initial wealth=$1,000. Dividend cash=20×$2=$40. New shares=40/48=0.833333… . Final shares=20.833333… . Ending reinvested wealth=20.833333…×54=**$1,125**. Reinvested total return=**12.5%**. Cash-held ending wealth=20×54+40=**$1,120**, or **12%**.

**Acceptance criteria:** Share counts and cash flows reconcile; the dividend is counted exactly once; fractional shares are supported; and all 12 reference unit tests pass.

## 13. Production data and SRE considerations

- Ingest corporate-action and dividend event data with authoritative effective dates.
- Distinguish declaration, record, ex-dividend, and payment dates.
- Track whether distributions are gross or net of withholding.
- Record whether prices are raw, split-adjusted, or total-return-adjusted.
- Prevent double-adjustment of corporate actions or double-counting reinvested dividends.
- Reconcile total-return indices against share-and-cash ledgers.
- Validate missing and duplicate distribution events.
- Preserve source timestamps, currency, and FX conversion rules.
- Maintain a documented methodology for total-return index construction.
