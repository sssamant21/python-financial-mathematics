## Learning objectives

Calculate drawdowns from a consistent wealth index; identify peaks, troughs, recovery points, maximum drawdown, and underwater duration; explain why losses require disproportionate recovery gains; distinguish drawdown risk from return volatility; implement an auditable Python drawdown monitor.

## 1. What is a drawdown?

A **drawdown** measures how far an investment's current wealth has fallen below its previous high-water mark. Unlike standard deviation, drawdown measures an actual decline relative to a historical peak, not dispersion around the average return.

For portfolio wealth W_t and running peak H_t:

**H_t = max(W_0, W_1, ..., W_t)**

**DD_t = W_t/H_t − 1.**

For nonnegative wealth, drawdown lies in **[−100%, 0%]**. A value of 0% indicates that wealth equals its historical peak; a negative value indicates that wealth remains below that peak.

The **maximum drawdown (MDD)** over a specified observation window is:

**MDD = min_t DD_t.**

This convention reports maximum drawdown as a **negative return**; some dashboards report its positive magnitude instead. Always label the sign convention.

## 2. Build a wealth index before calculating drawdowns

A drawdown calculation should use a consistent investment wealth series. For period simple returns R_t and starting wealth W_0:

**W_t = W_(t−1)(1+R_t).**

If dividends, distributions, fees, and external flows are relevant, select and disclose a consistent return/wealth methodology. A raw account-balance series with investor contributions and withdrawals can create artificial peaks or troughs.

### Worked example A — Wealth path

Start at $100 and earn +20%, then −25%, then +10%.

**W_1 = 100×1.20 = 120.**

**W_2 = 120×0.75 = 90.**

**W_3 = 90×1.10 = 99.**

The running peak is 100, 120, 120, 120.

Drawdowns are 0%, 0%, **−25%**, and **−17.5%**.

The maximum drawdown is **−25%**.

## 3. Peak, trough, and recovery

A **peak** is the wealth high preceding a drawdown. A **trough** is the lowest point reached during the drawdown episode. **Recovery** occurs when the wealth series returns to or exceeds the previous peak.

| Observation | Wealth | Running peak | Drawdown |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 0 | $100 | $100 | 0% |
| 1 | $120 | $120 | 0% |
| 2 | $90 | $120 | −25% |
| 3 | $105 | $120 | −12.5% |
| 4 | $120 | $120 | 0% |

The peak is observation 1, the trough is observation 2, and recovery is observation 4. The maximum drawdown is −25%.

A recovery to the old peak does not necessarily represent a new all-time high, but it ends the underwater episode.

## 4. Recovery gains are asymmetric

If wealth falls from P to P(1−L), with fractional loss L, the gain needed to return to P is:

**Required gain = 1/(1−L) − 1.**

Equivalently, for negative drawdown d:

**Required gain = 1/(1+d) − 1.**

| Loss from peak | Gain required to recover |
|---:|---:|
| −10% | 11.11% |
| −20% | 25.00% |
| −25% | 33.33% |
| −50% | 100.00% |
| −80% | 400.00% |

A −100% loss cannot be recovered through compounding of the remaining zero wealth without new capital or a different economic event.

### Worked example B — Why −50% and +50% do not cancel

Start with $100. After −50%, wealth is $50. A subsequent +50% produces $75, not $100.

The gain needed to recover from $50 to $100 is **100%**.

## 5. Maximum drawdown versus volatility

Volatility and drawdown answer different questions:

- **Volatility:** How widely do period returns fluctuate around their mean?
- **Drawdown:** How far has cumulative wealth fallen from its previous peak?

A strategy can exhibit modest average daily volatility yet experience a prolonged decline. Another strategy may show high short-term volatility without a deep sustained drawdown. Both measures are useful and neither substitutes for the other.

Maximum drawdown is **path-dependent**: reordering a fixed set of returns leaves the final compounded wealth unchanged but can change the maximum drawdown and the duration of underwater periods.

## 6. Drawdown duration and underwater periods

An investment is **underwater** whenever DD_t < 0.

A simple discrete duration metric counts consecutive observations below the running peak. If the series is sampled daily, that count measures observed days; if monthly, it measures observed months. It is not necessarily the number of calendar days.

For wealth [100, 120, 90, 100, 120]:

- Drawdowns are [0, 0, −25%, −16.67%, 0].
- The underwater run spans observations 2 and 3.
- Longest completed underwater run = **2 observations**.

For an unfinished drawdown, the duration is **ongoing**, not a completed peak-to-recovery interval. Reports should distinguish recovered and unrecovered episodes.

## 7. Drawdown frequency and observation bias

Maximum drawdown depends on how often valuations are observed. Intraday prices can reveal a deeper decline than end-of-day prices, and daily data can reveal a deeper decline than monthly marks.

For a fair comparison, use comparable valuation frequencies, time windows, and total-return methodologies. Missing prices, stale marks, and different trading calendars can distort both depth and duration.

## 8. Total-return versus price-only drawdown

A price-only index excludes distributions, while a total-return index reflects them under a defined reinvestment convention. For dividend-paying securities, price-only drawdown can overstate the economic decline experienced by an investor who receives and reinvests distributions.

Do not add dividends again to a series already adjusted for reinvestment. The wealth index used for drawdowns must be reconciled with the return methodology in Sections 13.7–13.9.

## 9. External cash flows and portfolio drawdown

Suppose a portfolio is worth $100 and an investor deposits $100. The account balance jumps to $200 without any investment gain. If the investor later withdraws $100, the balance falls to $100 without any investment loss.

A drawdown calculated directly from these account balances could incorrectly show a **−50% drawdown**. Instead, construct a **unitized or time-weighted wealth index** that removes external contributions and withdrawals before calculating performance drawdowns.

## 10. Maximum drawdown limitations

Maximum drawdown is sensitive to a single extreme peak-to-trough episode. It does not capture every dimension of risk:

- It does not measure how frequently smaller drawdowns occur.
- It may not distinguish a short sharp decline from a long underwater period.
- It depends on the observation horizon and sampling frequency.
- It is historical and does not guarantee a future loss bound.
- It can be distorted by missing or illiquid asset valuations.

Complement MDD with underwater duration, volatility, downside deviation, and appropriate scenario analysis.

## 11. Operational drawdown alerts

A production monitor may implement threshold levels, such as a warning when drawdown falls below −10% and a critical review below −20%. These are **illustrative**, not universal trading or risk limits.

An operationally useful alert includes:

- Strategy or portfolio identifier.
- Current wealth index and prior high-water mark.
- Current drawdown and maximum drawdown over the stated horizon.
- Peak and trough timestamps.
- Current underwater duration.
- Return source, calculation version, and valuation timestamp.

Alerts should be evaluated with risk policy, liquidity, and data-quality context rather than treated as automatic trade instructions.

The reference lab constructs a wealth path from returns, calculates running drawdowns and maximum drawdown, identifies the worst peak-to-trough episode, measures underwater runs, and calculates recovery gains. It contains **12 reference unit tests**. Independent runtime verification remains pending.

```python
import math
import unittest

def validated_wealth(values):
    if not values:
        raise ValueError("nonempty wealth path required")
    out = []
    for v in values:
        if isinstance(v, bool) or not isinstance(v, (int, float)) or not math.isfinite(v) or v < 0:
            raise ValueError("wealth must be finite and nonnegative")
        out.append(float(v))
    if out[0] <= 0:
        raise ValueError("initial wealth must be positive")
    return out

def wealth_from_returns(initial, returns):
    if isinstance(initial, bool) or not isinstance(initial, (int,float)) or not math.isfinite(initial) or initial <= 0:
        raise ValueError("positive initial wealth required")
    wealth = [float(initial)]
    for r in returns:
        if isinstance(r, bool) or not isinstance(r, (int,float)) or not math.isfinite(r) or r < -1:
            raise ValueError("simple return must be finite and >= -100%")
        wealth.append(wealth[-1] * (1+r))
    return wealth

def drawdown_series(values):
    wealth = validated_wealth(values)
    peak = wealth[0]
    result = []
    for value in wealth:
        peak = max(peak, value)
        result.append(value / peak - 1.0)
    return result

def max_drawdown(values):
    return min(drawdown_series(values))

def longest_underwater_run(values):
    drawdowns = drawdown_series(values)
    longest = current = 0
    for dd in drawdowns:
        current = current + 1 if dd < 0 else 0
        longest = max(longest, current)
    return longest

def worst_drawdown_episode(values):
    wealth = validated_wealth(values)
    peak = wealth[0]
    peak_index = 0
    worst = 0.0
    best_pair = (0, 0)
    for i, value in enumerate(wealth):
        if value > peak:
            peak, peak_index = value, i
        dd = value / peak - 1
        if dd < worst:
            worst, best_pair = dd, (peak_index, i)
    return {"depth": worst, "peak_index": best_pair[0], "trough_index": best_pair[1]}

def recovery_gain(drawdown):
    if isinstance(drawdown, bool) or not isinstance(drawdown,(int,float)) or not math.isfinite(drawdown) or not -1 <= drawdown <= 0:
        raise ValueError("drawdown must be in [-1, 0]")
    if drawdown == -1:
        return math.inf
    return 1 / (1 + drawdown) - 1

class DrawdownTests(unittest.TestCase):
    def test_01_flat(self): self.assertEqual(drawdown_series([100,100]),[0,0])
    def test_02_peak(self): self.assertEqual(drawdown_series([100,120]),[0,0])
    def test_03_decline(self): self.assertAlmostEqual(drawdown_series([100,120,90])[-1],-.25)
    def test_04_max(self): self.assertAlmostEqual(max_drawdown([100,120,90,105]),-.25)
    def test_05_recovery(self): self.assertAlmostEqual(recovery_gain(-.2),.25)
    def test_06_total_loss(self): self.assertTrue(math.isinf(recovery_gain(-1)))
    def test_07_returns(self): self.assertEqual(wealth_from_returns(100,[.2,-.25]),[100,120,90])
    def test_08_episode(self): self.assertEqual(worst_drawdown_episode([100,120,90,110])["trough_index"],2)
    def test_09_peak_index(self): self.assertEqual(worst_drawdown_episode([100,120,90])["peak_index"],1)
    def test_10_underwater(self): self.assertEqual(longest_underwater_run([100,120,90,100,120]),2)
    def test_11_recovered(self): self.assertEqual(drawdown_series([100,120,90,120])[-1],0)
    def test_12_invalid(self):
        for fn in (lambda:drawdown_series([]),
                   lambda:max_drawdown([0,10]),
                   lambda:wealth_from_returns(100,[-1.1]),
                   lambda:drawdown_series([100,float("nan")]),
                   lambda:recovery_gain(.1)):
            with self.assertRaises(ValueError): fn()

if __name__ == "__main__":
    wealth = wealth_from_returns(100,[.20,-.25,.10,.10])
    print("Wealth:",wealth)
    print("Drawdowns:",[round(x,4) for x in drawdown_series(wealth)])
    print("Maximum drawdown:",f"{max_drawdown(wealth):.2%}")
    unittest.main(verbosity=2)
```

## 13. Exercises with worked solutions

1. **Running peak:** Wealth [100, 120, 90]. **Answer:** [100, 120, 120].
2. **Current drawdown:** Wealth $90, running peak $120. **Answer:** 90/120−1=−25%.
3. **Maximum drawdown:** Wealth [100, 120, 90, 105]. **Answer:** −25%.
4. **Partial recovery:** Wealth $105, prior peak $120. **Answer:** −12.5%.
5. **Full recovery:** Wealth $120, prior peak $120. **Answer:** 0% drawdown.
6. **Required recovery gain:** Drawdown −10%. **Answer:** 1/0.9−1≈11.11%.
7. **Required recovery gain:** Drawdown −20%. **Answer:** 25%.
8. **Required recovery gain:** Drawdown −25%. **Answer:** 33.33%.
9. **Required recovery gain:** Drawdown −50%. **Answer:** 100%.
10. **Required recovery gain:** Drawdown −80%. **Answer:** 400%.
11. **Total loss:** Drawdown −100%. **Answer:** No finite percentage gain on zero remaining wealth restores the original capital.
12. **Compounding:** $100, +20%, −25%. **Answer:** $90 ending wealth.
13. **Return after trough:** $90 increases by 10%. **Answer:** $99, still −17.5% below $120 peak.
14. **Underwater run:** Wealth [100,120,90,100,120]. **Answer:** 2 observations below peak.
15. **Peak index:** Wealth [100,120,90]. **Answer:** Peak occurs at observation 1.
16. **Trough index:** Same series. **Answer:** Trough occurs at observation 2.
17. **External cash flow:** Deposit doubles account balance without market gain. **Answer:** Do not treat the deposit as investment return; use a flow-adjusted wealth index.
18. **Path dependence:** Does reordering returns change maximum drawdown? **Answer:** It can, even if final compounded wealth is unchanged.

## 15. Production and SRE considerations

- Use total-return or flow-adjusted indices with a documented methodology.
- Validate that the wealth index and running peak are nonnegative and finite.
- Detect stale and missing valuations before calculating drawdown.
- Keep a stable timestamp and sampling frequency.
- Record the lookback horizon and whether a drawdown episode is ongoing.
- Reconcile maximum drawdown against the minimum drawdown-series observation.
- Treat late-arriving price corrections as recomputation triggers.
- Preserve alert threshold versions and audit logs.
- Distinguish market decline from capital withdrawals.
- Pair depth with duration and recovery statistics.

## 16. Completion and next section

Drawdown measures realized peak-to-trough declines in investment wealth. It complements volatility by showing how far an investment has fallen from its historical peak and how long recovery takes.
