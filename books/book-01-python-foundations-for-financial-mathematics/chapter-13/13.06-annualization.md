## Learning objectives

Convert cumulative returns into equivalent annual-effective rates; reverse annualization; annualize periodic and irregularly timed returns; distinguish annual-effective and nominal quoted rates; handle losses, total-loss boundaries, and day-count conventions; and validate calculations with Python.

## 1. What annualization means

A return of 8% earned over one month is not directly comparable to a return of 8% earned over one year. **Annualization** expresses a measured holding-period return as a *constant equivalent compound rate per year*.

It does **not** assert that the investment actually earned that rate every year, and it is not a forecast of future performance.

Let R_T be the cumulative effective return over T years, with T>0. If the gross return factor is positive:

**1+R_T=(1+r_a)^T**.

Solving for the annual-effective rate:

**r_a=(1+R_T)^(1/T)−1**.

For computational accuracy near zero, use:

**r_a=expm1[log1p(R_T)/T]**.

This requires R_T>−1. At the −100% total-loss boundary, the compound-equivalent annual rate can be assigned −100% for any positive horizon, but the logarithmic implementation needs an explicit special case.

## 2. Worked example — Two-year growth

An investment grows from $10,000 to $12,100 over exactly two years.

**Cumulative return = 12,100/10,000−1=21%.**

**Annualized return = (1.21)^(1/2)−1=10%.**

Check: $10,000×1.10²=$12,100.

The annualized 10% is a *geometric equivalent*. Actual year-by-year returns might have been +25% and −3.2%, which also compound to +21%.

## 3. Worked example — Six-month gain

An investment gains 5% over half a year.

**Annualized effective return = (1.05)²−1=10.25%.**

Multiplying 5% by two gives 10%, which is a *simple linear extrapolation*, not the equivalent compounded annual rate.

Annualizing a short period magnifies observed gains or losses and can produce misleading impressions of stability or repeatability.

## 4. Worked example — Multi-year loss

An investment loses 19% over two years.

**Gross factor = 0.81**.

**Annualized return = sqrt(0.81)−1=−10%.**

The arithmetic average of the cumulative loss, −19%/2=−9.5%, does not reproduce the ending wealth under compounding.

## 5. Reversing annualization

Given an annual-effective return r_a over T years:

**R_T=(1+r_a)^T−1**.

For 10% annual-effective growth over three years:

**R_3=(1.10)^3−1=33.1%.**

For −10% per year over two years:

**R_2=(0.90)^2−1=−19%.**

Annualization and deannualization should be inverse operations, within numerical tolerance and consistent domain assumptions.

## 6. Regularly spaced observations

Suppose there are n returns R_1,…,R_n, each covering one of k equal-length periods per year. The cumulative factor is:

**G=∏\[i=1..n\](1+R_i)**.

The equivalent annual-effective rate is:

**r_a=G^(k/n)−1**.

For strictly positive gross factors, the numerically stable form is:

**r_a=expm1[(k/n)Σlog1p(R_i)]**.

Common *conventions*, not universal facts, include 12 monthly observations or 252 trading days per year. The correct frequency depends on the return observations, calendar, asset, and reporting policy.

### Worked example — Two quarterly returns

An investment gains 3% in Q1 and loses 1% in Q2.

**G=1.03×0.99=1.0197**.

Two quarters represent half a year under an equal-quarter convention.

**Annualized return = (1.0197)²−1 ≈ 3.9788%.**

This is an equivalent annual rate for the observed six-month window, not an estimate guaranteed to persist.

## 7. Irregular time intervals and day counts

When observations span unequal intervals, do not blindly assume each observation represents one month or one quarter. Determine the elapsed time between the beginning and ending valuation dates.

One simple, explicit convention is:

**T = actual elapsed calendar days / 365**.

Then:

**r_a=(1+R_T)^(365/days)−1**.

This is an **Actual/365 Fixed** convention for this educational model. Other financial products or performance standards may use Actual/Actual, 30/360, business-day counts, or precisely defined time-weighted conventions. A choice of convention can change the result.

### Worked example — 90-day gain

A cumulative 2% gain over 90 actual calendar days gives:

**r_a=(1.02)^(365/90)−1≈8.362%.**

This is a mechanically annualized rate; the uncertainty of projecting 90 days of realized performance over a full year can be substantial.

## 8. Annualized log returns

The cumulative log return is:

**L=ln(1+R_T)**.

For T years, annualized log growth is:

**g_a=L/T**.

The equivalent annual-effective simple return is:

**r_a=exp(g_a)−1**.

For 21% cumulative over two years:

**g_a=ln(1.21)/2=ln(1.10)≈0.09531018**.

The annual-effective rate is **10%**, not 9.5310%. Log and simple annual rates use different numerical conventions.

## 9. Annual-effective versus nominal annual rates

Annualization of investment performance normally produces an **effective annual compound rate**, unless another convention is explicitly specified.

A nominal annual rate j compounded m times per year produces:

**EAR=(1+j/m)^m−1**.

At 6% nominal with semiannual compounding:

**EAR=(1.03)²−1=6.09%.**

Conversely:

**j=m[(1+EAR)^(1/m)−1]**.

For EAR=6.09% and m=2, the corresponding nominal semiannual-compounded rate is **6%**.

The nominal annual rate is a quotation convention. It should not be silently substituted for an effective annual investment return.

## 10. Annualized return versus volatility

A common mistake is to apply the same annualization rule to **returns** and **volatility**.

For a constant compounded-growth interpretation, annualized return uses geometric compounding. Under suitable assumptions about independent, identically distributed period returns, standard deviation of returns is often scaled by **sqrt(k)**, not compounded as a return.

That volatility scaling is assumption-dependent; autocorrelation, changing volatility, overlapping observations, and fat tails can invalidate a simple square-root-of-time approximation. Chapter 13.11 covers volatility in detail.

## 11. Total-loss and invalid-data boundaries

- **R_T=−100%:** ending gross factor is zero; assign −100% annual-equivalent rate only with an explicit long-only total-loss policy.
- **R_T below −100%:** invalid in the introductory unlevered wealth-ratio model.
- **T≤0:** annualization is undefined.
- **Missing or non-finite values:** reject or investigate; do not silently impute.
- **Short time windows:** annualized figures can be mathematically correct but economically unstable.
- **Mixed periods:** normalize the timing convention before comparing observations.

Use Python 3.9 or later and its standard library; no prior listing or external data is needed. Date-based annualization accepts calendar dates rather than timestamps. Inputs use their decimal text, and periodic inputs are all validated before applying the total-loss policy. Rational factors and high-precision Decimal powers precede checked float reporting. The lab covers cumulative annualization, inverse transformations, periodic-return annualization, date-based elapsed years, and nominal/effective rate conversions. It contains **12 reference unit tests**; independent runtime verification remains pending.

```python
import math
import unittest
from datetime import date, datetime
from decimal import Context, Decimal, DecimalException, localcontext
from fractions import Fraction

def _number(value,label,positive=False,signed=False):
    if isinstance(value,bool) or not isinstance(value,(int,float)):
        raise ValueError(f"invalid {label}")
    try:
        finite=math.isfinite(value)
    except OverflowError:
        finite=False
    if not finite or (not signed and value<0) or (positive and value==0):
        raise ValueError(f"invalid {label}")
    return Fraction(str(value))

_MIN_FLOAT=Fraction(math.ulp(0.0))

def _report(value,label):
    try:
        if 0<abs(value)<_MIN_FLOAT:
            raise OverflowError
        result=float(value)
    except OverflowError as exc:
        raise ValueError(f"{label} outside supported range") from exc
    if not math.isfinite(result):
        raise ValueError(f"{label} outside supported range")
    return result

def finite_number(x,name):
    _number(x,name,signed=True)

def _return(value):
    r=_number(value,"return",signed=True)
    if r<-1:
        raise ValueError("gross return cannot be negative in this model")
    return r

def _reported_return(value):
    result=_report(value,"return")
    if value>-1 and result==-1:
        raise ValueError("positive gross return rounds to total loss")
    return result

def _linked(returns):
    factors=[1+_return(r) for r in returns]
    if not factors:
        raise ValueError("returns required")
    return math.prod(factors),len(factors)

def _equivalent(factor,years,scale=1):
    try:
        with localcontext(Context(prec=750)):
            g=Decimal(factor.numerator)/Decimal(factor.denominator)
            t=Decimal(years.numerator)/Decimal(years.denominator)
            result=(g.ln()/t).exp()-1
            if result==-1 or (result==0 and factor!=1):
                raise ValueError("return outside supported precision")
            result*=scale
    except DecimalException as exc:
        raise ValueError("equivalent return outside supported range") from exc
    report=_report(result,"equivalent return")
    if report<=-scale:
        raise ValueError("reported rate is outside the supported domain")
    return report

def finite(x,name):
    _number(x,name,signed=True)

def annualize_cumulative(total_return,years):
    r=_return(total_return)
    t=_number(years,"years",positive=True)
    return -1.0 if r==-1 else _equivalent(1+r,t)

def deannualize(annual_return,years):
    r=_return(annual_return)
    t=_number(years,"years",positive=True)
    return -1.0 if r==-1 else _equivalent(1+r,1/t)

def annualize_periods(returns,periods_per_year):
    frequency=_number(periods_per_year,"periods per year",positive=True)
    factor,count=_linked(returns)
    return -1.0 if factor==0 else _equivalent(factor,count/frequency)

def _elapsed(start,end):
    if type(start) is not date or type(end) is not date or end<=start:
        raise ValueError("ordered calendar dates required")
    return Fraction((end-start).days,365)

def years_actual_365(start,end):
    return _report(_elapsed(start,end),"elapsed years")

def annualize_dates(total_return,start,end):
    r=_return(total_return)
    t=_elapsed(start,end)
    return -1.0 if r==-1 else _equivalent(1+r,t)

def _frequency(frequency):
    if type(frequency) is not int or frequency<=0:
        raise ValueError("positive integer frequency required")

def nominal_apr_from_effective(effective_annual,frequency):
    _frequency(frequency)
    r=_return(effective_annual)
    if r==-1:
        raise ValueError("positive effective gross factor required")
    return _equivalent(1+r,Fraction(frequency),frequency)

def effective_from_nominal(nominal_apr,frequency):
    _frequency(frequency)
    base=1+_number(nominal_apr,"nominal rate",signed=True)/frequency
    if base<=0:
        raise ValueError("invalid compounding base")
    return _equivalent(base,Fraction(1,frequency))

class AnnualizationTests(unittest.TestCase):
    def test_01_two_year(self):self.assertAlmostEqual(annualize_cumulative(.21,2),.1)
    def test_02_one_year(self):self.assertAlmostEqual(annualize_cumulative(.08,1),.08)
    def test_03_half_year(self):self.assertAlmostEqual(annualize_cumulative(.05,.5),.1025)
    def test_04_loss(self):self.assertAlmostEqual(annualize_cumulative(-.19,2),-.1)
    def test_05_total_loss(self):self.assertEqual(annualize_cumulative(-1,2),-1)
    def test_06_inverse(self):self.assertAlmostEqual(deannualize(.1,2),.21)
    def test_07_periods(self):self.assertAlmostEqual(annualize_periods([.1,.1],1),.1)
    def test_08_daily(self):self.assertAlmostEqual(annualize_periods([.001]*252,252),(1.001)**252-1)
    def test_09_dates(self):self.assertAlmostEqual(years_actual_365(date(2025,1,1),date(2026,1,1)),1)
    def test_10_nominal(self):self.assertAlmostEqual(effective_from_nominal(.06,2),.0609)
    def test_11_roundtrip(self):self.assertAlmostEqual(nominal_apr_from_effective(.0609,2),.06)
    def test_12_invalid(self):
        cases=[lambda:annualize_cumulative(.1,0),lambda:annualize_cumulative(-1.1,2),
               lambda:annualize_periods([],252),lambda:annualize_periods([.1],0),
               lambda:years_actual_365(date(2026,1,1),date(2025,1,1)),
               lambda:effective_from_nominal(-3,2)]
        for fn in cases:
            with self.assertRaises(ValueError):fn()
        with self.assertRaises(ValueError): annualize_periods([-1,float("nan")],252)
        with self.assertRaises(ValueError): years_actual_365(datetime(2025,1,1),datetime(2025,4,1,12))

if __name__=='__main__':
    print('21% over 2 years:',f'{annualize_cumulative(.21,2):.4%}')
    print('5% over 6 months:',f'{annualize_cumulative(.05,.5):.4%}')
    print('6% nominal semiannual:',f'{effective_from_nominal(.06,2):.4%}')
    unittest.main(verbosity=2)
```

## 13. Exercises with worked solutions

1. **One-year return:** 8% over one year. **Solution:** Annualized 8%.
2. **Two-year gain:** 21% over two years. **Solution:** sqrt(1.21)−1=10%.
3. **Two-year gain:** 44% over two years. **Solution:** sqrt(1.44)−1=20%.
4. **Six-month gain:** 5% over half a year. **Solution:** 1.05²−1=10.25%.
5. **Two-year loss:** −19% over two years. **Solution:** sqrt(0.81)−1=−10%.
6. **One-year loss:** −8% over one year. **Solution:** −8%.
7. **Three-year growth:** 10% annual-effective over three years. **Solution:** 1.1³−1=33.1% cumulative.
8. **Two-year loss:** −10% annual-effective over two years. **Solution:** 0.9²−1=−19% cumulative.
9. **Quarterly growth:** +3%, then −1%. **Solution:** Cumulative 1.97%.
10. **Quarterly annualization:** Same two quarters. **Solution:** 1.0197²−1≈3.9788%.
11. **Daily annualization:** 0.1% every trading day for 252 days. **Solution:** 1.001^252−1≈28.64%.
12. **Actual/365:** 2% cumulative over 90 days. **Solution:** 1.02^(365/90)−1≈8.362%.
13. **Log annualization:** 21% over two years. **Solution:** ln(1.21)/2≈0.09531018 annual log growth.
14. **Log to effective:** Annual log growth ln(1.10). **Solution:** 10% annual-effective.
15. **Nominal to effective:** 6% nominal compounded semiannually. **Solution:** 6.09% EAR.
16. **Effective to nominal:** 6.09% EAR, semiannual compounding. **Solution:** 6% nominal.
17. **Total loss:** −100% cumulative over three years. **Solution:** −100% equivalent annual return under the stated long-only policy; log form undefined.
18. **Invalid horizon:** Why reject T=0? **Solution:** The exponent 1/T is undefined, so no finite annualization exists.

## 15. Production and SRE considerations

- Record observation timestamps and the applicable time zone.
- Store the day-count convention alongside annualized metrics.
- Do not mix 252-trading-day and 365-calendar-day conventions without disclosure.
- Validate beginning and ending valuations and external cash-flow treatment.
- Handle total-loss events explicitly instead of feeding them to logarithms.
- Record whether results are gross or net of fees.
- Preserve unrounded values for calculations and reconciliation.
- Flag very short sample windows as potentially misleading when annualized.
- Include round-trip tests between annualized and cumulative return functions.

## 16. Completion and next section

Annualization makes performance across unequal time horizons more comparable when its compounding and time conventions are explicit. It cannot remove differences in risk, liquidity, cash-flow timing, or sample reliability.
