## Learning objectives

By the end of this section, you should be able to define investment return; distinguish price, income, total, gross, net, nominal, and real returns; compute a holding-period return; interpret losses and compounding; recognize external-cash-flow complications; and implement validated return calculations in Python.

## 1. Why measuring returns matters

An investment's dollar gain alone does not tell us how well it performed. A $100 gain on a $1,000 investment is economically different from a $100 gain on a $100,000 investment. **Return** relates gain or loss to the capital invested over a defined measurement period.

A useful performance measure must specify:

- **Beginning and ending dates** and the valuation convention.
- **Beginning and ending market values**, in the same currency.
- **Distributions** such as dividends or interest.
- **Fees, transaction costs, and taxes**, and whether the measure is before or after them.
- **External contributions or withdrawals**, if any.
- **Compounding, annualization, and inflation adjustments**, when applicable.

A return number without these details can be misleading even when its arithmetic is correct.

## 2. Dollar gain versus percentage return

Let V0 be beginning value and V1 ending value, with **V0 > 0**.

**Dollar price gain = V1 − V0.**

**Price return R_price = (V1 − V0)/V0 = V1/V0 − 1.**

### Worked example A — Price appreciation

Buy a share for $100 and observe a value of $108 at the end of the period.

Dollar gain = 108−100 = **$8**.

Price return = 8/100 = **8%**.

A second investment that rises from $1,000 to $1,008 also earns $8 but only **0.8%**. Percentage return permits a more meaningful capital-relative comparison.

## 3. Holding-period total return

An investor may receive cash income while holding an asset. For a simple unlevered investment with no external contributions or withdrawals during the measurement period, let:

- V0 = beginning value.
- V1 = ending value, excluding separately received distributions.
- I = cash income received during the period.
- C = investor-borne costs paid during the period and not already reflected in V1 or I.

Then:

**R_total = (V1 − V0 + I − C)/V0.**

This is a **holding-period return (HPR)** for the specified interval. The formula assumes distributions are not reinvested during the interval and cash flows are treated as part of terminal wealth; if distributions are reinvested or occur at different times, timing and valuation conventions must be handled explicitly.

### Worked example B — Dividends and costs

An investor buys a share for $100. At the end of the year, its price is $105. The investor receives a $3 dividend and pays $1 in eligible costs.

**Price return = (105−100)/100 = 5%.**

**Gross total return before costs = (105−100+3)/100 = 8%.**

**Net total return after these costs = (105−100+3−1)/100 = 7%.**

If the ending market value already reflects a deducted fee, subtracting the same fee again would double-count it.

## 4. Gross return versus net return

The word **gross** is used in two distinct ways in finance:

1. **Gross-of-fees performance:** a performance return before specified fees or costs.
2. **Gross return factor:** the mathematical multiplier **G=1+R**, where R is a simple return.

These meanings should not be confused.

For a simple return of 8%, the **gross return factor** is **1.08**. A −20% return corresponds to a factor of **0.80**.

In an unlevered long-only investment with nonnegative ending wealth, a simple return cannot fall below −100%. Leveraged positions and obligations can produce losses greater than the initial capital; the introductory Python model intentionally excludes those cases.

## 5. Positive and negative returns

Suppose an investment falls from $100 to $80.

**R = (80−100)/100 = −20%.**

To recover from $80 to $100 requires:

**Recovery return = (100−80)/80 = 25%.**

A 20% loss followed by a 20% gain does not restore the original capital:

**100 × 0.8 × 1.2 = 96**.

This illustrates why arithmetic changes and compounded returns are different.

## 6. Multi-period compounding

If consecutive period returns are R1, R2, …, Rn and there are no intervening external capital flows that disrupt the return linkage:

**G_total = ∏\[k=1..n\](1+Rk).**

**R_total = G_total − 1.**

For a +10% first period followed by a −10% second period:

**(1.10)(0.90)−1 = −1%.**

The arithmetic sum (+10%−10%=0%) is not the compounded investment return.

The mechanics of simple, gross, logarithmic, and multi-period returns are developed more fully in Sections 13.2–13.5.

## 7. Annualized return

For a cumulative effective return R earned over **T years**, with positive gross return:

**R_annualized = (1+R)^(1/T) − 1.**

If an investment grows by 21% over two years:

**(1.21)^(1/2) − 1 = 10% per year.**

Annualization is a **geometric equivalent rate**, not a guarantee of achieving that rate in any individual future year. Time fractions and observation calendars must be specified consistently.

## 8. Nominal versus real returns

Nominal returns are measured in money units; **real returns** adjust for purchasing-power changes.

If nominal return is R_nom and inflation is π over the same period:

**1+R_real = (1+R_nom)/(1+π).**

**R_real = (1+R_nom)/(1+π) − 1.**

At 8% nominal return and 3% inflation:

**R_real = 1.08/1.03−1 ≈ 4.8544%.**

Simply subtracting inflation (8%−3%=5%) is an approximation, not the exact multiplicative calculation.

## 9. External cash flows complicate performance

If an investor contributes $5,000 halfway through a year, ending wealth reflects both investment performance and the new capital. Computing **(ending wealth − beginning wealth)/beginning wealth − 1** would misclassify that contribution as investment gain.

Two common approaches address this:

- **Time-weighted return (TWR):** chain subperiod returns separated by external cash flows to reduce the influence of their timing on the performance measure.
- **Money-weighted return (MWR):** solve for a rate that reflects the amounts and dates of investor cash flows.

TWR and MWR can differ substantially. Their complete treatment is reserved for Sections 13.9 and 13.10. This section's basic HPR formula applies only when external contributions and withdrawals do not occur within the measured interval.

## 10. Returns across asset classes

| Asset | Relevant components | Common caution |
|---|---|---|
| Equity | Price change and dividends | Corporate actions, splits, and dividend timing |
| Bond | Price change, coupons, accrued interest | Clean versus dirty pricing and reinvestment |
| Fund | NAV change and distributions | Fees and distribution-adjusted NAV |
| Real estate | Property value and net operating cash flow | Leverage, transaction costs, and appraisal lag |
| Foreign-currency investment | Local asset return and FX translation | Currency denomination and FX conversion timing |

For a foreign asset, the domestic-currency gross return is the product of the local-asset gross return and the relevant currency gross return, under consistent FX quote direction.

```python
import math
from fractions import Fraction
from math import expm1, log1p, prod
import unittest

def _number(value,label,positive=False,signed=False):
    if isinstance(value,bool) or not isinstance(value,(int,float)):
        raise ValueError(f"invalid {label}")
    try:
        finite=math.isfinite(value)
    except OverflowError:
        finite=False
    if not finite or (not signed and value<0) or (positive and value==0):
        raise ValueError(f"invalid {label}")
    return Fraction(str(value))

_MIN_FLOAT=Fraction(math.ulp(0.0))

def _report(value,label):
    try:
        if 0<abs(value)<_MIN_FLOAT:
            raise OverflowError
        result=float(value)
    except OverflowError as exc:
        raise ValueError(f"{label} outside supported range") from exc
    if not math.isfinite(result):
        raise ValueError(f"{label} outside supported range")
    return result

def finite_number(x,name):
    _number(x,name,signed=True)

def _return(value):
    r=_number(value,"return",signed=True)
    if r<-1:
        raise ValueError("gross return cannot be negative in this model")
    return r

def _reported_return(value):
    result=_report(value,"return")
    if value>-1 and result==-1:
        raise ValueError("positive gross return rounds to total loss")
    return result

def holding_period_return(start,end,income=0.0,costs=0.0):
    initial=_number(start,"start",positive=True)
    terminal=_number(end,"end")+_number(income,"income")-_number(costs,"costs")
    if terminal<0:
        raise ValueError("negative closing wealth is outside this model")
    return _reported_return((terminal-initial)/initial)

def price_return(start,end):
    return holding_period_return(start,end)

def gross_return(simple_return):
    return _report(1+_return(simple_return),"gross return")

def compound_returns(returns):
    gross=[1+_return(r) for r in returns]
    if not gross:
        raise ValueError("returns required")
    return _reported_return(prod(gross)-1)

def _expm1_return(log_growth):
    try:
        result=expm1(log_growth)
    except OverflowError as exc:
        raise ValueError("return outside supported range") from exc
    if not math.isfinite(result) or result==-1:
        raise ValueError("return outside supported range")
    return result

def annualize(effective_return,years):
    r=_return(effective_return)
    t=_number(years,"years",positive=True)
    if r==-1:
        raise ValueError("annualization requires a positive gross factor")
    value=log1p(float(r))
    growth=_report(Fraction(value)/t,"annual log growth")
    return _expm1_return(growth)

def log_return(simple_return):
    r=_return(simple_return)
    if r==-1:
        raise ValueError("log return requires a positive gross factor")
    return log1p(float(r))

def return_from_log(value):
    finite_number(value,"log return")
    return _expm1_return(value)

def real_return(nominal_return,inflation):
    n=_return(nominal_return)
    i=_number(inflation,"inflation",signed=True)
    if i<=-1:
        raise ValueError("inflation factor must be positive")
    return _reported_return((n-i)/(1+i))

def weighted_portfolio_return(weights,returns):
    if len(weights)!=len(returns) or not weights:
        raise ValueError("nonempty matching weights and returns required")
    w=[_number(v,"weight") for v in weights]
    r=[_return(v) for v in returns]
    total=sum(w)
    if abs(total-1)>Fraction("1e-10"):
        raise ValueError("long-only weights must sum to one")
    return _reported_return(sum(a*b for a,b in zip(w,r))/total)

class ReturnTests(unittest.TestCase):
    def test_01_price_gain(self):self.assertAlmostEqual(price_return(100, 110), .1)
    def test_02_price_loss(self):self.assertAlmostEqual(price_return(100, 90), -.1)
    def test_03_dividend(self):self.assertAlmostEqual(holding_period_return(100, 105, 3), .08)
    def test_04_costs(self):self.assertAlmostEqual(holding_period_return(100, 105, 3, 1), .07)
    def test_05_gross(self):self.assertAlmostEqual(gross_return(.08), 1.08)
    def test_06_compound(self):self.assertAlmostEqual(compound_returns([.1, -.1]), -.01)
    def test_07_annualization(self):self.assertAlmostEqual(annualize(.21, 2), .1)
    def test_08_log_inverse(self):self.assertAlmostEqual(return_from_log(log_return(.08)), .08)
    def test_09_real(self):self.assertAlmostEqual(real_return(.08, .03), 1.08 / 1.03 - 1)
    def test_10_portfolio(self):self.assertAlmostEqual(weighted_portfolio_return([.6,.4],[.1,-.05]),.04)
    def test_11_total_loss(self):self.assertEqual(holding_period_return(100, 0), -1)
    def test_12_invalid(self):
        cases = [lambda:price_return(0, 110),
                 lambda:holding_period_return(100, -1),
                 lambda:compound_returns([]),
                 lambda:annualize(-1, 1),
                 lambda:log_return(-1),
                 lambda:weighted_portfolio_return([.4,.4],[.1,.2])]
        for fn in cases:
            with self.assertRaises(ValueError):fn()
        self.assertEqual(compound_returns([1e-30]),1e-30)
        with self.assertRaises(ValueError): holding_period_return(100,0,0,1)

if __name__ == '__main__':
    print('Price return:', f'{price_return(100,105):.2%}')
    print('Total return with dividend and costs:', f'{holding_period_return(100,105,3,1):.2%}')
    print('Two-period compounded return:', f'{compound_returns([.10,-.10]):.2%}')
    print('Annualized two-year 21%:', f'{annualize(.21,2):.2%}')
    print('Inflation-adjusted 8%:', f'{real_return(.08,.03):.4%}')
    unittest.main(verbosity=2)
```

## 12. Worked exercises and solutions

1. **Dollar gain:** An asset moves from $50 to $57. **Answer:** $7.
2. **Price return:** Same asset. **Answer:** 7/50=14%.
3. **Loss:** $200 falls to $170. **Answer:** −15%.
4. **Dividend return:** Buy at $100, sell at $105, receive $3 dividend. **Answer:** 8%.
5. **Costs:** In Exercise 4, pay $1 in costs. **Answer:** 7%.
6. **Gross factor:** A return of 12%. **Answer:** 1.12.
7. **Gross factor after a loss:** Return of −30%. **Answer:** 0.70.
8. **Compounding:** +10%, then −10%. **Answer:** −1%.
9. **Recovery:** Loss of 20%; gain needed to restore capital? **Answer:** 25%.
10. **Annualization:** 21% cumulative over two years. **Answer:** 10% annually.
11. **Annualization:** 44% cumulative over two years. **Answer:** 20% annually.
12. **Real return:** 8% nominal, 3% inflation. **Answer:** Approximately 4.8544%.
13. **Price versus total:** Price rises 4%, dividend yield 2% of beginning value. **Answer:** 6% simple total return, assuming no costs and no interim reinvestment.
14. **Cash flow:** Why not count a new contribution as investment gain? **Answer:** It is additional invested capital, not earned return.
15. **Time-weighted:** What is its purpose? **Answer:** Reduce the effect of external cash-flow timing on reported performance.
16. **Money-weighted:** What does it incorporate? **Answer:** Amounts and timing of external cash flows.
17. **Log return:** Convert simple 10% return. **Answer:** ln(1.10)≈0.0953102.
18. **Inverse log return:** Convert log return ln(1.08). **Answer:** exp[ln(1.08)]−1=8%.

| Period | Beginning value | Ending account value before current distribution and costs | Cash distribution | Costs |

The ending account value includes cash retained from prior periods. Add the current distribution and subtract current costs once to get net ending wealth. ## 14. Production considerations and common errors

- Adjust historical prices for splits and other corporate actions where appropriate.
- Define whether dividends are included and whether they are reinvested.
- Keep fees, taxes, and transaction costs consistent across comparisons.
- Separate asset performance from investor contributions and withdrawals.
- Use consistent pricing timestamps and valuation sources.
- Avoid annualizing short, volatile windows without stating the methodology.
- Do not mix local-currency and base-currency returns without FX conversion.
- Record missing data and stale prices rather than silently filling them.
- Distinguish hypothetical return calculations from realized investor outcomes.
