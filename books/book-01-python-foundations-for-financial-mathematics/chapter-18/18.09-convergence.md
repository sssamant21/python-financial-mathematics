## Learning objectives

Explain convergence of Monte Carlo estimates; distinguish convergence in probability and almost sure convergence from a single observed run; relate independent-sample standard error to sample size; distinguish sampling error from bias and monitoring error; and design a reproducible convergence study.

## 1. What converges?

A Monte Carlo estimator targets a specified model quantity, such as μ=E[X]. For independent identically distributed (IID) samples X₁,…,Xₙ with finite mean, the sample mean is:

**X̄ₙ=(1/N)ΣᵢXᵢ.**

The law of large numbers says that the sample mean converges to μ under its conditions. In particular, the strong law gives almost sure convergence for IID samples with finite absolute mean. Convergence in probability means that for every ε>0, P(|X̄ₙ−μ|>ε) tends to zero.

Neither statement says every observed estimate must improve when N increases. Nor does it say the model or its inputs describe reality.

## 2. Sampling rate and the central limit theorem

If IID observations have finite, positive variance σ², then √N(X̄ₙ−μ)/σ approaches a standard normal distribution in distribution as N grows. This supports approximate intervals when its conditions and finite-sample behavior are suitable.

The exact standard error for independent samples is **σ/√N**; estimate it by **s/√N** with sample standard deviation s. Four times as many independent samples approximately halve the standard error. The distribution of a single error is random, so the observed error need not halve.

A heavy-tailed or dependent sample may need different methods; finite variance and independence must not be assumed without review.

## 3. Analytical benchmark

Suppose X is 80, 100 or 120 with probabilities .25, .50 and .25. Then E[X]=100 and Var(X)=200. The theoretical standard error of the sample mean is:

| Independent paths | Theoretical SE |
|---:|---:|
| 100 | 1.41421 |
| 400 | .70711 |
| 10,000 | .14142 |
| 40,000 | .07071 |

These describe variation across repeated independent samples. They are not guaranteed bounds on a particular run.

## 4. Nested sample sizes

A convergence table may report the first 100, 400 and 10,000 observations of one stream. These estimates share observations and are dependent; the table shows how one running estimate evolves. It does not provide independent replications or prove coverage.

Independent replications, using properly constructed independent streams, help assess variation between runs. Record how streams were created; arbitrarily different seeds alone do not establish mathematical independence.

## 5. Error sources

| Source | Example | Will more paths fix it? |
|---|---|---|
| Sampling error | Finite independent draws | Usually reduces its scale |
| Model error | Wrong return distribution | No |
| Parameter error | Uncertain volatility estimate | Not by itself |
| Time discretization | Approximate path dynamics | Not by itself |
| Monitoring error | Barrier crossing between observation dates | Not by itself |
| Implementation error | Wrong payoff or units | No |
| Floating-point error | Overflow or cancellation | Not necessarily |

A simulation can converge toward the expectation of the wrong model. Separate path count, time-step refinement and assumptions when designing experiments.

## 6. Convergence in a financial payoff

For the three-state payoff above, discounting one year at an annual effective rate of 5% gives an exact model mean **100/1.05≈95.2381**. Discount each sample consistently and compare its sample mean with this analytical value.

This is an expectation under the stated probabilities and discount rate. It is not automatically a market-consistent price; a risk-neutral pricing model requires appropriate measure and discounting assumptions.

## 7. Rare-event convergence

For IID Bernoulli indicators of an event with probability p, the event frequency estimates p, with theoretical standard error √[p(1−p)/N]. If p=.001 and N=1,000, the expected event count is only one. An observed count of zero does not establish that p=0.

The plug-in standard error is zero when the observed frequency is zero or one; it is misleading as a measure of uncertainty at the boundary. Use an appropriate binomial interval or rare-event method and assess whether the sample size is adequate.

## 8. Confidence intervals and stopping

A fixed-sample nominal 95% normal interval is **X̄±1.96s/√N** where the approximation is appropriate. It addresses sampling variability under assumptions and does not automatically include model error or bias. Small or skewed samples and rare events may need other methods.

Repeatedly checking a fixed-N interval and stopping as soon as it becomes narrow can alter its coverage. Predefine a sample size or use a confidence sequence or other method designed for sequential stopping.

## 9. Time-grid refinement

For a path-dependent payoff, compare results at different time steps while separately controlling sampling uncertainty. Exact GBM updates at grid times do not reveal a barrier crossing between those times. A finer discrete grid changes the monitoring approximation; continuous monitoring may require a bridge or other validated method.

When comparing grids, paired random inputs can reduce the variance of differences if constructed consistently. Estimate uncertainty from independent paired differences. Pairing does not guarantee variance reduction.

## 10. Reproducible convergence study

Record the estimand, model and measure, units, benchmark, sample sizes, stream design, seed, generator, software versions and stopping rules. Run multiple replications when feasible and report estimate, SE and error relative to a reference. Do not use one convenient seed as validation.

Test an analytically known special case, deterministic boundaries, input domains and nonfinite outcomes. Preserve actual output and investigate discrepancies instead of adjusting tolerances to make them pass.

## 11. Reference implementation

Save as `monte_carlo_convergence.py`. It generates a nested stream for the three-state benchmark. The reported prefix estimates are dependent because they share samples.

~~~python
import math
import random
import unittest
from fractions import Fraction
from decimal import Decimal,localcontext

def _number(x):
    if type(x) not in (int,float):
        raise ValueError("finite int/float required; booleans excluded")
    try:
        if not math.isfinite(x):
            raise ValueError("finite number required")
    except OverflowError as exc:
        raise ValueError("number outside finite float range") from exc
    return Fraction(x) if type(x) is int else Fraction(str(x))

def _float(x):
    try:
        value=float(x)
    except (ValueError,OverflowError) as exc:
        raise ValueError("unreportable numeric result") from exc
    if not math.isfinite(value) or (x!=0 and value==0):
        raise ValueError("unreportable numeric result")
    return value

def _report(value):
    # Preserve exact whole-number outcomes/means within the stated finite range.
    result=_float(value)
    return value.numerator if value.denominator==1 else result

def _sqrt(value):
    with localcontext() as ctx:
        ctx.prec=100+len(str(abs(value.numerator)))+len(str(value.denominator))
        return _float((Decimal(value.numerator)/Decimal(value.denominator)).sqrt())

def sample_payoffs(count, seed):
    if type(count) is not int or count < 2 or type(seed) is not int:
        raise ValueError("count >= 2 and integer seed required")
    rng = random.Random(seed)
    result = []
    for _ in range(count):
        u = rng.random()
        result.append(80.0 if u < .25 else (100.0 if u < .75 else 120.0))
    return result

def summary(values, reference):
    reference=_number(reference)
    try:vals=list(values)
    except TypeError as exc:raise ValueError("iterable observations required") from exc
    if len(vals)<2:raise ValueError("at least two observations required")
    vals=list(map(_number,vals));n=len(vals);mean=sum(vals)/n
    variance=sum((x-mean)**2 for x in vals)/(n-1)
    return {"count":n,"mean":_report(mean),"estimated_se":_sqrt(variance/n),
            "absolute_error":_report(abs(mean-reference))}

def prefix_study(samples, sizes, reference):
    try:vals,sizes=list(samples),list(sizes)
    except TypeError as exc:raise ValueError("iterable samples and sizes required") from exc
    if not sizes or any(type(n) is not int or n < 2 or n > len(vals) for n in sizes):
        raise ValueError("sizes must be integers between 2 and sample length")
    if sizes != sorted(set(sizes)):
        raise ValueError("sizes must be strictly increasing")
    return [summary(vals[:n],reference) for n in sizes]

def theoretical_se(standard_deviation, count):
    standard_deviation=_number(standard_deviation)
    if standard_deviation<0 or type(count) is not int or count<1:
        raise ValueError("nonnegative finite SD and positive integer count required")
    return _sqrt(standard_deviation*standard_deviation/count)

class ConvergenceTests(unittest.TestCase):
    def test_replay(self):
        self.assertEqual(sample_payoffs(20,42),sample_payoffs(20,42))
    def test_support(self):
        self.assertTrue(all(x in (80,100,120) for x in sample_payoffs(100,1)))
    def test_known_mean(self):
        self.assertEqual(summary([80,100,120],100)["mean"],100)
    def test_known_se(self):
        self.assertAlmostEqual(summary([80,100,120],100)["estimated_se"],20/math.sqrt(3))
    def test_constant(self):
        self.assertEqual(summary([100,100],100)["estimated_se"],0)
    def test_error(self):
        self.assertEqual(summary([80,80],100)["absolute_error"],20)
    def test_prefix_counts(self):
        self.assertEqual([x["count"] for x in prefix_study([80,100,120,100],[2,4],100)],[2,4])
    def test_fourfold(self):
        self.assertAlmostEqual(theoretical_se(10,400),theoretical_se(10,100)/2)
    def test_bad_sizes(self):
        with self.assertRaises(ValueError): prefix_study([1,2,3],[3,2],0)
    def test_bad_count(self):
        with self.assertRaises(ValueError): sample_payoffs(1,42)
    def test_nonfinite(self):
        with self.assertRaises(ValueError): summary([1,float("nan")],0)
    def test_bad_sd(self):
        with self.assertRaises(ValueError): theoretical_se(-1,100)

if __name__ == "__main__":
    samples = sample_payoffs(10000,42)
    for row in prefix_study(samples,[100,400,10000],100):
        print(row)
    unittest.main(verbosity=2)
~~~

This simple implementation stores all samples and recalculates each prefix; larger production workloads may need streaming summaries and a reviewed numerical algorithm. It does not calculate a confidence sequence or certify stream independence.

Numeric observations/reference/SD inputs must be finite int/float values within finite float range, excluding booleans. Integer values remain exact and floats use displayed-decimal values. Invalid inputs and nonzero unreportable outputs (overflow or underflow to zero) raise `ValueError`. Absolute error is calculated before rounding the reported mean, and SE is calculated directly from rational variance/N so an intermediate SD need not be reportable. Summary accepts finite signed observations/reference and requires at least two values.

`theoretical_se` accepts a nonnegative finite SD and an arbitrary positive integer count (including one); it computes the scaled result without first converting the count to float. This supports theoretical counts beyond float range when the final SE is reportable, without allocating that many samples. Such numerical count support is not a claim that the corresponding simulation was run or is feasible. Source sampler counts still require at least two actual draws.

Samples/sizes may be one-pass iterables. Prefix sizes must be nonempty, strictly increasing integer values between two and the sample length. Only observations in requested prefixes are validated; unused trailing records are ignored. A prefix table is dependent, and neither numerical precision nor finite examples certify LLN/CLT conditions, independence or coverage.

## 14. Troubleshooting

| Symptom | Possible cause | Next check |
|---|---|---|
| Error rises at a larger N | Ordinary sampling variation | Examine repeated runs and SE |
| SE does not scale as expected | Dependence or changing model | Inspect independent units and inputs |
| Estimates stabilize at wrong value | Model or implementation bias | Compare analytical benchmark and assumptions |
| Zero rare events | Event is rare or N is small | Use suitable interval and sample-size analysis |
| Barrier estimate changes with grid | Monitoring approximation | Study grid and continuous-monitoring method |
| Same seed differs across environments | Generator or operation order changed | Compare versions and run metadata |
