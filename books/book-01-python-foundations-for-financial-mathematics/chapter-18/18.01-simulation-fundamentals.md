## Learning objectives

Explain simulation and Monte Carlo estimation; distinguish a model, a scenario and a random sample; simulate a simple financial outcome; estimate a mean and its sampling uncertainty; use a seed responsibly; and identify limitations and validation checks.

## 1. What is simulation?

Simulation uses a model to generate possible outcomes under stated assumptions. A deterministic simulation produces the same output from the same inputs; a stochastic simulation includes random draws. Monte Carlo simulation uses repeated random samples to estimate quantities such as an expected payoff or a loss probability.

The output describes the chosen model. It does not establish that the assumed distribution, dependence, market regime or future behavior is correct.

## 2. Why use simulation in finance?

Some payoffs depend on several uncertain variables or an entire path, making a closed-form solution difficult or unavailable. Simulation can explore portfolio scenarios, estimate an expected payoff, study loss distributions, and examine the consequences of changes in assumptions.

It is not always necessary: when a reliable exact solution exists, use it as a benchmark. A simulation also introduces sampling uncertainty and may introduce discretization bias.

## 3. Basic vocabulary

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Model | Mathematical description of inputs and their relationships |
| Scenario | One specified combination or path of inputs |
| Random variable | A quantity whose outcome is uncertain under the model |
| Distribution | The model's probabilities for possible outcomes |
| Sample | Generated outcomes used for estimation |
| Path | A sequence of states through time |
| Payoff | The value calculated from an outcome or path |
| Estimator | A rule for estimating a model quantity from samples |
| Seed | An initialization used to reproduce a pseudorandom stream |

A generated path is one possible model outcome, not a forecast that must occur.

## 4. A two-outcome example

Suppose an asset worth 100 ends one year at 110 with probability 0.5 and 90 with probability 0.5. Its expected terminal value under this model is:

**E[V] = 0.5 × 110 + 0.5 × 90 = 100.**

A simulated sample of four outcomes, [110, 90, 110, 110], has sample mean 105. It need not equal the exact expectation because a sample fluctuates. Its 3/4 proportion of upward outcomes also differs from the model probability 1/2.

The model is illustrative; it does not assert that real returns have only two outcomes or equal probabilities.

## 5. Simulation workflow

1. Define the question, horizon, units and target quantity.
2. Choose a model and probability distribution consistent with the intended use.
3. Validate parameters and dependency assumptions.
4. Select a generator, seed and sample count.
5. Generate outcomes or paths and compute payoffs.
6. Summarize results and quantify sampling uncertainty where justified.
7. Compare with an analytical benchmark or independent calculation.
8. Run sensitivity and robustness studies; record limitations and evidence.

The target must be explicit: expected terminal value, probability of loss, discounted expected payoff, or a risk measure are different quantities.

## 6. Scenarios, samples and probabilities

A scenario can be selected deliberately, such as a 20% market decline. Monte Carlo samples are generated according to an assumed distribution. A deterministic stress scenario need not have an assigned probability, while a simulated loss frequency estimates a probability under the model.

Changing an input distribution changes the model and can change results more than increasing the number of samples.

## 7. From returns to values

For initial value V₀ and one-period simple return R:

**V₁ = V₀(1+R).**

For returns R₁,…,Rₜ, compounded value is **Vₜ=V₀∏ᵢ(1+Rᵢ)**. Returns may be negative; for a nonnegative asset price this simple one-period model requires R≥−1. A normal distribution for simple returns has unbounded support and can generate returns below −100%; the model must address such outcomes rather than silently treating them as valid prices.

## 8. Sample mean and expectation

For generated payoffs X₁,…,Xₙ, the Monte Carlo estimate of the expected payoff is:

**X̄ = (1/N)Σᵢ Xᵢ.**

For independent identically distributed draws with a finite mean, the law of large numbers supports convergence of the sample mean to the model expectation as N increases. It does not say that every larger sample must be closer than the previous one, or that the model expectation matches reality.

## 9. Standard error

For independent, identically distributed observations with finite variance, estimate the standard error of X̄ by **s/√N**, where s is the sample standard deviation. Increasing independent samples by a factor of four roughly halves standard error. Correlated observations, weighted samples and variance-reduced estimators need appropriate uncertainty calculations.

For the two-outcome example, the model standard deviation is 10. With 10,000 independent samples, the theoretical standard error is **10/100 = 0.1** currency units.

A nominal 95% interval X̄ ± 1.96 s/√N is a normal approximation under suitable conditions. It is not a guarantee for every sample and does not account for model misspecification, discretization bias or implementation errors.

## 10. Seeds and reproducibility

A pseudorandom generator deterministically produces a sequence from its internal state. A seed supports reproducible experiments, provided the generator, implementation, versions and draw order are also controlled. Different seeds can help study sampling variability. A fixed seed does not make a model correct or prove its samples are independent.

Simulation generators in this chapter are for educational modeling, not cryptographic uses.

## 11. Discounting a payoff

If a payoff X at one year is discounted at a specified annual effective rate r > −1, its simulated present value is X/(1+r). For constant r, the mean discounted payoff equals the mean payoff divided by 1+r.

The choice of probability measure and discounting assumptions matters. A real-world expected outcome and a risk-neutral valuation are not generally interchangeable. This simple example does not calibrate or justify either measure.

## 12. Probability of loss

Define loss before calculating it. If loss means terminal value below initial value, estimate its probability by:

**number of simulated V₁<V₀ outcomes / N.**

In the two-outcome model, the exact probability is 0.5. The result depends on the threshold and model; a different definition, such as discounted value below initial cost, can give a different answer.

## 13. Reference implementation

Save as `simulation_fundamentals.py`. It uses the standard library and independent pseudorandom draws from a local generator. It is educational, uses a two-point distribution, and is not a production pricing or risk system.

~~~python
import math
import random
import statistics
import unittest

def simulate_values(initial, up_return, down_return, up_probability,
                    count, seed):
    values = (initial, up_return, down_return, up_probability)
    if any(type(x) not in (int, float) or not math.isfinite(x) for x in values):
        raise ValueError("finite numeric model inputs required")
    if initial <= 0 or up_return < -1 or down_return < -1:
        raise ValueError("positive initial value and returns >= -1 required")
    if not 0 <= up_probability <= 1:
        raise ValueError("probability must be between 0 and 1")
    if type(count) is not int or count < 2:
        raise ValueError("at least two samples required")
    if type(seed) is not int:
        raise ValueError("integer seed required")
    up_value = initial * (1 + up_return)
    down_value = initial * (1 + down_return)
    if not math.isfinite(up_value) or not math.isfinite(down_value):
        raise ValueError("nonfinite terminal value")
    rng = random.Random(seed)
    return [up_value if rng.random() < up_probability else down_value
            for _ in range(count)]

def summarize(values, initial):
    values = list(values)
    if len(values) < 2 or not math.isfinite(initial):
        raise ValueError("at least two samples and a finite threshold required")
    if any(not math.isfinite(v) for v in values):
        raise ValueError("finite sample values required")
    mean = statistics.mean(values)
    sd = statistics.stdev(values)
    return {
        "count": len(values),
        "mean": mean,
        "sample_sd": sd,
        "standard_error": sd / math.sqrt(len(values)),
        "loss_frequency": sum(v < initial for v in values) / len(values),
    }

def discounted(value, rate):
    if not all(type(x) in (int, float) and math.isfinite(x)
               for x in (value, rate)) or rate <= -1:
        raise ValueError("finite value and rate greater than -1 required")
    result = value / (1 + rate)
    if not math.isfinite(result):
        raise ValueError("nonfinite discounted value")
    return result

class SimulationTests(unittest.TestCase):
    def test_seed_reproducibility(self):
        args = (100, .1, -.1, .5, 20, 42)
        self.assertEqual(simulate_values(*args), simulate_values(*args))
    def test_all_up(self):
        self.assertEqual(simulate_values(100,.1,-.1,1,5,1), [110.0]*5)
    def test_all_down(self):
        self.assertEqual(simulate_values(100,.1,-.1,0,5,1), [90.0]*5)
    def test_known_sample(self):
        result = summarize([110,90,110,110],100)
        self.assertEqual(result["mean"],105)
        self.assertEqual(result["loss_frequency"],.25)
    def test_constant_standard_error(self):
        self.assertEqual(summarize([100,100],100)["standard_error"],0)
    def test_discount(self):
        self.assertAlmostEqual(discounted(110,.1),100)
    def test_bad_probability(self):
        with self.assertRaises(ValueError):
            simulate_values(100,.1,-.1,1.1,10,1)
    def test_bad_count(self):
        with self.assertRaises(ValueError):
            simulate_values(100,.1,-.1,.5,1,1)
    def test_bad_return(self):
        with self.assertRaises(ValueError):
            simulate_values(100,.1,-1.1,.5,10,1)
    def test_bad_rate(self):
        with self.assertRaises(ValueError):
            discounted(100,-1)

if __name__ == "__main__":
    sample = simulate_values(100,.1,-.1,.5,10000,42)
    print(summarize(sample,100))
    unittest.main(verbosity=2)
~~~

## 15. Forty worked exercises

1. **Simulation:** What is a model? **Answer:** A specified representation of the system and its assumptions.
2. **Monte Carlo:** What is repeated? **Answer:** Random sampling under the model.
3. **Scenario:** Does a stress scenario need a probability? **Answer:** No; a deterministic stress can be specified directly.
4. **Path:** What does it contain? **Answer:** A sequence of modeled states over time.
5. **Payoff:** What is it? **Answer:** A value calculated from an outcome or path.
6. **Terminal value:** 100 with +10% return. **Answer:** 110.
7. **Down outcome:** 100 with −10% return. **Answer:** 90.
8. **Expectation:** Equal probabilities of 110 and 90. **Answer:** 100.
9. **Sample mean:** [110,90,110,110]. **Answer:** 105.
10. **Loss frequency:** Same sample, loss below 100. **Answer:** 1/4.
11. **Model loss probability:** Equal-probability model. **Answer:** 1/2.
12. **Unequal probability:** Up probability .6. **Answer:** Expected value .6(110)+.4(90)=102.
13. **Discount:** 110 discounted one year at 10%. **Answer:** 100.
14. **Discounted expectation:** E[V]=100 and rate 10%. **Answer:** 100/1.1≈90.9091 under the stated assumptions.
15. **Measure:** Is real-world expectation automatically a risk-neutral price? **Answer:** No.
16. **Standard deviation:** Equal values 90 and 110 about mean 100. **Answer:** Model standard deviation 10.
17. **Standard error:** SD 10, N=10,000 independent draws. **Answer:** 0.1.
18. **More draws:** Increase N fourfold. **Answer:** Standard error approximately halves under the stated assumptions.
19. **Convergence:** Must every larger run be closer? **Answer:** No.
20. **Seed:** What does it support? **Answer:** Reproducibility under controlled generator and environment.
21. **Seed validity:** Does a seed prove model correctness? **Answer:** No.
22. **Version:** Why record it? **Answer:** Generator behavior or implementation may change.
23. **Probability:** Is 1.1 valid? **Answer:** No.
24. **Simple return:** Can a nonnegative price have a return below −100%? **Answer:** No.
25. **Normal simple return:** Why examine its tails? **Answer:** It can generate returns below −100%.
26. **Bias:** Can more samples remove wrong model assumptions? **Answer:** No.
27. **Discretization:** Can more paths remove time-step bias? **Answer:** Not by itself.
28. **Confidence interval:** Does it include model risk automatically? **Answer:** No.
29. **Dependence:** Can correlated paths use the independent-sample standard error blindly? **Answer:** No.
30. **Exact benchmark:** Why use one? **Answer:** It checks the estimator and implementation against a known result.
31. **Initial 200:** Equal ±10% outcomes. **Answer:** 220 and 180, expectation 200.
32. **Probability one:** Up outcome only. **Answer:** All generated values are 110 under the example.
33. **Probability zero:** Down outcome only. **Answer:** All generated values are 90.
34. **Zero variance sample:** What is its sample standard error? **Answer:** Zero, though that alone does not validate the model.
35. **Threshold:** Is V₁=100 a loss under V₁<100? **Answer:** No.
36. **Compounding:** 100 grows 10% then falls 10%. **Answer:** 100×1.1×.9=99.
37. **Average return:** Are +10% and −10% compounded back to the initial value? **Answer:** No; their product is .99.
38. **N=1:** Can ordinary sample standard deviation be estimated? **Answer:** Not with the usual N−1 formula.
39. **Output:** Is a simulated path a guaranteed forecast? **Answer:** No.
40. **Audit:** What should be retained? **Answer:** Inputs, assumptions, generator, seed, version, sample count, outputs, benchmarks and limitations.

## 16. Troubleshooting

| Symptom | Possible cause | Next step |
|---|---|---|
| Different results with the same seed | Different generator, version or draw order | Record environment and isolate generator state |
| Estimate differs from exact value | Sampling variation or defect | Compare standard error and inspect implementation |
| Negative asset value | Simple-return model allows below −100% | Reconsider distribution or model |
| Very narrow interval but implausible result | Model bias or wrong inputs | Review assumptions and benchmarks |
| Larger sample does not improve one run | Sampling fluctuation | Compare repeated runs and uncertainty |
| Reported uncertainty too small | Dependent samples | Use a dependence-aware method |
