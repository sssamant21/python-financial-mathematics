## Learning objectives

Construct deterministic and probabilistic financial scenarios; apply consistent shocks to positions; distinguish stress tests from forecasts; calculate portfolio profit and loss; interpret sensitivity and dependence; and document assumptions, limitations and reproducibility.

## 1. What is scenario analysis?

Scenario analysis asks what a specified position or model would produce under a defined set of conditions. A scenario might describe a stock decline, a rate increase, a currency move, or several changes together. It is a conditional calculation: **if these inputs occur under these assumptions, what is the result?**

A scenario is not inherently a forecast. A deterministic stress can be useful without an assigned probability. A weighted expected outcome is meaningful only when probabilities are explicitly specified, internally consistent and suitable for the question.

## 2. Define the position and baseline

Specify the valuation date, horizon, currency, exposures, units, baseline prices, valuation method and applicable costs. This section uses a simple two-asset portfolio:

- Asset A: 60 units of currency initially.
- Asset B: 40 units of currency initially.
- Initial portfolio value: 100.
- One-period simple returns; no cash flows, rebalancing, fees or taxes unless stated.

The terminal portfolio value is **60(1+Rₐ)+40(1+Rᵦ)**. Profit and loss (P&L) is terminal value minus initial value. This linear calculation assumes the exposures remain fixed over the single period and represents fully funded positions; it is not a complete derivatives valuation.

## 3. Deterministic scenarios

| Scenario | A return | B return | Terminal value | P&L |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Base | +5% | +2% | 103.8 | +3.8 |
| Downside | −20% | −10% | 84 | −16 |
| Upside | +15% | +8% | 112.2 | +12.2 |

For the downside, 60×.8+40×.9=48+36=84. The scenario loss is 16 currency units. These cases are illustrative, not assigned likelihoods.

## 4. Scenario versus sensitivity

A **sensitivity** changes one input while holding other inputs fixed; a **scenario** can change multiple inputs together. A sensitivity of the portfolio to a one-percentage-point move in A, with B unchanged, is 60×.01=.6 currency units for this linear model. A one-basis-point return move is .0001 in decimal units and changes A's value by .006.

For nonlinear positions, sensitivities can vary with shock size and direction. Full revaluation and documented assumptions may be more appropriate than a linear approximation.

## 5. Assigning probabilities

Suppose, only for illustration, that the three rows have probabilities .5, .3 and .2. They are mutually exclusive and exhaustive **within this model**, and sum to one.

Expected terminal value = .5(103.8)+.3(84)+.2(112.2)=**99.54**. Expected P&L = **−.46**. The model probability of a strictly negative P&L is .3.

Do not infer these probabilities from the labels “base,” “downside” and “upside.” Overlapping scenarios or probabilities that do not sum to one cannot be used directly in this weighted calculation without a suitable model.

## 6. Shock consistency

State whether a shock is absolute or relative. A yield change of +100 basis points means +.01 in decimal yield, not a 100% relative increase. A price decline of 20% means multiply the baseline price by .8. A foreign-exchange scenario must specify quotation direction and currency conversion.

Define the horizon and whether shocks happen simultaneously or sequentially. For a simple-return asset, +10% followed by −10% yields 1.1×.9−1=−1%, not zero.

## 7. Joint moves and dependence

A joint scenario may change several risk factors together. It explicitly specifies the combination; it does not establish how often the combination occurs. In Monte Carlo modeling, dependence must be defined through a joint distribution or another documented structure.

Correlation is not causation and may change in stressed markets. Independently shocking assets when the risk question requires correlated moves can understate or overstate outcomes. Historical relationships and stressed assumptions should be reviewed.

## 8. Interest-rate example

A zero-coupon payment of 100 due in one year has price 100/(1+y), using an annual effective yield y>−1. At y=.05 its price is approximately **95.2381**. At y=.06 it is approximately **94.3396**. The +100 bp yield shock produces P&L of approximately **−.8985** for one unit.

This is a full revaluation of a simplified one-year instrument. Real instruments require dates, day counts, compounding, cash-flow and curve conventions. A parallel yield shift is not equivalent to all possible curve changes.

## 9. Currency example

An asset is worth EUR 100. If the exchange rate is USD per EUR, a baseline of 1.10 gives USD value 110. A decline to 1.05 gives USD value 105 and currency P&L of −5, assuming the EUR asset value is unchanged.

If the quote is EUR per USD, the conversion is different. Always state the quote direction before applying the shock.

## 10. Portfolio costs and constraints

Fees, taxes, liquidity, transaction costs, funding and position limits can alter outcomes. Specify whether each cost is paid at the start or end and whether it depends on the scenario. This section's reference implementation uses a fixed end-of-period fee subtracted once.

A calculated scenario can be mathematically consistent but infeasible for the portfolio. Validate positions, instrument constraints and any rebalancing assumption before using the result.

## 11. Monte Carlo scenarios

A Monte Carlo run samples outcomes from a stated joint model and estimates a distribution of P&L. Record the generator, seed, sample count, horizon, model parameters and dependence. Sampling uncertainty should be reported for the quantity of interest under its assumptions.

A high number of paths does not correct wrong inputs, model bias or unmodeled liquidity. A rare loss may not appear in a small sample; zero observed occurrences is not proof that its probability is zero.

## 12. Validation and evidence

Check units, P&L signs, scenario probabilities, input ranges, baseline reconciliation and independently derived examples. Recalculate sensitive cases with a separate method where possible. Keep model version, source and timestamp, positions, shocks, outputs, code version and limitations.

Never present an estimated probability as an established fact beyond the specified model. Define a review process for assumptions and identify missing risk factors.

## 13. Reference implementation

Save as `scenario_analysis.py`. It values the simplified two-asset portfolio under specified one-period simple returns and a fixed end-period fee.

~~~python
import math
import unittest

def finite(x):
    return type(x) in (int,float) and math.isfinite(x)

def value_scenario(exposures, returns, fee=0.0):
    exposures, returns = list(exposures), list(returns)
    if not exposures or len(exposures) != len(returns):
        raise ValueError("matching nonempty exposures and returns required")
    if not all(finite(x) and x >= 0 for x in exposures):
        raise ValueError("finite nonnegative exposures required")
    if not all(finite(x) and x >= -1 for x in returns):
        raise ValueError("finite simple returns >= -1 required")
    if not finite(fee) or fee < 0:
        raise ValueError("finite nonnegative fee required")
    try:
        baseline = math.fsum(exposures)
        terminal = math.fsum(e*(1+r) for e,r in zip(exposures,returns))-fee
        pnl = terminal-baseline
    except OverflowError as exc:
        raise ValueError("scenario calculation overflow") from exc
    if not all(math.isfinite(x) for x in (baseline,terminal,pnl)):
        raise ValueError("nonfinite scenario result")
    return {"baseline": baseline, "terminal": terminal, "pnl": pnl}

def weighted_summary(results, probabilities):
    results, probabilities = list(results), list(probabilities)
    if not results or len(results) != len(probabilities):
        raise ValueError("matching nonempty results and probabilities required")
    if any(not finite(p) or p < 0 for p in probabilities):
        raise ValueError("finite nonnegative probabilities required")
    if not math.isclose(math.fsum(probabilities),1.0,rel_tol=0,abs_tol=1e-12):
        raise ValueError("probabilities must sum to one")
    if any(not finite(r["terminal"]) or not finite(r["pnl"]) for r in results):
        raise ValueError("finite results required")
    return {
        "expected_terminal": math.fsum(p*r["terminal"] for p,r in zip(probabilities,results)),
        "expected_pnl": math.fsum(p*r["pnl"] for p,r in zip(probabilities,results)),
        "loss_probability": math.fsum(p for p,r in zip(probabilities,results) if r["pnl"] < 0),
    }

class ScenarioTests(unittest.TestCase):
    def test_base(self):
        self.assertAlmostEqual(value_scenario([60,40],[.05,.02])["terminal"],103.8)
    def test_downside(self):
        self.assertAlmostEqual(value_scenario([60,40],[-.2,-.1])["pnl"],-16)
    def test_upside(self):
        self.assertAlmostEqual(value_scenario([60,40],[.15,.08])["pnl"],12.2)
    def test_zero_shock(self):
        self.assertEqual(value_scenario([60,40],[0,0])["pnl"],0)
    def test_fee(self):
        self.assertEqual(value_scenario([60,40],[0,0],2)["pnl"],-2)
    def test_weighted(self):
        scenarios=[value_scenario([60,40],r) for r in ([.05,.02],[-.2,-.1],[.15,.08])]
        result=weighted_summary(scenarios,[.5,.3,.2])
        self.assertAlmostEqual(result["expected_terminal"],99.54)
        self.assertAlmostEqual(result["expected_pnl"],-.46)
        self.assertAlmostEqual(result["loss_probability"],.3)
    def test_bad_probabilities(self):
        with self.assertRaises(ValueError):
            weighted_summary([value_scenario([100],[0])],[.9])
    def test_bad_return(self):
        with self.assertRaises(ValueError): value_scenario([100],[-1.1])
    def test_bad_length(self):
        with self.assertRaises(ValueError): value_scenario([60,40],[.1])
    def test_nonfinite(self):
        with self.assertRaises(ValueError): value_scenario([float("nan")],[0])

if __name__ == "__main__":
    cases=[value_scenario([60,40],r) for r in ([.05,.02],[-.2,-.1],[.15,.08])]
    print("scenarios:",cases)
    print("weighted:",weighted_summary(cases,[.5,.3,.2]))
    unittest.main(verbosity=2)
~~~

The implementation rejects returns below −100% because it models nonnegative fully funded assets. It does not represent short positions, leverage, derivatives, nonlinear revaluation or dynamic rebalancing. Its weighted summary assumes scenarios refer to the same baseline and horizon; check that consistency externally.

## 16. Troubleshooting

| Symptom | Possible cause | Next step |
|---|---|---|
| P&L sign is reversed | Baseline and terminal values swapped | Recalculate a simple known case |
| Weighted probabilities fail | Values do not sum to one or states overlap | Review model and scenario definitions |
| Currency result is inverted | Quote direction misunderstood | State units and conversion explicitly |
| Extreme scenario appears benign | Missing exposures, nonlinear effects or dependence | Review scope and revaluation method |
| Expected result changes after fee | Fee timing or double counting | Trace fee application |
| Small simulation error but unrealistic risk | Model or parameter assumptions | Review independent stress cases and calibration |
