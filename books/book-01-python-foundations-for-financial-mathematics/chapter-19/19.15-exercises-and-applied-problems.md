## Part A — Sources and formats

### 1. Market data

**Problem:** Distinguish a trade from a quote.

**Solution:** A trade records an execution; a quote generally describes bid and/or ask prices and possibly sizes. Their timestamps and venues may differ.

### 2. Price definition

**Problem:** Why is “close=100” insufficient?

**Solution:** It lacks instrument, date, currency, source, price type and adjustment conventions. The value may also have a publication or revision time.

### 3. Reference data

**Problem:** Is a ticker guaranteed to be a permanent unique instrument identifier?

**Solution:** No. Symbols can change, be reused or differ by venue. Record identifier type and effective mapping.

### 4. Corporate actions

**Problem:** Why can a raw price fall sharply after a stock split without representing an equivalent economic loss?

**Solution:** A split changes shares and per-share price. Compare compatible adjusted or unadjusted data under a documented method.

### 5. Format choice

**Problem:** Does using Parquet or JSON guarantee accurate values?

**Solution:** No. A format defines representation, not source accuracy, completeness or financial semantics.

### 6. CSV quoting

**Problem:** Why is splitting `EXAMPLE,"Example, Class A",100` on every comma incorrect?

**Solution:** The comma inside quotes belongs to one field. Use a CSV parser configured for the dialect.

### 7. Encoding

**Problem:** A UTF-8 BOM appears before the first CSV header. What should the pipeline do?

**Solution:** Follow the documented encoding policy; `utf-8-sig` can remove a leading UTF-8 BOM. Preserve original bytes when byte identity is required.

### 8. JSON missingness

**Problem:** Are an absent key, `null`, `0` and `""` equivalent?

**Solution:** No. They are different structural or value states. Apply the source contract rather than coercing them silently.

### 9. Duplicate JSON keys

**Problem:** Why reject or explicitly handle two `close` keys in one object?

**Solution:** Consumers can resolve duplicate keys differently, making the intended value ambiguous.

### 10. Spreadsheet formulas

**Problem:** Does reading an Excel workbook with `openpyxl` calculate its formulas?

**Solution:** No. Formula cells and cached results have different meanings; cached results may be absent or stale.

## Part B — APIs, parsing and schema

### 11. API status

**Problem:** Does an HTTP success response prove all requested financial records were returned?

**Solution:** No. Validate body schema, pagination, coverage, timestamps and provider-specific completion criteria.

### 12. Pagination

**Problem:** The first page includes a next cursor. Is the dataset complete?

**Solution:** Not under a contract requiring additional pages. Follow the documented cursor until completion or report an incomplete result.

### 13. Retry

**Problem:** Why should a client not retry a write blindly?

**Solution:** Repetition can create side effects or duplicates. Check operation semantics, idempotency keys and provider policy.

### 14. Credentials

**Problem:** Should an API key be written into source code or logs?

**Solution:** No. Use approved secret handling and redact sensitive request information.

### 15. Parsing versus validation

**Problem:** A close parses as a decimal but uses an unexpected currency. Has it passed financial validation?

**Solution:** No. Type conversion succeeds, but the currency contract fails.

### 16. Decimal text

**Problem:** Why might a contract require `"101.50"` as text rather than converting an arbitrary binary float?

**Solution:** Decimal text preserves the specified decimal value for deliberate parsing. Precision, rounding and financial meaning still require a policy.

### 17. Nonfinite values

**Problem:** Should `NaN` be accepted as a positive close?

**Solution:** No. It is nonfinite and fails the example's positive finite price contract.

### 18. Precision and scale

**Problem:** Under a contract allowing six integral digits and two fractional digits, is `123456.78` accepted?

**Solution:** Yes. `1234567.00` exceeds integral width; `1.234` exceeds scale.

### 19. Record identity

**Problem:** Is instrument/date always an adequate duplicate key?

**Solution:** No. Venue, source, price type, currency or revision may be required.

### 20. Schema evolution

**Problem:** A provider changes a rate field from percent to decimal but retains its numeric type. Is this breaking?

**Solution:** Yes for consumers relying on the old meaning. Physical type alone does not capture units.

## Part C — Missing values and quality

### 21. Missing price

**Problem:** Why is replacing a missing close with zero dangerous?

**Solution:** It can create a false −100% return and distort risk measures. Use an explicit missing-data policy.

### 22. Source marker

**Problem:** Does `N/A` have a universal meaning?

**Solution:** No. Its treatment depends on the source contract.

### 23. Negative rates

**Problem:** Must every negative yield be rejected?

**Solution:** No. Domain checks depend on field meaning and market context.

### 24. Outlier

**Problem:** A price changes by 40%. Should it be automatically removed?

**Solution:** No. It may reflect a genuine move, corporate action or error; flag and investigate.

### 25. Duplicate revision

**Problem:** Two records share an effective date but have different publication times. Should the latest always overwrite the earlier one?

**Solution:** No. Preserve revisions when point-in-time use requires them, and apply a documented precedence policy.

### 26. Missing-rate denominator

**Problem:** One of four submitted rows has a missing close. Calculate the missing rate.

**Solution:** **25%**, if the rule counts one missing row and uses all four submitted rows as the denominator.

### 27. Empty batch

**Problem:** What is the missing rate for zero submitted rows?

**Solution:** It is undefined under a missing/submitted ratio. Report the empty batch explicitly.

### 28. Quarantine

**Problem:** What does quarantining a record mean?

**Solution:** Separating it with an identifiable reason for review rather than silently discarding or correcting it.

### 29. Completeness

**Problem:** Can every submitted row be valid while the dataset is incomplete?

**Solution:** Yes. Expected dates or instruments may be absent; completeness requires an expected coverage definition.

### 30. Reconciliation

**Problem:** Two price sources disagree. What should be aligned before interpreting the difference?

**Solution:** Instrument, venue, price type, currency, time, adjustment, units and rounding.

## Part D — Time and units

### 31. Date versus instant

**Problem:** Does `2026-01-05` identify a UTC instant?

**Solution:** No. It is a calendar date without a time or timezone.

### 32. UTC conversion

**Problem:** Convert 2026-01-05 09:30 at UTC−05:00 to UTC.

**Solution:** **2026-01-05 14:30 UTC**. The instant remains the same.

### 33. Daylight saving

**Problem:** Can a local wall time occur twice?

**Solution:** Yes, during some fall-back transitions. Require an offset or a documented disambiguation rule.

### 34. Availability

**Problem:** A record is published at 12:00Z and ingested at 12:01Z. Under a rule requiring both by the decision, is it available at 12:00:30Z?

**Solution:** No. Ingestion occurs after the decision.

### 35. Rate conversion

**Problem:** Convert 5% and 100 basis points to decimal rates.

**Solution:** 5%=**.05**; 100 bp=**.01**.

### 36. Percentage-point change

**Problem:** A rate rises from 5% to 6%. Express the change in percentage points, basis points and relative terms.

**Solution:** +1 percentage point, +100 bp, and a 20% relative increase.

### 37. Currency denomination

**Problem:** Convert 10,000 USD cents to dollars under the stated USD convention.

**Solution:** **USD 100**. Do not assume all currencies have the same minor-unit convention.

### 38. FX quote

**Problem:** Convert EUR 100 at 1.10 USD per EUR.

**Solution:** 100×1.10=**USD 110**. Quote direction determines the operation.

### 39. FX movement

**Problem:** EUR 100 remains constant while USD/EUR changes from 1.10 to 1.05. Calculate USD P&L.

**Solution:** USD value falls from 110 to 105; P&L is **−USD 5**, before costs.

### 40. Rounding

**Problem:** Under half-even rounding, round 1.005 to two decimal places.

**Solution:** **1.00**. This is an illustrative policy, not a universal settlement rule.

## Part E — Reproducibility and point-in-time analysis

### 41. Input hash

**Problem:** What does a SHA-256 digest of raw file bytes establish?

**Solution:** It identifies the bytes hashed for comparison; it does not by itself prove authenticity, completeness or accuracy.

### 42. Whitespace

**Problem:** Can changing JSON whitespace change the raw hash without changing the parsed object?

**Solution:** Yes. Byte identity and semantic equivalence are different; define canonicalization if needed.

### 43. Determinism

**Problem:** Does a deterministic transformation make an append-only sink idempotent?

**Solution:** No. A retry can append duplicate outputs unless the sink has a suitable key and write policy.

### 44. Late data

**Problem:** Can a watermark based only on maximum event time miss a late correction?

**Solution:** Yes. Define late-arrival, revision and reprocessing behavior.

### 45. Look-ahead

**Problem:** A quarter ends March 31, its value is published May 1, and a decision occurs April 15. May the decision use it?

**Solution:** No, under the stated timeline. Effective period and availability time differ.

### 46. Revision

**Problem:** A value is 100 on May 1 and corrected to 105 on May 10. Which version is available on May 5, assuming publication and ingestion occurred together?

**Solution:** **100**. Using 105 on May 5 would leak a later correction.

### 47. Rolling window

**Problem:** Why can a centered rolling mean leak?

**Solution:** It can include future observations relative to the decision point. A trailing window also needs an availability check.

### 48. Preprocessing

**Problem:** Why fit a scaler using training data only?

**Solution:** Fitting it on the full dataset lets evaluation-period information influence training. Apply training-fitted parameters to later data.

### 49. Survivorship

**Problem:** What can be missed if a historical universe includes only instruments that survive to the end?

**Solution:** Failed or delisted instruments, creating survivorship bias. Point-in-time membership and delisting coverage matter.

### 50. Pipeline acceptance

**Problem:** Two comparable USD closes 100.00 and 101.50 are both available before a decision. Calculate simple return and state what remains to validate.

**Solution:** Return=101.50/100−1=**.015**, or 1.5%. Validate source identity, price type, currency, corporate actions, availability evidence and revision history.

## Reference snippet — decimal return

~~~python
from decimal import Decimal, localcontext

with localcontext() as ctx:
    ctx.prec = 28
    start = Decimal("100.00")
    end = Decimal("101.50")
    result = end / start - Decimal("1")
    print("simple return:", result)
~~~

This snippet demonstrates the arithmetic only. It does not validate source, currency, availability, price type or corporate actions. Its inclusion is not evidence of execution.
