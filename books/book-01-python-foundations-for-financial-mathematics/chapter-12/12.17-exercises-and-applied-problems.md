## Formula reference

For face F, annual coupon c, coupon frequency m, remaining coupon periods n, and nominal annual yield y:

**Coupon C = Fc/m.**

**P(y) = Σ[k=1..n] CF(k)/(1+y/m)^k**, where CF(n) includes principal.

**D_Mac = Σ[k×PV(CF(k))]/(mP).**

**D_Mod = D_Mac/(1+y/m).**

**Convexity = Σ[k(k+1)×PV(CF(k))]/[m²(1+y/m)²P].**

**DV01 = P×D_Mod×0.0001.**

**ΔP ≈ −P×D_Mod×Δy + 0.5×P×Convexity×(Δy)².**

For annual-effective spot rate s(t):

**DF(t) = (1+s(t))^(−t)**, and **PV=ΣCF(t)DF(t)**.

For annual-effective forward rate over t1 to t2:

**f(t1,t2) = [DF(t1)/DF(t2)]^[1/(t2−t1)] − 1**.

## Part A — Bond structure and cash flows

### Exercise 1 — Coupon amount

A $10,000 face bond has an 8% annual coupon, paid semiannually. Find each coupon.

**Solution:** C=10,000×0.08/2=**$400**.

### Exercise 2 — Final payment

The bond in Exercise 1 has three years remaining. How many payments and what is the final payment?

**Solution:** n=3×2=**6 payments**. Final payment=$400+$10,000=**$10,400**.

### Exercise 3 — Zero-coupon structure

A $5,000 face zero-coupon bond matures in four years. What contractual cash flows occur?

**Solution:** No periodic coupons. There is **one $5,000 payment at maturity**.

### Exercise 4 — Premium and discount intuition

A bond pays an 8% coupon, while the market YTM is 6%, under matching conventions. Is it priced at premium or discount?

**Solution:** **Premium**. Coupon cash flows are larger than those required by the 6% market yield, so price exceeds par.

## Part B — Pricing and yield

### Exercise 5 — Par bond

Price a $1,000 face bond with a 6% annual coupon, semiannual payments, three years remaining, and 6% nominal annual YTM.

**Solution:** C=$30; n=6; periodic yield=3%. The coupon equals yield under matching conventions, so **P=$1,000**.

### Exercise 6 — Zero-coupon price

Find the price of a $1,000 face zero-coupon bond due in two years at 4% nominal annual YTM compounded semiannually.

**Solution:** P=1000/(1.02)^4 ≈ **$923.85**.

### Exercise 7 — Yield-price direction

If a fixed-cash-flow bond's yield rises from 5% to 6%, what happens to its price?

**Solution:** Price **falls** because each positive cash flow is discounted more heavily.

### Exercise 8 — YTM inversion

A conventional bond with the terms in Exercise 5 trades for $1,000. What is its nominal annual YTM?

**Solution:** **6%**, since the price equals face and the coupon rate is 6%, under the same semiannual compounding convention.

## Part C — Accrued interest

### Exercise 9 — Clean versus dirty price

A bond's clean price is $99.25 per $100 face and accrued interest is $0.80 per $100 face. Find dirty price.

**Solution:** Dirty=clean+accrued=**$100.05 per $100 face**.

### Exercise 10 — Simplified accrued interest

A bond has a $30 semiannual coupon and 40 of 180 days have elapsed since the previous coupon date, under a simplified actual/actual coupon-period fraction. Calculate accrued interest.

**Solution:** AI=$30×40/180≈**$6.67**. Actual settlement conventions can differ; this is a stated classroom simplification.

## Part D — Duration and convexity

### Exercise 11 — Macaulay versus modified duration

A bond's Macaulay duration is 4.2 years and periodic yield is 2.5%. Find modified duration.

**Solution:** D_Mod=4.2/1.025≈**4.09756**.

### Exercise 12 — Duration-only P&L

A bond position has market value $50,000 and modified duration 4.0. Estimate the value change for a +25-bp yield shock.

**Solution:** Δy=0.0025. ΔP≈−50,000×4×0.0025=**−$500**.

### Exercise 13 — DV01

For the position in Exercise 12, calculate DV01.

**Solution:** DV01=50,000×4×0.0001=**$20 per bp**.

### Exercise 14 — Convexity adjustment

A bond has P=$1,000, D_Mod=4.5, convexity=25, and yield rises 100 bp. Estimate the price change using duration plus convexity.

**Solution:** Duration term=−1000×4.5×0.01=−$45. Convexity term=0.5×1000×25×(0.01)²=$1.25. Total=**−$43.75**.

### Exercise 15 — Approximation accuracy

Why does duration-only pricing differ from exact repricing after a large yield shock?

**Solution:** Duration uses a first-order tangent approximation. The true price/yield function is nonlinear; convexity and higher-order terms affect the result.

## Part E — Yield curves and forwards

### Exercise 16 — Spot discount factor

Calculate the two-year discount factor at a 4% annual-effective spot rate.

**Solution:** DF(2)=1/(1.04)²≈**0.92455621**.

### Exercise 17 — Spot valuation

A security pays $30 at year 1 and $1,030 at year 2. Annual-effective spot rates are 4% and 5%. Find present value.

**Solution:** P=30/1.04+1030/(1.05)²≈**$963.09**.

### Exercise 18 — Forward rate

The 1-year spot rate is 3% and the 2-year spot rate is 3.5%, both annual-effective. Find the 1-year forward beginning in year 1.

**Solution:** f(1,2)=(1.035)²/1.03−1≈**4.0024%**.

## Part F — Advanced applied problems

### Exercise 19 — Portfolio DV01

Three long bond positions have DV01 values of $12, $18, and $25 per bp. Find portfolio DV01 under a common parallel yield shock.

**Solution:** **$55 per bp**. A +10-bp shock implies an approximate **−$550** duration-only price change.

### Exercise 20 — Hedging

A portfolio has positive DV01 of $100/bp. A hedge position has signed DV01 of −$80/bp. Find net signed DV01.

**Solution:** 100−80=**+$20/bp**. The hedge reduces but does not eliminate first-order parallel-rate exposure.

### Exercise 21 — Curve shape

The 1-year rate is 4.8% and the 10-year rate is 4.1%. Describe the endpoint slope.

**Solution:** 4.1%−4.8%=**−70 bp**, an inverted endpoint slope.

### Exercise 22 — Compounding equivalence

Convert nominal 6% with semiannual compounding to an annual-effective rate.

**Solution:** (1+0.06/2)²−1=**6.09%**.

### Exercise 23 — Negative rates

Can a discount factor exceed one? Give a valid example.

**Solution:** Yes. At an annual-effective spot rate of −1% for two years, DF(2)=1/(0.99)²≈**1.02030**.

### Exercise 24 — Model limitations

A bond has an embedded call option. Is positive fixed-cash-flow convexity always sufficient to model its rate risk?

**Solution:** **No.** Exercise behavior can change expected cash flows and cause effective convexity to differ substantially, including potentially negative convexity.

This reference lab covers bond pricing, risk metrics, spot valuation, forwards, scenarios, and **12 unit tests**. Independent runtime verification remains pending.

```python
import math, unittest
def price(face, coupon, periods, y, m=2):
    if any(isinstance(v,bool) or not isinstance(v,(int,float)) or not math.isfinite(v) for v in (face,coupon,y)) or face<=0 or coupon<0:raise ValueError('invalid terms')
    if any(isinstance(v,bool) or not isinstance(v,int) or v<1 for v in (periods,m)) or y/m<=-1:raise ValueError('invalid frequency/periods/yield')
    c=face*coupon/m; q=y/m
    return math.fsum((c+(face if k==periods else 0))/(1+q)**k for k in range(1,periods+1))
def metrics(face,coupon,periods,y,m=2):
    p=price(face,coupon,periods,y,m);q=y/m;c=face*coupon/m
    values=[(k,(c+(face if k==periods else 0))/(1+q)**k) for k in range(1,periods+1)]
    mac=math.fsum(k*v for k,v in values)/(m*p)
    mod=mac/(1+q)
    convex=math.fsum(k*(k+1)*v for k,v in values)/(m*m*(1+q)**2*p)
    return p,mac,mod,convex,p*mod*.0001
def spot_pv(cashflows,spots):
    if not cashflows:raise ValueError('cashflows required')
    terms=[]
    for t,cf in cashflows:
        if t not in spots:raise ValueError('missing spot')
        r=spots[t]
        if not all(isinstance(v,(int,float)) and not isinstance(v,bool) and math.isfinite(v) for v in (t,cf,r)) or t<=0 or r<=-1:raise ValueError('invalid cashflow/spot')
        terms.append(cf/(1+r)**t)
    return math.fsum(terms)
def forward(df1,df2,t1,t2):
    if not all(isinstance(v,(int,float)) and not isinstance(v,bool) and math.isfinite(v) for v in (df1,df2,t1,t2)) or df1<=0 or df2<=0 or not 0<=t1<t2:raise ValueError('invalid forward')
    return math.expm1((math.log(df1)-math.log(df2))/(t2-t1))
def shock(face,coupon,periods,y,bp,m=2):
    if isinstance(bp,bool) or not isinstance(bp,(int,float)) or not math.isfinite(bp):raise ValueError('invalid shock')
    p,_,d,c,_=metrics(face,coupon,periods,y,m);dy=bp/10000
    exact=price(face,coupon,periods,y+dy,m)-p
    linear=-p*d*dy;second=linear+.5*p*c*dy*dy
    return exact,linear,second
class ReviewTests(unittest.TestCase):
    def test_01_par(self):self.assertAlmostEqual(price(1000,.06,6,.06),1000)
    def test_02_premium(self):self.assertGreater(price(1000,.08,6,.06),1000)
    def test_03_discount(self):self.assertLess(price(1000,.04,6,.06),1000)
    def test_04_zero(self):self.assertAlmostEqual(price(1000,0,4,.04),1000/1.02**4)
    def test_05_price_yield(self):self.assertLess(price(1000,.06,6,.07),price(1000,.06,6,.06))
    def test_06_duration(self):self.assertGreater(metrics(1000,.06,6,.06)[2],0)
    def test_07_convexity(self):self.assertGreater(metrics(1000,.06,6,.06)[3],0)
    def test_08_dv01(self):
        p,_,d,_,v=metrics(1000,.06,6,.06);self.assertAlmostEqual(v,p*d*.0001)
    def test_09_spot(self):self.assertAlmostEqual(spot_pv([(1,30),(2,1030)],{1:.04,2:.05}),30/1.04+1030/1.05**2)
    def test_10_forward(self):self.assertAlmostEqual(forward(1/1.03,1/1.035**2,1,2),1.035**2/1.03-1)
    def test_11_approximation(self):
        exact,linear,second=shock(1000,.06,6,.06,100);self.assertLess(abs(exact-second),abs(exact-linear))
    def test_12_invalid(self):
        for fn in (lambda:price(0,.06,6,.06),lambda:price(1000,.06,0,.06),lambda:spot_pv([(2,100)],{1:.03}),lambda:forward(.9,.8,2,1)):
            with self.assertRaises(ValueError):fn()
if __name__=='__main__':
    for y in (.04,.05,.06,.07,.08):print(f'YTM {y:.1%}: price={price(1000,.06,6,y):.4f}')
    print('Analytics:',metrics(1000,.06,6,.06))
    for bp in (-200,-100,-50,50,100,200):print('Shock',bp,'bp:',shock(1000,.06,6,.06,bp))
    unittest.main(verbosity=2)
```
