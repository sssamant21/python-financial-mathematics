## 1. The central relationship

For a conventional fixed-coupon bond with positive promised cash flows, a higher discount yield means a **lower price**. Conversely, a lower yield means a **higher price**.

This is a mathematical relationship holding the bond's cash-flow schedule fixed. It does not mean that every market price movement can be explained by a change in a single yield: credit spreads, liquidity, embedded options, and other factors may also change.

## 2. The price function

Let F denote face value, c annual coupon rate, m payment frequency, n remaining coupon periods, C=Fc/m, and y the nominal annual yield compounded m times annually.

The periodic yield is **i=y/m**, and the price is:

**P(y)=sum from k=1 to n of CF(k)/(1+y/m)^k**, where the final cash flow includes principal.

For a conventional bullet bond:

**CF(k)=C** for k<n, and **CF(n)=C+F**.

This section assumes positive contractual cash flows, equal periods, a valuation date on a coupon date, and no change in the promised payment schedule.

## 3. Deriving the inverse price/yield relationship

Differentiate with respect to the nominal annual yield y:

**dP/dy = -(1/m) sum from k=1 to n of k×CF(k)/(1+y/m)^(k+1).**

For CF(k)>0 and y/m>-1, each term in the sum is positive before the negative sign. Therefore:

**dP/dy<0.**

The bond's price is a strictly decreasing function of yield. This also establishes why the basic yield-to-maturity equation has a unique solution for a positive-cash-flow bond within the model's yield domain.

## 4. Worked example — Three-year coupon bond

Consider a $1,000 face-value bond paying a 6% annual coupon semiannually with three years to maturity.

- Semiannual coupon: **$30**.
- Remaining coupon periods: **6**.
- Final payment: **$1,030**.

At a 6% nominal annual yield, the price is **$1,000**. At 4%, the price rises above par; at 8%, it falls below par.

The exact values can be produced by the Python lab rather than rounding intermediate discount factors.

|---:|---|---|

| 5% | Premium | Higher price |
| 6% | Par | Baseline |

| 8% | Discount | Lower price |

## 5. Why the price/yield curve is convex

Differentiate the price function a second time:

**d²P/dy² = (1/m²) sum from k=1 to n of k(k+1)×CF(k)/(1+y/m)^(k+2).**

For positive cash flows and valid yields:

**d²P/dy²>0.**

Thus, the price/yield curve is **convex**: it slopes downward but bends upward.

For equal-sized positive and negative yield changes, the price gain from the decrease in yield generally exceeds the price loss from the increase, in absolute terms, for a conventional option-free bond.

This is **positive convexity**. Callable and mortgage-related securities may have more complex or negative effective convexity over some ranges because their expected cash flows can change as yields move.

## 6. Modified duration as the normalized slope

Define **modified duration**:

**D_modified = -(1/P)(dP/dy).**

For a small yield change Δy:

**ΔP/P ≈ -D_modified×Δy.**

For example, if modified duration is 2.7 years and nominal annual yield rises by 0.01 (100 basis points), the first-order estimate is:

**ΔP/P ≈ -2.7×0.01 = -2.7%.**

This is an approximation, not the exact repricing result. The yield change must be entered as a decimal, not as the number 1.

## 7. Convexity as normalized curvature

Define **convexity** under the same yield convention:

**Convexity = (1/P)(d²P/dy²).**

A second-order price-change approximation is:

**ΔP/P ≈ -D_modified×Δy + (1/2)×Convexity×(Δy)².**

The convexity correction is positive for either sign of Δy when convexity is positive.

## 8. Coupon, maturity, and yield effects

For otherwise comparable conventional bonds:

- **Longer maturity** generally increases interest-rate sensitivity because cash flows extend further into the future.
- **Lower coupon rates** generally increase duration because more of the present value is concentrated in the principal payment.
- **Higher yields** generally reduce the modified duration of a fixed-cash-flow bond, although exact comparisons depend on the structure and conventions.
- **Zero-coupon bonds** concentrate all promised payments at maturity; their Macaulay duration equals maturity.

These are useful comparative tendencies, not substitutes for calculating actual cash-flow-weighted sensitivities.

## 9. Basis points and scenario analysis

One basis point (bp) equals **0.01 percentage point**, or **0.0001** in decimal yield.

- +25 bp = +0.0025.
- +50 bp = +0.0050.
- +100 bp = +0.0100.

For a yield shock, calculate the **exact repriced bond value** and compare it with first- and second-order approximations.

For a fixed-cash-flow bond, exact repricing provides the benchmark; duration and convexity are sensitivity summaries.

## 10. Finite-difference validation

Analytical derivatives can be checked numerically using a small yield step h:

**dP/dy ≈ [P(y+h)-P(y-h)]/(2h).**

**d²P/dy² ≈ [P(y+h)-2P(y)+P(y-h)]/h².**

These are central finite-difference approximations. Extremely small h can amplify floating-point rounding errors; overly large h increases truncation error.

```python
"""Chapter 12.7: bond price/yield derivatives and finite differences."""
import math
import unittest

def check(face, coupon_rate, periods, frequency, yield_rate):
    for x in (face,coupon_rate,yield_rate):
        if isinstance(x,bool) or not isinstance(x,(int,float)) or not math.isfinite(x):
            raise ValueError("finite numeric input required")
    if face<=0 or coupon_rate<0:raise ValueError("invalid bond terms")
    if isinstance(periods,bool) or not isinstance(periods,int) or periods<1:raise ValueError("periods must be positive integer")
    if isinstance(frequency,bool) or not isinstance(frequency,int) or frequency<1:raise ValueError("frequency must be positive integer")
    if yield_rate/frequency<=-1:raise ValueError("periodic yield must exceed -100%")

def cashflows(face,coupon_rate,periods,frequency=2):
    check(face,coupon_rate,periods,frequency,0)
    coupon=face*coupon_rate/frequency
    return [coupon+(face if k==periods else 0.0) for k in range(1,periods+1)]

def metrics(face,coupon_rate,periods,yield_rate,frequency=2):
    check(face,coupon_rate,periods,frequency,yield_rate)
    i=yield_rate/frequency
    flows=cashflows(face,coupon_rate,periods,frequency)
    logd=math.log1p(i)
    terms=[cf*math.exp(-k*logd) for k,cf in enumerate(flows,1)]
    p=math.fsum(terms)
    d1=-math.fsum(k*pv/(frequency*(1+i)) for k,pv in enumerate(terms,1))
    d2=math.fsum(k*(k+1)*pv/(frequency**2*(1+i)**2) for k,pv in enumerate(terms,1))
    return {"price":p,"first_derivative":d1,"second_derivative":d2,
            "modified_duration":-d1/p,"convexity":d2/p}

def finite_difference(face,coupon_rate,periods,yield_rate,step=1e-4,frequency=2):
    if not math.isfinite(step) or step<=0:raise ValueError("step must be positive finite")
    p0=metrics(face,coupon_rate,periods,yield_rate,frequency)["price"]
    up=metrics(face,coupon_rate,periods,yield_rate+step,frequency)["price"]
    down=metrics(face,coupon_rate,periods,yield_rate-step,frequency)["price"]
    return {"slope":(up-down)/(2*step),"curvature":(up-2*p0+down)/(step**2)}

class Tests(unittest.TestCase):
    def setUp(self):self.a=(1000,.06,6,.06,2)
    def test_01_par(self):self.assertAlmostEqual(metrics(*self.a)["price"],1000)
    def test_02_price_down(self):self.assertLess(metrics(1000,.06,6,.07)["price"],1000)
    def test_03_price_up(self):self.assertGreater(metrics(1000,.06,6,.05)["price"],1000)
    def test_04_negative_slope(self):self.assertLess(metrics(*self.a)["first_derivative"],0)
    def test_05_positive_curvature(self):self.assertGreater(metrics(*self.a)["second_derivative"],0)
    def test_06_duration_positive(self):self.assertGreater(metrics(*self.a)["modified_duration"],0)
    def test_07_convexity_positive(self):self.assertGreater(metrics(*self.a)["convexity"],0)
    def test_08_slope_finite_difference(self):
        a=metrics(*self.a)
        f=finite_difference(1000,.06,6,.06)
        self.assertAlmostEqual(a["first_derivative"],f["slope"],delta=.01)
    def test_09_curvature_finite_difference(self):
        a=metrics(*self.a)
        f=finite_difference(1000,.06,6,.06)
        self.assertAlmostEqual(a["second_derivative"],f["curvature"],delta=.1)
    def test_10_zero_coupon_duration(self):
        self.assertAlmostEqual(metrics(1000,0,6,.06)["modified_duration"],3/1.03)
    def test_11_longer_zero_coupon(self):
        self.assertGreater(metrics(1000,0,10,.06)["modified_duration"],
                           metrics(1000,0,6,.06)["modified_duration"])
    def test_12_validation(self):
        for fn in (lambda:metrics(0,.06,6,.06),lambda:metrics(1000,-.01,6,.06),
                   lambda:metrics(1000,.06,0,.06),lambda:metrics(1000,.06,6,-2),
                   lambda:finite_difference(1000,.06,6,.06,0)):
            with self.assertRaises(ValueError):fn()

if __name__=="__main__":
    for y in (.04,.05,.06,.07,.08):
        m=metrics(1000,.06,6,y)
        print(f"Yield={y:.1%}; price={m['price']:.2f}; duration={m['modified_duration']:.4f}; convexity={m['convexity']:.4f}")
    unittest.main(verbosity=2)
```

## 12. Exercises with worked answers

1. **Direction:** What happens to a conventional bond price when yield rises? **Answer:** It falls, holding promised cash flows fixed.
2. **Why:** Why does higher yield reduce price? **Answer:** Each future cash flow is discounted more heavily.
3. **Coupon:** Find the semiannual coupon on $1,000 at 6% annually. **Answer:** $30.
4. **Periods:** How many periods remain in three years semiannually? **Answer:** Six.
5. **Par:** What is price when coupon rate and yield are both 6% under the same convention? **Answer:** $1,000.
6. **Premium:** Is price above or below par at 4% yield? **Answer:** Above par.
7. **Discount:** Is price above or below par at 8% yield? **Answer:** Below par.
8. **First derivative:** What is the sign of dP/dy for positive cash flows? **Answer:** Negative.
9. **Second derivative:** What is the sign of d²P/dy² for positive fixed cash flows? **Answer:** Positive.
10. **Convexity:** What does positive convexity mean? **Answer:** The downward-sloping price/yield curve bends upward.
11. **Modified duration:** Define it. **Answer:** -(1/P)(dP/dy).
12. **Duration approximation:** Estimate price change for duration 2.7 and +100 bp. **Answer:** Approximately -2.7%.
13. **Basis points:** Convert 50 bp to decimal yield. **Answer:** 0.005.
14. **Convexity approximation:** Give the second-order formula. **Answer:** ΔP/P≈-D_modifiedΔy+0.5 Convexity(Δy)².
15. **Finite difference:** Give a central approximation to dP/dy. **Answer:** [P(y+h)-P(y-h)]/(2h).
16. **Maturity:** Why does longer maturity often increase sensitivity? **Answer:** More value depends on distant discounted cash flows.
17. **Zero coupon:** What is the Macaulay duration of a five-year zero-coupon bond? **Answer:** Five years.
18. **Options:** Why might a callable bond not show the same positive-convexity behavior? **Answer:** Expected payments can change when the call option becomes valuable.
