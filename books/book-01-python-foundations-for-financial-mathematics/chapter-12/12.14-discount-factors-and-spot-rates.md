## 1. The discount factor as the core pricing quantity

A **discount factor** DF(t) converts one unit of currency payable at future time t into its present value at the valuation date, under a specified discounting curve and currency.

**PV of CF at t = CF × DF(t).**

For a sequence of dated cash flows:

**P = Σ CF(k) × DF(t(k)).**

This representation separates contractual cash flows from the discount curve. It is the foundation of spot-curve valuation and many fixed-income pricing systems.

## 2. Spot rates

A **spot rate** (or zero-coupon rate) for maturity t is the interest rate that corresponds to a discount factor for a single payment at t, under an explicitly specified compounding convention.

Spot rates are not inherently the same as the yield to maturity of a coupon bond. A coupon bond has payments at multiple dates, whereas a spot rate applies to one maturity.

## 3. Annual-effective compounding

For annual-effective spot rate r(t) and time t measured in years:

**DF(t) = 1/[1+r(t)]^t**, assuming **1+r(t)>0**.

Inverting:

**r(t) = DF(t)^(-1/t) − 1.**

For a two-year spot rate of 4%:

**DF(2) = 1/(1.04)^2 ≈ 0.92455621.**

A $1,000 zero-coupon payment at two years has present value approximately **$924.56**.

## 4. Periodic nominal compounding

For nominal annual spot rate r(t) compounded m times per year:

**DF(t) = [1+r(t)/m]^(-mt).**

The inverse is:

**r(t) = m[DF(t)^(-1/(mt))−1].**

For a nominal 4% rate with semiannual compounding, t=2, and m=2:

**DF(2) = (1.02)^(-4) ≈ 0.92384543.**

This differs from the annual-effective 4% discount factor. Comparing rates without identifying compounding is an avoidable valuation error.

## 5. Continuous compounding

For continuously compounded spot rate z(t):

**DF(t) = exp[−z(t)t].**

Inverting:

**z(t) = −ln[DF(t)]/t.**

At z=4% and t=2:

**DF(2) = exp(−0.08) ≈ 0.92311635.**

The three discount factors above differ because the numerical rate 4% has different meanings under the three conventions.

## 6. Compounding equivalence

Different quoted spot rates can describe the **same economic discount factor** if converted consistently.

For annual-effective r and continuous z at the same maturity:

**z = ln(1+r).**

**r = exp(z)−1.**

For nominal m-times-compounded rate j and continuous z:

**z = m ln(1+j/m).**

**j = m[exp(z/m)−1].**

Never compare two quoted yields as though they were equivalent until their compounding and day-count bases are aligned.

## 7. Worked example — Discounting multiple payments

Suppose a security pays $30 at year 1 and $1,030 at year 2. Annual-effective spot rates are:

| Maturity | Spot rate | Discount factor |
|---:|---:|---|
| 1 year | 4% | 1/1.04 |
| 2 years | 5% | 1/(1.05)^2 |

The present value is:

**P = 30/(1.04) + 1030/(1.05)^2.**

Numerically:

**P ≈ $963.07.**

Each payment uses the discount factor for its own maturity. Using one YTM for both cash flows would be a different pricing assumption.

## 8. Discount factors and zero-coupon securities

A default-free zero-coupon instrument with face amount F payable at t has theoretical price:

**P_zero = F × DF(t).**

If F=$1,000 and DF(2)=0.92455621, the theoretical value is approximately $924.56.

The appropriate discount curve depends on collateral, funding, issuer credit, currency, and market conventions. The term “risk-free discount factor” should not be used without specifying the underlying benchmark.

## 9. Negative rates and discount factors above one

If the relevant spot rate is negative, a discount factor can exceed 1.

For example, with an annual-effective spot rate of −1% over two years:

**DF(2) = 1/(0.99)^2 > 1.**

This is mathematically valid under the specified compounding model. Therefore **DF(t) must be positive**, but it is **not universally required to be at most one**.

In a deterministic discount-factor framework, further no-arbitrage checks depend on the modeled instruments, funding assumptions, and whether negative rates are permitted.

## 10. Implied forward rates from discount factors

For 0 < t1 < t2, define the annual-effective forward rate over [t1,t2] by:

**[1+f(t1,t2)]^(t2−t1) = DF(t1)/DF(t2).**

Thus:

**f(t1,t2) = [DF(t1)/DF(t2)]^[1/(t2−t1)] − 1.**

This follows from consistency between discounting directly to t2 and discounting first to t1 then over the forward interval.

The forward is **implied by today's curve**, not a guaranteed realized future rate. Section 12.15 develops forward-rate calculations and applications.

## 11. Bootstrapping intuition

Observed coupon-bond prices can constrain unknown discount factors. If earlier payment-date discount factors are known, the remaining discount factor may be solved from:

**P = Σ earlier CF(k)DF(t(k)) + CF(final)DF(T).**

Rearrange:

**DF(T) = [P − Σ earlier CF(k)DF(t(k))]/CF(final).**

This simplified sequential idea is a foundation for **bootstrapping**, but real curve construction must account for instrument-specific cash-flow calendars, settlement, accrued interest, interpolation, collateral, and calibration.

## 12. Data quality and operational controls

- Validate maturity t > 0 for inverse spot calculations.
- Require strictly positive discount factors.
- Identify compounding and day-count conventions for every rate.
- Avoid confusing clean market quotes with dirty cash-flow present values.
- Check for missing curve nodes and out-of-range interpolation.
- Use numerically stable functions such as `log1p` and `expm1` when rates are small.
- Do not claim arbitrage-free calibration merely because an interpolated curve produces positive discount factors.

import math, unittest
def check(t,x):
    if any(isinstance(v,bool) or not isinstance(v,(int,float)) or not math.isfinite(v) for v in (t,x)) or t<=0:raise ValueError('invalid tenor or rate/factor')
def df_from_spot(rate,t,compounding='annual',frequency=2):
    check(t,rate)
    if compounding=='annual':
        if rate<=-1:raise ValueError('rate outside domain')
        return math.exp(-t*math.log1p(rate))
    if compounding=='continuous':return math.exp(-rate*t)
    if compounding=='periodic':
        if isinstance(frequency,bool) or not isinstance(frequency,int) or frequency<1 or rate/frequency<=-1:raise ValueError('invalid periodic terms')
        return math.exp(-frequency*t*math.log1p(rate/frequency))
    raise ValueError('unknown compounding')
def spot_from_df(df,t,compounding='annual',frequency=2):
    check(t,df)
    if df<=0:raise ValueError('discount factor must be positive')
    z=-math.log(df)/t
    if compounding=='annual':return math.expm1(z)
    if compounding=='continuous':return z
    if compounding=='periodic':
        if isinstance(frequency,bool) or not isinstance(frequency,int) or frequency<1:raise ValueError('invalid frequency')
        return frequency*math.expm1(z/frequency)
    raise ValueError('unknown compounding')
def pv(cashflows,rate_by_time,compounding='annual'):
    if not cashflows:raise ValueError('cashflows required')
    total=[]
    for t,amount in cashflows:
        if isinstance(amount,bool) or not isinstance(amount,(int,float)) or not math.isfinite(amount):raise ValueError('invalid cashflow')
        if t not in rate_by_time:raise ValueError('missing spot rate')
        total.append(amount*df_from_spot(rate_by_time[t],t,compounding))
    return math.fsum(total)
def implied_forward(df1,df2,t1,t2):
    if any(isinstance(v,bool) or not isinstance(v,(int,float)) or not math.isfinite(v) for v in (df1,df2,t1,t2)) or df1<=0 or df2<=0 or not 0<t1<t2:raise ValueError('invalid forward inputs')
    return math.expm1(math.log(df1/df2)/(t2-t1))
class Tests(unittest.TestCase):
    def test_01_annual(self):self.assertAlmostEqual(df_from_spot(.04,2),1/1.04**2)
    def test_02_continuous(self):self.assertAlmostEqual(df_from_spot(.04,2,'continuous'),math.exp(-.08))
    def test_03_periodic(self):self.assertAlmostEqual(df_from_spot(.04,2,'periodic',2),1/1.02**4)
    def test_04_annual_inverse(self):self.assertAlmostEqual(spot_from_df(df_from_spot(.04,2),2),.04)
    def test_05_cont_inverse(self):self.assertAlmostEqual(spot_from_df(df_from_spot(.04,2,'continuous'),2,'continuous'),.04)
    def test_06_periodic_inverse(self):self.assertAlmostEqual(spot_from_df(df_from_spot(.04,2,'periodic',2),2,'periodic',2),.04)
    def test_07_zero_rate(self):self.assertEqual(df_from_spot(0,2),1)
    def test_08_negative_rate(self):self.assertGreater(df_from_spot(-.01,2),1)
    def test_09_pv(self):self.assertAlmostEqual(pv([(1,30),(2,1030)],{1:.04,2:.05}),30/1.04+1030/1.05**2)
    def test_10_forward(self):self.assertAlmostEqual(implied_forward(1/1.03,1/1.035**2,1,2),(1.035**2)/1.03-1)
    def test_11_linearity(self):self.assertAlmostEqual(pv([(2,100),(2,200)],{2:.04}),pv([(2,300)],{2:.04}))
    def test_12_invalid(self):
        for f in (lambda:df_from_spot(-1,2),lambda:df_from_spot(.04,0),lambda:spot_from_df(0,2),lambda:df_from_spot(.04,2,'periodic',0),lambda:pv([(2,100)],{1:.04}),lambda:implied_forward(.9,.8,2,1)):
            with self.assertRaises(ValueError):f()
if __name__=='__main__':

    print('Coupon bond PV:',pv([(1,30),(2,1030)],{1:.04,2:.05}))

## 14. Exercises with worked answers

1. **Definition:** What does DF(t) represent? **Answer:** Present value of one unit payable at time t.
2. **Single cash flow:** Give its PV formula. **Answer:** PV=CF×DF(t).
3. **Multiple cash flows:** Give the price formula. **Answer:** P=ΣCF(k)DF(t(k)).
4. **Annual DF:** Formula? **Answer:** (1+r)^(-t).
5. **Annual inverse:** Formula? **Answer:** DF^(-1/t)−1.
6. **Annual example:** DF at 4% for two years? **Answer:** Approximately 0.92455621.
7. **Zero price:** PV of $1,000 at that DF? **Answer:** Approximately $924.56.
8. **Periodic DF:** Formula for nominal rate r and frequency m? **Answer:** (1+r/m)^(-mt).
9. **Semiannual example:** DF at nominal 4%, m=2, t=2? **Answer:** Approximately 0.92384543.
10. **Continuous DF:** Formula? **Answer:** exp(−zt).
11. **Continuous example:** DF at 4% for two years? **Answer:** Approximately 0.92311635.
12. **Continuous inverse:** Formula? **Answer:** −ln(DF)/t.
13. **Rate conversion:** Annual-effective r to continuous z? **Answer:** ln(1+r).
14. **Cash-flow pricing:** PV of $30 at 1y at 4%, and $1,030 at 2y at 5%? **Answer:** Approximately $963.07.
15. **Negative rates:** Can DF exceed one? **Answer:** Yes, under negative spot rates.
16. **Forward relation:** Express f from two discount factors. **Answer:** [DF(t1)/DF(t2)]^[1/(t2−t1)]−1.
17. **Bootstrapping:** What does it solve? **Answer:** Discount factors consistent with observed instrument values under stated conventions.
18. **Quality control:** Why specify compounding? **Answer:** Identical numerical rate quotes can imply different discount factors.
