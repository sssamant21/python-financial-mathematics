## Learning objectives

## 1. From weighted payment time to price sensitivity

Section 12.9 introduced **Macaulay duration** as the present-value-weighted average time to a bond's promised payments. **Modified duration** translates that timing statistic into a measure of how the bond's price changes when its yield changes.

For a conventional fixed-cash-flow bond, holding promised payments unchanged:

**Modified duration = -(1/P)(dP/dy).**

Here P is the bond's full price and y is a **nominal annual yield expressed as a decimal** under the stated compounding convention.

A positive modified duration means that price generally decreases when yield increases.

## 2. Derivation from the price function

Let F be face value, c the annual coupon rate, m payments per year, n remaining coupon periods, C=Fc/m, and y the nominal annual yield compounded m times per year.

The periodic yield is i=y/m and the bond price is:

**P(y)=Σ from k=1 to n of CF(k)/(1+y/m)^k.**

Differentiate with respect to y:

**dP/dy = -(1/m) Σ k×CF(k)/(1+y/m)^(k+1).**

Divide by -P:

**D_Mod = [Σ (k/m)×CF(k)/(1+i)^k] / [P(1+i)].**

The numerator divided by P is Macaulay duration. Therefore:

**D_Mod = D_Mac/(1+y/m).**

This relationship requires consistent payment timing and yield compounding conventions. It is not a universal conversion for all yield definitions.

## 3. Worked example — Three-year coupon bond

Consider a $1,000 face-value bond, 6% annual coupon, semiannual payments, three years to maturity, and 6% nominal annual yield.

- Coupon C = **$30**.
- Number of payments n = **6**.
- Periodic yield i = **3%**.
- Price P = **$1,000**.

Compute the present value of each payment, multiply by its payment time in years, add, and divide by P to obtain Macaulay duration.

Then:

**D_Mod = D_Mac / 1.03.**

The Python lab reports the precise Macaulay duration, modified duration, and DV01 without rounding intermediate present values.

## 4. Percentage price change

For a small nominal annual yield change Δy:

**ΔP/P ≈ -D_Mod × Δy.**

If modified duration is 2.7 and yield rises **50 basis points**, Δy=0.005:

**ΔP/P ≈ -2.7×0.005 = -0.0135 = -1.35%.**

A bond priced at $1,000 would have an estimated first-order loss of **$13.50**.

For a yield decline of 50 basis points, the corresponding first-order estimate is **+1.35%**.

## 5. Dollar duration

Multiplying the normalized sensitivity by price gives a currency-denominated slope:

**dP/dy = -P×D_Mod.**

The magnitude **P×D_Mod** is often called dollar duration per unit change in decimal yield. Because a unit yield change means 100 percentage points, it is usually more practical to report sensitivity per basis point.

If P=$1,000 and D_Mod=2.7:

**|dP/dy| = 2700 currency units per 1.0 decimal yield unit.**

## 6. DV01 / PV01

One basis point is **0.0001** in decimal yield.

For a conventional fixed-cash-flow bond:

**DV01 ≈ P×D_Mod×0.0001.**

At P=$1,000 and D_Mod=2.7:

**DV01 ≈ $0.27 per basis point.**

A +1-bp yield change implies approximately a **$0.27 price decline**; a -1-bp move implies approximately a $0.27 price increase.

DV01 may be quoted as a positive magnitude or a signed sensitivity, depending on convention. Always identify which interpretation is being used.

## 7. Exact repricing versus first-order approximation

The first-order duration estimate uses the tangent line to the price/yield curve at the starting yield. The exact change is:

**ΔP_exact = P(y+Δy)-P(y).**

The duration-only approximation is:

**ΔP_duration ≈ -P(y)×D_Mod(y)×Δy.**

For larger shocks, the difference increases because the bond-price function is curved. Section 12.11 develops convexity and second-order correction.

## 9. Portfolio sensitivity

For a portfolio of positions indexed by j, approximate dollar price change under a **common parallel yield shock** Δy is:

**ΔV ≈ -Δy × Σ[P(j)D_Mod(j)].**

Therefore:

**Portfolio DV01 ≈ Σ[P(j)D_Mod(j)×0.0001]**, provided prices are expressed as the cash value of each position and units are consistent.

This aggregation is only a first-order approximation. Bonds can experience different yield changes, credit-spread changes, or nonparallel curve movements. For those cases, scenario repricing and key-rate exposures are more informative.

## 10. Numerical verification

A central finite-difference estimate of the price derivative is:

**dP/dy ≈ [P(y+h)-P(y-h)]/(2h).**

Therefore:

**D_Mod ≈ -[P(y+h)-P(y-h)]/(2hP(y)).**

Choose h small enough to approximate the local slope but not so small that floating-point cancellation dominates.

For a +1-bp shock, the exact price change will be close to the DV01 estimate in many conventional cases; the approximation is not guaranteed to be exact.

## 11. Limitations and operational cautions

- Duration assumes the modeled cash flows remain fixed as yield changes.
- Embedded options, mortgage prepayments, and floating-rate resets may require **effective duration** instead.
- A single modified duration is a sensitivity to a **particular yield measure**, not to every curve point independently.
- Clean price and dirty price must be reconciled consistently at settlement.
- The same yield basis, compounding convention, and notional must be used throughout.
- DV01 for a $100 quote differs from DV01 for a $1 million position.

## 12. Python hands-on lab

Save as **modified_duration.py**. Run `python modified_duration.py` and `python -m compileall -q modified_duration.py`.

Use Python 3.9 or later and its standard library; no prior file or external data is needed. Inputs are interpreted from their decimal text, and the model uses rational arithmetic until reporting checked floats. Both the base and bumped yields must be in the model domain. The program calculates price, Macaulay and modified duration, analytical price slope, DV01, and exact versus approximate changes across yield shocks. It contains **12 reference unit tests**.
```python
import math
import unittest
from fractions import Fraction
def _number(value,label,positive=False,signed=False):
    if isinstance(value,bool) or not isinstance(value,(int,float)):
        raise ValueError(f"invalid {label}")
    try:
        finite=math.isfinite(value)
    except OverflowError:
        finite=False
    if not finite or (not signed and value<0) or (positive and value==0):
        raise ValueError(f"invalid {label}")
    return Fraction(str(value))

_MIN_FLOAT=Fraction(math.ulp(0.0))

def _report(value,label):
    try:
        if 0<abs(value)<_MIN_FLOAT:
            raise OverflowError
        result=float(value)
    except OverflowError as exc:
        raise ValueError(f"{label} outside supported range") from exc
    if not math.isfinite(result):
        raise ValueError(f"{label} outside supported range")
    return result

def _positive_int(value,label):
    if type(value) is not int or value<=0:
        raise ValueError(f"{label} must be a positive integer")

def _terms(face,coupon_rate,periods,frequency):
    _positive_int(periods,"periods")
    _positive_int(frequency,"frequency")
    F=_number(face,"face",positive=True)
    return F,F*_number(coupon_rate,"coupon rate")/frequency

def _values(F,C,n,y,m):
    base=1+y/m
    if base<=0:
        raise ValueError("periodic yield must exceed -100%")
    values=[(C+(F if k==n else 0))/base**k for k in range(1,n+1)]
    return base,values

def _stats(F,C,n,y,m):
    base,values=_values(F,C,n,y,m)
    price=sum(values)
    weighted=sum(Fraction(k,m)*value for k,value in enumerate(values,1))
    mac=weighted/price
    mod=mac/base
    return price,mac,mod,-weighted/base

def bond(face,coupon_rate,periods,yield_rate,frequency=2):
    F,C=_terms(face,coupon_rate,periods,frequency)
    y=_number(yield_rate,"yield",signed=True)
    p,mac,mod,slope=_stats(F,C,periods,y,frequency)
    values=dict(price=p,macaulay=mac,modified=mod,derivative=slope,dv01=-slope/10000)
    return {key:_report(value,key) for key,value in values.items()}

def shock(face,coupon_rate,periods,yield_rate,change,frequency=2):
    F,C=_terms(face,coupon_rate,periods,frequency)
    y=_number(yield_rate,"yield",signed=True)
    dy=_number(change,"yield shock",signed=True)
    p,_,_,slope=_stats(F,C,periods,y,frequency)
    _,bumped=_values(F,C,periods,y+dy,frequency)
    return _report(sum(bumped)-p,"exact change"),_report(slope*dy,"first-order change")

class Tests(unittest.TestCase):
    def test_01_par(self):self.assertAlmostEqual(bond(1000,.06,6,.06)['price'],1000)
    def test_02_modified_positive(self):self.assertGreater(bond(1000,.06,6,.06)['modified'],0)
    def test_03_formula(self):
        a=bond(1000,.06,6,.06);self.assertAlmostEqual(a['modified'],a['macaulay']/1.03)
    def test_04_derivative(self):
        a=bond(1000,.06,6,.06);self.assertAlmostEqual(a['derivative'],-a['price']*a['modified'])
    def test_05_dv01(self):
        a=bond(1000,.06,6,.06);self.assertAlmostEqual(a['dv01'],a['price']*a['modified']*.0001)
    def test_06_up(self):self.assertLess(shock(1000,.06,6,.06,.001)[0],0)
    def test_07_down(self):self.assertGreater(shock(1000,.06,6,.06,-.001)[0],0)
    def test_08_zero_coupon(self):self.assertAlmostEqual(bond(1000,0,6,.06)['modified'],3/1.03)
    def test_09_finite_difference(self):
        a=bond(1000,.06,6,.06);h=.00001
        slope=(bond(1000,.06,6,.06+h)['price']-bond(1000,.06,6,.06-h)['price'])/(2*h)
        self.assertAlmostEqual(slope,a['derivative'],delta=.001)
    def test_10_small_shock(self):
        exact,approx=shock(1000,.06,6,.06,.0001);self.assertAlmostEqual(exact,approx,delta=.0002)
    def test_11_longer_zero(self):self.assertGreater(bond(1000,0,10,.06)['modified'],bond(1000,0,6,.06)['modified'])
    def test_12_validation(self):
        for fn in (lambda:bond(0,.06,6,.06),lambda:bond(1000,-.01,6,.06),lambda:bond(1000,.06,0,.06),lambda:bond(1000,.06,6,-2),lambda:shock(1000,.06,6,.06,float('nan'))):
            with self.assertRaises(ValueError):fn()
        with self.assertRaises(ValueError): bond(1000,.06,6,.06,0)
        self.assertLess(shock(1000,.06,6,.06,1e-25)[0],0)
if __name__=='__main__':
    a=bond(1000,.06,6,.06)
    print('Price',round(a['price'],4),'Modified duration',round(a['modified'],6),'DV01',round(a['dv01'],6))
    for bp in (-100,-50,25,50,100):
        exact,approx=shock(1000,.06,6,.06,bp/10000)
        print(f'{bp:+} bp: exact={exact:.4f}; first-order={approx:.4f}')
    unittest.main(verbosity=2)
```

## 13. Exercises with worked answers

1. **Definition:** What does modified duration measure? **Answer:** The negative percentage price derivative with respect to yield.
2. **Formula:** Define D_Mod. **Answer:** -(1/P)(dP/dy).
3. **Macaulay link:** Express D_Mod using D_Mac. **Answer:** D_Mac/(1+y/m).
4. **Periodic yield:** What is i for 6% nominal annual, semiannual? **Answer:** 0.03.
5. **Coupon:** Semiannual coupon on $1,000 at 6%? **Answer:** $30.
6. **Periods:** Three years semiannually? **Answer:** Six.
7. **Par price:** Price with 6% coupon and 6% yield? **Answer:** $1,000.
8. **Direction:** Price response to higher yield? **Answer:** Price decreases for positive fixed cash flows.
9. **Basis points:** Convert 50 bp to decimal yield. **Answer:** 0.005.
10. **Percent estimate:** D_Mod=2.7, +50 bp? **Answer:** -1.35%.
11. **Dollar estimate:** P=$1,000, D_Mod=2.7, +50 bp? **Answer:** -$13.50.
12. **Negative shock:** D_Mod=2.7, -50 bp? **Answer:** Approximately +1.35%.
13. **Dollar duration:** Formula? **Answer:** P×D_Mod in magnitude per unit decimal yield.
14. **DV01:** Formula? **Answer:** P×D_Mod×0.0001.
15. **DV01 example:** P=$1,000 and D_Mod=2.7? **Answer:** $0.27 per basis point.
16. **Exact price change:** State the formula. **Answer:** P(y+Δy)-P(y).
17. **Finite difference:** How do you estimate modified duration numerically? **Answer:** -[P(y+h)-P(y-h)]/(2hP(y)).
18. **Limitation:** Why is effective duration useful for callable bonds? **Answer:** Their expected cash flows may change as interest rates move.
