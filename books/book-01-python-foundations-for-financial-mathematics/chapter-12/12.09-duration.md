## Learning objectives

Define Macaulay duration as present-value-weighted cash-flow time, derive the weighting formula, compute duration for coupon and zero-coupon bonds, connect it to interest-rate sensitivity, and validate the result with Python.

## 1. Duration measures the timing of value

A bond can promise many payments at different dates. **Macaulay duration** summarizes when those payments arrive, weighted by their contribution to the bond's present value. It is measured in **years** when cash-flow times are measured in years.

Duration is not the same as contractual maturity. A conventional coupon bond pays some value before maturity, so its Macaulay duration is usually shorter than its maturity. A zero-coupon bond makes only one maturity payment, so its Macaulay duration equals its time to maturity.

## 2. Pricing and present-value weights

For cash flows CF(k) at times t(k) and a flat nominal annual yield y compounded m times per year, let i=y/m. With t(k)=k/m:

**PV(k) = CF(k)/(1+i)^k.**

**P = Σ PV(k).**

Define the normalized present-value weight:

**w(k) = PV(k)/P.**

For positive payments, every weight is positive and **Σw(k)=1**. The weights provide a distribution over cash-flow payment dates.

## 3. Macaulay duration formula

**D_Mac = Σ[t(k) × w(k)] = [Σ t(k)PV(k)]/P.**

For a conventional fixed-coupon bullet bond with n periods, coupon C=Fc/m, and final principal F:

**D_Mac = [Σ from k=1 to n of (k/m)C/(1+i)^k + (n/m)F/(1+i)^n] / P.**

The formula explicitly counts the final coupon through the sum and counts the final principal once through the additional term.

## 4. Worked example — Three-year 6% coupon bond

Assume face value **$1,000**, coupon rate **6%**, semiannual frequency **m=2**, three years to maturity, and nominal annual yield **y=6%**.

The coupon is **$30**, periodic yield **3%**, and n=6. Because coupon and yield rates match, **P=$1,000**.

| Payment time (years) | Cash flow | Present value | PV weight |
|---:|---:|---|---|
| 0.5 | $30 | 30/(1.03) | PV/1000 |
| 1.0 | $30 | 30/(1.03)^2 | PV/1000 |
| 1.5 | $30 | 30/(1.03)^3 | PV/1000 |
| 2.0 | $30 | 30/(1.03)^4 | PV/1000 |
| 2.5 | $30 | 30/(1.03)^5 | PV/1000 |
| 3.0 | $1,030 | 1030/(1.03)^6 | PV/1000 |

Multiply each time by its present-value weight and add the results. The resulting Macaulay duration is **less than three years** because some value arrives before maturity.

The Python lab calculates the full-precision value without rounding intermediate cash-flow weights.

## 5. Zero-coupon bond duration

A zero-coupon bond has one positive promised payment at maturity T. Its present-value weight is 1, so:

**D_Mac = T × 1 = T.**

For a three-year zero-coupon bond, Macaulay duration is **three years** regardless of its yield, provided the single-payment model applies.

## 6. Coupon-rate and maturity effects

Holding other features comparable:

- Higher coupons move more present value to earlier dates and generally **reduce Macaulay duration**.
- Lower coupons concentrate more value at maturity and generally **increase duration**.
- Longer maturity usually increases duration, although comparisons involving unusual structures or yields require calculation.
- Zero-coupon bonds have the largest Macaulay duration among otherwise comparable positive-cash-flow bullet structures with the same maturity.

Duration is a **cash-flow-weighted average time**, not a prediction of the date on which an investor recovers their purchase price.

## 7. Connection to modified duration

Macaulay duration and modified duration are related but distinct.

For a flat yield with nominal annual compounding m times per year:

**D_Mod = D_Mac/(1+y/m).**

Modified duration normalizes the derivative of price with respect to nominal annual yield:

**D_Mod = -(1/P)(dP/dy).**

Therefore for a small yield change Δy:

**ΔP/P ≈ -D_Mod × Δy.**

This is the first-order approximation. Section 12.10 studies modified duration in detail.

## 8. Example of a yield shock

Suppose a bond has price $1,000 and modified duration 2.7. A **+25 basis point** yield change equals **+0.0025**.

The duration-only price approximation is:

**ΔP ≈ -1000 × 2.7 × 0.0025 = -$6.75.**

The estimated new price is **$993.25**. Exact repricing may differ because the price/yield curve has curvature.

## 9. Units and interpretation

- **Macaulay duration:** years, as a present-value-weighted average payment time.
- **Modified duration:** sensitivity to a unit change in yield expressed in decimal form; often described in years under the stated compounding convention.
- **Dollar duration:** approximate currency price change per unit yield change, equal to P×D_Mod in magnitude.
- **DV01/PV01:** approximate currency price change for a one-basis-point yield move, commonly **P×D_Mod×0.0001** for a parallel change under this model.

Always specify the yield convention, currency notional, and whether sensitivity is per unit, per $100 face, or for a full position.

## 10. Limitations

Macaulay duration assumes known cash flows and discounting conventions. It does not fully describe callable, prepayable, floating-rate, or default-sensitive instruments when expected cash flows change with interest rates.

A single duration number also cannot describe exposure to every point on a nonparallel yield curve. Key-rate duration and scenario-based repricing are useful extensions.

## 11. Python hands-on lab

Save the following as **bond_duration.py**. Run `python bond_duration.py` and `python -m compileall -q bond_duration.py`.

The lab computes cash flows, present-value weights, Macaulay and modified duration, and a first-order yield-shock estimate. It includes **12 reference unit tests**; independent runtime verification remains pending.

```python
import math, unittest
def cashflows(face,coupon_rate,periods,frequency=2):
    if any(isinstance(x,bool) or not isinstance(x,(int,float)) or not math.isfinite(x) for x in (face,coupon_rate)) or face<=0 or coupon_rate<0: raise ValueError('invalid terms')
    if any(isinstance(x,bool) or not isinstance(x,int) or x<1 for x in (periods,frequency)): raise ValueError('invalid periods or frequency')
    c=face*coupon_rate/frequency
    return [(k/frequency,c+(face if k==periods else 0)) for k in range(1,periods+1)]
def analytics(face,coupon_rate,periods,yield_rate,frequency=2):
    rows=cashflows(face,coupon_rate,periods,frequency)
    if isinstance(yield_rate,bool) or not isinstance(yield_rate,(int,float)) or not math.isfinite(yield_rate) or yield_rate/frequency<=-1: raise ValueError('invalid yield')
    i=yield_rate/frequency
    values=[cf*math.exp(-k*math.log1p(i)) for k,(_,cf) in enumerate(rows,1)]
    p=math.fsum(values)
    weighted=math.fsum(t*pv for (t,_),pv in zip(rows,values))
    macaulay=weighted/p
    return {'price':p,'macaulay_duration':macaulay,'modified_duration':macaulay/(1+i),'weights':[pv/p for pv in values]}
def approximate_change(face,coupon_rate,periods,yield_rate,delta_y,frequency=2):
    if not isinstance(delta_y,(int,float)) or not math.isfinite(delta_y): raise ValueError('invalid shock')
    a=analytics(face,coupon_rate,periods,yield_rate,frequency)
    return -a['price']*a['modified_duration']*delta_y
class Tests(unittest.TestCase):
    def test_01_par(self):self.assertAlmostEqual(analytics(1000,.06,6,.06)['price'],1000)
    def test_02_weights(self):self.assertAlmostEqual(sum(analytics(1000,.06,6,.06)['weights']),1)
    def test_03_duration_positive(self):self.assertGreater(analytics(1000,.06,6,.06)['macaulay_duration'],0)
    def test_04_less_than_maturity(self):self.assertLess(analytics(1000,.06,6,.06)['macaulay_duration'],3)
    def test_05_zero_coupon(self):self.assertAlmostEqual(analytics(1000,0,6,.06)['macaulay_duration'],3)
    def test_06_modified(self):
        a=analytics(1000,.06,6,.06)
        self.assertAlmostEqual(a['modified_duration'],a['macaulay_duration']/1.03)
    def test_07_price_sensitivity(self):self.assertLess(approximate_change(1000,.06,6,.06,.001),0)
    def test_08_negative_shock(self):self.assertGreater(approximate_change(1000,.06,6,.06,-.001),0)
    def test_09_longer_zero(self):self.assertGreater(analytics(1000,0,10,.06)['macaulay_duration'],analytics(1000,0,6,.06)['macaulay_duration'])
    def test_10_lower_coupon(self):self.assertGreater(analytics(1000,.02,6,.06)['macaulay_duration'],analytics(1000,.08,6,.06)['macaulay_duration'])
    def test_11_finite_difference(self):
        a=analytics(1000,.06,6,.06);h=1e-5
        slope=(analytics(1000,.06,6,.06+h)['price']-analytics(1000,.06,6,.06-h)['price'])/(2*h)
        self.assertAlmostEqual(-slope/a['price'],a['modified_duration'],delta=1e-7)
    def test_12_validation(self):
        for fn in (lambda:analytics(0,.06,6,.06),lambda:analytics(1000,-.01,6,.06),lambda:analytics(1000,.06,0,.06),lambda:analytics(1000,.06,6,-2)):
            with self.assertRaises(ValueError):fn()
if __name__=='__main__':
    a=analytics(1000,.06,6,.06)
    print('Price',round(a['price'],4),'Macaulay',round(a['macaulay_duration'],6),'Modified',round(a['modified_duration'],6))
    unittest.main(verbosity=2)
```

## 12. Exercises with worked answers

1. **Definition:** What is Macaulay duration? **Answer:** The PV-weighted average time to promised payments.
2. **Units:** What units does Macaulay duration use? **Answer:** Years if time is in years.
3. **Weight:** Define w(k). **Answer:** PV(k)/P.
4. **Weight sum:** What is Σw(k)? **Answer:** One.
5. **Coupon:** Semiannual coupon on $1,000 at 6%? **Answer:** $30.
6. **Periods:** Three years semiannually? **Answer:** Six.
7. **Price:** Price at matching 6% coupon and yield? **Answer:** $1,000.
8. **Maturity:** Is a conventional coupon bond's Macaulay duration typically shorter than maturity? **Answer:** Yes, for positive interim coupons.
9. **Zero coupon:** Duration of a five-year zero-coupon bond? **Answer:** Five years.
10. **Higher coupon:** What is the usual duration effect? **Answer:** Duration decreases.
11. **Lower coupon:** What is the usual duration effect? **Answer:** Duration increases.
12. **Modified relation:** State D_Mod. **Answer:** D_Mac/(1+y/m).
13. **Yield units:** Convert 25 bp to decimal yield. **Answer:** 0.0025.
14. **Shock:** Estimate ΔP for P=$1,000, D_Mod=2.7, and +25 bp. **Answer:** -$6.75.
15. **New price:** What is the approximate price? **Answer:** $993.25.
16. **Dollar duration:** What is its magnitude for P and D_Mod? **Answer:** P×D_Mod per unit decimal yield.
17. **DV01:** Give the approximate formula. **Answer:** P×D_Mod×0.0001.
18. **Limitation:** Why may duration fail for callable bonds? **Answer:** Their expected cash flows can change as yields change.
