## 1. Why cash-flow construction comes first

A bond's market value is built from its future cash flows. Before calculating price, yield, duration, or convexity, identify **who pays what, when, and under which contractual assumptions**.

A plain fixed-coupon bond typically pays periodic interest and returns face value at maturity. Other instruments may have no coupons, amortize principal gradually, or have payments contingent on floating rates, inflation, credit events, or embedded options.

This section focuses on **promised cash flows**, not guaranteed realized payments.

## 2. Bond terms and notation

Let:

- **F:** Face value, or promised maturity principal.
- **c:** Annual coupon rate expressed as a decimal.
- **m:** Coupon payments per year.
- **T:** Years to maturity.
- **n=mT:** Number of coupon periods, assumed to be an integer.
- **C=Fc/m:** Coupon amount per period.
- **CF(k):** Total cash flow at the end of period k.

For a conventional bullet bond:

**CF(k)=C** for k=1,...,n-1.

**CF(n)=C+F.**

The principal is paid once at maturity. Do not count it again as an extra coupon.

## 3. Worked example — Six semiannual cash flows

Consider a $1,000 face-value bond with a 6% annual coupon rate, paid semiannually, and three years to maturity.

**C=1000×0.06/2=$30** per half-year.

**n=3×2=6** periods.

| Period | Time (years) | Coupon | Principal | Total |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | 0.5 | $30 | $0 | $30 |
| 2 | 1.0 | $30 | $0 | $30 |
| 3 | 1.5 | $30 | $0 | $30 |
| 4 | 2.0 | $30 | $0 | $30 |
| 5 | 2.5 | $30 | $0 | $30 |
| 6 | 3.0 | $30 | $1,000 | $1,030 |

The promised undiscounted totals are:

**Total coupons = 6×30 = $180.**

**Total principal = $1,000.**

**Total promised cash flow = $1,180.**

These totals describe nominal promised payments. They are not the bond's present price.

## 4. Cash-flow timeline

```text
Purchase    0.5y     1.0y     1.5y     2.0y     2.5y     3.0y
   |---------|--------|--------|--------|--------|--------|
  -P        +30      +30      +30      +30      +30     +1030
                                                          maturity
```

From the investor's perspective, the purchase price is an initial outflow, while future contractual payments are inflows. The issuer has the opposite cash-flow direction.

## 5. Discounting the cash flows

Under a constant nominal annual yield y with the same compounding frequency m, the periodic yield is **i=y/m**.

**Price = sum from k=1 to n of CF(k)/(1+i)^k.**

For the example at a 6% nominal annual yield compounded semiannually, **i=0.03** and:

**P=30/(1.03)^1+30/(1.03)^2+...+1030/(1.03)^6=$1,000.**

The price is at par because the coupon rate and yield match under the same convention. This calculation assumes a valuation exactly on a coupon date and excludes accrued-interest adjustments.

## 6. Zero-coupon bond cash flows

A zero-coupon bond has **C=0**. Its contractual cash-flow schedule consists of no periodic coupons and one maturity payment of face value.

For a $1,000 face amount with six semiannual periods remaining and a nominal annual yield of 6%:

**P=1000/(1.03)^6.**

The investment's economic return arises from the difference between the purchase price and the promised maturity amount, subject to credit and other risks.

## 7. Frequency and timing conventions

Common coupon frequencies are annual (m=1), semiannual (m=2), and quarterly (m=4). Coupon frequency affects both the size and timing of payments.

The annual coupon rate does **not** mean that the full annual coupon is paid at every payment date. The periodic coupon is always **F×c/m** for this simple fixed-rate structure.

A real bond may use actual calendar coupon dates, holiday rules, day-count conventions, irregular first or final coupons, and settlement-date accrued interest. The equal-period model used here deliberately excludes these complexities.

## 8. Cash-flow reconciliation

A correct schedule should satisfy:

1. **Number of rows = n**.
2. **Coupon per row = Fc/m**.
3. **Principal is zero except at maturity**.
4. **Maturity principal = F**.
5. **Row total = coupon + principal**.
6. **Total coupons = nC**.
7. **Total principal = F**.
8. **Total promised cash flow = nC+F**.

These are contractual arithmetic checks, independent of market yield.

## 9. Other cash-flow structures

**Amortizing bond:** Principal is returned in installments rather than entirely at maturity.

**Floating-rate note:** Coupon amounts depend on future reference-rate resets and contractual spreads.

**Inflation-linked bond:** Payments may depend on inflation-index adjustments.

**Callable bond:** The issuer may redeem early, altering the expected schedule.

**Defaultable bond:** Promised cash flows can differ from realized cash flows.

The Python lab below intentionally models only the simple **fixed-rate bullet** and **zero-coupon** cases.

The implementation uses Python's standard library, produces structured rows, calculates nominal totals and discounted price, and includes **12 unit tests**. Independent runtime execution and editorial review remain pending.

```python
"""Bond cash-flow schedule: fixed coupon, zero coupon, stub final period."""
import math
import unittest

def schedule(face, annual_coupon, years, frequency=2):
    for value in (face,annual_coupon,years):
        if isinstance(value,bool) or not isinstance(value,(int,float)) or not math.isfinite(value):
            raise ValueError("finite numeric inputs required")
    if face<=0 or annual_coupon<0 or years<=0: raise ValueError("invalid bond terms")
    if isinstance(frequency,bool) or not isinstance(frequency,int) or frequency<1:
        raise ValueError("positive integer frequency required")
    periods=years*frequency
    if not math.isclose(periods,round(periods),rel_tol=0,abs_tol=1e-9):
        raise ValueError("whole coupon periods required")
    n=int(round(periods))
    coupon=face*annual_coupon/frequency
    return [{"period":k,"time_years":k/frequency,"coupon":coupon,
             "principal":face if k==n else 0.0,
             "cash_flow":coupon+(face if k==n else 0.0)}
            for k in range(1,n+1)]

def price(rows, annual_yield, frequency=2):
    if not rows: raise ValueError("cash flows required")
    if isinstance(annual_yield,bool) or not isinstance(annual_yield,(int,float)) or not math.isfinite(annual_yield) or annual_yield<0:
        raise ValueError("nonnegative finite yield required")
    if isinstance(frequency,bool) or not isinstance(frequency,int) or frequency<1:
        raise ValueError("positive integer frequency required")
    i=annual_yield/frequency
    return math.fsum(row["cash_flow"]*math.exp(-row["period"]*math.log1p(i)) for row in rows)

def totals(rows):
    if not rows: raise ValueError("cash flows required")
    return {"coupon":math.fsum(x["coupon"] for x in rows),
            "principal":math.fsum(x["principal"] for x in rows),
            "cash_flow":math.fsum(x["cash_flow"] for x in rows)}

class Tests(unittest.TestCase):
    def setUp(self): self.rows=schedule(1000,.06,3,2)
    def test_01_period_count(self): self.assertEqual(len(self.rows),6)
    def test_02_coupon(self): self.assertEqual(self.rows[0]["coupon"],30)
    def test_03_final_payment(self): self.assertEqual(self.rows[-1]["cash_flow"],1030)
    def test_04_first_time(self): self.assertEqual(self.rows[0]["time_years"],.5)
    def test_05_last_time(self): self.assertEqual(self.rows[-1]["time_years"],3)
    def test_06_coupon_total(self): self.assertEqual(totals(self.rows)["coupon"],180)
    def test_07_principal_total(self): self.assertEqual(totals(self.rows)["principal"],1000)
    def test_08_cash_flow_total(self): self.assertEqual(totals(self.rows)["cash_flow"],1180)
    def test_09_par_price(self): self.assertAlmostEqual(price(self.rows,.06,2),1000,places=8)
    def test_10_zero_coupon(self):
        z=schedule(1000,0,3,2)
        self.assertEqual(totals(z)["coupon"],0)
        self.assertAlmostEqual(price(z,.06,2),1000/1.03**6)
    def test_11_zero_yield(self): self.assertAlmostEqual(price(self.rows,0,2),1180)
    def test_12_invalid(self):
        with self.assertRaises(ValueError): schedule(1000,.06,3.1,2)
        with self.assertRaises(ValueError): schedule(0,.06,3,2)
        with self.assertRaises(ValueError): price(self.rows,-.01,2)

if __name__=="__main__":
    rows=schedule(1000,.06,3,2)
    for row in rows: print(row)
    print("Totals:",totals(rows))
    print("Price at 6% nominal yield:",round(price(rows,.06,2),2))
    unittest.main(verbosity=2)
```

## 11. Exercises and worked answers

1. **Coupon formula:** State the fixed periodic coupon formula. **Answer:** C=Fc/m.
2. **Coupon size:** Find the semiannual coupon on $1,000 at 6%. **Answer:** $30.
3. **Period count:** Three years of semiannual payments means how many periods? **Answer:** Six.
4. **First payment:** What is CF(1) in the worked example? **Answer:** $30.
5. **Final payment:** What is CF(6)? **Answer:** $1,030.
6. **Total coupons:** Sum the six coupons. **Answer:** $180.
7. **Principal total:** Sum all principal payments. **Answer:** $1,000.
8. **Total nominal cash:** Sum all promised payments. **Answer:** $1,180.
9. **Quarterly coupons:** Find each coupon on $2,000 at 8% annually, quarterly. **Answer:** $40.
10. **Quarterly periods:** How many quarterly periods are in five years? **Answer:** Twenty.
11. **Zero coupon:** What are the coupon payments for a zero-coupon bond? **Answer:** Zero.
12. **Zero-coupon price:** Price $1,000 in three years at 6% nominal with semiannual compounding. **Answer:** 1000/(1.03)^6.
13. **Periodic yield:** Convert 6% nominal annual yield to a semiannual rate. **Answer:** 3%.
14. **Par check:** Why is the example price $1,000 at 6% yield? **Answer:** Coupon rate equals yield under the same frequency convention.
15. **Issuer perspective:** How does the issuer's cash-flow direction differ? **Answer:** Coupons and principal are issuer outflows.
16. **Amortizing bond:** How does principal timing differ from a bullet bond? **Answer:** Principal is returned in multiple installments.
17. **Settlement convention:** Why might actual quoted prices differ from this model? **Answer:** Accrued interest, day counts, settlement timing, and market conventions are omitted.
18. **Credit risk:** Are these promised payments certain to be received? **Answer:** No; the issuer may fail to perform.
