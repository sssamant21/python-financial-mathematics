## Learning objectives

Define yield to maturity (YTM), distinguish it from coupon rate and current yield, formulate the bond-price equation as a root-finding problem, solve for YTM with a robust bisection method, and interpret the limitations of a quoted yield.

## 1. What is yield to maturity?

**Yield to maturity** is the discount rate that equates the present value of a bond's remaining **promised** payments to its observed price under a stated compounding and cash-flow convention.

For a conventional fixed-rate bond with positive remaining payments, the relationship between price and yield is decreasing. Thus, a valid positive market price identifies a unique periodic yield greater than -100% in the simple mathematical model.

YTM is an **internal rate of return on promised cash flows**. It is not a guarantee of the investor's realized return: default, early sale, calls, taxes, transaction costs, and coupon reinvestment conditions can change the outcome.

## 2. Bond-price equation

Let F be face value, c the annual coupon rate, m coupon periods per year, n remaining periods, C=Fc/m the coupon, and P the observed bond price.

Under a nominal annual yield y compounded m times per year, set i=y/m. Then:

**P = C/(1+i) + C/(1+i)^2 + ... + (C+F)/(1+i)^n.**

Equivalently, for i≠0:

**P = C[1-(1+i)^(-n)]/i + F/(1+i)^n.**

When i=0, P=nC+F.

Unlike pricing, where y is an input, **YTM estimation makes y the unknown**.

## 3. Deriving the root-finding problem

Define:

**f(y)=sum from k=1 to n of CF(k)/(1+y/m)^k-P_market.**

The objective is to find y such that:

**f(y)=0.**

For positive future cash flows and y/m>-1, the derivative is:

**f'(y)=-(1/m) sum from k=1 to n of k×CF(k)/(1+y/m)^(k+1)<0.**

Therefore f is strictly decreasing. This supports **bisection**, which is straightforward to implement and does not require a derivative.

The yield convention must match the payment frequency and pricing convention. For a bond quoted between coupon dates, clean versus dirty price and accrued interest require additional care.

## 4. Worked example — Par bond

Consider F=$1,000, annual coupon rate c=6%, semiannual payments m=2, and three years remaining, so n=6 and C=$30.

At market price **P=$1,000**, the periodic yield is **3%** and the nominal annual YTM is **6%**, because:

**1000 = 30/(1.03) + 30/(1.03)^2 + ... + 1030/(1.03)^6.**

This equality holds for the plain bond under identical coupon and yield compounding conventions.

## 5. Premium and discount cases

Using the same promised cash flows:

| Observed market price | Yield relation | Explanation |
|---:|---|---|
| $950 | YTM above 6% | Discount price requires a higher discount rate |
| $1,000 | YTM equals 6% | Par price |
| $1,050 | YTM below 6% | Premium price requires a lower discount rate |

Do not use the coupon rate as a substitute for YTM when price differs from par.

## 6. Coupon rate, current yield, and YTM

**Coupon rate** is contractual:

**Coupon rate = Annual contractual coupon / Face value.**

**Current yield** is an income-to-price ratio:

**Current yield = Annual contractual coupon / Current market price.**

For the $1,000 bond with $60 annual coupons and a $1,050 price:

**Current yield = 60/1050 ≈ 5.7143%.**

**YTM** additionally incorporates the timing of all coupons and the difference between market price and face value at maturity. Current yield does not account for that capital gain or loss.

## 7. Why bisection works

Bisection begins with a lower and upper yield bound for which the price residual changes sign. Each iteration halves the interval and retains the half containing the root.

For positive-cash-flow bonds:

- If the trial price is **above** the observed market price, the trial yield is **too low**.
- If the trial price is **below** the observed market price, the trial yield is **too high**.

A simple nonnegative-yield bracket starts at y=0 when the observed price does not exceed the zero-yield price. The upper bound can be expanded until it produces a price below the market price.

If the observed price is above the zero-yield price, the implied yield may be negative. The mathematical domain is **y>-m**. A production solver should enforce the domain and report any failed bracket or convergence.

## 8. Numerical tolerance and convergence

Financial solvers should check both:

1. **Yield convergence:** The bracket width is sufficiently small.
2. **Price residual:** The price calculated at the returned yield matches the market price within tolerance.

Using only an iteration count is insufficient as a general correctness guarantee. Bisection has predictable interval convergence, but pricing conventions and input accuracy remain essential.

For near-zero periodic yields, evaluate discount factors with `log1p` and `exp` to reduce numerical error. Use `expm1` when converting between logarithmic and periodic rates.

## 9. Zero-coupon YTM

For a zero-coupon bond with face value F, market price P, m periods per year, and T years to maturity:

**P=F/(1+y/m)^(mT).**

Solving explicitly:

**y=m[(F/P)^(1/(mT))-1].**

Thus a zero-coupon bond has a closed-form YTM under this convention, while a coupon bond generally requires numerical root finding.

## 10. Interpreting YTM carefully

YTM is often presented as a single summary of bond pricing. But its interpretation depends on assumptions:

- The investor receives the modeled payments as promised.
- The cash-flow schedule is not changed by calls, defaults, or other events.
- The bond is held to maturity for the usual hold-to-maturity return interpretation.
- A single quoted YTM does not specify the rates at which interim coupons can be reinvested.
- Taxes, fees, bid-ask spreads, and market settlement conventions may affect actual returns.

A bond's **realized compounded return** need not equal its initial YTM, even if it pays every coupon on time.

The reference program uses standard-library Python, prices conventional fixed-coupon bonds, solves for YTM using bisection, compares current yield, and includes **12 unit tests**. It supports negative yields above the mathematical lower bound but does not implement market-specific settlement or day-count conventions.

```python
"""Yield to maturity for conventional fixed-coupon bullet bonds."""
import math
import unittest

def validate(face, coupon_rate, periods, frequency):
    if isinstance(face,bool) or not isinstance(face,(int,float)) or not math.isfinite(face) or face<=0: raise ValueError("invalid face")
    if isinstance(coupon_rate,bool) or not isinstance(coupon_rate,(int,float)) or not math.isfinite(coupon_rate) or coupon_rate<0: raise ValueError("invalid coupon")
    if isinstance(periods,bool) or not isinstance(periods,int) or periods<1: raise ValueError("invalid periods")
    if isinstance(frequency,bool) or not isinstance(frequency,int) or frequency<1: raise ValueError("invalid frequency")

def price(face, coupon_rate, periods, annual_yield, frequency=2):
    validate(face,coupon_rate,periods,frequency)
    if isinstance(annual_yield,bool) or not isinstance(annual_yield,(int,float)) or not math.isfinite(annual_yield): raise ValueError("invalid yield")
    i=annual_yield/frequency
    if i<=-1: raise ValueError("periodic yield must exceed -100%")
    c=face*coupon_rate/frequency
    logd=math.log1p(i)
    return math.fsum(c*math.exp(-k*logd) for k in range(1,periods+1))+face*math.exp(-periods*logd)

def ytm(face, coupon_rate, periods, market_price, frequency=2, tolerance=1e-11):
    validate(face,coupon_rate,periods,frequency)
    if isinstance(market_price,bool) or not isinstance(market_price,(int,float)) or not math.isfinite(market_price) or market_price<=0:raise ValueError("invalid price")
    # In the usual positive-yield case, search [0, upper].
    if market_price<=price(face,coupon_rate,periods,0,frequency):
        lo=0.0;hi=0.1
        while price(face,coupon_rate,periods,hi,frequency)>market_price:
            hi=2*hi+0.1
            if hi>1e6:raise ValueError("yield outside supported bracket")
    else:
        # Negative yield: periodic yield strictly above -100%.
        lo=-frequency*(1-1e-12);hi=0.0
    for _ in range(250):
        mid=(lo+hi)/2
        calculated=price(face,coupon_rate,periods,mid,frequency)
        if calculated>market_price:lo=mid
        else:hi=mid
        if abs(calculated-market_price)<=tolerance*max(1.0,market_price):
            return mid
    return (lo+hi)/2

def current_yield(face,coupon_rate,market_price):
    if market_price<=0 or not math.isfinite(market_price):raise ValueError("invalid price")
    return face*coupon_rate/market_price

class Tests(unittest.TestCase):
    def test_01_par(self):self.assertAlmostEqual(ytm(1000,.06,6,1000),.06,places=9)
    def test_02_premium(self):self.assertLess(ytm(1000,.06,6,1050),.06)
    def test_03_discount(self):self.assertGreater(ytm(1000,.06,6,950),.06)
    def test_04_roundtrip(self):
        p=price(1000,.06,6,.0475)
        self.assertAlmostEqual(ytm(1000,.06,6,p),.0475,places=9)
    def test_05_zero_coupon(self):
        p=price(1000,0,6,.05)
        self.assertAlmostEqual(ytm(1000,0,6,p),.05,places=9)
    def test_06_zero_yield(self):self.assertAlmostEqual(ytm(1000,.06,6,1180),0,places=9)
    def test_07_negative_yield(self):
        p=price(1000,.06,6,-.01)
        self.assertAlmostEqual(ytm(1000,.06,6,p),-.01,places=9)
    def test_08_annual(self):
        p=price(1000,.04,5,.05,1)
        self.assertAlmostEqual(ytm(1000,.04,5,p,1),.05,places=9)
    def test_09_current_yield(self):self.assertAlmostEqual(current_yield(1000,.06,1050),60/1050)
    def test_10_price_monotonic(self):self.assertGreater(price(1000,.06,6,.04),price(1000,.06,6,.08))
    def test_11_small_positive(self):
        p=price(1000,.06,6,.0001)
        self.assertAlmostEqual(ytm(1000,.06,6,p),.0001,places=8)
    def test_12_validation(self):
        for fn in (lambda:ytm(1000,.06,6,0),lambda:ytm(0,.06,6,1000),
                   lambda:ytm(1000,-.01,6,1000),lambda:price(1000,.06,6,-2,2),
                   lambda:ytm(1000,.06,0,1000)):
            with self.assertRaises(ValueError):fn()

if __name__=="__main__":
    for p in (950,1000,1050):
        y=ytm(1000,.06,6,p)
        print(f"Market price: {p:.2f}; YTM: {y:.6%}; repriced: {price(1000,.06,6,y):.6f}")
    unittest.main(verbosity=2)
```

## 12. Exercises and worked answers

1. **Definition:** What is YTM? **Answer:** The discount rate that equates promised remaining cash-flow PV to market price under a specified convention.
2. **Unknown:** Which variable is solved for in a YTM calculation? **Answer:** The yield, not the market price.
3. **Coupon:** Find the semiannual coupon on $1,000 at 6%. **Answer:** $30.
4. **Periods:** Three years with semiannual coupons means how many periods? **Answer:** Six.
5. **Par:** What is YTM when this bond trades at $1,000? **Answer:** 6% nominal annual with semiannual compounding.
6. **Discount:** Is YTM higher or lower than 6% at a $950 price? **Answer:** Higher.
7. **Premium:** Is YTM higher or lower than 6% at a $1,050 price? **Answer:** Lower.
8. **Current yield:** Find current yield for $60 annual coupons and $1,050 price. **Answer:** 60/1050≈5.7143%.
9. **Root function:** Write f(y). **Answer:** PV of promised payments at y minus market price.
10. **Derivative sign:** What is the sign of f'(y) for positive cash flows? **Answer:** Negative.
11. **Bisection step:** If trial price exceeds market price, which direction should yield move? **Answer:** Upward.
12. **Zero-coupon formula:** State the closed-form nominal YTM. **Answer:** m[(F/P)^(1/(mT))-1].
13. **Zero yield:** What is a coupon bond's price at y=0? **Answer:** nC+F.
14. **Negative yields:** Can a positive-cash-flow bond have negative YTM? **Answer:** Yes, if price exceeds its zero-yield undiscounted promised total in this model.
15. **Repricing check:** How do you validate a numerical YTM? **Answer:** Reprice at the solved yield and compare with the observed price.
16. **Conventions:** Why does compounding frequency matter? **Answer:** It changes the periodic yield and the quoted annualization.
17. **Reinvestment:** Does YTM guarantee coupon reinvestment at the same rate? **Answer:** No.
18. **Credit risk:** Does a computed YTM guarantee payment? **Answer:** No; it discounts promised cash flows.
