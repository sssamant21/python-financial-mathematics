## 1. What is a coupon bond?

A **coupon bond** promises periodic interest payments determined by its terms and a repayment of principal according to its contract. A conventional fixed-rate bullet bond pays a fixed coupon each period and returns face value at maturity. Unlike an amortizing loan, its principal usually remains outstanding until the final payment.

A fixed coupon does not mean a fixed **market price**. The market value of the promised cash flows changes when discount rates and other valuation inputs change.

## 2. Notation and assumptions

Let F denote face value, c the annual contractual coupon rate, m the number of coupon periods per year, T years remaining, n=mT the number of periods, y the nominal annual yield under matching frequency compounding, and i=y/m the periodic yield.

The contractual coupon is:

**C = Fc/m.**

For the simple fixed-rate bullet bond:

**CF(k) = C**, for periods k=1 through n-1.

**CF(n) = C+F**, at maturity.

Assume n is a whole number, the valuation date coincides with a coupon date, coupons are paid at the end of each period, and no credit losses or embedded options alter promised cash flows.

## 3. Worked example — Semiannual coupon bond

Consider a bond with face value $1,000, annual coupon rate 6%, semiannual payments, and three years to maturity.

**C = 1000×0.06/2 = $30.**

**n = 3×2 = 6.**

| Period | Coupon | Principal | Total payment |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | $30 | $0 | $30 |
| 2 | $30 | $0 | $30 |
| 3 | $30 | $0 | $30 |
| 4 | $30 | $0 | $30 |
| 5 | $30 | $0 | $30 |
| 6 | $30 | $1,000 | $1,030 |

The promised lifetime coupon income is **6×$30=$180**. The total undiscounted cash flow is **$1,180**. Neither is the bond's present market value.

## 4. Pricing from individual cash flows

The price of the promised payments at a constant periodic yield i is:

**P = sum from k=1 to n of C/(1+i)^k + F/(1+i)^n.**

Equivalently, discount the first five $30 coupons and the final $1,030 cash flow directly.

At a nominal annual yield of 6% compounded semiannually, **i=0.03**:

**P = 30/(1.03)+30/(1.03)^2+...+1030/(1.03)^6 = $1,000.**

This is the **par** case: coupon rate equals yield under the same annualization and compounding convention.

## 5. Pricing with the annuity formula

The coupons form an ordinary annuity and the principal is a single maturity payment:

**P = C[1-(1+i)^(-n)]/i + F/(1+i)^n**, for i>0.

When i=0:

**P = nC+F.**

The annuity formula and the direct cash-flow sum must agree. A disagreement usually signals incorrect frequency conversion, a missing final coupon, or inconsistent timing.

For small i, the numerical expression **-expm1(-n log1p(i))/i** helps avoid loss of precision in the annuity factor.

## 6. Premium, par, and discount

For the worked example:

| Nominal annual yield | Periodic yield | Classification |
|---:|---:|---|
| 4% | 2% | Premium (price above $1,000) |
| 6% | 3% | Par (price $1,000) |
| 8% | 4% | Discount (price below $1,000) |

A lower market yield increases the present value of the fixed promised coupons. A higher market yield decreases it.

**Important:** A premium or discount describes the relation between price and face value, not the likelihood of default or whether the investment is attractive after adjusting for risk.

## 7. Coupon rate, current yield, and yield to maturity

The **coupon rate** is contractual: annual coupon amount divided by face value.

The **current yield** is a simple income ratio:

**Current yield = annual coupon / market price.**

For the example, annual coupon income is $60. If the bond trades at $1,050, current yield is **60/1050≈5.7143%**.

**Yield to maturity** is the discount rate that equates the bond's price to its promised cash flows under a stated compounding convention. It incorporates both coupons and the difference between purchase price and principal repayment. Current yield and yield to maturity are not interchangeable.

A detailed yield solver appears in Section 12.6.

## 8. Coupon frequency matters

A $1,000 bond with an annual coupon rate of 8% pays:

- **Annual (m=1):** $80 once per year.
- **Semiannual (m=2):** $40 twice per year.
- **Quarterly (m=4):** $20 four times per year.

These schedules promise the same $80 coupon total per full year, but their timing differs. Consequently, their present values at a stated yield convention can differ.

## 9. Coupon bonds versus zero-coupon bonds

A fixed-coupon bond has multiple interest payments plus principal at maturity. A zero-coupon bond promises no periodic coupons, so the basic valuation is only the discounted maturity principal:

**P_zero = F/(1+i)^n.**

The next section treats zero-coupon bonds in detail.

## 10. Financial risks and contract details

Coupon bond valuation is exposed to market interest rates, issuer creditworthiness, reinvestment opportunities, liquidity, inflation, currency, and embedded call or put features.

Real market instruments may use settlement dates between coupons, accrued interest, clean/dirty price conventions, day-count rules, irregular coupons, and a yield curve rather than a single flat discount rate. These are deliberately outside the simplified reference model.

Both pricing routes use rational arithmetic before reporting floats, and classify before rounding. This implementation is for teaching-sized terms and nonnegative yields. It creates coupon cash flows, values the bond by both direct discounting and an annuity formula, classifies prices, and provides **12 reference unit tests**.

```python
"""Chapter 12.3 coupon bond valuation, standard-library reference."""
import math
import unittest
from fractions import Fraction

def _number(value,label,positive=False):
    if isinstance(value,bool) or not isinstance(value,(int,float)):
        raise ValueError(f"invalid {label}")
    try:
        finite=math.isfinite(value)
    except OverflowError:
        finite=False
    if not finite or value<0 or (positive and value==0):
        raise ValueError(f"invalid {label}")
    return Fraction(str(value))

_MIN_FLOAT=Fraction(math.ulp(0.0))

def _report(value,label):
    try:
        if 0<value<_MIN_FLOAT:
            raise OverflowError
        result=float(value)
    except OverflowError as exc:
        raise ValueError(f"{label} outside supported range") from exc
    if not math.isfinite(result):
        raise ValueError(f"{label} outside supported range")
    return result

def terms(face,coupon_rate,years,frequency,yield_rate):
    _number(face,"face",positive=True)
    _number(coupon_rate,"coupon rate")
    maturity=_number(years,"years",positive=True)
    _number(yield_rate,"yield")
    if type(frequency) is not int or frequency<1:
        raise ValueError("frequency must be a positive integer")
    periods=maturity*frequency
    if periods.denominator!=1:
        raise ValueError("years * frequency must be a positive integer")
    return periods.numerator

def _inputs(face,coupon_rate,years,yield_rate,frequency):
    n=terms(face,coupon_rate,years,frequency,yield_rate)
    F=Fraction(str(face))
    return F,F*Fraction(str(coupon_rate))/frequency,n,Fraction(str(yield_rate))/frequency

def _flows(F,C,n):
    return ((k,C+(F if k==n else 0)) for k in range(1,n+1))

def cashflows(face,coupon_rate,years,frequency=2):
    F,C,n,_=_inputs(face,coupon_rate,years,0,frequency)
    return [(k,_report(value,"cash flow")) for k,value in _flows(F,C,n)]

def _direct(F,C,n,i):
    discount=1/(1+i)
    return sum(value*discount**k for k,value in _flows(F,C,n))

def price(face,coupon_rate,years,yield_rate,frequency=2):
    return _report(_direct(*_inputs(face,coupon_rate,years,yield_rate,frequency)),"price")

def annuity_price(face,coupon_rate,years,yield_rate,frequency=2):
    F,C,n,i=_inputs(face,coupon_rate,years,yield_rate,frequency)
    if i==0:
        return _report(F+n*C,"price")
    discount=(1+i)**-n
    return _report(C*(1-discount)/i+F*discount,"price")

def coupon_income(face,coupon_rate,years,frequency=2):
    _,C,n,_=_inputs(face,coupon_rate,years,0,frequency)
    return _report(n*C,"coupon income")

def label(face,coupon_rate,years,yield_rate,frequency=2):
    inputs=_inputs(face,coupon_rate,years,yield_rate,frequency)
    p=_direct(*inputs)
    _report(p,"price")
    F=inputs[0]
    return "par" if p==F else "premium" if p>F else "discount"

class Tests(unittest.TestCase):
    def test_01_coupon_amount(self):self.assertEqual(cashflows(1000,.06,3,2)[0][1],30)
    def test_02_periods(self):self.assertEqual(len(cashflows(1000,.06,3,2)),6)
    def test_03_maturity(self):self.assertEqual(cashflows(1000,.06,3,2)[-1][1],1030)
    def test_04_coupon_income(self):self.assertAlmostEqual(coupon_income(1000,.06,3,2),180)
    def test_05_par(self):self.assertAlmostEqual(price(1000,.06,3,.06,2),1000)
    def test_06_premium(self):self.assertGreater(price(1000,.06,3,.04,2),1000)
    def test_07_discount(self):self.assertLess(price(1000,.06,3,.08,2),1000)
    def test_08_annuity_equivalence(self):
        self.assertAlmostEqual(price(1000,.06,3,.04,2),annuity_price(1000,.06,3,.04,2))
    def test_09_zero_yield(self):self.assertAlmostEqual(price(1000,.06,3,0,2),1180)
    def test_10_zero_coupon(self):self.assertAlmostEqual(price(1000,0,3,.06,2),1000/1.03**6)
    def test_11_monotonic(self):
        self.assertGreater(price(1000,.06,3,.03,2),price(1000,.06,3,.07,2))
        self.assertEqual(label(1000,.06,3,.06,2),"par")
    def test_12_invalid(self):
        for args in ((0,.06,3,.06,2),(1000,-.01,3,.06,2),
                     (1000,.06,3,-.01,2),(1000,.06,3,.06,0),
                     (1000,.06,3.1,.06,2)):
            with self.assertRaises(ValueError):price(*args)
        with self.assertRaises(ValueError): price(1000,.06,1e-12,.06)
        self.assertEqual(coupon_income(1e100,1e-100,1,1),1)

if __name__=="__main__":
    for y in (.04,.06,.08):
        print(f"Yield {y:.1%}: price={price(1000,.06,3,y,2):.2f};",
              label(1000,.06,3,y,2))
    unittest.main(verbosity=2)
```

## 12. Exercises with worked answers

1. **Definition:** What does a conventional fixed-coupon bond promise? **Answer:** Periodic fixed coupons and repayment of contractual principal at maturity.
2. **Coupon formula:** Express periodic coupon C. **Answer:** C=Fc/m.
3. **Semiannual coupon:** Find C on $1,000 at 6% annually. **Answer:** $30.
4. **Number of periods:** Three years semiannual means how many coupons? **Answer:** Six.
5. **Final payment:** What is the final payment for the worked example? **Answer:** $1,030.
6. **Coupon total:** What is the total contractual coupon income? **Answer:** $180.
7. **Undiscounted total:** What is total promised cash flow? **Answer:** $1,180.
8. **Periodic yield:** Convert 6% nominal annual semiannual yield. **Answer:** 3%.
9. **Par:** Why is price equal to face at a 6% yield? **Answer:** Coupon and yield rates match under the same compounding convention.
10. **Premium:** What happens when yield falls to 4%? **Answer:** Price rises above $1,000.
11. **Discount:** What happens when yield rises to 8%? **Answer:** Price falls below $1,000.
12. **Annual coupon:** What is annual coupon income on $1,000 at 6%? **Answer:** $60.
13. **Current yield:** Calculate current yield if price is $1,050. **Answer:** 60/1050≈5.7143%.
14. **Annuity factor:** Give the coupon present-value factor. **Answer:** [1-(1+i)^(-n)]/i for i>0, or n at i=0.
15. **Zero yield:** What is price at zero discount rate? **Answer:** $1,180.
16. **Zero coupon:** What changes if coupon rate is zero? **Answer:** Only the discounted maturity principal remains.
17. **Quarterly coupon:** Find each payment on $2,000 at 8% annually, quarterly. **Answer:** $40.
18. **Reconciliation:** How can direct and annuity pricing validate each other? **Answer:** Both methods must produce the same price within a numerical tolerance.
