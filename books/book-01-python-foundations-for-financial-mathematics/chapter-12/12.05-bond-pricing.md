## 1. Bond pricing as present-value mathematics

The fundamental fixed-income valuation identity is:

**Price = present value of future cash flows.**

For a sequence of promised cash flows CF(k) at times t(k), and appropriate discount factors D(t(k)):

**P = sum from k=1 to n of CF(k) × D(t(k)).**

The cash flows are determined by the contract. The discount factors reflect the valuation assumptions, including timing, interest-rate structure, and potentially credit and liquidity adjustments. Contractual cash flows are not guaranteed realized payments.

A bond price is therefore not determined by its coupon rate alone.

## 2. Flat-yield pricing

For a fixed-coupon bullet bond, define:

- F = face value.
- c = annual coupon rate.
- m = payments per year.
- n = remaining whole coupon periods.
- C = Fc/m = coupon per period.
- y = nominal annual yield compounded m times per year.
- i = y/m = periodic yield.

Assuming settlement on a coupon date and equal periods:

**P = sum from k=1 to n of C/(1+i)^k + F/(1+i)^n.**

Equivalently, because the final cash flow contains both coupon and principal:

**P = sum from k=1 to n of CF(k)/(1+i)^k.**

This is a simplified flat-yield model; market conventions can use irregular periods and accrued-interest adjustments.

## 3. Worked example — A $1,000 coupon bond

Consider a bond with face value $1,000, 6% annual coupon rate, semiannual payments, and three years remaining.

- Coupon C = 1000×.06/2 = **$30**.
- Remaining periods n = 3×2 = **6**.
- Final cash flow = **$1,030**.

| Period | Promised cash flow |
|---:|---:|
| 1 | $30 |
| 2 | $30 |
| 3 | $30 |
| 4 | $30 |
| 5 | $30 |
| 6 | $1,030 |

At nominal annual yield y=6% compounded semiannually, i=.03:

**P = 30/(1.03) + 30/(1.03)^2 + ... + 1030/(1.03)^6 = $1,000.**

The price equals face because the coupon rate and yield coincide under identical compounding conventions.

## 4. Coupon-annuity plus principal method

The coupon cash flows are an ordinary annuity. For i≠0:

**P = C[1-(1+i)^(-n)]/i + F/(1+i)^n.**

For i=0:

**P = nC + F.**

This form provides a useful independent calculation of the same price.

### Derivation

Start with the finite geometric sum:

**S = sum from k=1 to n of (1+i)^(-k).**

For i≠0, multiply by (1+i) and subtract:

**iS = 1-(1+i)^(-n).**

Therefore:

**S = [1-(1+i)^(-n)]/i.**

Multiply S by C and add the discounted principal F/(1+i)^n to obtain the bond-pricing formula.

## 5. Premium, par, and discount prices

For a conventional positive-coupon bullet bond under matching coupon and yield conventions:

- **Yield below coupon rate:** Price above face value (premium).
- **Yield equal to coupon rate:** Price equals face value (par).
- **Yield above coupon rate:** Price below face value (discount).

| Coupon rate | Nominal annual yield | Expected classification |
|---:|---:|---|
| 6% | 4% | Premium |
| 6% | 6% | Par |
| 6% | 8% | Discount |

These labels compare price with face value, not with intrinsic value or credit quality.

## 6. Why bond prices move opposite yields

Differentiate the flat-yield price with respect to periodic yield i:

**dP/di = -sum from k=1 to n of k×CF(k)/(1+i)^(k+1).**

For positive future cash flows and i>-1, each term is negative, so **dP/di<0**.

Because i=y/m, the derivative with respect to nominal annual yield is:

**dP/dy = (1/m)×dP/di < 0.**

Thus, when the discount yield increases, the present value of the same promised positive cash flows decreases. This is the mathematical basis for bond price/yield sensitivity.

## 7. Discount-factor pricing with a yield curve

A flat yield is not always the best discounting model. If each cash-flow date has its own market-consistent discount factor D(t), price is:

**P = sum from k=1 to n of CF(k) D(t(k)).**

For example, with six semiannual payments:

**P = 30D(0.5) + 30D(1.0) + ... + 1030D(3.0).**

When D(k/m)=(1+y/m)^(-k), this reproduces flat-yield pricing.

With nonflat discount factors, there is generally no single coupon-annuity factor that prices every payment using one common periodic yield. Sections 12.13–12.15 develop yield curves, spot rates, discount factors, and forward rates.

## 8. Clean price, dirty price, and settlement timing

The simple formula above assumes valuation on a coupon date, so it does not need to separate accrued interest.

In real bond markets:

**Dirty price = Clean price + Accrued interest**, subject to market conventions.

A clean quote excludes accrued interest; the dirty price includes it. Between coupon dates, valuation must account for settlement timing, day counts, and remaining fractional periods.

Section 12.8 addresses these details. Do not interpret the simple on-coupon-date result as a complete settlement calculation.

## 9. Numerical stability and validation

Financial software should:

1. Convert nominal annual yield to the correct periodic rate.
2. Confirm the number of remaining coupon periods and payment dates.
3. Include face value exactly once at maturity.
4. Avoid rounding intermediate discount factors.
5. Reconcile direct cash-flow pricing with annuity pricing when a flat-yield model applies.
6. Check that higher yields produce lower prices for positive cash flows.
7. Reject invalid or nonfinite inputs.
8. Keep full-precision valuation separate from display rounding.

For small rates, `math.log1p` and `math.expm1` reduce cancellation errors in powers and annuity factors.

The standalone Python program constructs coupon-bond cash flows, prices them using a flat yield or supplied discount factors, compares the annuity formula, calculates a yield-shock price change, and includes **12 reference tests**.

```python
"""12.5 Bond pricing reference: flat-yield and term-specific discount factors."""
import math
import unittest

def cash_flows(face, coupon_rate, periods, frequency=2):
    if not isinstance(periods,int) or isinstance(periods,bool) or periods<=0:
        raise ValueError("periods must be a positive integer")
    if not isinstance(frequency,int) or isinstance(frequency,bool) or frequency<=0:
        raise ValueError("frequency must be a positive integer")
    for x in (face,coupon_rate):
        if isinstance(x,bool) or not isinstance(x,(int,float)) or not math.isfinite(x):
            raise ValueError("non-finite or nonnumeric bond term")
    if face<=0 or coupon_rate<0:raise ValueError("invalid bond term")
    coupon=face*coupon_rate/frequency
    return [(k,k/frequency,coupon+(face if k==periods else 0.0))
            for k in range(1,periods+1)]

def flat_price(rows, annual_yield, frequency=2):
    if not rows:raise ValueError("empty cash flows")
    if not isinstance(frequency,int) or isinstance(frequency,bool) or frequency<=0:
        raise ValueError("invalid frequency")
    if isinstance(annual_yield,bool) or not isinstance(annual_yield,(int,float)) or not math.isfinite(annual_yield):
        raise ValueError("invalid yield")
    i=annual_yield/frequency
    if i<=-1:raise ValueError("periodic yield must exceed -100%")
    log_discount=math.log1p(i)
    return math.fsum(amount*math.exp(-k*log_discount) for k,_,amount in rows)

def curve_price(rows, discount_factors):
    if not rows or len(rows)!=len(discount_factors):
        raise ValueError("one discount factor required per cash flow")
    if any(isinstance(d,bool) or not isinstance(d,(int,float)) or not math.isfinite(d) or d<=0
           for d in discount_factors):
        raise ValueError("discount factors must be positive finite numbers")
    return math.fsum(amount*d for (_,_,amount),d in zip(rows,discount_factors))

def annuity_price(face,coupon_rate,periods,annual_yield,frequency=2):
    rows=cash_flows(face,coupon_rate,periods,frequency)
    i=annual_yield/frequency
    if not math.isfinite(i) or i<=-1:raise ValueError("invalid yield")
    coupon=face*coupon_rate/frequency
    if i==0:return face+periods*coupon
    logd=math.log1p(i)
    return coupon*(-math.expm1(-periods*logd))/i+face*math.exp(-periods*logd)

def parallel_shock(rows,yield_rate,shock,frequency=2):
    return flat_price(rows,yield_rate+shock,frequency)-flat_price(rows,yield_rate,frequency)

class Tests(unittest.TestCase):
    def setUp(self):self.rows=cash_flows(1000,.06,6,2)
    def test_01_count(self):self.assertEqual(len(self.rows),6)
    def test_02_first(self):self.assertEqual(self.rows[0][2],30)
    def test_03_final(self):self.assertEqual(self.rows[-1][2],1030)
    def test_04_par(self):self.assertAlmostEqual(flat_price(self.rows,.06),1000)
    def test_05_premium(self):self.assertGreater(flat_price(self.rows,.04),1000)
    def test_06_discount(self):self.assertLess(flat_price(self.rows,.08),1000)
    def test_07_annuity(self):self.assertAlmostEqual(flat_price(self.rows,.05),annuity_price(1000,.06,6,.05))
    def test_08_zero_yield(self):self.assertAlmostEqual(flat_price(self.rows,0),1180)
    def test_09_zero_coupon(self):self.assertAlmostEqual(flat_price(cash_flows(1000,0,6),.06),1000/1.03**6)
    def test_10_curve_flat_equivalence(self):
        dfs=[1.03**(-k) for k in range(1,7)]
        self.assertAlmostEqual(curve_price(self.rows,dfs),flat_price(self.rows,.06))
    def test_11_parallel_shock(self):self.assertLess(parallel_shock(self.rows,.06,.001),0)
    def test_12_validation(self):
        for fn in (lambda:cash_flows(0,.06,6),lambda:cash_flows(1000,.06,0),
                   lambda:flat_price(self.rows,-2.1),lambda:curve_price(self.rows,[1.0]),
                   lambda:curve_price(self.rows,[1.0]*5+[0])):
            with self.assertRaises(ValueError):fn()

if __name__=="__main__":
    rows=cash_flows(1000,.06,6)
    for y in (.04,.06,.08):
        print(f"Yield {y:.1%}: price {flat_price(rows,y):.2f}")
    print("100bp yield rise price change:",round(parallel_shock(rows,.06,.01),2))
    unittest.main(verbosity=2)
```

## 11. Exercises and worked answers

1. **Core identity:** What determines a bond's modeled price? **Answer:** Sum of discounted future cash flows.
2. **Coupon:** Find the semiannual coupon on $1,000 at 6% annually. **Answer:** $30.
3. **Periods:** How many semiannual periods remain in three years? **Answer:** Six.
4. **Final cash flow:** What is the last payment? **Answer:** $1,030.
5. **Periodic yield:** Convert 6% nominal annual with semiannual compounding. **Answer:** 3%.
6. **Par pricing:** What is the worked example's price at 6% yield? **Answer:** $1,000.
7. **Annuity method:** State the coupon present-value component. **Answer:** C[1-(1+i)^(-n)]/i.
8. **Principal component:** State the maturity principal present value. **Answer:** F/(1+i)^n.
9. **Zero yield:** What is price if the discount rate is zero? **Answer:** nC+F=$1,180.
10. **Premium:** Is the price above or below par at 4% yield? **Answer:** Above par.
11. **Discount:** Is the price above or below par at 8% yield? **Answer:** Below par.
12. **Price/yield sign:** What is the sign of dP/dy for positive future cash flows? **Answer:** Negative.
13. **Flat discount factor:** Give D(k/m) for nominal yield y. **Answer:** (1+y/m)^(-k).
14. **Curve pricing:** How do you price using maturity-specific discount factors? **Answer:** Sum CF(k)×D(t(k)).
15. **Zero coupon:** How is a zero-coupon bond priced? **Answer:** F×D(T).
16. **Double counting:** What happens if face value is included twice at maturity? **Answer:** Price is overstated by one discounted principal amount.
17. **Dirty price:** How does it relate to clean price? **Answer:** Dirty price equals clean price plus accrued interest under the applicable convention.
18. **Model limits:** Name two features excluded from the lab. **Answer:** For example, default risk and irregular settlement dates.
