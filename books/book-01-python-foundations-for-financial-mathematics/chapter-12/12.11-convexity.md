## Learning objectives

Derive bond convexity from the second derivative of price with respect to yield, explain positive convexity for conventional fixed cash flows, combine convexity with modified duration, verify derivatives numerically, and analyze yield-shock approximation errors.

## 1. Why duration alone is not enough

Modified duration measures the **local slope** of the price/yield curve. It approximates the percentage price response to a small yield change using a tangent line.

But a conventional fixed-coupon bond's price/yield relationship is curved, not straight. **Convexity** measures the curvature and improves the estimate of price changes for larger yield shocks.

A first-order estimate is:

**ΔP/P ≈ −D_Mod × Δy.**

A second-order estimate is:

**ΔP/P ≈ −D_Mod × Δy + 0.5 × Convexity × (Δy)².**

Here Δy is a nominal annual yield change expressed as a decimal, consistent with the bond's compounding convention.

## 2. Bond price function

Let F be face value, c the annual coupon rate, m coupon payments per year, n remaining periods, and i=y/m the periodic yield.

For promised payments CF(k):

**P(y) = Σ from k=1 to n of CF(k)/(1+y/m)^k.**

For a fixed-coupon bullet bond, CF(k)=Fc/m except the final cash flow also includes F.

The derivations below hold cash flows fixed as yield changes and require **y/m > −1**.

## 3. First derivative — Modified duration

Differentiate price with respect to nominal annual yield y:

**dP/dy = −(1/m) Σ k × CF(k)/(1+y/m)^(k+1).**

Because all cash flows are positive, the derivative is negative. Normalizing its magnitude by price gives:

**D_Mod = −(1/P)(dP/dy).**

This is the duration term in the first-order price approximation.

## 4. Second derivative — Convexity

Differentiate again:

**d²P/dy² = (1/m²) Σ k(k+1) × CF(k)/(1+y/m)^(k+2).**

Define the yield-based convexity measure:

**Convexity = (1/P)(d²P/dy²).**

Therefore:

**Convexity = [Σ k(k+1) × CF(k)/(1+y/m)^(k+2)] / (m²P).**

For positive fixed cash flows, all terms are positive, so **Convexity > 0**.

The convexity value depends on how yield is defined. A measure calculated using semiannual nominal yield is not numerically interchangeable with one calculated using a different yield convention without adjustment.

## 5. The second-order Taylor approximation

Expand the price function around the starting yield y:

**P(y+Δy) ≈ P(y) + (dP/dy)Δy + 0.5(d²P/dy²)(Δy)².**

Divide by P(y):

**ΔP/P ≈ −D_ModΔy + 0.5 Convexity(Δy)².**

The quadratic correction is positive for both positive and negative shocks when convexity is positive.

For example, suppose **D_Mod=2.7**, **Convexity=9.5**, and yield rises by **100 bp** (Δy=0.01).

- Duration-only percentage change: **−2.70%**.
- Convexity correction: **0.5×9.5×0.01² = 0.000475 = +0.0475%**.
- Duration-plus-convexity estimate: **−2.6525%**.

These are illustrative sensitivities, not exact results for the worked bond below.

## 6. Worked bond — Three-year semiannual coupon

Consider:

| Input | Value |
|---|---:|
| Face value | $1,000 |
| Annual coupon | 6% |
| Payment frequency | 2 per year |
| Remaining periods | 6 |
| Nominal annual yield | 6% |
| Periodic yield | 3% |
| Coupon per period | $30 |
| Base price | $1,000 |

Calculate each cash flow's present value, weight by k(k+1), and sum to obtain the second derivative.

**Convexity = [Σ k(k+1)PV(k)]/[m²P(1+i)²].**

This equivalent form is convenient for numerical implementation because PV(k) already contains the factor (1+i)^(-k).

The lab computes exact duration, convexity, and prices after positive and negative yield shocks.

## 7. Why gains and losses are asymmetric

For a conventional positive-convexity bond, a yield decrease raises the price and a yield increase lowers it.

For equal-sized shocks around the same initial yield, the **gain from the yield decline** is typically larger than the **loss from the yield increase**, measured in currency magnitude, because the curve bends upward.

The quadratic correction is positive in either direction. This is a local property of the fixed-cash-flow price function; it should not be generalized to all option-embedded instruments.

## 8. Zero-coupon bond convexity

For a zero-coupon bond with n periods:

**P = F/(1+i)^n.**

Its convexity under nominal annual yield y with frequency m is:

**Convexity_zero = n(n+1)/[m²(1+i)²].**

For n=6, m=2, and i=0.03:

**Convexity_zero = 6×7/[4×1.03²].**

The Python unit tests verify this closed-form result.

## 9. Exact repricing and error analysis

For each yield shock Δy, compare:

**Exact change = [P(y+Δy)−P(y)]/P(y).**

**Duration estimate = −D_ModΔy.**

**Duration + convexity estimate = −D_ModΔy + 0.5 Convexity(Δy)².**

The second-order estimate will often be more accurate for moderate shocks, but it is still an approximation. Higher-order terms, yield-convention mismatches, and changes in expected cash flows can all matter.

## 10. Finite-difference verification

Approximate the second derivative using a central difference with a small step h:

**d²P/dy² ≈ [P(y+h)−2P(y)+P(y−h)]/h².**

Then divide by P(y) to estimate convexity.

Extremely small h can magnify floating-point cancellation; overly large h creates truncation error. A practical test compares finite-difference and analytical results with a stated tolerance.

## 11. Effective and negative convexity

The positive-convexity derivation assumes fixed, positive cash flows.

For callable bonds, mortgage-backed securities, and other option-sensitive assets, cash flows may change when rates move. Their **effective convexity** can become low or negative in some rate ranges. In such cases, price scenarios should revalue the instrument using rate-dependent expected cash flows rather than simply applying the fixed-cash-flow formula.

## 12. Python hands-on lab

Save as **bond_convexity.py**. Run `python bond_convexity.py` and `python -m compileall -q bond_convexity.py`.

The program calculates price, modified duration, analytical convexity, and exact versus first- and second-order price changes. It includes **12 reference unit tests**. Independent runtime verification remains pending.

```python
import math, unittest
def analytics(face,coupon_rate,periods,yield_rate,frequency=2):
    if any(isinstance(x,bool) or not isinstance(x,(int,float)) or not math.isfinite(x) for x in (face,coupon_rate,yield_rate)): raise ValueError('finite numeric inputs required')
    if any(isinstance(x,bool) or not isinstance(x,int) or x<1 for x in (periods,frequency)): raise ValueError('invalid frequency or periods')
    if face<=0 or coupon_rate<0 or yield_rate/frequency<=-1: raise ValueError('invalid bond terms')
    i=yield_rate/frequency;c=face*coupon_rate/frequency
    pv=[(k,(c+(face if k==periods else 0))*math.exp(-k*math.log1p(i))) for k in range(1,periods+1)]
    p=math.fsum(v for _,v in pv)
    first=-math.fsum(k*v for k,v in pv)/(frequency*(1+i))
    second=math.fsum(k*(k+1)*v for k,v in pv)/(frequency**2*(1+i)**2)
    return {'price':p,'modified_duration':-first/p,'convexity':second/p,'slope':first,'curvature':second}
def scenario(face,coupon_rate,periods,yield_rate,change,frequency=2):
    if isinstance(change,bool) or not isinstance(change,(int,float)) or not math.isfinite(change):raise ValueError('invalid yield change')
    a=analytics(face,coupon_rate,periods,yield_rate,frequency)
    new=analytics(face,coupon_rate,periods,yield_rate+change,frequency)['price']
    first=-a['modified_duration']*change
    second=first+.5*a['convexity']*change**2
    return {'exact_pct':(new-a['price'])/a['price'],'duration_pct':first,'duration_convexity_pct':second}
class Tests(unittest.TestCase):
    def test_01_par(self):self.assertAlmostEqual(analytics(1000,.06,6,.06)['price'],1000)
    def test_02_negative_slope(self):self.assertLess(analytics(1000,.06,6,.06)['slope'],0)
    def test_03_positive_curvature(self):self.assertGreater(analytics(1000,.06,6,.06)['curvature'],0)
    def test_04_positive_convexity(self):self.assertGreater(analytics(1000,.06,6,.06)['convexity'],0)
    def test_05_duration_positive(self):self.assertGreater(analytics(1000,.06,6,.06)['modified_duration'],0)
    def test_06_zero_coupon(self):
        a=analytics(1000,0,6,.06);self.assertAlmostEqual(a['convexity'],6*7/(4*1.03**2))
    def test_07_central_difference(self):
        a=analytics(1000,.06,6,.06);h=.0001
        second=(analytics(1000,.06,6,.06+h)['price']-2*a['price']+analytics(1000,.06,6,.06-h)['price'])/h**2
        self.assertAlmostEqual(second,a['curvature'],delta=.1)
    def test_08_up(self):self.assertLess(scenario(1000,.06,6,.06,.01)['exact_pct'],0)
    def test_09_down(self):self.assertGreater(scenario(1000,.06,6,.06,-.01)['exact_pct'],0)
    def test_10_improved_positive_shock(self):
        s=scenario(1000,.06,6,.06,.01)
        self.assertLess(abs(s['exact_pct']-s['duration_convexity_pct']),abs(s['exact_pct']-s['duration_pct']))
    def test_11_improved_negative_shock(self):
        s=scenario(1000,.06,6,.06,-.01)
        self.assertLess(abs(s['exact_pct']-s['duration_convexity_pct']),abs(s['exact_pct']-s['duration_pct']))
    def test_12_validation(self):
        for fn in (lambda:analytics(0,.06,6,.06),lambda:analytics(1000,-.01,6,.06),lambda:analytics(1000,.06,0,.06),lambda:analytics(1000,.06,6,-2),lambda:scenario(1000,.06,6,.06,float('nan'))):
            with self.assertRaises(ValueError):fn()
if __name__=='__main__':
    a=analytics(1000,.06,6,.06)
    print('Price',round(a['price'],4),'Duration',round(a['modified_duration'],6),'Convexity',round(a['convexity'],6))
    for bp in (-100,-50,25,50,100):
        s=scenario(1000,.06,6,.06,bp/10000)
        print(bp,'bp',s)
    unittest.main(verbosity=2)
```

## 13. Exercises with worked answers

1. **Concept:** What does convexity measure? **Answer:** Curvature of the price/yield relationship.
2. **Derivative:** Which derivative determines convexity? **Answer:** The second derivative of price with respect to yield.
3. **Definition:** State the formula. **Answer:** Convexity=(1/P)(d²P/dy²).
4. **Sign:** What is the sign for positive fixed cash flows? **Answer:** Positive.
5. **First order:** State the duration-only approximation. **Answer:** ΔP/P≈−D_ModΔy.
6. **Second order:** Add the convexity term. **Answer:** +0.5 Convexity(Δy)².
7. **Units:** Convert +100 bp to decimal yield. **Answer:** +0.01.
8. **Duration:** D_Mod=2.7 and +100 bp gives what? **Answer:** −2.70%.
9. **Correction:** Convexity=9.5 and Δy=0.01 gives what? **Answer:** +0.0475%.
10. **Combined:** Add Exercises 8 and 9. **Answer:** −2.6525%.
11. **Negative shock:** Does the quadratic correction change sign? **Answer:** No, because Δy is squared.
12. **Coupon:** Semiannual coupon on $1,000 at 6%? **Answer:** $30.
13. **Periods:** Three years semiannually? **Answer:** Six.
14. **Par:** Price when coupon and yield both equal 6%? **Answer:** $1,000.

16. **Finite difference:** State a numerical curvature formula. **Answer:** [P(y+h)−2P(y)+P(y−h)]/h².
17. **Approximation:** Is the duration-plus-convexity estimate exact? **Answer:** No; higher-order terms remain.
