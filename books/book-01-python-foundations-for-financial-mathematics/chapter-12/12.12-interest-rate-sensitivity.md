## 1. What interest-rate sensitivity means

Interest-rate sensitivity describes how a fixed-income instrument's market value responds to changes in the rates used to discount its future cash flows. For a conventional fixed-cash-flow bond, higher yield reduces price, and lower yield increases price.

A **yield-to-maturity shock** is not always the same as a shift in the risk-free yield curve. YTM includes the effect of a bond's full cash-flow schedule and can incorporate credit and liquidity compensation. Risk reports should name the shocked variable explicitly.

## 2. Exact repricing

Let P(y) denote a bond's dirty/full price under a flat nominal annual yield y. The exact change under a yield shock Δy is:

**ΔP_exact = P(y+Δy) − P(y).**

For a fixed-coupon bullet bond with coupon C, face F, n coupon periods, and frequency m:

**P(y) = Σ from k=1 to n of CF(k)/(1+y/m)^k.**

The model assumes unchanged cash flows and a constant yield for discounting all payments. For more realistic term-structure analysis, individual cash flows are discounted at their own relevant spot rates.

## 3. Duration as first-order sensitivity

Modified duration measures the normalized price slope:

**D_Mod = −(1/P)(dP/dy).**

The first-order price approximation is:

**ΔP_duration ≈ −P×D_Mod×Δy.**

If a $1,000 bond has modified duration 2.7 and yield rises by 50 basis points:

**Δy = 50/10000 = 0.005.**

**ΔP_duration ≈ −1000×2.7×0.005 = −$13.50.**

The approximate new price is $986.50. The exact repriced value may differ because the price/yield relationship is curved.

## 4. Convexity as second-order sensitivity

For positive fixed cash flows, the price/yield curve generally has positive convexity:

**Convexity = (1/P)(d²P/dy²).**

The second-order approximation is:

**ΔP_second ≈ P[−D_Mod×Δy + 0.5×Convexity×(Δy)²].**

The convexity term corrects the linear approximation in the positive direction for either sign of yield change.

A second-order approximation is not exact: higher-order terms remain, and cash-flow changes or embedded options may invalidate the fixed-cash-flow assumptions.

## 5. DV01 and basis-point units

One basis point (bp) equals **0.0001** in decimal yield.

For a conventional fixed-cash-flow position:

**DV01 ≈ P×D_Mod×0.0001.**

If P=$1,000 and D_Mod=2.7, DV01 is approximately **$0.27 per bp**.

Some reports express DV01 as a positive magnitude; others report a signed derivative. In this manuscript, **DV01 is the positive loss magnitude for a +1-bp rise**, under the conventional positive-duration model.

A +25-bp shock produces a first-order loss of approximately 25×DV01. For larger moves, exact repricing and convexity become more important.

## 7. Portfolio aggregation

For a portfolio of bonds indexed by j and a **common parallel YTM shock**, the first-order dollar change is:

**ΔV ≈ −Δy Σ[P(j)×D_Mod(j)].**

The portfolio DV01 is approximately:

**DV01_portfolio ≈ Σ[P(j)×D_Mod(j)×0.0001].**

Here P(j) must represent each position's **cash value**, not merely its quoted price per $100 face. A long position contributes positive DV01 magnitude under the convention used here; short positions require signed position values in a more general portfolio model.

The reference lab demonstrates aggregation for long-only conventional bonds under a common shock.

## 8. Parallel and nonparallel yield-curve movements

A parallel shift changes every relevant maturity's rate by the same amount. Actual yield curves also **steepen, flatten, twist, or change locally**.

A single YTM duration cannot fully describe exposure to these movements. Key-rate duration measures sensitivity to selected curve nodes while holding other curve nodes fixed under a defined interpolation method.

A first-order key-rate approximation is:

**ΔV ≈ Σ[(∂V/∂r(k))×Δr(k)].**

This requires a specified term-structure model and bump methodology; key-rate exposures cannot be inferred uniquely from a bond's single YTM duration.

## 9. Risk decomposition

Market value can change because of:

- Risk-free rate movements.
- Credit-spread widening or tightening.
- Liquidity premiums.
- Changes in expected cash flows.
- Embedded option exercise or prepayment behavior.
- Time passage, coupon payments, and carry.

A YTM scenario combines some of these effects into one scalar discount rate. A production risk engine should separate them when data and models permit.

## 10. Scenario design and stress testing

An operational scenario report should specify the valuation date, positions, face amounts, pricing convention, rate basis, compounding, shock magnitude, and whether shocks are parallel or nonparallel.

For every scenario, report:

1. Base price and value.
2. Exact shocked price and dollar P&L.
3. Duration-only estimated P&L.
4. Duration-plus-convexity estimated P&L.
5. Approximation error.
6. Portfolio total and largest contributors.

Include sufficiently large shocks to expose the limitations of linear approximations, while ensuring shocked yields remain within the pricing function's valid domain.

## 11. Hedging intuition and limitations

An investor can reduce first-order rate exposure by offsetting positive DV01 with an appropriate short bond or interest-rate derivative position. Matching DV01 can neutralize a small **parallel** shock locally.

However, DV01-neutral portfolios can retain convexity risk, curve-shape risk, basis risk, credit-spread risk, and model risk. Hedging effectiveness must be tested across scenarios and over time.

## 12. Python hands-on lab

Save as **interest_rate_sensitivity.py** and run `python interest_rate_sensitivity.py` and `python -m compileall -q interest_rate_sensitivity.py`.

The program calculates exact price changes, modified duration, convexity, DV01, and portfolio shock results. It includes **12 reference unit tests**. Independent runtime verification remains pending.

```python
import math, unittest
def bond(face, coupon_rate, periods, yield_rate, frequency=2):
    if any(isinstance(x,bool) or not isinstance(x,(int,float)) or not math.isfinite(x) for x in (face,coupon_rate,yield_rate)): raise ValueError('finite numeric terms required')
    if any(isinstance(x,bool) or not isinstance(x,int) or x<1 for x in (periods,frequency)): raise ValueError('positive integer periods and frequency required')
    if face<=0 or coupon_rate<0 or yield_rate/frequency<=-1: raise ValueError('invalid bond terms')
    i=yield_rate/frequency; c=face*coupon_rate/frequency
    pv=[(k,(c+(face if k==periods else 0))*math.exp(-k*math.log1p(i))) for k in range(1,periods+1)]
    p=math.fsum(v for _,v in pv)
    slope=-math.fsum(k*v for k,v in pv)/(frequency*(1+i))
    curvature=math.fsum(k*(k+1)*v for k,v in pv)/(frequency**2*(1+i)**2)
    return {'price':p,'duration':-slope/p,'convexity':curvature/p,'dv01':-slope*1e-4}
def shock(face,coupon_rate,periods,yield_rate,bp,frequency=2):
    if isinstance(bp,bool) or not isinstance(bp,(int,float)) or not math.isfinite(bp): raise ValueError('invalid shock')
    dy=bp/10000; a=bond(face,coupon_rate,periods,yield_rate,frequency)
    p1=bond(face,coupon_rate,periods,yield_rate+dy,frequency)['price']
    return {'exact':p1-a['price'],'duration':-a['price']*a['duration']*dy,'second_order':a['price']*(-a['duration']*dy+.5*a['convexity']*dy**2)}
def portfolio(positions, bp):
    if not positions: raise ValueError('positions required')
    results=[shock(*p,bp) for p in positions]
    return {key:math.fsum(r[key] for r in results) for key in ('exact','duration','second_order')}
class Tests(unittest.TestCase):
    def test_01_par(self):self.assertAlmostEqual(bond(1000,.06,6,.06)['price'],1000)
    def test_02_duration(self):self.assertGreater(bond(1000,.06,6,.06)['duration'],0)
    def test_03_convexity(self):self.assertGreater(bond(1000,.06,6,.06)['convexity'],0)
    def test_04_dv01(self):
        a=bond(1000,.06,6,.06);self.assertAlmostEqual(a['dv01'],a['price']*a['duration']*.0001)
    def test_05_rise(self):self.assertLess(shock(1000,.06,6,.06,100)['exact'],0)
    def test_06_fall(self):self.assertGreater(shock(1000,.06,6,.06,-100)['exact'],0)
    def test_07_zero(self):
        a=shock(1000,.06,6,.06,0);self.assertAlmostEqual(a['exact'],0);self.assertEqual(a['duration'],0)
    def test_08_quadratic_improves_up(self):
        a=shock(1000,.06,6,.06,100);self.assertLess(abs(a['exact']-a['second_order']),abs(a['exact']-a['duration']))
    def test_09_quadratic_improves_down(self):
        a=shock(1000,.06,6,.06,-100);self.assertLess(abs(a['exact']-a['second_order']),abs(a['exact']-a['duration']))
    def test_10_portfolio_additivity(self):
        a=(1000,.06,6,.06);r=portfolio([a,a],50);s=shock(*a,50)
        for key in s:self.assertAlmostEqual(r[key],2*s[key])
    def test_11_notional_scaling(self):
        a=bond(1000,.06,6,.06);b=bond(10000,.06,6,.06)
        self.assertAlmostEqual(b['dv01'],10*a['dv01'])
    def test_12_invalid(self):
        for f in (lambda:bond(0,.06,6,.06),lambda:bond(1000,-.01,6,.06),lambda:bond(1000,.06,0,.06),lambda:bond(1000,.06,6,-2),lambda:shock(1000,.06,6,.06,float('nan')),lambda:portfolio([],50)):
            with self.assertRaises(ValueError):f()
if __name__=='__main__':
    a=bond(1000,.06,6,.06)
    print('Base analytics:',a)
    for bp in (-200,-100,-50,-25,25,50,100,200):print(bp,'bp:',shock(1000,.06,6,.06,bp))
    unittest.main(verbosity=2)
```

## 13. Exercises with worked answers

1. **Definition:** What is interest-rate sensitivity? **Answer:** How value changes when discount rates or yields change.
2. **Price direction:** What happens when yield rises for a fixed-cash-flow bond? **Answer:** Price falls.
3. **Exact change:** Give the formula. **Answer:** P(y+Δy)−P(y).
4. **Duration:** State the first-order dollar approximation. **Answer:** −P×D_Mod×Δy.
5. **Convexity:** State the second-order correction. **Answer:** 0.5×P×Convexity×(Δy)².
6. **Basis points:** Convert 50 bp to decimal. **Answer:** 0.005.
7. **Dollar shock:** P=$1,000, D_Mod=2.7, +50 bp? **Answer:** Approximately −$13.50.
8. **New price:** Approximate new price from Exercise 7? **Answer:** $986.50.
9. **DV01:** State the magnitude formula. **Answer:** P×D_Mod×0.0001.
10. **DV01 example:** P=$1,000, D_Mod=2.7? **Answer:** $0.27/bp.
11. **25-bp loss:** Using Exercise 10, estimate loss for +25 bp. **Answer:** Approximately $6.75.
12. **Portfolio:** What does DV01 aggregation require? **Answer:** Consistent cash position values and shock conventions.
13. **Parallel shift:** Define it. **Answer:** Equal changes at all relevant curve maturities.
14. **Steepening:** What happens to the curve? **Answer:** Long rates rise relative to short rates, or short rates fall relative to long rates.
15. **Key-rate risk:** What does it capture? **Answer:** Sensitivity to specified yield-curve nodes.
16. **Credit spread:** Can a bond price move without a risk-free rate change? **Answer:** Yes, because spreads and other factors can change.
17. **Hedge:** Does matching DV01 eliminate all risk? **Answer:** No; convexity, curve, spread, and basis risks remain.
18. **Exact versus approximate:** Which should benchmark a scenario? **Answer:** Exact repricing under the specified pricing model.
