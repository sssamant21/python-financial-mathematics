## 1. What is a forward rate?

A **forward interest rate** is the rate implied today for a specified future interval, such as the one-year period beginning one year from now. It is extracted from today's term structure under an explicitly defined discounting and compounding convention.

A **spot rate** describes the discounting of a payment from today to a given maturity. A **forward rate** describes the rate consistent with discounting between two future dates.

An implied forward rate is **not a guaranteed forecast** of the future realized short-term rate. Market forwards can reflect term premiums, liquidity, funding and collateral conventions, and other risk factors.

## 2. Discount-factor consistency

Let DF(t) be the present value today of one unit paid at maturity t. For dates **0 ≤ t1 < t2**, a forward discount factor from t1 to t2 is:

**DF(t1,t2) = DF(t2)/DF(t1).**

Its reciprocal is the corresponding implied growth factor:

**G(t1,t2) = DF(t1)/DF(t2).**

These relationships follow from consistent valuation of a dated payment. The interval length is **τ=t2−t1** in years under the chosen year-fraction convention.

## 3. Annual-effective forward rates

For annual-effective compounding, the forward rate f(t1,t2) satisfies:

**[1+f(t1,t2)]^τ = DF(t1)/DF(t2).**

Therefore:

**f(t1,t2) = [DF(t1)/DF(t2)]^(1/τ) − 1.**

If the annual-effective spot rates are s1 and s2, then:

**DF(t1) = (1+s1)^(-t1)** and **DF(t2) = (1+s2)^(-t2)**.

Substitution yields:

**f(t1,t2) = [(1+s2)^t2/(1+s1)^t1]^[1/(t2−t1)] − 1.**

This is a no-arbitrage consistency identity under the stated discount-factor model, not an independent prediction.

## 4. Worked example — One-year forward beginning in one year

Assume annual-effective spot rates:

| Maturity | Spot rate |
|---|---:|
| 1 year | 3.00% |
| 2 years | 3.50% |

The discount factors are:

**DF(1) = 1/1.03**.

**DF(2) = 1/(1.035)^2**.

The one-year forward beginning in one year is:

**1+f(1,2) = DF(1)/DF(2) = (1.035)^2/1.03**.

**f(1,2) ≈ 4.0024%.**

The forward rate exceeds both spot rates because the two-year discount factor incorporates a higher rate over the second year than over the first.

## 5. Forward rates over intervals longer than one year

Suppose a 1-year spot rate is 3% and a 3-year spot rate is 4%. The **two-year forward beginning in year 1** is:

**f(1,3) = [(1.04)^3/1.03]^(1/2) − 1.**

The exponent is **1/2** because the forward interval lasts two years, not three.

Common mistakes include using the final maturity rather than the interval length as the exponent and confusing a one-year forward with a forward spanning multiple years.

## 6. Continuously compounded forwards

For continuously compounded spot rates z(t):

**DF(t) = exp[−z(t)t].**

Let f_c(t1,t2) be the continuously compounded forward rate over the interval. Then:

**DF(t2)/DF(t1) = exp[−f_c(t1,t2)(t2−t1)].**

Therefore:

**f_c(t1,t2) = [z(t2)t2 − z(t1)t1]/(t2−t1)**.

Equivalently:

**f_c(t1,t2) = ln[DF(t1)/DF(t2)]/(t2−t1)**.

For continuously compounded 1-year spot rate 3% and 2-year spot rate 4%:

**f_c(1,2) = 2×4% − 1×3% = 5%.**

## 7. Periodic nominal forward rates

For nominal annual compounding frequency m, define the forward rate j such that:

**[1+j/m]^[m(t2−t1)] = DF(t1)/DF(t2).**

Therefore:

**j = m{[DF(t1)/DF(t2)]^[1/(m(t2−t1))] − 1}.**

Rates quoted under different conventions may be economically equivalent but numerically different. Convert through discount factors to avoid mixing conventions.

## 8. Implied forwards from a spot-rate curve

Consider this illustrative **annual-effective spot curve**:

| Tenor | Spot rate |
|---:|---:|
| 1 year | 3.00% |
| 2 years | 3.50% |
| 3 years | 4.00% |
| 5 years | 4.50% |

Construct the discount factors for each maturity. Then compute interval forwards for:

- 0–1 years
- 1–2 years
- 2–3 years
- 3–5 years

The last interval is **two years long**, so its implied forward is an annual-effective rate for the full 3-to-5-year interval. It is not a separately observed 4-to-5-year forward.

## 9. Reconstructing discount factors

An essential validation check is that a forward rate reconstructs the later discount factor from the earlier one.

For annual-effective compounding:

**DF(t2) = DF(t1)/[1+f(t1,t2)]^(t2−t1).**

For continuous compounding:

**DF(t2) = DF(t1)×exp[−f_c(t1,t2)(t2−t1)].**

If the reconstructed value differs materially from the curve's DF(t2), the calculation may contain a convention, date, or rounding error.

## 10. Forward rates and expectations

The **expectations hypothesis** links long rates to expected future short rates under restrictive assumptions. Real-world forward rates can also contain term premiums and other effects.

A forward rate is therefore an observable or model-implied pricing quantity, not an unbiased forecast by definition.

In production reporting, distinguish **implied forward**, **forecast short rate**, and **contracted forward rate**. Their meanings and sources differ.

## 11. Applications in fixed-income analytics

Forward rates are used in:

- Forward-starting loans and deposits.
- Forward-rate agreements and interest-rate derivatives.
- Floating-rate cash-flow projection under a specified index curve.
- Interest-rate scenario construction.
- Curve calibration and consistency checks.
- Relative-value comparisons across future intervals.

In multi-curve derivative pricing, the **projection curve** for an index may differ from the **discount curve** used for collateralized valuation. This chapter's single-curve examples are educational simplifications.

## 12. Negative forward rates and curve behavior

A forward rate can be negative when discount factors imply a growth factor below one for the specified interval.

For example, if **DF(t2)>DF(t1)**, the annual-effective implied forward rate is negative, provided the discount factors remain positive.

This is mathematically possible in negative-rate environments. A negative forward is not automatically an implementation error. However, surprising values should trigger checks of market inputs, curve conventions, and interpolation.

## 13. Operational and numerical safeguards

- Validate **0 ≤ t1 < t2**.
- Require finite, positive discount factors.
- Use year fractions consistently with day-count rules.
- Specify whether rates are annual-effective, periodic nominal, or continuous.
- Use stable `log` and `expm1` calculations for small rates.
- Do not interpolate spot or forward rates without documenting the interpolation method.
- Reconstruct discount factors to verify consistency.
- Do not label forward rates as certain future outcomes.

```python
import math
import unittest
from decimal import Context, Decimal, DecimalException, localcontext
from fractions import Fraction

def _number(value,label,positive=False,signed=False):
    if isinstance(value,bool) or not isinstance(value,(int,float)):
        raise ValueError(f"invalid {label}")
    try:
        finite=math.isfinite(value)
    except OverflowError:
        finite=False
    if not finite or (not signed and value<0) or (positive and value==0):
        raise ValueError(f"invalid {label}")
    return Fraction(str(value))

_MIN_FLOAT=Fraction(math.ulp(0.0))

def _report(value,label):
    try:
        if 0<abs(value)<_MIN_FLOAT:
            raise OverflowError
        result=float(value)
    except OverflowError as exc:
        raise ValueError(f"{label} outside supported range") from exc
    if not math.isfinite(result):
        raise ValueError(f"{label} outside supported range")
    return result

def _evaluate(operation,label,positive=False):
    try:
        with localcontext(Context(prec=750)):
            exact=operation()
            result=float(exact)
    except (DecimalException,OverflowError) as exc:
        raise ValueError(f"{label} outside supported range") from exc
    if not math.isfinite(result) or (result==0 and (positive or exact!=0)):
        raise ValueError(f"{label} outside supported range")
    return result

def _evaluate(operation,label,positive=False):
    try:
        with localcontext(Context(prec=750)):
            exact=operation()
            result=float(exact)
    except (DecimalException,OverflowError) as exc:
        raise ValueError(f"{label} outside supported range") from exc
    if not math.isfinite(result) or (result==0 and (positive or exact!=0)):
        raise ValueError(f"{label} outside supported range")
    return result

def _basis(compounding,frequency):
    if compounding=="annual":
        return 1
    if compounding=="continuous":
        return None
    if compounding=="periodic" and type(frequency) is int and frequency>0:
        return frequency
    raise ValueError("invalid compounding or periodic frequency")

def _decimal(value):
    return Decimal(value.numerator)/Decimal(value.denominator)

def _rate(rate,basis):
    r=_number(rate,"spot rate",signed=True)
    if basis is not None and r<=-basis:
        raise ValueError("periodic rate must exceed -100%")
    return r

def _log_discount(rate,t,basis):
    if basis is None:
        return -_decimal(rate*t)
    return -_decimal(t*basis)*_decimal(1+rate/basis).ln()

def require_number(value,label):
    _number(value,label,signed=True)

def _interval(start,end):
    a=_number(start,"start")
    b=_number(end,"end")
    if a>=b:
        raise ValueError("invalid forward interval")
    return a,b

def _anchor(df,start):
    factor=_number(df,"discount factor",positive=True)
    if start==0 and factor!=1:
        raise ValueError("time-zero discount factor must be one")
    return factor

def _from_log(log_growth,tau,basis):
    def calculate():
        z=log_growth()/_decimal(tau)
        return z if basis is None else basis*((z/basis).exp()-1)
    result=_evaluate(calculate,"forward rate")
    if basis is not None and result<=-basis:
        raise ValueError("forward rounds outside the model domain")
    return result

def discount_factor(spot,tenor,convention="annual",frequency=2):
    basis=_basis(convention,frequency)
    r=_rate(spot,basis)
    t=_number(tenor,"tenor")
    return _evaluate(lambda:_log_discount(r,t,basis).exp(),"discount factor",positive=True)

def forward_from_dfs(df_start,df_end,start,end,convention="annual",frequency=2):
    basis=_basis(convention,frequency)
    a,b=_interval(start,end)
    first=_anchor(df_start,a)
    second=_number(df_end,"end discount factor",positive=True)
    return _from_log(lambda:_decimal(first/second).ln(),b-a,basis)

def forward_from_spots(r_start,r_end,start,end,convention="annual",frequency=2):
    basis=_basis(convention,frequency)
    a,b=_interval(start,end)
    first,second=_rate(r_start,basis),_rate(r_end,basis)
    return _from_log(lambda:_log_discount(first,a,basis)-_log_discount(second,b,basis),
                     b-a,basis)

def forward_df(df_start,forward_rate,start,end,convention="annual",frequency=2):
    basis=_basis(convention,frequency)
    a,b=_interval(start,end)
    first=_anchor(df_start,a)
    rate=_rate(forward_rate,basis)
    return _evaluate(lambda:(_decimal(first).ln()+_log_discount(rate,b-a,basis)).exp(),
                     "reconstructed discount factor",positive=True)

def forward_table(nodes,convention="annual",frequency=2):
    basis=_basis(convention,frequency)
    previous_t=previous_r=Fraction(0)
    result=[]
    for tenor,spot in nodes:
        t=_number(tenor,"tenor",positive=True)
        r=_rate(spot,basis)
        if t<=previous_t:
            raise ValueError("tenors must strictly increase")
        df=_evaluate(lambda:_log_discount(r,t,basis).exp(),"discount factor",positive=True)
        fwd=_from_log(lambda:_log_discount(previous_r,previous_t,basis)-_log_discount(r,t,basis),
                      t-previous_t,basis)
        result.append((float(previous_t),float(t),float(r),df,fwd))
        previous_t,previous_r=t,r
    if not result:
        raise ValueError("spot nodes required")
    return result

class ForwardRateTests(unittest.TestCase):
    def test_01_annual_spot_identity(self):
        self.assertAlmostEqual(forward_from_spots(.03, .035, 1, 2), 1.035**2/1.03-1)
    def test_02_continuous_identity(self):
        self.assertAlmostEqual(forward_from_spots(.03, .04, 1, 2, 'continuous'), .05)
    def test_03_flat_annual(self):
        self.assertAlmostEqual(forward_from_spots(.04, .04, 1, 3), .04)
    def test_04_flat_continuous(self):
        self.assertAlmostEqual(forward_from_spots(.04, .04, 1, 3, 'continuous'), .04)
    def test_05_periodic_identity(self):
        f = forward_from_spots(.03, .04, 1, 2, 'periodic', 2)
        self.assertAlmostEqual((1+f/2)**2, (1+.04/2)**4/(1+.03/2)**2)
    def test_06_reconstruct_df(self):
        a = discount_factor(.03, 1); b = discount_factor(.035, 2)
        f = forward_from_dfs(a, b, 1, 2)
        self.assertAlmostEqual(forward_df(a, f, 1, 2), b)
    def test_07_start_zero(self):
        self.assertAlmostEqual(forward_from_spots(0, .035, 0, 2), .035)
    def test_08_negative_forward(self):
        self.assertLess(forward_from_spots(.05, .02, 1, 2), 0)
    def test_09_table(self):
        rows = forward_table([(1, .03), (2, .035), (3, .04)])
        self.assertEqual(len(rows), 3)
        self.assertAlmostEqual(rows[0][4], .03)
    def test_10_df_zero(self):self.assertEqual(discount_factor(.04, 0), 1)
    def test_11_conversion_consistency(self):
        a = discount_factor(.03, 1); b = discount_factor(.035, 2)
        continuous = forward_from_dfs(a, b, 1, 2, 'continuous')
        annual = forward_from_dfs(a, b, 1, 2, 'annual')
        self.assertAlmostEqual(math.log1p(annual), continuous)
    def test_12_invalid(self):
        for fn in (lambda:forward_from_dfs(0, .9, 1, 2),
                   lambda:forward_from_dfs(.9, .8, 2, 1),
                   lambda:discount_factor(-1, 2),
                   lambda:forward_table([(2, .03), (1, .04)]),
                   lambda:forward_from_dfs(.9, .8, 1, 2, 'periodic', 0)):
            with self.assertRaises(ValueError):fn()
        with self.assertRaises(ValueError): forward_from_dfs(.9,.8,0,1)
        self.assertEqual(forward_from_spots(2,2,1000,1001),2)

if __name__ == '__main__':
    for start, end, spot, df, fwd in forward_table([(1, .03), (2, .035), (3, .04), (5, .045)]):
        print(f'{start:g}-{end:g}y spot={spot:.3%} DF={df:.8f} implied_forward={fwd:.4%}')
    unittest.main(verbosity=2)
```

## 15. Exercises with worked answers

1. **Definition:** What is a forward rate? **Answer:** A rate implied today for a specified future interval.
2. **Spot versus forward:** What differs? **Answer:** Spot discounting starts today; forward discounting spans two future dates.
3. **Forward DF:** Formula? **Answer:** DF(t2)/DF(t1).
4. **Growth factor:** Formula? **Answer:** DF(t1)/DF(t2).
5. **Interval:** What is τ? **Answer:** t2−t1.
6. **Annual formula:** State f(t1,t2). **Answer:** [DF(t1)/DF(t2)]^(1/τ)−1.
7. **One-year DF:** Spot 3% at 1y? **Answer:** 1/1.03.
8. **Two-year DF:** Spot 3.5% at 2y? **Answer:** 1/(1.035)^2.
9. **Forward example:** Implied f(1,2) from Exercises 7–8? **Answer:** Approximately 4.0024%.
10. **Long interval:** Which exponent for f(1,3)? **Answer:** 1/2.
11. **Continuous formula:** State f_c(t1,t2). **Answer:** ln[DF(t1)/DF(t2)]/(t2−t1).
12. **Continuous example:** z(1)=3%, z(2)=4%? **Answer:** f_c(1,2)=5%.
13. **Periodic formula:** How is j obtained? **Answer:** m{[DF(t1)/DF(t2)]^[1/(mτ)]−1}.
14. **Reconstruction:** State the annual-effective identity. **Answer:** DF(t2)=DF(t1)/(1+f)^τ.
15. **Forward table:** Is the 3-to-5-year forward a one-year rate? **Answer:** No, it spans two years.
16. **Negative forward:** Can it occur? **Answer:** Yes, if the implied growth factor is below one.
17. **Expectations:** Is an implied forward a guaranteed realized rate? **Answer:** No.
18. **Multi-curve:** Why might projection and discount curves differ? **Answer:** Index projection and collateralized discounting can use different market curves.
