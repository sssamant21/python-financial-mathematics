## 1. What is a zero-coupon bond?

A **zero-coupon bond** makes no periodic coupon payments. Under its contract, the investor is entitled to receive a specified principal amount at maturity, subject to the issuer's payment performance. The investor typically purchases the security at a discount to face value when yields are positive.

For a conventional fixed-coupon bond, the investor receives coupons along the way. For a zero-coupon bond, all promised cash flow arrives at maturity. This makes it a fundamental building block for present-value calculations and yield-curve construction.

A zero-coupon bond can trade **above** face value if its yield is negative under the chosen convention; the word “zero” refers to its coupon rate, not to its market price or risk.

## 2. Cash-flow timeline

Consider a three-year zero-coupon bond with face value **F=$1,000**.

```text
Today          0.5y      1.0y      1.5y      2.0y      2.5y      3.0y
  |-------------|---------|---------|---------|---------|---------|
 -P             0         0         0         0         0       +1000
                                                                  maturity
```

The investor pays **P** today and expects a single promised payment of **$1,000** at maturity. There are no interim coupons.

## 3. Zero-coupon pricing formula

Let F be face value, y a **nominal annual yield compounded m times per year**, T the years to maturity, and n=mT the number of periods.

The periodic yield is **i=y/m**. The price is:

**P = F/(1+i)^n = F/(1+y/m)^(mT).**

For a three-year $1,000 zero-coupon bond at a nominal annual yield of 6%, compounded semiannually:

**P = 1000/(1+0.06/2)^6 = 1000/(1.03)^6 ≈ $837.48.**

The difference **F-P ≈ $162.52** is the discount to face value. It is not a periodic cash payment.

## 4. Implied yield from a market price

Rearrange the price equation:

**P/F = (1+y/m)^(-mT).**

Taking the reciprocal and the power 1/(mT):

**1+y/m = (F/P)^(1/(mT)).**

Therefore:

**y = m[(F/P)^(1/(mT))-1].**

This is the **nominal annual yield with m-times-per-year compounding**. It is not automatically an effective annual rate or a regulatory disclosure yield.

Using the unrounded theoretical price in the inverse formula recovers the original 6% yield within numerical precision. If the actual input price is $837.48, use that price; its implied yield differs slightly from 6%.

## 5. Effective annual and continuously compounded yields

The effective annual rate corresponding to nominal annual yield y with frequency m is:

**EAR = (1+y/m)^m-1.**

For y=6% and m=2:

**EAR = (1.03)^2-1 = 6.09%.**

For a continuously compounded annual yield z:

**P=F exp(-zT)**, and **z=ln(F/P)/T**.

To equate the semiannual-compounding and continuous conventions:

**z=m ln(1+y/m).**

For y=6% with m=2, **z=2 ln(1.03)**, approximately **5.9118%**.

These conventions produce the same price only when the yields are converted consistently.

## 6. Discount factors

A **discount factor** D(T) converts a single unit of promised payment at maturity T into its present value:

**D(T)=P/F=(1+y/m)^(-mT).**

For the worked example:

**D(3)=(1.03)^(-6)≈0.83748.**

A promised $1,000 payment in three years has a modeled present value of about $837.48 under the stated constant yield.

Discount factors are central to pricing coupon bonds: each coupon and the maturity principal can be multiplied by a discount factor corresponding to its payment date. A market yield curve may require **different** discount factors at different maturities.

## 7. Yield, maturity, and price sensitivity

Holding F and T fixed, the price falls as yield increases because the promised maturity cash flow is discounted more heavily.

Holding F and a **positive** yield fixed, a longer maturity reduces the present price of a zero-coupon bond. With negative yields, the direction can reverse.

For the discrete-compounding model, the derivative with respect to nominal annual yield y is:

**dP/dy = -T P/(1+y/m).**

Hence the **modified duration** is:

**D_modified = -(1/P)(dP/dy)=T/(1+y/m).**

The **Macaulay duration** of a single-payment zero-coupon bond is exactly its time to maturity, **T years**, under this convention.

The second derivative gives **convexity**:

**Convexity = (1/P)(d²P/dy²) = n(n+1)/[m²(1+y/m)²].**

Duration and convexity are developed in detail later in Chapter 12. Here they provide an early sensitivity interpretation.

## 8. Zero-coupon versus coupon bond

| Feature | Zero-coupon bond | Fixed-coupon bullet bond |
|---|---|---|
| Interim coupons | None | Periodic payments |
| Principal | Due at maturity | Generally due at maturity |
| Cash-flow dates | One payment | Multiple payments |
| Reinvestment of coupons | None | Coupon reinvestment may matter |
| Macaulay duration | Equals maturity | Typically less than maturity for positive coupons and yields |
| Pricing | Single discount factor | Sum of discounted coupons and principal |

A zero-coupon bond is not necessarily risk-free. Credit, liquidity, inflation, currency, and market-rate risks can still be material.

## 9. Real-world conventions and limitations

Some zero-coupon securities are issued at a discount; others are created by stripping principal and interest components from coupon-bearing securities. Actual instruments can follow specialized settlement, quotation, day-count, tax, and market conventions.

For example, U.S. Treasury bills are commonly quoted using bill-specific discount and investment-rate conventions. Do **not** automatically apply the nominal semiannual-compounding formula in this section to interpret a Treasury bill's published quotation.

The mathematical reference model assumes a single promised maturity payment, deterministic maturity, a constant yield, and no default adjustment.

```python
"""Zero-coupon bonds: valuation, implied yield, and duration."""
import math
import unittest
from decimal import Context, Decimal, DecimalException, localcontext

def _number(value,label,positive=False):
    if isinstance(value,bool) or not isinstance(value,(int,float)):
        raise ValueError(f"{label} must be finite")
    try:
        finite=math.isfinite(value)
    except OverflowError:
        finite=False
    if not finite or (positive and value<=0):
        raise ValueError(f"invalid {label}")
    return Decimal(str(value))

def check(face,years,frequency):
    _number(face,"face",positive=True)
    _number(years,"years",positive=True)
    if type(frequency) is not int or frequency<1:
        raise ValueError("frequency must be a positive integer")

def _periodic(annual_yield,frequency):
    i=_number(annual_yield,"yield")/frequency
    if i<=-1:
        raise ValueError("periodic yield must exceed -100%")
    return i

def _evaluate(operation,label,positive=False):
    try:
        with localcontext(Context(prec=750)):
            exact=operation()
            result=float(exact)
    except (DecimalException,OverflowError) as exc:
        raise ValueError(f"{label} outside supported range") from exc
    if not math.isfinite(result) or (result==0 and (positive or exact!=0)):
        raise ValueError(f"{label} outside supported range")
    return result

def price(face,annual_yield,years,frequency=2):
    check(face,years,frequency)
    def calculate():
        i=_periodic(annual_yield,frequency)
        n=Decimal(str(years))*frequency
        # Scale the face inside the exponential, before float conversion.
        return (Decimal(str(face)).ln()-n*(1+i).ln()).exp()
    return _evaluate(calculate,"price",positive=True)

def implied_yield(face,market_price,years,frequency=2):
    check(face,years,frequency)
    market=_number(market_price,"price",positive=True)
    def calculate():
        n=Decimal(str(years))*frequency
        log_ratio=(Decimal(str(face))/market).ln()
        return frequency*((log_ratio/n).exp()-1)
    result=_evaluate(calculate,"implied yield")
    if result<=-frequency:
        raise ValueError("implied yield rounds to or below the periodic lower bound")
    return result

def discount_factor(annual_yield,years,frequency=2):
    return price(1.0,annual_yield,years,frequency)

def modified_duration(annual_yield,years,frequency=2):
    check(1,years,frequency)
    return _evaluate(lambda:Decimal(str(years))/(1+_periodic(annual_yield,frequency)),
                     "duration",positive=True)

def convexity(annual_yield,years,frequency=2):
    check(1,years,frequency)
    def calculate():
        t=Decimal(str(years))
        base=1+_periodic(annual_yield,frequency)
        return (t/base)*((t+Decimal(1)/frequency)/base)
    return _evaluate(calculate,"convexity",positive=True)

class Tests(unittest.TestCase):
    def test_01_zero_yield(self):self.assertEqual(price(1000,0,3),1000)
    def test_02_discount(self):self.assertAlmostEqual(price(1000,.06,3),1000/1.03**6)
    def test_03_inverse(self):self.assertAlmostEqual(implied_yield(1000,price(1000,.06,3),3),.06)
    def test_04_par_yield(self):self.assertAlmostEqual(implied_yield(1000,1000,3),0)
    def test_05_negative_yield(self):self.assertGreater(price(1000,-.01,3),1000)
    def test_06_higher_yield(self):self.assertLess(price(1000,.08,3),price(1000,.04,3))
    def test_07_longer_maturity(self):self.assertLess(price(1000,.06,5),price(1000,.06,3))
    def test_08_discount_factor(self):self.assertAlmostEqual(discount_factor(.06,3),price(1000,.06,3)/1000)
    def test_09_duration(self):self.assertAlmostEqual(modified_duration(.06,3),3/1.03)
    def test_10_convexity(self):self.assertAlmostEqual(convexity(.06,3),6*7/(4*1.03**2))
    def test_11_annual_frequency(self):self.assertAlmostEqual(price(1000,.06,3,1),1000/1.06**3)
    def test_12_invalid(self):
        for call in (lambda:price(0,.06,3),lambda:price(1000,-2,3,2),
                     lambda:price(1000,.06,0),lambda:implied_yield(1000,0,3),
                     lambda:price(1000,.06,3,0)):
            with self.assertRaises(ValueError):call()
        with self.assertRaises(ValueError): modified_duration(True,3)
        self.assertAlmostEqual(implied_yield(1e308,1e-308,4,1)/1e154,1)

if __name__=="__main__":
    F,y,T=1000,.06,3
    p=price(F,y,T)
    print("Price:",round(p,2))
    print("Implied nominal annual yield:",round(implied_yield(F,p,T),6))
    print("Discount factor:",round(discount_factor(y,T),8))
    print("Modified duration:",round(modified_duration(y,T),6))
    unittest.main(verbosity=2)
```

## 11. Exercises with worked answers

2. **Cash-flow count:** How many positive promised payments does a simple zero-coupon bond have? **Answer:** One, at maturity.
3. **Price formula:** State the nominal m-compounded pricing equation. **Answer:** P=F/(1+y/m)^(mT).
4. **Periodic rate:** Convert nominal annual 6% with semiannual compounding. **Answer:** 3% per half-year.

10. **Continuous yield:** Give the equivalent continuously compounded annual rate. **Answer:** z=2 ln(1.03)≈5.9118%.
11. **Discount factor:** What is D(3) in the example? **Answer:** (1.03)^(-6)≈0.83748.
12. **Zero yield:** What is price if the yield is zero? **Answer:** Face value.
