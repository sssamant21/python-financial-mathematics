# Section 11.11 — Worked answers and lab steps

| Prompt | Review and scope |
|---|---|
| 1. Net proceeds | 10,000 less 200 is 9,800. Checked in source and demo. |
| 2. Payment basis | The schedule amortizes face principal 10,000. The independent discount sum gives 443.21 to cents. |
| 3. Rate equation | The discount sum at the implied rate equals 9,800, not 10,000. |
| 4. Zero fees | Note rate discounts contractual installments to face principal. The source returns the note rate. |
| 5. Fee effect | With payments fixed, higher fees lower net proceeds and raise the root. Two independently derived roots were compared. |
| 6. Nominal | 12 × 1% = 12%. |
| 7. Effective | The exact rational growth factor gives approximately 12.6825%. |
| 8. Comparison | Positive compounding over 12 periods makes the effective rate larger. |
| 9. Zero note rate | A 1,200 face amount with 50 withheld over 12 periods yields a positive root despite zero stated interest. |
| 10. Bisection | PV strictly declines for positive level installments. A finite annuity PV is below the perpetuity bound at the proposed upper rate. |
| 11. Reconciliation | Discount the contractual payment at the solved rate and compare to net proceeds. The source and independent sum agree in the example. |
| 12. APR | Conceptual only. The educational cash-flow rate does not establish regulated APR. |
| 13. Financed fees | Conceptual only. Added principal raises payment under otherwise fixed terms. The lab models withheld fees. |
| 14. Dates | Conceptual only. Unequal intervals need date-specific discounting rather than this annuity factor. |
