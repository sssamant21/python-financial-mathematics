## Learning objectives

Describe yield and discount curves; relate zero rates, discount factors, and forward rates; explain bootstrapping from market instruments; price fixed cash flows from a curve; distinguish discount and projection curves; understand interpolation and day-count conventions; and design validation, repricing, sensitivity, and audit controls for curve construction.

## 1. Why curve construction matters

Interest rates are not one number. The market prices cash flows at different dates using a term structure. Curve construction transforms a set of observed market instrument prices and rates into a coherent representation that can be used to discount cash flows, project floating payments, and measure interest-rate risk.

A curve is a **model calibrated to observations**. Its shape depends on market quotes, instrument definitions, compounding, calendars, interpolation, and calibration choices.

## 2. Curve types and purposes

Common curve representations include:

- **Discount curve:** maps payment dates to present-value discount factors.
- **Zero-coupon yield curve:** maps maturities to zero rates.
- **Forward curve:** maps future intervals to implied forward rates.
- **Par yield curve:** maps tenors to coupon or swap rates that price instruments at par.
- **Projection curve:** estimates future floating-rate fixings for a given index.

These representations are related but not interchangeable without conventions and assumptions.

## 3. Discount factors

A discount factor D(t) expresses the time-zero value of one unit of currency payable at time t, under the chosen valuation framework.

For a continuously compounded zero rate z(t):

**D(t)=exp(−z(t)t)**.

For t>0 and D(t)>0:

**z(t)=−ln D(t)/t**.

Example: z(2)=5% implies:

**D(2)=exp(−.10)≈.904837**.

## 4. Compounding conventions

Discount factors depend on the rate convention.

For an annual effective rate y and integer years t:

**D(t)=(1+y)^(−t)**,

assuming the convention applies.

For nominal rate j compounded m times per year:

**D(t)=(1+j/m)^(−mt)**,

when m and t are compatible with the compounding schedule.

For continuously compounded z:

**D(t)=exp(−zt)**.

Do not mix rate quotes from different conventions without conversion.

## 5. Present value of fixed cash flows

For cash flows CFᵢ payable at dates represented by year fractions tᵢ:

**PV=Σᵢ CFᵢ D(tᵢ)**.

For a two-year bond paying coupon 5 at year 1 and principal plus coupon 105 at year 2:

**PV=5D(1)+105D(2)**.

If D(1)=exp(−.04) and D(2)=exp(−.10), this formula produces the model price.

The actual market convention may require accrued interest, settlement adjustments, and a specific coupon schedule.

## 6. Market instruments and input quotes

Curve calibration may use deposits, overnight indexed swaps, futures, interest-rate swaps, government bonds, and other liquid instruments.

Each instrument has its own:

- Quote definition.
- Payment schedule.
- Day-count fraction.
- Business-day convention.
- Settlement date.
- Collateral and discounting assumptions.
- Floating-rate index.
- Market liquidity and timestamp.

A market quote is not automatically a zero rate. Instrument-specific pricing equations are required.

## 7. Bootstrapping intuition

Bootstrapping solves for unknown curve values sequentially using market prices.

A simple one-year zero-coupon bond with face F and price P implies:

**D(1)=P/F**.

Suppose a two-year coupon bond pays C at year 1 and F+C at year 2, with market price P₂.

Then:

**P₂=C D(1)+(F+C)D(2)**.

Rearranging:

**D(2)=[P₂−C D(1)]/(F+C)**.

This example illustrates the mechanics. Real curves require accurate schedules and conventions and may involve nonlinear equations.

## 8. Worked bootstrap example

A one-year zero-coupon bond with face 100 and price 95 gives:

**D(1)=.95**.

A two-year bond with face 100, annual coupon 5, and price 100 satisfies:

**100=5(.95)+105D(2)**.

Thus:

**D(2)=(100−4.75)/105=95.25/105≈.907142857**.

The implied continuously compounded two-year zero rate is:

**z(2)=−ln(.907142857)/2**.

Check by repricing:

**5(.95)+105(.907142857)=100**, up to rounding.

## 9. Instrument repricing as calibration validation

A successfully calibrated curve should reproduce the observed market instruments within documented tolerances.

For each calibration instrument:

**residual=model_price−market_price**.

Residuals should be checked in appropriate units, such as price, basis points, or rate-equivalent error.

A solver success flag without repricing evidence is insufficient for curve acceptance.

## 10. Interpolation between curve nodes

Curve nodes usually do not coincide with every cash-flow date. Interpolation supplies intermediate values.

Common choices include:

- Linear zero-rate interpolation.
- Linear discount-factor interpolation.
- Linear log-discount interpolation.
- Cubic spline interpolation.
- Shape-preserving interpolation.

Each method implies different forward-rate behavior and may affect valuations and sensitivities.

## 11. Log-discount interpolation

For t between t₁ and t₂, define:

**λ=(t−t₁)/(t₂−t₁)**.

Then:

**ln D(t)=(1−λ)ln D(t₁)+λ ln D(t₂)**.

Therefore:

**D(t)=D(t₁)^(1−λ)D(t₂)^λ**.

Linear interpolation of log discount factors produces piecewise constant instantaneous forward rates between knots.

It preserves positive discount factors when endpoint discount factors are positive.

## 12. Forward rates from discount factors

The continuously compounded average forward rate over [t₁,t₂] is:

**f(t₁,t₂)=ln[D(t₁)/D(t₂)]/(t₂−t₁)**.

A discount curve increasing over an interval implies a negative forward rate there. This is not automatically invalid in negative-rate markets.

Any arbitrage check must reflect the market, collateral, currency, and modeling assumptions.

## 13. Day-count and calendar conventions

A maturity date must be converted to a year fraction using an appropriate day-count rule, such as ACT/360, ACT/365, or 30/360.

Payment schedules may require business-day adjustments, holiday calendars, stub periods, and settlement conventions.

Using 0.5 for every six-month period without checking the contract can introduce systematic valuation errors.

## 14. Single-curve versus multi-curve frameworks

In simple teaching examples, one curve may both discount cash flows and project floating rates.

In many modern interest-rate markets, discounting and projection are separated:

- A collateral-consistent discount curve values cash flows.
- A tenor- or index-specific projection curve estimates floating-rate payments.

For collateralized transactions, overnight indexed swap (OIS) curves often play a central discounting role, depending on the collateral agreement.

The correct framework is determined by the instrument and its contractual and market conventions.

## 15. Nonlinear curve calibration

Some instruments depend nonlinearly on unknown curve parameters.

A numerical bootstrap can solve:

**F(x)=model_price(x)−market_price=0**.

A bracketed root finder is often safer than an unconstrained Newton iteration when a valid bracket is available.

For overdetermined quote sets, a fitting problem may minimize weighted squared residuals. In that case, exact repricing of every quote is not guaranteed.

## 16. Curve risk and sensitivities

A curve is used not only for valuation but also for risk.

Common diagnostics include:

- Parallel rate shifts.
- Key-rate or tenor-specific bumps.
- Discount-factor sensitivity.
- PV01 or DV01 under an explicit sign convention.
- Forward-rate sensitivity.
- Stress scenarios for curve steepening, flattening, and inversion.

Sensitivity results depend on the **curve rebuild procedure**, not merely the final interpolated curve.

## 17. SciPy curve-calibration illustration

For a simple two-year bond, a root solver can recover the unknown discount factor:

```python
from scipy.optimize import brentq

price = 100.0
coupon = 5.0
face = 100.0
df_one = 0.95

def residual(df_two):
    return coupon*df_one + (face+coupon)*df_two - price

df_two = brentq(residual, 0.01, 2.0)
assert abs(residual(df_two)) < 1e-10
print("Bootstrapped D(2):", df_two)
```

This requires SciPy. The bracket must contain a sign change; production curve calibration should validate instrument assumptions and residuals.

## 18. Complete standard-library reference implementation

Save as `curve_construction.py`. This reference includes discount and zero-rate conversion, log-discount interpolation, forward-rate calculations, fixed cash-flow valuation, elementary bootstrap equations, and **12 reference unit tests**.

```python
"""17.10 Educational discount curve construction from zero-rate nodes."""
import math
import bisect
import unittest

def validate(tenors,zeros):
    if len(tenors)!=len(zeros) or len(tenors)<2:
        raise ValueError("at least two nodes required")
    if any(type(x) not in (int,float) or not math.isfinite(x)
           for x in (*tenors,*zeros)):
        raise ValueError("nonfinite curve node")
    if tenors[0]<=0 or any(tenors[i]>=tenors[i+1] for i in range(len(tenors)-1)):
        raise ValueError("positive, strictly increasing tenors required")

def discount_from_zero(t,z):
    if not all(type(x) in (int,float) and math.isfinite(x) for x in (t,z)) or t<0:
        raise ValueError("invalid tenor or rate")
    return math.exp(-z*t)

def log_discount_curve(tenors,zeros,t):
    validate(tenors,zeros)
    if type(t) not in (int,float) or not math.isfinite(t) or not tenors[0]<=t<=tenors[-1]:
        raise ValueError("tenor outside curve domain")
    logs=[-x*y for x,y in zip(tenors,zeros)]
    i=max(0,min(len(tenors)-2,bisect.bisect_right(tenors,t)-1))
    a,b=tenors[i],tenors[i+1]
    weight=(t-a)/(b-a)
    return math.exp((1-weight)*logs[i]+weight*logs[i+1])

def zero_from_discount(t,d):
    if type(t) not in (int,float) or type(d) not in (int,float) or not math.isfinite(t) or not math.isfinite(d) or t<=0 or d<=0:
        raise ValueError("positive tenor and discount required")
    return -math.log(d)/t

def forward_between(t1,t2,d1,d2):
    if not all(type(x) in (int,float) and math.isfinite(x) for x in (t1,t2,d1,d2)):
        raise ValueError("nonfinite input")
    if t1<0 or t2<=t1 or min(d1,d2)<=0:
        raise ValueError("invalid interval or discount")
    return math.log(d1/d2)/(t2-t1)

def price_cashflows(cashflows,tenors,zeros):
    validate(tenors,zeros)
    if not cashflows:raise ValueError("cashflows required")
    total=[]
    for t,amount in cashflows:
        if type(amount) not in (int,float) or not math.isfinite(amount):
            raise ValueError("nonfinite cashflow")
        total.append(amount*log_discount_curve(tenors,zeros,t))
    return math.fsum(total)

def bootstrap_one_year_df(price,face):
    if not all(type(x) in (int,float) and math.isfinite(x) for x in (price,face)) or price<=0 or face<=0:
        raise ValueError("positive price and face required")
    return price/face

def bootstrap_two_year_df(price,coupon,face,df_one):
    if not all(type(x) in (int,float) and math.isfinite(x) for x in (price,coupon,face,df_one)):
        raise ValueError("finite inputs required")
    if face<=0 or coupon<0 or df_one<=0:
        raise ValueError("invalid coupon or discount")
    df_two=(price-coupon*df_one)/(face+coupon)
    if df_two<=0:raise ValueError("nonpositive bootstrapped discount")
    return df_two

class CurveTests(unittest.TestCase):
    def test_01_discount(self):self.assertAlmostEqual(discount_from_zero(2,.05),math.exp(-.1))
    def test_02_zero(self):self.assertAlmostEqual(zero_from_discount(2,math.exp(-.1)),.05)
    def test_03_node(self):self.assertAlmostEqual(log_discount_curve([1,2],[.04,.05],1),math.exp(-.04))
    def test_04_midpoint(self):self.assertAlmostEqual(log_discount_curve([1,3],[.04,.06],2),math.exp(-.11))
    def test_05_flat_forward(self):self.assertAlmostEqual(forward_between(1,2,math.exp(-.05),math.exp(-.1)),.05)
    def test_06_cashflow(self):self.assertAlmostEqual(price_cashflows([(1,5),(2,105)],[1,2],[.04,.05]),5*math.exp(-.04)+105*math.exp(-.1))
    def test_07_bootstrap_one(self):self.assertAlmostEqual(bootstrap_one_year_df(95,100),.95)
    def test_08_bootstrap_two(self):self.assertAlmostEqual(bootstrap_two_year_df(100,5,100,.95),(100-4.75)/105)
    def test_09_unsorted(self):
        with self.assertRaises(ValueError):log_discount_curve([2,1],[.05,.04],1.5)
    def test_10_outside(self):
        with self.assertRaises(ValueError):log_discount_curve([1,2],[.04,.05],3)
    def test_11_bad_bootstrap(self):
        with self.assertRaises(ValueError):bootstrap_two_year_df(1,5,100,.95)
    def test_12_negative_rate(self):self.assertGreater(discount_from_zero(2,-.01),1)

if __name__=="__main__":
    times=[1.0,2.0,3.0]
    rates=[.04,.05,.055]
    print("DF 2.5y:",log_discount_curve(times,rates,2.5))
    print("Bond PV:",price_cashflows([(1,5),(2,105)],times,rates))
    unittest.main(verbosity=2)
```

## 19. Curve acceptance checklist

1. Identify valuation date, currency, and market close.
2. Record the exact quote snapshot and source.
3. Confirm instrument identifiers and maturities.
4. Apply the correct quote and compounding conventions.
5. Validate calendars and day-count fractions.
6. Check curve node ordering and duplicate maturities.
7. Confirm discount-factor positivity.
8. Reprice every calibration instrument.
9. Review forward-rate behavior and interpolation.
10. Define and test extrapolation rules.
11. Stress quotes and calculate risk sensitivities.
12. Retain solver settings, residuals, curve version, and approval evidence.

## 22. Troubleshooting

| Symptom | Likely cause | Corrective action |
|---|---|---|
| Curve fails to reprice bond | Cash-flow schedule or quote convention mismatch | Rebuild instrument cash flows |
| Negative bootstrapped discount | Inconsistent inputs or equation | Verify market data and pricing model |
| Curve discontinuity | Inconsistent nodes or interpolation | Inspect knot ordering and method |
| Forward rates unexpectedly extreme | Sparse quotes or curve fitting instability | Stress and review market inputs |
| Different PV across systems | Day count, settlement, or compounding mismatch | Reconcile conventions |
| Nonlinear bootstrap fails | No valid root bracket | Inspect residual and bounds |
| Out-of-range cash flow | Extrapolation not defined | Apply approved policy or reject |
| Risk changes sharply after small bump | Unstable calibration or interpolation | Rebuild curve and review sensitivities |
| Runtime tests not yet executed | Comprehensive QA deferred | Retain evidence in later QA phase |
