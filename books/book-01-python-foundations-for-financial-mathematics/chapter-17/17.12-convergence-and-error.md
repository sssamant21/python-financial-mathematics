## Learning objectives

Distinguish convergence from correctness; measure absolute, relative, and residual error; interpret convergence rates; choose stopping criteria; estimate discretization and sampling error; and assess the financial impact of numerical approximations.

## 1. Why convergence matters

Numerical methods produce approximations. A sequence of approximations converges to a limit if its distance from that limit tends to zero as the iteration count or resolution increases. Convergence to the wrong model, root, or local optimum does not make a result economically correct.

For example, a yield solver may return a rate whose bond price agrees closely with a specified market price, but an incorrect coupon schedule still produces an incorrect yield. Separate numerical error from input, model, and implementation error.

## 2. Error vocabulary

Let the exact value be x and an approximation be x̂.

- Signed error: x̂ − x.
- Absolute error: |x̂ − x|.
- Relative error: |x̂ − x|/|x| when x ≠ 0.
- Residual for f(x)=0: |f(x̂)|, expressed in the units of f.

An exact value is often unavailable. A high-accuracy reference, analytical special case, independent implementation, or refinement study can help, but agreement alone is not proof.

For a reference price of 100 and an estimate of 100.02, absolute error is 0.02 currency units and relative error is 0.0002, or 0.02%. For a reference value near zero, use a meaningful absolute tolerance rather than dividing by an insignificant reference.

## 3. Sources of error

| Source | Example | Can more iterations fix it? |
|---|---|---|
| Input or data | Incorrect market price | No |
| Model | Inappropriate cash-flow assumptions | No |
| Discretization | Approximating an integral on a finite grid | Often, within limits |
| Iteration | Stopping a root solver early | Sometimes |
| Roundoff | Finite floating-point precision | Not necessarily |
| Sampling | Finite Monte Carlo paths | Usually decreases statistically with more independent samples |
| Implementation | Wrong units or sign | No |

An error budget assigns acceptable limits to components in meaningful units. Components may interact; a simple sum is a conservative bound only where individual bounds are valid.

## 4. Convergence of iterative methods

Let eₖ = xₖ − x* for an exact solution x*. Locally, if |eₖ₊₁| ≈ C|eₖ|ᵖ with C > 0, p describes the order of convergence. Linear convergence has p = 1 and, for convergence, an asymptotic factor below 1. Quadratic convergence has p = 2. These are local descriptions and do not guarantee convergence from an arbitrary starting point.

Bisection reduces a valid sign-changing bracket by half per iteration. Newton's method can converge quadratically near a simple root with suitable smoothness and a good initial guess, but can fail or move to a different root elsewhere.

## 5. Bisection and an explicit bound

If f is continuous on [a,b] and the endpoints have opposite signs, at least one root lies in the bracket. After n bisection steps the bracket width is (b−a)/2ⁿ. Its midpoint is at most half the current width from **a root in the bracket**. Multiple roots can exist.

For [0,1] after 10 halvings, width is 1/1024 ≈ 0.00097656 and the midpoint bound is approximately 0.00048828. In yield units this bound corresponds to about 4.88 basis points, since one basis point is 0.0001 in decimal rate units.

## 6. Residual versus solution error

A small residual is useful but does not necessarily imply a small error in the root. Near a simple root, x̂ − x* ≈ f(x̂)/f′(x*) when the linear approximation is appropriate. A small derivative can magnify root error. With f(x) = (x−1)³ and x̂ = 1.01, the residual is 10⁻⁶ while the root error is 0.01.

For a yield calculation, price residual is in currency; yield error is in rate units. Approximate yield error by dividing price residual by the magnitude of price sensitivity only where the local derivative is meaningful and nonzero.

## 7. Stopping criteria

A mixed solution-space test is |xₖ₊₁−xₖ| ≤ atol + rtol |xₖ₊₁|. Combine it with a residual test, a valid bracket where available, and an iteration or evaluation limit. A small step may indicate stagnation instead of convergence. Scale tolerances to units and business requirements; extremely tight tolerances can exceed numerical or data precision.

Record termination reason, iterations, final bracket, residual, and solver settings. A maximum-iteration exit must remain distinguishable from success.

## 8. Financial example — a one-period yield

A cash flow of 110 due in one year priced at 100 has annual effective yield y satisfying 110/(1+y)−100=0, with y > −1. The exact yield is 0.10. Bracket [0, 0.2] has a positive residual at 0 and negative residual at 0.2. Bisection finds y = 0.1 immediately in exact arithmetic.

More complex coupon bonds require consistent dates, accrual conventions, compounding, settlement, and clean/dirty price conventions. A small numerical residual cannot validate these inputs.

## 9. SciPy root solver

```python
from scipy.optimize import root_scalar

def price_error(y):
    return 110.0 / (1.0 + y) - 100.0

result = root_scalar(price_error, bracket=(0.0, 0.2),
                     method="brentq", xtol=1e-12)
if not result.converged:
    raise RuntimeError(result.flag)
print("yield:", result.root)
print("price residual:", price_error(result.root))
print("iterations:", result.iterations)
```

Brent's method requires an appropriate sign-changing bracket for this call. The tolerance controls the solver's termination in the root variable; it is not by itself a bound on model or market-data error. Check the residual separately.

## 10. Discretization and refinement

For a smooth function on a fixed interval, the composite trapezoidal rule generally has global error O(h²), where h is the panel width. This assumes sufficient smoothness and does not eliminate roundoff. If halving h reduces the actual error by about four, the observed behavior is consistent with second-order convergence.

For ∫₀¹ x² dx = 1/3, composite trapezoidal approximations with n = 2 and 4 panels are 0.375 and 0.34375. Their errors are 1/24 and 1/96, respectively: a factor of four.

If I(h) = I + Chᵖ + smaller terms and the leading-order regime applies, the fine-grid error magnitude can be estimated by |I(h/2)−I(h)|/(2ᵖ−1). This estimate can fail when the leading term vanishes, the function is nonsmooth, or roundoff dominates. Compare several refinements and, where possible, an analytical benchmark.

## 11. Differentiation error

A forward difference often has truncation error O(h); a central difference often has O(h²), under suitable smoothness. Making h too small can amplify roundoff through subtraction. For a price P(y), a central approximation to sensitivity is [P(y+h)−P(y−h)]/(2h). Compare several h values, use an analytic benchmark when available, and distinguish a derivative per unit of decimal yield from a price change per basis point.

## 12. Numerical integration and SciPy

```python
import math
from scipy.integrate import quad

# Present value of a continuous cash flow at rate 5%.
value, estimated_error = quad(lambda t: 100 * math.exp(-0.05*t), 0, 2)
exact = 100 * (-math.expm1(-0.1)) / 0.05
print("PV:", value, "estimated integration error:", estimated_error)
print("actual benchmark error:", abs(value - exact))
```

The reported error is an algorithmic estimate, not a guarantee and not an estimate of errors in rate, model, or cash-flow inputs. Inspect warnings, known discontinuities, and difficult integrands.

## 13. Optimization convergence

A solver may terminate at a local optimum, a stationary point, a boundary, or a point failing constraints. Inspect its success flag and message, objective value, constraint residuals, bounds, and where appropriate gradient or optimality conditions. Compare initializations and economically sensible benchmarks. A stable objective value does not guarantee stable portfolio weights, especially with correlated assets and a poorly conditioned covariance matrix.

## 14. Monte Carlo error

For independent, identically distributed finite-variance discounted payoffs, the sample mean has estimated standard error s/√N. Roughly quadrupling independent paths halves standard error, not total model error. A nominal 95% normal-approximation interval is mean ± 1.96 standard errors when its assumptions and approximation are appropriate. It does not account for discretization bias, model risk, or poor random-number design. Correlated paths require a method that accounts for dependence.

Record random seed, generator, path count, variance reduction, discretization and software versions. A seed supports reproducibility, not correctness.

## 15. Convergence versus economic materiality

A price difference of $0.01 per unit can represent $10,000 across one million units. Conversely, a yield tolerance finer than data precision may create cost without meaningful benefit. Define tolerances for the output, volume, risk sensitivity, and decision. Avoid mixing percent, decimal rates and basis points.

## 16. Reference implementation and tests

Save as `convergence_error.py`. This standard-library example exposes the bisection bracket, iteration count, and residual. The yield test requests a tighter width tolerance and checks the price residual separately; a width tolerance alone is not a general residual guarantee. It demonstrates numerical concepts; it is not a production pricing library.

```python
import math
import unittest

def bisect(f, a, b, width_tol=1e-10, max_iter=200):
    if not (math.isfinite(a) and math.isfinite(b) and a < b):
        raise ValueError("finite ordered endpoints required")
    if not math.isfinite(width_tol) or width_tol <= 0:
        raise ValueError("width tolerance must be positive and finite")
    if type(max_iter) is not int or max_iter < 0:
        raise ValueError("max_iter must be a nonnegative integer")
    fa, fb = f(a), f(b)
    if not (math.isfinite(fa) and math.isfinite(fb)):
        raise ValueError("nonfinite endpoint value")
    if fa == 0: return a, a, a, 0, 0.0
    if fb == 0: return b, b, b, 0, 0.0
    if (fa > 0) == (fb > 0):
        raise ValueError("endpoints must bracket a sign change")
    for iteration in range(max_iter + 1):
        mid = a + (b-a)/2
        fm = f(mid)
        if not math.isfinite(fm):
            raise ValueError("nonfinite function value")
        if fm == 0: return mid, mid, mid, iteration, 0.0
        if b-a <= width_tol:
            return mid, a, b, iteration, abs(fm)
        if iteration == max_iter or mid == a or mid == b:
            raise RuntimeError("iteration limit or floating-point stagnation")
        if (fa > 0) != (fm > 0):
            b, fb = mid, fm
        else:
            a, fa = mid, fm
    raise RuntimeError("unreachable")

def trapezoid(f, a, b, n):
    if type(n) is not int or n < 1 or not (math.isfinite(a) and math.isfinite(b) and a < b):
        raise ValueError("ordered finite interval and positive integer panels required")
    h = (b-a)/n
    values = [f(a), f(b)] + [f(a+i*h) for i in range(1,n)]
    if not all(math.isfinite(v) for v in values):
        raise ValueError("nonfinite integrand")
    return h * math.fsum([values[0]/2, values[1]/2, *values[2:]])

def fine_error_estimate(coarse, fine, order):
    if not all(math.isfinite(v) for v in (coarse, fine, order)) or order <= 0:
        raise ValueError("finite approximations and positive order required")
    return abs(fine-coarse)/(2**order-1)

def central_difference(f, x, h):
    if not math.isfinite(x) or not math.isfinite(h) or h <= 0:
        raise ValueError("finite x and positive h required")
    return (f(x+h)-f(x-h))/(2*h)

class Tests(unittest.TestCase):
    def test_yield(self):
        x, a, b, n, residual = bisect(lambda y:110/(1+y)-100, 0, .2, width_tol=1e-12)
        self.assertAlmostEqual(x, .1)
        self.assertLess(residual, 1e-10)
    def test_bracket(self):
        x,a,b,n,r = bisect(lambda x:x*x-2, 0, 2, 1e-8)
        self.assertLessEqual(a, math.sqrt(2))
        self.assertGreaterEqual(b, math.sqrt(2))
        self.assertLessEqual(b-a, 1e-8)
    def test_bad_bracket(self):
        with self.assertRaises(ValueError): bisect(lambda x:x*x+1, -1, 1)
    def test_endpoint(self):
        self.assertEqual(bisect(lambda x:x, 0, 1)[0], 0)
    def test_limit(self):
        with self.assertRaises(RuntimeError): bisect(lambda x:x*x-2, 0, 2, max_iter=0)
    def test_trapezoid(self):
        self.assertAlmostEqual(trapezoid(lambda x:x*x, 0, 1, 2), .375)
    def test_order(self):
        c = trapezoid(lambda x:x*x, 0, 1, 2)
        f = trapezoid(lambda x:x*x, 0, 1, 4)
        self.assertAlmostEqual(fine_error_estimate(c,f,2), abs(f-1/3))
    def test_derivative(self):
        self.assertAlmostEqual(central_difference(lambda x:x*x, 2, 1e-4), 4, places=8)

if __name__ == "__main__":
    unittest.main(verbosity=2)
```

## 18. Worked exercises

1. **Absolute error:** Estimate 100.02, reference 100. Answer: 0.02.
2. **Relative error:** Same inputs. Answer: 0.02/100 = 0.0002 = 0.02%.
3. **Zero reference:** Why avoid relative error alone? Answer: division by zero is undefined; use an appropriate absolute scale.
4. **Bisection width:** Initial width 1, ten halvings. Answer: 1/1024.
5. **Midpoint bound:** Previous bracket. Answer: 1/2048 ≈ 0.00048828.
6. **Basis points:** Convert 0.0001 decimal yield. Answer: one basis point.
7. **Residual:** f(x)=(x−1)³ at 1.01. Answer: 10⁻⁶.
8. **Root error:** Same point. Answer: 0.01; residual alone is misleading.
9. **One-period yield:** Price 100, next-year cash flow 110. Answer: 10%.
10. **Price error:** Price 100.10 vs market 100. Answer: residual 0.10 in price units.
11. **Trapezoid n=2:** Integral of x² on [0,1]. Answer: 0.375.
12. **Trapezoid n=4:** Same integral. Answer: 0.34375.
13. **Error ratio:** Compare n=2 and n=4. Answer: (1/24)/(1/96)=4.
14. **Fine error estimate:** Difference between those approximations divided by 3. Answer: 1/96.
15. **Refinement:** If an O(h²) method is in its asymptotic regime, halve h. Answer: leading error is approximately quartered.
16. **Forward difference:** State the usual order for a smooth function. Answer: O(h).
17. **Central difference:** State the usual order for a smooth function. Answer: O(h²).
18. **Tiny derivative step:** Why can accuracy worsen? Answer: cancellation and roundoff can dominate.
19. **Monte Carlo paths:** Increase independent paths from N to 4N. Answer: standard error approximately halves.
20. **Monte Carlo bias:** Does increasing paths remove model bias? Answer: no.
21. **Stagnation:** Iterates stop changing with a large residual. Answer: do not call this successful convergence.
22. **Optimization:** Success flag but violated constraint. Answer: investigate feasibility and tolerance.
23. **Multiple roots:** A sign-changing bracket contains a root. Does it prove uniqueness? Answer: no.
24. **Input error:** Wrong coupon date but tiny residual. Answer: the computed solution may solve the wrong problem.
25. **Materiality:** $0.01 per unit on one million units. Answer: $10,000 aggregate.
26. **Tolerance units:** Price residual versus yield tolerance. Answer: currency versus decimal rate, respectively.
27. **Continuity:** Why does bisection need it for the existence guarantee? Answer: a sign change across a discontinuity need not contain a root.
28. **Newton iteration:** Is quadratic convergence global? Answer: no; it typically requires a good initial point near a simple root.
29. **Known benchmark:** Why compare with an analytical special case? Answer: it can reveal implementation or numerical mistakes.
30. **Refinement agreement:** Two similar estimates prove correctness? Answer: no; they may share bias.
31. **Sampling interval:** What does a nominal 95% interval omit? Answer: model and implementation error, among other sources.
32. **Roundoff floor:** Why might refinement stop improving results? Answer: finite precision can dominate.
33. **Solver status:** A maximum iteration exit with a small step. Answer: inspect residual and bracket; do not report unconditional success.
34. **Economic units:** 0.05 decimal yield equals what percent? Answer: 5%.
35. **Yield sensitivity:** Approximate yield error for price error 0.01 and |dP/dy|=100. Answer: 0.0001, about one basis point locally.
36. **Error budget:** Two valid worst-case absolute bounds of 0.01 and 0.02. Answer: their sum provides a conservative combined bound of 0.03 for additive errors.
37. **Constraint:** Weights sum to 1.001 when target is 1. Answer: budget residual is 0.001.
38. **Probability:** Does a seed establish statistical validity? Answer: no; it helps reproduce a run.
39. **Discretization:** Can a solver converge on every grid while the grid approximation remains biased? Answer: yes.
40. **Audit:** What should be saved? Answer: inputs and units, method, tolerances, termination, residual, versions and benchmark evidence.

## 19. Troubleshooting

| Symptom | Possible cause | Next check |
|---|---|---|
| Small step, large residual | Stagnation or poor scaling | Inspect bracket, precision and units |
| No sign change | Wrong bracket, model or no root there | Inspect function and economically valid domain |
| Error worsens on finer grid | Roundoff or nonsmooth integrand | Compare several grids and analytical cases |
| Solver success with unstable weights | Ill-conditioned optimization | Inspect sensitivity and constraints |
| Monte Carlo estimates fluctuate | Sampling variation | Record seed and report standard error |
| Low residual but inconsistent result | Wrong input, convention or root | Independently check assumptions and domain |
