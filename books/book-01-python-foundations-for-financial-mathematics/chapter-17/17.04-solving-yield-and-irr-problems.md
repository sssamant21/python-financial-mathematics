## Learning objectives

After this section, readers can build bond cash-flow schedules, distinguish bond price from yield, solve periodic IRR and yield-to-maturity equations, identify multiple or missing IRRs, calculate dated cash-flow XIRR using an explicit convention, choose justified root brackets, and validate financial results with independent repricing.

## 1. Financial motivation

Yield and internal rate of return are **inverse problems**. Discounting cash flows at a known rate is straightforward; inferring the rate from a known market price generally requires solving a nonlinear equation.

These problems appear in bond valuation, loan pricing, project appraisal, private investments, mortgage cash flows, and treasury analytics. Numerical correctness alone is insufficient: the discounting convention, dates, and cash-flow completeness determine whether the result is economically meaningful.

## 2. Periodic present value and net present value

For end-of-period cash flows CF₀ through CFₙ, define:

**NPV(r)=Σₜ₌₀ⁿ CFₜ/(1+r)ᵗ**, with **r>−1** under the reference's periodic convention.

The initial investment is typically a negative CF₀. Positive future receipts increase NPV. The function's units are currency.

An IRR is a rate r satisfying **NPV(r)=0**.

For [-100,110], **−100+110/(1+r)=0**, so **r=.10**.

## 3. Why numerical IRR is needed

With many irregular cash flows, the NPV equation is a polynomial-like rational equation in the discount factor. Closed-form expressions may not exist or may be impractical. A numerical root finder evaluates NPV at candidate rates until a root is isolated.

Before solving, determine whether the cash flows are periodic, whether the sign pattern permits multiple roots, and which rate interval is economically relevant.

## 4. Worked periodic IRR example

An investment costs $100 today and pays $60 at the end of each of the next two years.

**NPV(r)=−100+60/(1+r)+60/(1+r)²**.

At r=0, NPV=20. At r=.20, NPV=−100+50+41.6667≈−8.3333. Thus the continuous NPV function changes sign between 0% and 20%.

Bisection can locate the root. Repricing at the returned IRR should reproduce an NPV close to zero.

## 5. Multiple IRRs and nonconventional cash flows

Consider [-100,230,-132]:

**NPV(r)=−100+230/(1+r)−132/(1+r)²**.

Multiplying by (1+r)² yields a quadratic with **two IRRs: 10% and 20%**.

This is not a solver defect. It is a mathematical property of the cash-flow sequence. A single bracketed root finder does not enumerate all solutions.

For projects with alternating cash-flow signs, inspect the NPV profile and consider NPV at the relevant cost of capital rather than relying on one IRR.

## 6. When IRR does not exist or is not informative

Cash flows that are all negative have negative NPV for all r>−1, so no root exists in that domain.

If every cash flow is zero, every admissible rate is a root, and IRR is not identifiable.

IRR can also be misleading when comparing projects with different investment sizes, timing, or mutually exclusive choices. The NPV decision rule uses a separately justified discount rate and is often more informative.

## 7. Bond pricing from yield

For a bond with positive promised cash flows CF₁,...,CFₙ and annual end-period discounting:

**P(y)=Σₜ₌₁ⁿ CFₜ/(1+y)ᵗ**.

For a face value F, annual coupon rate c, and n years to maturity:

- Each coupon is **C=Fc**.
- For years 1 through n−1, cash flow is C.
- In year n, cash flow is C+F.

This simplified model assumes annual coupons, no default, no embedded options, exact annual periods, and no accrued interest. Real bond markets require additional conventions.

## 8. Worked bond price example

A three-year bond has face value $100 and an annual 5% coupon. At a 6% annual yield:

**P(.06)=5/1.06+5/1.06²+105/1.06³**.

The price is below par because the coupon rate is below the required yield.

At a 5% yield, the bond prices at $100. At a 4% yield, the bond prices above $100.

These three cases are useful unit tests for any yield engine.

## 9. Solving yield to maturity

Given observed market dirty price Pm, define:

**f(y)=P(y)−Pm**.

Find a yield y satisfying f(y)=0.

For conventional bonds with strictly positive future cash flows:

**dP/dy=−Σₜ₌₁ⁿ tCFₜ/(1+y)^(t+1)**,

which is negative for y>−1. Therefore, the simplified bond price is strictly decreasing with yield and the root is unique whenever a matching price lies in the range covered by the valid yield domain.

Use a bracket whose endpoint model prices straddle the market price. Do not silently assume that a 0%–100% bracket covers every real-world bond yield.

## 10. Clean price, dirty price, and accrued interest

In many bond markets:

**Dirty price = Clean price + Accrued interest**.

The present value of future cash flows corresponds to the full settlement value under the relevant convention. Comparing a modeled dirty price against a quoted clean price without adjusting for accrued interest produces an incorrect yield.

Settlement date, day count, ex-coupon treatment, coupon schedule, and payment calendars matter.

## 11. Coupon frequency and compounding

A semiannual coupon bond requires cash flows at half-year intervals and a compatible yield convention.

If the nominal annual yield is quoted with semiannual compounding, the periodic discount rate is y_nominal/2, and there are typically two coupon periods per year. An annual effective yield is not interchangeable with a nominal semiannual yield.

The reference code below intentionally models **annual end-period cash flows only**. Extend it explicitly rather than applying it unchanged to semiannual market quotes.

## 12. Dated cash flows and XIRR

Irregular cash-flow dates require a time convention. A common simplified dated NPV is:

**XNPV(r)=Σᵢ CFᵢ/(1+r)^((dᵢ−d₀)/365)**,

where d₀ is the reference date and the exponent uses actual elapsed days divided by 365 (**ACT/365 fixed**).

An XIRR solves XNPV(r)=0.

This is a particular convention, not a universal standard. Some systems use different day-count rules, payment-date assumptions, or compounding definitions. Compare results only after aligning conventions.

## 13. Worked dated example

A payment of $100 on January 1, 2025 is followed by $110 on January 1, 2026. There are 365 days between dates, so the exponent is exactly one under ACT/365 fixed.

The equation becomes:

**−100+110/(1+r)=0**.

Thus XIRR=10%.

For 182 days, the exponent would be 182/365, not .5 exactly.

## 14. Numerical methods for yields and IRR

**Bisection:** Requires a continuous sign-changing bracket. Simple, predictable, and robust.

**Brent-type methods:** Combine bracketing reliability with faster interpolation steps. Often preferable for practical scalar yield problems.

**Newton's method:** Can be fast with a good derivative and starting point, but may leave the valid rate domain or fail near flat regions.

For a production implementation, use a tested library such as SciPy and verify both its reported convergence status and the original pricing residual.

## 15. SciPy implementation example

```python
from scipy.optimize import root_scalar

flows = [-100.0, 60.0, 60.0]

def npv(rate):
    if rate <= -1.0:
        raise ValueError("rate outside discounting domain")
    return sum(cf / (1.0 + rate)**t for t, cf in enumerate(flows))

result = root_scalar(npv, bracket=[0.0, 0.20], method="brentq")
if not result.converged:
    raise RuntimeError("IRR solve did not converge")

print("IRR:", result.root)
print("NPV residual:", npv(result.root))
```

This example requires SciPy. The reference code in Section 17 uses only the standard library so the mathematical algorithm is visible.

## 16. Yield sensitivity, duration, and conditioning

For a small yield change Δy, bond price changes approximately as:

**ΔP/P≈−D_mod Δy**,

where D_mod is modified duration under a compatible yield convention.

The inverse sensitivity is approximately **Δy≈−(ΔP/P)/D_mod**.

This explains why yield uncertainty depends on the price-yield slope. A small price residual does not necessarily imply a small yield error when price is relatively insensitive to yield.

## 18. Complete standard-library Python reference

Save as `solving_yield_and_irr_problems.py`. This example includes periodic NPV, bracketed IRR, simplified annual-coupon bond pricing, YTM, and ACT/365-fixed XIRR, with **12 reference unit tests**.

```python
"""17.4: yield and IRR reference; annual end-period cash flows only."""
import math
import unittest

def finite(x):
    return type(x) in (int,float) and math.isfinite(x)

def pv(rate, cashflows):
    if not finite(rate) or rate <= -1:
        raise ValueError("rate must be finite and > -1")
    if not isinstance(cashflows,(list,tuple)) or len(cashflows)<2:
        raise ValueError("initial and future cash flows required")
    if any(not finite(c) for c in cashflows):
        raise ValueError("cash flows must be finite")
    return math.fsum(c/(1+rate)**t for t,c in enumerate(cashflows))

def bisection(f, lo, hi, xtol=1e-12, max_iter=250):
    if not all(finite(x) for x in (lo,hi,xtol)) or not lo<hi or xtol<=0:
        raise ValueError("invalid bracket")
    if type(max_iter) is not int or max_iter<1:
        raise ValueError("invalid iteration limit")
    flo,fhi=f(lo),f(hi)
    if not finite(flo) or not finite(fhi):
        raise ValueError("nonfinite endpoints")
    if flo==0:return {"root":lo,"residual":0.0,"iterations":0}
    if fhi==0:return {"root":hi,"residual":0.0,"iterations":0}
    if (flo>0)==(fhi>0):
        raise ValueError("endpoints do not bracket a sign change")
    for i in range(1,max_iter+1):
        mid=lo+(hi-lo)/2
        fm=f(mid)
        if not finite(fm):raise ValueError("nonfinite midpoint")
        if fm==0 or (hi-lo)/2<=xtol:
            return {"root":mid,"residual":fm,"iterations":i}
        if (fm>0)==(flo>0):lo,flo=mid,fm
        else:hi,fhi=mid,fm
    raise RuntimeError("iteration limit")

def periodic_irr(cashflows,lo=-.9,hi=1):
    if lo<=-1:raise ValueError("invalid lower rate")
    return bisection(lambda r:pv(r,cashflows),lo,hi)

def bond_dirty_price(face,coupon_rate,years,yield_rate):
    if not all(finite(x) for x in (face,coupon_rate,yield_rate)):
        raise ValueError("finite inputs required")
    if face<=0 or coupon_rate<0 or yield_rate<=-1 or type(years) is not int or years<1:
        raise ValueError("invalid bond assumptions")
    coupon=face*coupon_rate
    flows=[0]+[coupon]*(years-1)+[coupon+face]
    return pv(yield_rate,flows)

def bond_ytm(face,coupon_rate,years,market_price,lo=0,hi=1):
    if not finite(market_price) or market_price<=0:
        raise ValueError("positive market price required")
    return bisection(
        lambda y:bond_dirty_price(face,coupon_rate,years,y)-market_price,
        lo,hi
    )

def dated_xnpv(rate,base_date,dated_flows):
    from datetime import date
    if not finite(rate) or rate<=-1 or not isinstance(base_date,date):
        raise ValueError("invalid rate or base date")
    if not dated_flows:raise ValueError("dated cash flows required")
    terms=[]
    for d,c in dated_flows:
        if not isinstance(d,date) or not finite(c):
            raise ValueError("invalid dated flow")
        t=(d-base_date).days/365.0
        terms.append(c/(1+rate)**t)
    return math.fsum(terms)

def xirr(base_date,dated_flows,lo=-.9,hi=1):
    if lo<=-1:raise ValueError("invalid lower rate")
    return bisection(lambda r:dated_xnpv(r,base_date,dated_flows),lo,hi)

class YieldIRRTests(unittest.TestCase):
    def test_01_npv(self):self.assertAlmostEqual(pv(.1,[-100,110]),0)
    def test_02_irr(self):self.assertAlmostEqual(periodic_irr([-100,110])["root"],.1,places=9)
    def test_03_par_bond(self):self.assertAlmostEqual(bond_dirty_price(100,.05,3,.05),100)
    def test_04_discount_bond(self):self.assertLess(bond_dirty_price(100,.05,3,.06),100)
    def test_05_premium_bond(self):self.assertGreater(bond_dirty_price(100,.05,3,.04),100)
    def test_06_yield(self):
        price=bond_dirty_price(100,.05,3,.06)
        self.assertAlmostEqual(bond_ytm(100,.05,3,price)["root"],.06,places=9)
    def test_07_zero_coupon(self):self.assertAlmostEqual(bond_dirty_price(100,0,2,.1),100/1.1**2)
    def test_08_multiple_irrs(self):
        self.assertAlmostEqual(pv(.1,[-100,230,-132]),0,places=9)
        self.assertAlmostEqual(pv(.2,[-100,230,-132]),0,places=9)
    def test_09_bad_bracket(self):
        with self.assertRaises(ValueError):periodic_irr([-100,110],.2,.5)
    def test_10_bad_rate(self):
        with self.assertRaises(ValueError):pv(-1,[-100,110])
    def test_11_xirr(self):
        from datetime import date
        d=date(2025,1,1)
        flows=[(d,-100),(date(2026,1,1),110)]
        self.assertAlmostEqual(xirr(d,flows)["root"],.1,places=9)
    def test_12_price_monotonic(self):
        self.assertGreater(bond_dirty_price(100,.05,3,.03),bond_dirty_price(100,.05,3,.08))

if __name__=="__main__":
    from datetime import date
    print("3y 5% coupon price at 6% yield:",bond_dirty_price(100,.05,3,.06))
    print("IRR:",periodic_irr([-100,110]))
    unittest.main(verbosity=2)
```

## 21. Troubleshooting guide

| Problem | Likely cause | Resolution |
|---|---|---|
| Bond yield unexpectedly high | Clean price used as dirty price | Include accrued interest |
| Yield differs from broker | Coupon frequency or day-count mismatch | Align quotation conventions |
| IRR solver fails | No valid sign-changing bracket | Inspect NPV profile |
| Two IRRs appear | Nonconventional cash flows | Report ambiguity and evaluate NPV |
| XIRR differs across tools | Different date bases or date ordering | Reconcile ACT/365 and reference date |
| Tiny price residual but unstable yield | Ill-conditioned inverse problem | Perform sensitivity analysis |
| Bond price rises as yield rises | Cash-flow sign or discounting error | Recheck formula and schedule |
| Solver reports success outside allowed domain | Bounds not enforced | Validate final root independently |
| Numerical code runs but no audit evidence | Runtime validation deferred | Record test outputs during QA |
