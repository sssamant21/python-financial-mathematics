## Learning objectives

Explain interpolation and distinguish it from extrapolation; derive linear and piecewise-linear interpolation; recognize polynomial and cubic spline methods; understand shape-preserving interpolation; interpolate zero rates and log discount factors; calculate implied forward rates; use SciPy interpolation functions; and validate curve behavior and pricing consistency.

## 1. Why interpolation matters in financial mathematics

Market observations are available only at selected dates, strikes, maturities, or instrument tenors. Financial calculations often require values between those observations.

Examples include:

- Estimating a discount factor at a coupon date between quoted tenors.
- Constructing an interest-rate curve from a sparse set of instruments.
- Interpolating implied volatility across strikes or maturities.
- Estimating intermediate credit spread points.
- Evaluating a numerical solution on a different grid.

Interpolation fills gaps **using an explicit mathematical assumption**. It does not reveal the unknown true market value.

## 2. Interpolation versus extrapolation

Given observed pairs (x₀,y₀),...,(xₙ,yₙ), interpolation estimates y at x **inside** the observed range.

Extrapolation estimates values **outside** the observed range and is generally more model-sensitive.

A production curve library should enforce an explicit extrapolation policy. Silent extrapolation can introduce large valuation and risk errors.

## 3. Linear interpolation derivation

Between adjacent nodes (x₀,y₀) and (x₁,y₁), with x₀<x₁, define:

**λ=(x−x₀)/(x₁−x₀)**.

Then:

**y(x)=(1−λ)y₀+λy₁**.

For x between the nodes, λ∈[0,1].

Example: y₀=2 at x₀=1, and y₁=6 at x₁=3. At x=2, λ=.5, so y=4.

## 4. Piecewise-linear interpolation

For multiple ordered nodes, locate the interval containing the query x and apply the linear formula to that pair.

The interpolated function is continuous across knots, but its slope may change abruptly at knot locations.

Advantages: simple, transparent, no overshoot between adjacent values, and easy to audit.

Limitations: nondifferentiable knots, discontinuous instantaneous forward rates when used for certain curve quantities, and sensitivity to knot spacing.

## 5. Validation of interpolation nodes

Before interpolating, verify:

1. Node arrays have equal lengths.
2. There are at least two observations.
3. Every input is finite.
4. Tenors are strictly increasing.
5. Duplicate nodes are reconciled.
6. Units and compounding conventions match.
7. Missing observations are handled explicitly.
8. Query values lie inside the supported range unless extrapolation is authorized.

Sorting x values without applying the same permutation to y values corrupts the data.

## 6. Polynomial interpolation

With n+1 distinct nodes, a polynomial of degree at most n passes through every node.

A common representation is the Lagrange form:

**P(x)=Σᵢ yᵢ Lᵢ(x)**,

where

**Lᵢ(x)=Πⱼ≠ᵢ (x−xⱼ)/(xᵢ−xⱼ)**.

Polynomial interpolation can be exact at nodes but unstable between nodes when the degree is high or the node arrangement is unfavorable.

For financial curves, high-degree global polynomial interpolation is rarely a safe default.

## 7. Cubic splines

A cubic spline fits a cubic polynomial on each interval while enforcing continuity conditions across knots.

Common cubic splines maintain continuity of the function, first derivative, and second derivative.

Boundary conditions matter. A natural cubic spline, for example, typically sets second derivatives to zero at the endpoints.

Smoothness can be attractive for Greeks and curve sensitivity, but an unconstrained cubic spline may overshoot between observations.

## 8. Shape-preserving cubic interpolation

Piecewise cubic Hermite interpolating polynomial (PCHIP) methods are designed to preserve local shape and reduce overshoot.

For monotone data, a shape-preserving interpolator can be preferable to an unconstrained cubic spline.

However, shape preservation of quoted rates does not automatically guarantee economically valid discount factors or forward rates.

## 9. Financial curve quantities

An interest-rate term structure may be represented by:

- Zero rates z(t).
- Discount factors D(t).
- Log discount factors ln D(t).
- Instantaneous forward rates f(t).
- Par rates for specific instruments.

These quantities are related, but **linear interpolation in one representation is not equivalent to linear interpolation in another**.

Choosing the interpolated quantity is a modeling decision.

## 10. Zero rates and discount factors

For continuously compounded zero rate z(t):

**D(t)=exp(−z(t)t)**.

Conversely, for t>0 and D(t)>0:

**z(t)=−ln D(t)/t**.

For a 5% continuously compounded two-year zero rate:

**D(2)=exp(−.05×2)=exp(−.1)≈.904837**.

The reference implementation explicitly uses continuous compounding for zero-rate conversions.

## 11. Linear interpolation of zero rates

Suppose observed continuously compounded zero rates are:

- 1-year zero rate: 4%.
- 3-year zero rate: 6%.

Linear interpolation at 2 years yields 5%.

The corresponding discount factor is:

**D(2)=exp(−.05×2)=exp(−.1)**.

This differs from linearly interpolating the endpoint discount factors.

## 12. Log-discount interpolation

Suppose discount factors D₁ and D₂ are observed at tenors t₁ and t₂.

Interpolate **ln D(t)** linearly:

**ln D(t)=(1−λ)ln D₁+λ ln D₂**,

with **λ=(t−t₁)/(t₂−t₁)**.

Exponentiating gives:

**D(t)=D₁^(1−λ) D₂^λ**.

At the midpoint, the interpolated discount factor is the geometric mean of endpoint discount factors.

For D₁=.9 and D₂=.81:

**D_mid=√(.9×.81)≈.853815**.

## 13. Forward rates implied by discount factors

For two maturities t₁<t₂ and positive discount factors:

**f(t₁,t₂)=ln[D(t₁)/D(t₂)]/(t₂−t₁)**.

This is the continuously compounded average forward rate over the interval.

If ln D is linear between knots, the instantaneous forward rate is constant within each interval. It can jump at knots.

That is an intentional property of the chosen interpolation method, not a numerical error.

## 14. Negative rates and monotonicity

Positive discount factors are required for log interpolation.

Discount factors **do not always have to decline with maturity**: in negative-rate environments, they may increase over some intervals.

Similarly, a negative implied forward rate is not automatically an error.

Validation should distinguish mathematical consistency, economic assumptions, and market-specific no-arbitrage requirements.

## 15. Pricing cash flows from an interpolated curve

For deterministic cash flows CFᵢ at maturities tᵢ, price under the discount-curve model is:

**PV=Σᵢ CFᵢ D(tᵢ)**.

If D(tᵢ) is not directly observed, interpolation supplies an estimated discount factor.

The price depends on both the market quotes used to construct the curve and the interpolation convention.

A curve should be tested by repricing the market instruments used for calibration, subject to the intended tolerances.

## 16. SciPy interpolation examples

SciPy supports multiple interpolation families:

```python
import numpy as np
from scipy.interpolate import PchipInterpolator, CubicSpline

tenors = np.array([1.0, 2.0, 3.0, 5.0])
zero_rates = np.array([0.04, 0.045, 0.05, 0.055])

pchip = PchipInterpolator(tenors, zero_rates, extrapolate=False)
spline = CubicSpline(tenors, zero_rates, extrapolate=False)

print("PCHIP zero rate at 2.5y:", float(pchip(2.5)))
print("Cubic spline zero rate at 2.5y:", float(spline(2.5)))

discounts = np.exp(-tenors * zero_rates)
log_discounts = np.log(discounts)
log_df_at_2_5 = np.interp(2.5, tenors, log_discounts)
print("Log-discount interpolated DF:", float(np.exp(log_df_at_2_5)))
```

This example requires NumPy and SciPy. `np.interp` has its own out-of-range behavior; production code should validate bounds before calling it.

## 17. Complete standard-library reference implementation

Save as `interpolation.py`. The reference implements linear interpolation, log-discount interpolation, continuously compounded zero rates, and interval forward rates, with **12 reference unit tests**.

```python
"""17.9 Interpolation: linear, log-discount, and monotone PCHIP concepts."""
import math
import bisect
import unittest

def validate_nodes(xs,ys):
    if len(xs)!=len(ys) or len(xs)<2:
        raise ValueError("at least two paired nodes required")
    if any(type(v) not in (int,float) or not math.isfinite(v) for v in (*xs,*ys)):
        raise ValueError("nonfinite nodes")
    if any(xs[i]>=xs[i+1] for i in range(len(xs)-1)):
        raise ValueError("x nodes must be strictly increasing")

def linear_interpolate(xs,ys,x,extrapolate=False):
    validate_nodes(xs,ys)
    if type(x) not in (int,float) or not math.isfinite(x):
        raise ValueError("finite query required")
    if not extrapolate and not xs[0]<=x<=xs[-1]:
        raise ValueError("query outside interpolation domain")
    i=max(0,min(len(xs)-2,bisect.bisect_right(xs,x)-1))
    w=(x-xs[i])/(xs[i+1]-xs[i])
    return ys[i]+w*(ys[i+1]-ys[i])

def log_discount_interpolate(tenors,discounts,t):
    validate_nodes(tenors,discounts)
    if tenors[0]<0 or any(d<=0 for d in discounts):
        raise ValueError("nonnegative tenors and positive discount factors required")
    log_df=[math.log(d) for d in discounts]
    return math.exp(linear_interpolate(tenors,log_df,t))

def continuous_zero_rate(discount,t):
    if type(t) not in (int,float) or not math.isfinite(t) or t<=0:
        raise ValueError("positive maturity required")
    if type(discount) not in (int,float) or not math.isfinite(discount) or discount<=0:
        raise ValueError("positive discount required")
    return -math.log(discount)/t

def forward_rate(d1,d2,t1,t2):
    if not all(type(v) in (int,float) and math.isfinite(v) for v in (d1,d2,t1,t2)):
        raise ValueError("finite inputs required")
    if d1<=0 or d2<=0 or t1<0 or t2<=t1:
        raise ValueError("invalid discount or tenor")
    return math.log(d1/d2)/(t2-t1)

def piecewise_forward(tenors,discounts,t1,t2):
    if t1<tenors[0] or t2>tenors[-1] or t2<=t1:
        raise ValueError("forward interval outside curve")
    d1=log_discount_interpolate(tenors,discounts,t1)
    d2=log_discount_interpolate(tenors,discounts,t2)
    return forward_rate(d1,d2,t1,t2)

class InterpolationTests(unittest.TestCase):
    def test_01_linear_mid(self):self.assertAlmostEqual(linear_interpolate([1,3],[2,6],2),4)
    def test_02_endpoint(self):self.assertEqual(linear_interpolate([1,3],[2,6],3),6)
    def test_03_exact_node(self):self.assertEqual(linear_interpolate([1,2,3],[2,4,6],2),4)
    def test_04_outside(self):
        with self.assertRaises(ValueError):linear_interpolate([1,3],[2,6],4)
    def test_05_extrapolation(self):self.assertAlmostEqual(linear_interpolate([1,3],[2,6],4,True),8)
    def test_06_unsorted(self):
        with self.assertRaises(ValueError):linear_interpolate([2,1],[4,2],1.5)
    def test_07_duplicate(self):
        with self.assertRaises(ValueError):linear_interpolate([1,1],[2,3],1)
    def test_08_logdf_mid(self):self.assertAlmostEqual(log_discount_interpolate([1,3],[.9,.81],2),math.sqrt(.9*.81))
    def test_09_zero(self):self.assertAlmostEqual(continuous_zero_rate(math.exp(-.05*2),2),.05)
    def test_10_forward(self):self.assertAlmostEqual(forward_rate(math.exp(-.05),math.exp(-.1),1,2),.05)
    def test_11_invalid_df(self):
        with self.assertRaises(ValueError):log_discount_interpolate([1,2],[.9,0],1.5)
    def test_12_piecewise(self):
        self.assertAlmostEqual(piecewise_forward([1,2,3],[math.exp(-.05),math.exp(-.1),math.exp(-.15)],1.2,2.8),.05)

if __name__=="__main__":
    t=[1,2,3]
    d=[math.exp(-.05*x) for x in t]
    print("2.5y discount:",log_discount_interpolate(t,d,2.5))
    print("2.5y zero:",continuous_zero_rate(log_discount_interpolate(t,d,2.5),2.5))
    unittest.main(verbosity=2)
```

## 20. Troubleshooting

| Symptom | Likely cause | Corrective action |
|---|---|---|
| Division by zero | Duplicate x nodes | Deduplicate or reconcile quotes |
| Incorrect intermediate values | Unsorted or mispaired observations | Sort x and y together |
| Unrealistic curve oscillations | High-degree polynomial or cubic overshoot | Consider PCHIP or different representation |
| Forward rates jump at knots | Piecewise log-discount interpolation | Decide whether discontinuity is acceptable |
| Discount factors become nonpositive | Invalid interpolation or data | Validate positivity before log |
| Results differ across libraries | Different boundary or extrapolation conventions | Align methods and parameters |
| Bond does not reprice | Curve calibration or instrument convention mismatch | Reconcile cash flows and bootstrap |
| Curve changes sharply after one quote | Sparse data or sensitivity | Stress quote inputs and report impact |
| Tests not run | QA deferred | Execute and retain evidence during QA |
