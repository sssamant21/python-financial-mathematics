## Learning objectives

Design validation checks for financial calculations; distinguish verification from model validation; choose benchmarks and tolerances; inspect residuals, constraints, sensitivity and reproducibility; and retain evidence with clear limitations.

## 1. Why validation matters

A plausible result or solver success flag does not establish that inputs, formulas, implementation, and financial conventions are correct. Validation gathers evidence that an output is fit for its intended use. No single test proves correctness.

**Verification** asks whether implementation follows its specification. **Model validation** asks whether the model and assumptions are appropriate for its purpose. Both require input and convention checks.

Document units, cash-flow dates, day-count rules, settlement, compounding and rounding before comparing results. A rate of 5% means 0.05 as a decimal, and one basis point equals 0.0001.

## 2. Validation workflow

1. Define the calculation, intended use, units and assumptions.
2. Identify an analytical benchmark or independent reference.
3. Specify acceptance criteria before examining results.
4. Check input domains, missing values and finite values.
5. Test known cases, boundary conditions and invariants.
6. Inspect solver status, residuals and constraint violations.
7. Compare suitable methods or resolutions and test sensitivity.
8. Investigate failures rather than adjusting tolerances to pass.
9. Retain inputs, outputs, software versions and limitations.
10. Revalidate when code, data, model, dependencies or use materially change.

Two implementations sharing the same routine or assumptions are not fully independent. Agreement is useful evidence, not proof.

## 3. Choosing tolerances

A common comparison against reference b is:

**|a − b| ≤ atol + rtol |b|.**

Absolute tolerance has the units of the output; relative tolerance is dimensionless. Near zero, an absolute threshold is essential. Check for NaN and infinity explicitly. Select tolerances based on numerical uncertainty, financial materiality, data quality and intended use.

Example: price 100.02 versus 100.00, with atol = 0.01 and rtol = 0.00005. The threshold is 0.015, so the 0.02 difference fails. A price tolerance in dollars is not a yield tolerance in decimal rates.

## 4. Analytical benchmarks

Use cases with independently derived answers:

- A payment of 110 in one year discounted at 10% annually has PV 100.
- Cash flows −100 now and 110 in one year have NPV 0 at 10%.
- At zero rate, a payment of 100 in one period has PV 100.
- ∫₀¹ x² dx = 1/3.
- Two assets with weights 0.5 each, variances 0.04 and 0.09 and zero covariance have portfolio variance 0.0325.

These examples assume the stated simplified conventions. Real bonds require settlement, accrual, day-count, coupon timing, clean/dirty price and compounding conventions.

## 5. Residuals and conditioning

For f(x)=0, inspect |f(x̂)| in the units of f. A yield solver's price residual is calculated by repricing the instrument at its returned yield. A small residual does not necessarily imply a small yield error: if the price derivative is small, the root can be sensitive.

For Ax=b, compare ||Ax−b|| with a meaningful scale, for example ||A||·||x||+||b|| when nonzero. A small residual can coexist with a large solution error if the matrix is ill-conditioned. Solver status, solution step and residual answer different questions.

## 6. Invariants and metamorphic checks

A metamorphic check changes inputs in a controlled way and tests an expected relationship.

- With a fixed discount rate and schedule, doubling all cash flows doubles PV.
- Adding a zero cash flow leaves PV unchanged.
- For a positive future payment in the annual discount model, increasing a valid rate lowers PV.
- A fully invested portfolio has weights summing to one within a stated tolerance.
- A positive-semidefinite covariance matrix yields nonnegative variance in exact arithmetic.
- Consistently changing currency units scales monetary outputs, but not a dimensionless yield.

These relationships require their assumptions. A negative cash flow, for example, reverses the monotonicity statement.

## 7. Boundary cases

Test zero and small rates, valid negative rates, single or empty inputs, zero flows, invalid intervals, extreme magnitudes, NaN and infinity. Specify whether the expected outcome is a value or an exception.

For the annual effective model used here, r must be greater than −1. Other conventions have different domains. Very large magnitudes can overflow or underflow; the examples do not guarantee support for every representable input.

## 8. Independent cross-checks and refinement

Compare a numerical integral with an analytical result when available, and study several grid resolutions. Compare a root with an independent bracketed method and reprice the original equation. For optimization, examine the objective, bounds, each constraint residual and, where applicable, optimality conditions. Repeat with multiple starting points and small input perturbations.

Agreement among estimates can hide shared bias. A tighter stopping tolerance cannot repair incorrect data, a wrong equation, inappropriate model assumptions or a faulty convention.

A local optimizer's success flag does not prove global optimality. Portfolio weights may be sensitive even when the objective changes little, especially for highly correlated assets.

## 9. Yield and IRR

For a conventional cash-flow example, verify the valid rate domain, bracket, solver status and repricing residual. Nonconventional cash flows can have multiple IRRs. One returned root does not establish uniqueness; inspect plausible brackets and consider NPV at a specified discount rate.

For [−100, 110], the annual IRR is 10%. At 11%, NPV = −100 + 110/1.11 ≈ −0.9009. A rise from 10% to 11% is 100 basis points.

## 10. Monte Carlo checks

Compare with an analytical benchmark when possible. For independent, finite-variance discounted payoffs, the sample mean's estimated standard error is s/√N. Roughly quadrupling independent paths halves standard error. A nominal normal-approximation confidence interval depends on its assumptions and does not cover model bias or discretization error automatically.

Record generator, seed, paths, time steps and variance reduction. A seed supports reproducibility, not correctness. Correlated samples require an appropriate dependence-aware uncertainty estimate.

## 11. Evidence and reproducibility

Retain input sources and timestamps, units, transformations, code revision, Python and library versions, platform, random settings, algorithm, tolerances, termination, residuals, reference provenance, results and limitations. Record the reviewer and decision when applicable. Handle sensitive data according to organizational policy.

## 12. Reference implementation

Save the following as `validate_numerics.py`. It demonstrates annual effective discounting with integer periods and a two-asset variance. It does not implement production bond conventions or promise support for all extreme inputs.

~~~python
import math
import unittest

def finite_number(x):
    return type(x) in (int, float) and math.isfinite(x)

def close_to_reference(actual, reference, atol, rtol):
    if not all(finite_number(x) for x in (actual, reference, atol, rtol)):
        raise ValueError("finite values required")
    if atol < 0 or rtol < 0:
        raise ValueError("nonnegative tolerances required")
    return abs(actual-reference) <= atol + rtol*abs(reference)

def npv(cashflows, rate):
    flows = list(cashflows)
    if not flows or not finite_number(rate) or rate <= -1:
        raise ValueError("nonempty flows and finite rate > -1 required")
    if not all(finite_number(c) for c in flows):
        raise ValueError("finite cash flows required")
    try:
        result = math.fsum(c * math.exp(-t*math.log1p(rate))
                           for t, c in enumerate(flows))
    except OverflowError as exc:
        raise ValueError("discount computation overflow") from exc
    if not math.isfinite(result):
        raise ValueError("nonfinite NPV")
    return result

def price_residual(price, future_cashflows, rate):
    if not finite_number(price):
        raise ValueError("finite price required")
    # The first cash flow is at time zero; price is not included in it.
    return npv(future_cashflows, rate) - price

def two_asset_variance(w1, w2, v1, v2, covariance):
    if not all(finite_number(x) for x in (w1,w2,v1,v2,covariance)):
        raise ValueError("finite inputs required")
    if v1 < 0 or v2 < 0 or abs(covariance) > math.sqrt(v1)*math.sqrt(v2):
        raise ValueError("covariance matrix must be positive semidefinite")
    return w1*w1*v1 + w2*w2*v2 + 2*w1*w2*covariance

class ValidationTests(unittest.TestCase):
    def test_known_npv(self):
        self.assertTrue(close_to_reference(npv([-100,110],.1),0,1e-12,0))
    def test_zero_rate(self):
        self.assertEqual(npv([0,100],0),100)
    def test_linearity(self):
        self.assertTrue(close_to_reference(npv([0,200,100],.05),
                        2*npv([0,100,50],.05),1e-12,1e-12))
    def test_monotonic_positive_flow(self):
        self.assertGreater(npv([0,100],.05),npv([0,100],.06))
    def test_price_residual(self):
        self.assertLess(abs(price_residual(100,[0,110],.1)),1e-12)
    def test_mixed_tolerance_failure(self):
        self.assertFalse(close_to_reference(100.02,100,.01,.00005))
    def test_variance(self):
        self.assertAlmostEqual(two_asset_variance(.5,.5,.04,.09,0),.0325)
    def test_bad_rate(self):
        with self.assertRaises(ValueError):
            npv([100],-1)

if __name__ == "__main__":
    unittest.main(verbosity=2)
~~~

Run `python validate_numerics.py` and `python -m compileall -q validate_numerics.py` during QA. The eight tests are reference material; they have not been executed for this publication and do not certify the model.

## 13. SciPy cross-check

~~~python
from scipy.optimize import root_scalar

def price_error(y):
    return 110/(1+y) - 100

result = root_scalar(price_error, bracket=(0, .2), method="brentq",
                     xtol=1e-12)
if not result.converged:
    raise RuntimeError(result.flag)
print("yield:", result.root)
print("price residual:", price_error(result.root))
~~~

Compare with the independently derived yield of 0.10 and use a preselected currency tolerance for repricing. Record the SciPy version, method and termination status.

## 16. Troubleshooting

| Symptom | Possible cause | Next check |
|---|---|---|
| Result differs by factor 100 | Percent versus decimal | Check units |
| Solver succeeds but repricing fails | Wrong root or conventions | Inspect schedule, bracket and residual |
| Methods agree but benchmark fails | Shared error | Review independent derivation |
| Refinement stalls | Roundoff or bias | Compare resolutions and benchmark |
| Negative portfolio variance | Invalid covariance or roundoff | Inspect matrix and scale |
| Random results differ | Sampling or configuration | Record seed, generator and standard error |
| Near-zero comparison fails | Unsuitable relative tolerance | Choose justified absolute tolerance |
