## Learning objectives

Apply numerical root finding, optimization, integration, interpolation, curve construction, stability, convergence and validation to financial examples. Explain assumptions, interpret numerical output, and distinguish a plausible calculation from a validated result.

## How to use this section

Attempt the exercises before reading the worked answers. Unless stated otherwise, rates are decimal annual effective rates, cash flows occur at integer year ends, and time-zero cash flows occur immediately. Prices and cash flows use the same currency units. Answers are rounded for presentation; retain full precision in intermediate calculations.

A root, price or optimizer result is meaningful only for its specified model and inputs. Record the numerical method, tolerances, assumptions and residuals. These examples are educational; actual instruments require explicit calendars, day-count and settlement conventions.

## Part A — Numerical foundations and root finding

### 1. Analytical and numerical answers

**Problem:** A payment of 110 is received after one year. Find its present value at 10%.

**Solution:** PV = 110/1.10 = **100**. This closed-form value is a benchmark for a numerical implementation.

### 2. Root equation

**Problem:** An investment pays −100 now and 110 in one year. Define the yield equation.

**Solution:** f(r) = −100 + 110/(1+r) = 0 for r > −1. The solution is **r = 0.10**, or 10% per year.

### 3. Verify a bracket

**Problem:** For Exercise 2, check [0, 0.2].

**Solution:** f(0)=10 and f(0.2)=−8.3333. The function is continuous on this interval and changes sign, so at least one root exists in it.

### 4. Bisection step

**Problem:** Find the midpoint of [0, 0.2] and evaluate the preceding NPV.

**Solution:** Midpoint = 0.1; NPV = 0 in exact arithmetic. This is the root.

### 5. Bisection bound

**Problem:** An initial bracket has width 0.2. What are width and midpoint error bound after 10 halvings?

**Solution:** Width = 0.2/2¹⁰ = 0.0001953125. The midpoint is at most half this width from a root retained in the bracket: **0.00009765625**, about **0.9766 basis points**.

### 6. Invalid bracket

**Problem:** f(r) above is evaluated at 0.2 and 0.3. Both are negative. Can a sign-change solver use that bracket?

**Solution:** No; its sign-change requirement is not met. Inspect the model and choose an economically meaningful bracket. Same-sign endpoints alone do not prove that every possible function has no root inside.

### 7. Residual

**Problem:** A solver reports r=0.101. Calculate f(r) for [−100,110].

**Solution:** f(0.101)=−100 + 110/1.101 ≈ **−0.090827** currency units. Compare the absolute residual with a price tolerance.

### 8. Rate and price tolerances

**Problem:** Are a rate tolerance of 10⁻⁸ and an NPV tolerance of 10⁻⁸ interchangeable?

**Solution:** No. The first is in decimal rate units; the second is in currency units. Local price sensitivity connects them approximately, subject to conditioning and model assumptions.

### 9. Multiple IRRs

**Problem:** Show that [−100,230,−132] has roots 10% and 20%.

**Solution:** Multiply its NPV by (1+r)² and set u=1+r: −100u²+230u−132=0. This factors as −100(u−1.1)(u−1.2)=0, giving **r=.1 and .2**. One solver result does not establish uniqueness.

### 10. Endpoint root

**Problem:** For [−100,100], what is the rate at the lower endpoint of [0,0.2]?

**Solution:** At r=0, NPV = −100+100=0. The endpoint itself is a root.

## Part B — Optimization and portfolio risk

### 11. Quadratic minimum

**Problem:** Minimize f(x)=(x−3)²+2 over real x.

**Solution:** Since the square is nonnegative, the unique minimum is **x=3**, f(3)=2.

### 12. Bound constraint

**Problem:** Minimize the same function subject to 0≤x≤2.

**Solution:** The unconstrained minimum 3 is infeasible. The constrained minimum is at **x=2**, with objective 3.

### 13. Budget constraint

**Problem:** A portfolio has weights 0.4 and 0.6. Check its fully invested constraint.

**Solution:** 0.4+0.6=1, so the budget residual is 0.

### 14. Feasibility

**Problem:** An optimizer returns weights 0.4 and 0.61 with a target sum of 1. Find the residual.

**Solution:** 0.4+0.61−1=**0.01**. Compare it with the explicitly defined feasibility tolerance.

### 15. Portfolio variance

**Problem:** Two assets have weights 0.5 each, variances 0.04 and 0.09, covariance 0.

**Solution:** Variance = .5²(.04)+.5²(.09)+2(.5)(.5)(0)=**0.0325**. Volatility = √.0325 ≈ **0.18028**, or 18.03%.

### 16. Covariance effect

**Problem:** Repeat Exercise 15 with covariance 0.03.

**Solution:** Add 2(.5)(.5)(.03)=.015. Variance becomes **.0475**, volatility ≈ **.21794**. The covariance is feasible because .03 ≤ √(.04×.09)=.06.

### 17. Positive semidefiniteness

**Problem:** Is covariance 0.07 consistent with variances 0.04 and 0.09?

**Solution:** No. The magnitude must not exceed √(.04×.09)=.06 for this two-asset covariance matrix to be positive semidefinite.

### 18. Global versus local optimum

**Problem:** A local optimizer reports success. What additional evidence is needed before calling its result globally optimal?

**Solution:** Analyze objective and constraints, problem structure, feasibility and relevant optimality conditions; consider multiple starts or an appropriate global method. A success flag alone is insufficient.

### 19. Scaling

**Problem:** Portfolio weights are dimensionless, while one objective component is expressed in dollars and another in basis points. What must be checked?

**Solution:** Convert units consistently and document the coefficients and intended economic tradeoffs. Numerical scaling should not silently change the objective.

### 20. Sensitivity

**Problem:** Small changes to estimated correlations cause large changes in unconstrained weights. What might this indicate?

**Solution:** A poorly conditioned covariance matrix or unstable estimates. Inspect eigenvalues, input uncertainty, constraints and regularization; regularization changes the model and must be documented.

## Part C — Integration and interpolation

### 21. Known integral

**Problem:** Evaluate ∫₀¹x² dx.

**Solution:** [x³/3]₀¹=**1/3**.

### 22. Trapezoid approximation

**Problem:** Approximate that integral using two equal panels.

**Solution:** h=.5; T₂=.5[(0+1)/2+.25]=**.375**. Error is .375−1/3=**1/24**.

### 23. Grid refinement

**Problem:** Four-panel trapezoidal approximation is .34375. Compare errors with Exercise 22.

**Solution:** Error for four panels is 1/96, a quarter of 1/24. This is consistent with second-order behavior for this smooth example.

### 24. Continuous annuity

**Problem:** A continuous payment intensity is 100 per year for two years, with a continuously compounded rate of 5%. Find PV.

**Solution:** 100(1−e⁻⁰·¹)/.05 ≈ **190.32516**. At rate zero the limit is 200.

### 25. Stable annuity calculation

**Problem:** How should 1−exp(−x) be computed when x is very small?

**Solution:** Use **−expm1(−x)** to avoid subtracting nearly equal numbers.

### 26. Linear interpolation

**Problem:** A rate is 4% at year 1 and 6% at year 3. Find the linearly interpolated rate at year 2.

**Solution:** The midpoint is **5%**. State that the interpolated quantity is the rate and that this is a chosen interpolation assumption.

### 27. Interpolation versus extrapolation

**Problem:** Is estimating the same curve at year 4 interpolation?

**Solution:** No; year 4 is outside the supplied interval, so it is extrapolation and requires an explicit policy.

### 28. Log discount factors

**Problem:** D(1)=e⁻⁰·⁰⁴ and D(2)=e⁻⁰·¹. Find the continuously compounded forward rate for [1,2].

**Solution:** [ln D(1)−ln D(2)]/(2−1) = −.04−(−.1)=**.06**, or 6%.

### 29. Repricing a curve

**Problem:** Why reprice calibration instruments after constructing a curve?

**Solution:** Compare model prices with the input quotes under identical conventions. The resulting residuals test calibration but do not prove the chosen interpolation or market data is appropriate.

### 30. Quadrature error estimate

**Problem:** A numerical integrator reports an estimated error of 10⁻⁸. Does that cover wrong cash-flow assumptions?

**Solution:** No. It is an estimate of numerical integration error under the algorithm's conditions, not model or input error.

## Part D — Stability and validation

### 31. Floating-point addition

**Problem:** Why might (10¹⁶+1)−10¹⁶ evaluate to zero in binary64?

**Solution:** The unit increment is below the representable spacing at that magnitude and may be rounded away.

### 32. Accurate summation

**Problem:** How can [10¹⁶,1,−10¹⁶] be summed more accurately in Python?

**Solution:** Use `math.fsum`; for this example it returns 1. It is not a guarantee against all overflow or input errors.

### 33. Log return

**Problem:** A price rises from 100 to 101. What is its log return?

**Solution:** ln(101/100)=ln(1.01)≈**0.00995033**. For small simple returns use `math.log1p(r)`.

### 34. Overflow

**Problem:** Why can direct computation of log(exp(1000)+exp(1000)) fail?

**Solution:** exp(1000) overflows binary64. Log-sum-exp gives **1000+ln 2** without evaluating exp(1000).

### 35. Relative error

**Problem:** Estimate 100.02 compared with reference 100. What are absolute and relative errors?

**Solution:** Absolute error .02; relative error .02/100=.0002=**.02%**.

### 36. Near-zero comparison

**Problem:** Why is relative error alone unsuitable against a reference of zero?

**Solution:** Division by zero is undefined. Use a justified absolute tolerance, optionally combined with a relative term.

### 37. Mixed tolerance

**Problem:** Is 100.02 acceptable versus 100 with atol=.01 and rtol=.00005, using |a−b|≤atol+rtol|b|?

**Solution:** Threshold .01+.00005×100=.015. Error .02 exceeds it, so **fail**.

### 38. Residual and conditioning

**Problem:** For f(x)=(x−1)³ and x̂=1.01, compare residual and root error.

**Solution:** |f(x̂)|=10⁻⁶, while |x̂−1|=.01. A small residual alone does not guarantee an accurate root.

### 39. Convergence

**Problem:** An iteration stops moving but the residual remains large. Is that evidence of success?

**Solution:** No. It may have stagnated due to precision, scaling or method failure. Inspect termination and residual.

### 40. Validation evidence

**Problem:** What should be retained for a numerical result?

**Solution:** Inputs, units, conventions, code and dependency versions, method, tolerances, termination, residual, benchmark, outcome, limitations and reviewer decision.

## Part E — Applied cases

### 41. Coupon cash flows

**Problem:** Calculate the PV of 5 at year 1 and 105 at year 2 at 5% annual effective yield.

**Solution:** 5/1.05+105/1.05²=4.7619048+95.2380952=**100**. This is a simplified annual bond example without accrued interest.

### 42. Coupon repricing

**Problem:** If an instrument with the preceding cash flows is quoted at 100 and a solver returns yield 5%, what is its price residual?

**Solution:** Model price minus quoted price = **0** in exact arithmetic. Verify the same settlement and cash-flow conventions before comparing actual bond prices.

### 43. Yield change

**Problem:** Price a single payment of 110 one year from now at 11%.

**Solution:** 110/1.11≈**99.099099**. Relative to a price of 100, the change is approximately −.900901.

### 44. One-basis-point shift

**Problem:** Reprice the same payment at 10.01%, starting from 10%.

**Solution:** 110/1.1001≈**99.990910**; the change is approximately **−.009090**. This is a finite price change for +1 bp.

### 45. Currency scaling

**Problem:** Scale [−100,110] by 1,000. What happens to NPV and IRR?

**Solution:** NPV scales by 1,000 at each rate; roots remain the same, subject to numerical tolerances.

### 46. Two-period NPV

**Problem:** Compute NPV for [−100,60,60] at 10%.

**Solution:** −100+60/1.1+60/1.1²≈**4.13223**.

### 47. Two-period IRR

**Problem:** Find the positive IRR of [−100,60,60].

**Solution:** Let u=1+r. Then −100u²+60u+60=0, with positive u=(60+√27600)/200≈1.130662; **r≈.130662**, or 13.0662%.

### 48. Position materiality

**Problem:** A price discrepancy is .02 per unit across 500,000 units.

**Solution:** Aggregate discrepancy = .02×500,000=**10,000** currency units. Materiality depends on the decision and applicable policy.

### 49. Sampling error

**Problem:** For independent samples with estimated standard deviation 20 and N=10,000, estimate the standard error of the mean.

**Solution:** 20/√10,000=**0.2**. This does not capture model bias or discretization error.

### 50. Review decision

**Problem:** The solver converged and passes a residual threshold, but coupon dates are unverified. Is the result ready for use?

**Solution:** Numerical convergence is established only within the stated solver criteria. The input schedule still needs verification before an appropriate use decision.

## Optional SciPy reference snippet

~~~python
from scipy.optimize import root_scalar

def npv(rate, flows):
    return sum(c/(1+rate)**t for t, c in enumerate(flows))

flows = [-100.0, 60.0, 60.0]
result = root_scalar(lambda r: npv(r, flows),
                     bracket=(0.0, 0.3), method="brentq")
if not result.converged:
    raise RuntimeError(result.flag)
print("rate:", result.root)
print("NPV residual:", npv(result.root, flows))
~~~

This compact example illustrates the method. Add domain validation, numerical safeguards, explicit tolerances and evidence handling before using it as a general solver. Its inclusion is not a claim that it was run or independently validated.
