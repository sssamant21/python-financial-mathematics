## 1. Why sampling matters

Financial analysts rarely observe every relevant future outcome. They use finite historical datasets, cross-sectional panels, surveys, or transaction samples to estimate unknown quantities. An **estimand** is the quantity of interest (for example, the average return over a defined target population); an **estimator** is the rule used to calculate an estimate from observed data.

A historical mean is a **point estimate**, not a forecast guarantee. Any inference depends on how observations were collected and whether the population and sampling assumptions match the question.

## 2. Population and sample design

A population is the complete set of eligible units for the question. A sample is a subset of those units.

Examples:

- All bonds eligible under a specified mandate on a given date versus 10 randomly selected eligible bonds.
- Every trade executed by a venue in a period versus a transaction sample.
- All trading days in a defined historical interval versus a shorter selected interval.

Define the observation unit, eligibility criteria, date boundaries, sampling frame, treatment of missing values, and inclusion probabilities. A convenient collection of available records is not necessarily a probability sample.

## 3. Random sampling methods

**Simple random sampling without replacement** selects distinct units, giving each subset of a given size equal probability under an ideal design.

**Sampling with replacement** allows repeated selections. The nonparametric iid bootstrap uses this approach to approximate the sampling distribution of a statistic from the empirical distribution.

**Stratified sampling** samples within defined subgroups, such as bond ratings or sectors, to improve representation. Inference may require weighting by inclusion probabilities.

**Cluster sampling** selects groups, such as trading sessions or firms. Correlation within clusters affects uncertainty.

For time-series returns, independent sampling of individual dates may destroy serial dependence and volatility clustering. Block bootstrap methods can sometimes better preserve temporal structure, subject to their own assumptions.

## 4. Point estimates and sample variance

For observations x₁,…,xₙ, the sample mean is:

**x̄=(1/n)Σxᵢ.**

The conventional sample variance is:

**s²=[1/(n−1)]Σ(xᵢ−x̄)²**, for n≥2.

For decimal returns **[.01,.02,.03]**:

- Sample mean: **.02=2%**.
- Sample variance: **[(−.01)²+0²+(.01)²]/2=.0001**.
- Sample SD: **.01=1 percentage point**.

Under iid sampling with finite variance, the sample mean is unbiased for the population mean, and its variance is σ²/n. These results are conditional on the model, not universal facts about market returns.

## 5. Sampling distributions and standard errors

A **sampling distribution** describes how an estimator would vary over repeated samples under a specified sampling process.

For iid observations, an estimated standard error of the mean is:

**SE(x̄)=s/√n**.

For the three-return example:

**SE=.01/√3≈.0057735**, or approximately **0.57735 percentage points**.

This is a measure of estimator uncertainty under the iid assumption, not the SD of individual returns and not a prediction interval.

Increasing n by a factor of four halves the iid standard error if the underlying variance is unchanged.

## 6. Finite population correction

For simple random sampling **without replacement** from a finite population of size N, a standard correction to the iid-style standard error is:

**FPC=√[(N−n)/(N−1)]**, for N>1.

For N=100 and n=10:

**FPC=√(90/99)≈.95346**.

At a full census (n=N), sampling uncertainty for the finite-population mean is zero, although measurement errors and future uncertainty may remain.

This correction is not appropriate for arbitrary convenience samples or dependent financial time series.

## 7. Bias, variance, and mean squared error

Estimator bias is the difference between its expected value under the sampling model and the target estimand:

**Bias(θ̂)=E[θ̂]−θ**.

Estimator variance describes dispersion of θ̂ across repeated samples.

**MSE(θ̂)=Var(θ̂)+Bias(θ̂)²**, assuming finite second moments.

A low-variance estimate can still be badly biased. Larger samples do not automatically fix selection bias, survivorship bias, look-ahead bias, or faulty market-data adjustments.

## 8. Bootstrap estimation

The ordinary nonparametric bootstrap resamples n observations **with replacement** from an observed dataset of size n. For each replicate, compute the statistic of interest, such as the mean. The sample SD of the replicate estimates approximates its standard error under suitable assumptions.

Procedure:

1. Preserve the original sample and its observation units.
2. Choose a replicate count B and a reproducible random seed.
3. Draw B bootstrap samples of size n with replacement.
4. Calculate the statistic for each replicate.
5. Summarize the replicate distribution and its variability.
6. Document the assumptions and limitations.

A bootstrap does not correct systematic data selection bias. Naive iid resampling may also misrepresent uncertainty in autocorrelated or heteroskedastic financial returns.

## 9. Common finance-specific sampling failures

| Risk | Example | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| Survivorship bias | Excluding delisted stocks | Include historical constituents and delistings |
| Look-ahead bias | Using revised future information | Enforce as-of timestamps |
| Selection bias | Sampling only high-liquidity names | Define population and inclusion process |
| Calendar misalignment | Mixing different trading dates | Align timestamps and eligibility |
| Nonstationarity | Treating regimes as identical | Segment and stress assumptions |
| Serial correlation | Treating daily returns as iid | Consider autocovariance-aware SE or block methods |
| Volatility clustering | Independent bootstrap of individual days | Examine dependence-preserving resampling |
| Missing-not-at-random data | Unobserved distressed trades | Investigate missingness mechanism |

## 10. Python reference lab

Save the following as `sampling_and_estimation.py`. It implements sample moments, iid standard error, reproducible sampling without replacement, an iid bootstrap, and finite-population correction, with **12 reference unit tests**.

```python
import math
import random
import statistics
import unittest

def validate(xs):
    if not isinstance(xs,(list,tuple)) or not xs:
        raise ValueError("nonempty list or tuple required")
    if any(type(x) not in (int,float) or not math.isfinite(x) for x in xs):
        raise ValueError("finite numeric values required")
    return [float(x) for x in xs]

def sample_mean(xs):
    v=validate(xs)
    return math.fsum(v)/len(v)

def sample_variance(xs):
    v=validate(xs)
    if len(v)<2:
        raise ValueError("at least two observations required")
    m=sample_mean(v)
    return math.fsum((x-m)**2 for x in v)/(len(v)-1)

def standard_error_mean(xs):
    v=validate(xs)
    return math.sqrt(sample_variance(v)/len(v))

def sample_without_replacement(population,n,seed=42):
    p=validate(population)
    if type(n) is not int or not 1<=n<=len(p):
        raise ValueError("invalid sample size")
    return random.Random(seed).sample(p,n)

def bootstrap_means(xs,replicates=1000,seed=42):
    v=validate(xs)
    if type(replicates) is not int or replicates<1:
        raise ValueError("positive replicate count required")
    rng=random.Random(seed)
    n=len(v)
    return [math.fsum(rng.choices(v,k=n))/n for _ in range(replicates)]

def bootstrap_standard_error(xs,replicates=1000,seed=42):
    estimates=bootstrap_means(xs,replicates,seed)
    return statistics.stdev(estimates)

def finite_population_correction(n,N):
    if type(n) is not int or type(N) is not int or not 1<=n<=N:
        raise ValueError("require 1 <= n <= N")
    if N==1:
        return 0.0
    return math.sqrt((N-n)/(N-1))

class SamplingTests(unittest.TestCase):
    def test_01_mean(self): self.assertAlmostEqual(sample_mean([.01,.02,.03]),.02)
    def test_02_variance(self): self.assertAlmostEqual(sample_variance([.01,.02,.03]),.0001)
    def test_03_standard_error(self): self.assertAlmostEqual(standard_error_mean([.01,.02,.03]),.01/math.sqrt(3))
    def test_04_sample_size(self): self.assertEqual(len(sample_without_replacement([1,2,3,4],2)),2)
    def test_05_no_replacement(self):
        s=sample_without_replacement([1,2,3,4],4)
        self.assertEqual(sorted(s),[1,2,3,4])
    def test_06_reproducible_sample(self):
        self.assertEqual(sample_without_replacement(list(range(20)),5,11),
                         sample_without_replacement(list(range(20)),5,11))
    def test_07_bootstrap_length(self): self.assertEqual(len(bootstrap_means([1,2,3],25)),25)
    def test_08_bootstrap_reproducible(self):
        self.assertEqual(bootstrap_means([1,2,3],20,7),bootstrap_means([1,2,3],20,7))
    def test_09_bootstrap_constant(self): self.assertEqual(bootstrap_standard_error([2,2,2],20),0)
    def test_10_fpc_census(self): self.assertEqual(finite_population_correction(5,5),0)
    def test_11_fpc_example(self): self.assertAlmostEqual(finite_population_correction(10,100),math.sqrt(90/99))
    def test_12_invalid(self):
        for f in (lambda:sample_mean([]),lambda:sample_variance([1]),
                  lambda:sample_without_replacement([1,2],3),
                  lambda:bootstrap_means([1,2],0),
                  lambda:finite_population_correction(0,10),
                  lambda:sample_mean([float("nan")])):
            with self.assertRaises(ValueError):f()

if __name__=="__main__":
    r=[.01,.02,.03,.04,.05]
    print("Mean:",sample_mean(r))
    print("Naive iid standard error:",standard_error_mean(r))
    print("Bootstrap SE:",bootstrap_standard_error(r,1000,42))
    unittest.main(verbosity=2)
```

## 12. Twenty-four worked exercises

1. **Population:** All 100 eligible bonds. **Worked solution:** Population size N=100.

2. **Sample:** 10 randomly selected bonds. **Worked solution:** Sample size n=10.

3. **Sample fraction:** n=10, N=100. **Worked solution:** 10/100=10%.

4. **Sample mean:** Returns 1%,2%,3%. **Worked solution:** Mean=2%.

5. **Sample variance:** Returns 1%,2%,3%. **Worked solution:** s²=.0001 in decimal-return².

6. **Sample SD:** Same returns. **Worked solution:** s=.01=1 percentage point.

7. **Standard error:** s=.01, n=3. **Worked solution:** SE=.01/√3≈.0057735.

8. **Larger sample:** n increases 4× with stable variance. **Worked solution:** iid SE is halved.

9. **Finite population correction:** n=10, N=100. **Worked solution:** √[(100−10)/(100−1)]=√(90/99)≈.95346.

10. **Census:** n=N. **Worked solution:** FPC=0 for a finite population census.

11. **Random seed:** seed=42. **Worked solution:** Reproduces a pseudo-random sample for a fixed runtime and algorithm.

12. **Without replacement:** Sample 5 from 5 unique IDs. **Worked solution:** Every ID appears once.

13. **With replacement:** Bootstrap sample of n observations. **Worked solution:** Repeated original observations are allowed.

14. **Bootstrap statistic:** B bootstrap samples. **Worked solution:** Calculate B replicate estimates.

15. **Bootstrap SE:** Replicate estimates 1,2,3. **Worked solution:** Sample SD of replicates=1.

16. **Selection bias:** Only surviving stocks included. **Worked solution:** Survivorship bias; random sampling within survivors cannot repair missing delisted firms.

17. **Look-ahead bias:** Use future earnings in historical signal. **Worked solution:** Invalid information set; enforce as-of timestamps.

18. **Convenience sample:** Only most liquid stocks. **Worked solution:** Inference does not automatically generalize to all stocks.

19. **Time dependence:** Daily returns autocorrelated. **Worked solution:** iid SE may understate or overstate uncertainty.

20. **Block bootstrap:** Time-series dependent returns. **Worked solution:** Resample blocks instead of isolated dates when assumptions justify it.

21. **Point estimate:** Historical mean return 2%. **Worked solution:** 2% is an estimate, not a guarantee of future returns.

22. **Sampling distribution:** Repeated samples of size n. **Worked solution:** Distribution of an estimator across hypothetical samples.

23. **Missing observations:** Market closure or stale quote. **Worked solution:** Define eligibility and missingness before estimation.

24. **Estimator quality:** Bias, variance, MSE. **Worked solution:** MSE=variance+bias² when the required moments exist.

## 13. Troubleshooting and operational controls

| Symptom | Likely cause | Action |
|---|---|---|
| Different samples on each run | Seed not fixed | Record deterministic seed |
| Duplicate records in bootstrap | With-replacement sampling | Expected behavior |
| Duplicate records in SRS without replacement | Sampling implementation error | Verify unique sampled IDs |
| SE implausibly small | Dependence or data duplication | Check effective sample size and timestamps |
| Sample SD fails for n=1 | n−1 denominator | Report undefined |
| Larger sample still biased | Nonrandom inclusion | Correct the sampling frame |
| Bootstrap appears stable but unreliable | Tiny or biased original sample | Review data design and assumptions |
| Historical average interpreted as forecast | Misuse of estimator | State inferential limitations |
