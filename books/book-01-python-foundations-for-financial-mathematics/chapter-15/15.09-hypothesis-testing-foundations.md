## Learning objectives

Formulate null and alternative hypotheses; understand significance levels, p-values, Type I and II errors, power, and effect sizes; compute a one-sample t statistic and exact sign-test p-value; distinguish normal and Student-t reference distributions; and recognize financial-data inference pitfalls.

## 1. Why hypothesis tests matter

A financial analyst may ask whether a strategy's average return differs from zero, whether a trading signal has predictive value, or whether an operational change affects execution slippage. Hypothesis testing supplies a decision framework for assessing evidence against a precisely stated **null hypothesis**.

A test does not prove a hypothesis, guarantee a future effect, or replace economic judgment. Financial returns frequently violate simple independent, identically distributed (iid) assumptions.

## 2. Null and alternative hypotheses

For a population mean return μ:

- **H₀: μ=0** — the null hypothesis.
- **H₁: μ≠0** — a two-sided alternative.
- **H₁: μ>0** — a one-sided positive alternative.
- **H₁: μ<0** — a one-sided negative alternative.

The alternative and direction must be chosen **before** examining the same dataset. Switching from two-sided to one-sided after seeing the sign of the result invalidates nominal inference.

## 3. Test statistics and reference distributions

A test statistic measures the discrepancy between data and a null model. Its distribution under H₀ provides the reference for assessing extremeness.

For n observations, with sample mean x̄ and sample standard deviation s:

**t = (x̄−μ₀)/(s/√n)**, for n≥2 and s>0.

Under independent normal observations with unknown variance, the statistic has a Student's t distribution with **n−1 degrees of freedom** under H₀.

For larger samples, asymptotic approximations may be available under appropriate conditions. Do not substitute a normal p-value for a small-sample t p-value without justification.

## 4. Worked one-sample t statistic

Suppose the hypothetical daily returns are **[.01,.02,.03]** and H₀: μ=0.

- Sample mean x̄=.02.
- Sample SD s=.01.
- Estimated standard error s/√3≈.00577350.
- Test statistic **t=.02/(.01/√3)=2√3≈3.4641**.
- Degrees of freedom **df=2**.

The statistic is not itself a p-value. For this example, a valid t test uses the Student's t distribution with 2 degrees of freedom, **not** a standard normal reference distribution.

With n=3, the test has limited evidential strength and depends heavily on the normal iid model.

## 5. p-values: correct interpretation

For a specified test and null model, a **p-value** is the probability, **assuming H₀ and the test assumptions hold**, of obtaining a test statistic at least as extreme as the observed statistic under the chosen alternative.

A p-value is **not**:

- The probability that H₀ is true.
- The probability the result occurred by chance.
- A measure of economic importance.
- The probability a trading strategy will remain profitable.
- Proof that an alternative is correct.

For a standard normal Z, a two-sided p-value is:

**p = 2[1−Φ(|z|)] = erfc(|z|/√2)**.

At z≈1.96, p≈.05. This formula is for a **normal-reference** test, not a generic Student-t statistic.

## 6. Significance level and decisions

The significance level α is a predeclared Type I error rate for a valid testing procedure. Common examples include α=.05 and α=.01.

With the rule **reject H₀ if p≤α**:

| p-value | α | Decision |
|---:|---:|---|
| .01 | .05 | Reject H₀ |
| .20 | .05 | Do not reject H₀ |
| .05 | .05 | Reject H₀ under the specified boundary rule |

**Do not reject** is not equivalent to **accept H₀** or prove no effect. It may reflect low power or noisy data.

## 7. Type I error, Type II error, and power

- **Type I error:** Reject a true null hypothesis (false positive).
- **Type II error:** Fail to reject a false null hypothesis (false negative).
- **Power:** Probability of rejecting H₀ when a specified alternative is true; power=1−β.

Power depends on effect size, variability, sample size, test design, and assumptions. A test with low power can miss an economically important effect.

## 8. Statistical versus economic significance

A tiny but statistically detectable return difference may be erased by fees, slippage, financing costs, taxes, or risk. Conversely, a large estimated effect with a very wide confidence interval may be too uncertain for deployment.

Report the **estimated effect**, **units**, **uncertainty interval**, **p-value**, **sample size**, **model assumptions**, and **economic decision threshold** together.

## 9. Exact two-sided sign test

The sign test assesses whether positive and negative deviations relative to a hypothesized median occur with equal probability under a suitable independent, continuous null model.

Procedure:

1. Compute differences xᵢ−m₀.
2. Discard exact zero differences.
3. Count n nonzero differences and k positive differences.
4. Under H₀, K follows Binomial(n,.5).
5. A common exact two-sided p-value is **min(1, 2P[K≤min(k,n−k)])**.

For five strictly positive differences:

**p=2×(1/2⁵)=.0625**.

At α=.05, this does not reject H₀, despite all five signs being positive. The sign test ignores magnitude and still requires an appropriate independence assumption.

## 10. Confidence intervals and tests

For compatible two-sided tests and confidence intervals built from the same model, rejecting H₀: μ=μ₀ at level α corresponds to μ₀ lying outside the matching 100(1−α)% confidence interval.

This equivalence is **not** automatic when mixing methods, such as a t test with a naive normal interval or a bootstrap percentile interval.

## 11. Multiple comparisons and research selection

Searching hundreds of strategies, time windows, and thresholds increases the risk of false discoveries. If 20 independent true null hypotheses are each tested at α=.05, the expected number of false rejections is **20×.05=1**. Independence is not required for that expectation, but it affects the probability distribution of the count.

Possible safeguards include prespecified hypotheses, holdout samples, out-of-sample testing, family-wise error controls, false-discovery-rate procedures, and transparent reporting of all attempted analyses.

## 12. Financial time-series limitations

| Risk | Consequence | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| Serial correlation | Miscalibrated iid standard errors | Use dependence-aware inference |
| Volatility clustering | Incorrect test reference distribution | Examine conditional heteroskedasticity |
| Heavy tails | Poor small-sample approximation | Robust methods and stress tests |
| Nonstationarity | Inference changes across regimes | Assess stability and windows |
| Survivorship bias | Biased estimate and p-value | Reconstruct point-in-time universe |
| Look-ahead bias | Artificial predictability | Enforce as-of timestamps |
| Data snooping | Inflated false discoveries | Track trials and use holdouts |
| Overlapping returns | Induced dependence | Adjust standard errors |

A small p-value does not validate a flawed dataset or test design.

## 13. Python reference implementation

Save as `hypothesis_testing.py`. The code computes a one-sample t statistic, a standard-normal two-sided p-value, an exact sign-test p-value, and an explicit decision rule. It includes **12 reference unit tests**.

**Important:** The normal p-value function is intentionally separate from the t statistic. A Student-t p-value requires the appropriate t-distribution CDF; do not pass a small-sample t statistic to the normal function and call the result a t-test p-value.

```python
import math
import statistics
import unittest

def finite_sample(values):
    if not isinstance(values,(list,tuple)) or not values:
        raise ValueError("nonempty sequence required")
    if any(type(x) not in (int,float) or not math.isfinite(x) for x in values):
        raise ValueError("finite numeric values required")
    return [float(x) for x in values]

def one_sample_t_statistic(values, null_mean=0.0):
    xs=finite_sample(values)
    if len(xs)<2 or type(null_mean) not in (int,float) or not math.isfinite(null_mean):
        raise ValueError("n >= 2 and finite null mean required")
    mean=math.fsum(xs)/len(xs)
    ss=math.fsum((x-mean)**2 for x in xs)
    if ss==0:
        raise ValueError("t statistic undefined for zero sample variance")
    sd=math.sqrt(ss/(len(xs)-1))
    return (mean-null_mean)/(sd/math.sqrt(len(xs)))

def normal_cdf(z):
    if type(z) not in (int,float) or not math.isfinite(z):
        raise ValueError("finite z required")
    return .5*math.erfc(-z/math.sqrt(2))

def normal_two_sided_p(z):
    if type(z) not in (int,float) or not math.isfinite(z):
        raise ValueError("finite z required")
    return math.erfc(abs(z)/math.sqrt(2))

def exact_sign_test_p(values, null_median=0.0):
    xs=finite_sample(values)
    if type(null_median) not in (int,float) or not math.isfinite(null_median):
        raise ValueError("finite null median required")
    diffs=[x-null_median for x in xs]
    positives=sum(d>0 for d in diffs)
    negatives=sum(d<0 for d in diffs)
    n=positives+negatives
    if n==0:
        raise ValueError("no nonzero differences")
    k=min(positives,negatives)
    tail=sum(math.comb(n,j) for j in range(k+1))/(2**n)
    return min(1.0,2*tail)

def decision(p_value,alpha=.05):
    if type(p_value) not in (int,float) or not math.isfinite(p_value) or not 0<=p_value<=1:
        raise ValueError("p-value in [0,1] required")
    if type(alpha) not in (int,float) or not math.isfinite(alpha) or not 0<alpha<1:
        raise ValueError("alpha in (0,1) required")
    return "reject H0" if p_value<=alpha else "do not reject H0"

class HypothesisTestingTests(unittest.TestCase):
    def test_01_t_statistic(self):
        self.assertAlmostEqual(one_sample_t_statistic([.01,.02,.03]),2*math.sqrt(3))
    def test_02_t_zero(self):
        self.assertAlmostEqual(one_sample_t_statistic([.01,.02,.03],.02),0)
    def test_03_normal_cdf_zero(self): self.assertAlmostEqual(normal_cdf(0),.5)
    def test_04_normal_p_zero(self): self.assertAlmostEqual(normal_two_sided_p(0),1)
    def test_05_normal_p_196(self): self.assertAlmostEqual(normal_two_sided_p(1.95996398454),.05,places=8)
    def test_06_normal_symmetry(self): self.assertAlmostEqual(normal_two_sided_p(-2),normal_two_sided_p(2))
    def test_07_sign_all_positive(self): self.assertAlmostEqual(exact_sign_test_p([1,2,3,4,5]),.0625)
    def test_08_sign_balanced(self): self.assertAlmostEqual(exact_sign_test_p([-1,-2,1,2]),1)
    def test_09_sign_ties_removed(self): self.assertAlmostEqual(exact_sign_test_p([0,1,2,3]),.25)
    def test_10_reject(self): self.assertEqual(decision(.01,.05),"reject H0")
    def test_11_do_not_reject(self): self.assertEqual(decision(.2,.05),"do not reject H0")
    def test_12_invalid(self):
        for f in (lambda:one_sample_t_statistic([1]),
                  lambda:one_sample_t_statistic([1,1,1]),
                  lambda:normal_two_sided_p(float("nan")),
                  lambda:exact_sign_test_p([0,0]),
                  lambda:decision(-.1),
                  lambda:decision(.1,0)):
            with self.assertRaises(ValueError):f()

if __name__=="__main__":
    returns=[.01,.02,.03]
    print("One-sample t statistic:",one_sample_t_statistic(returns))
    print("Illustrative normal two-sided p at z=1.96:",normal_two_sided_p(1.96))
    print("Exact sign-test p:",exact_sign_test_p([1,2,3,4,5]))
    unittest.main(verbosity=2)
```

## 15. Twenty-four worked exercises

1. **Null hypothesis:** Claim mean daily return is zero. **Worked solution:** H0: μ=0.

2. **Two-sided alternative:** Test whether mean differs from zero. **Worked solution:** H1: μ≠0.

3. **One-sided alternative:** Test positive mean return. **Worked solution:** H1: μ>0, chosen before data inspection.

4. **Significance level:** α=.05. **Worked solution:** Nominal Type I error probability 5% under a valid test.

5. **Sample mean:** 1%,2%,3%. **Worked solution:** x̄=.02.

6. **Sample SD:** Same sample. **Worked solution:** s=.01.

7. **Mean standard error:** Same sample. **Worked solution:** s/√3=.01/√3.

8. **t statistic:** H0: μ=0, same sample. **Worked solution:** t=.02/(.01/√3)=2√3≈3.4641.

9. **Degrees of freedom:** One-sample t, n=3. **Worked solution:** df=2.

10. **t versus z:** n=3, unknown variance. **Worked solution:** Use t distribution only if model assumptions hold; normal z p-value is not the t p-value.

11. **Normal p at zero:** z=0, two-sided. **Worked solution:** p=1.

12. **Normal p at 1.96:** z=1.96, two-sided. **Worked solution:** p≈.049996.

13. **Decision reject:** p=.01, α=.05. **Worked solution:** Reject H0.

14. **Decision not reject:** p=.20, α=.05. **Worked solution:** Do not reject H0; do not assert H0 is true.

15. **Boundary decision:** p=.05, α=.05. **Worked solution:** Reject under the predeclared p≤α rule.

16. **Type I error:** H0 true, reject H0. **Worked solution:** False positive.

17. **Type II error:** H1 true, fail to reject H0. **Worked solution:** False negative.

18. **Power:** Probability of rejecting false H0. **Worked solution:** Power=1−β under a specified alternative.

19. **Effect size:** Estimated mean .02. **Worked solution:** Economic significance requires costs, risk, and units, not p-value alone.

20. **Sign test:** Five strictly positive differences. **Worked solution:** Two-sided exact p=2/2⁵=.0625.

21. **Sign test ties:** [0,1,2,3] against zero. **Worked solution:** Discard zero; three positive, two-sided p=.25.

22. **Multiple tests:** 20 independent null tests at α=.05. **Worked solution:** Expected false rejections under all true nulls=1; dependence affects distribution.

23. **Look-ahead bias:** Select test after seeing return signs. **Worked solution:** Invalid nominal inference unless selection accounted for.

24. **Time dependence:** Autocorrelated daily returns. **Worked solution:** Naive iid t-test p-values may have wrong calibration.

## 16. Troubleshooting and operational checks

| Symptom | Likely cause | Action |
|---|---|---|
| Tiny p-value with tiny effect | Large sample or weak economic relevance | Report effect and costs |
| Normal and t p-values differ | Different null reference distributions | Match test to assumptions |
| p-value treated as P(H₀ true) | Interpretation error | Explain conditioning on H₀ |
| Failure to reject called proof of no effect | Low-power misinterpretation | Evaluate power and interval |
| Repeated strategy testing yields many winners | Multiple-comparison problem | Control selection and holdout leakage |
| Test statistic undefined | Zero variance or too few observations | Validate data and model |
| Strong result disappears out of sample | Overfitting or regime change | Validate prospectively |
| Returns overlap or autocorrelate | Incorrect iid calibration | Use dependence-aware methods |
