## Objectives

Apply known-value, boundary, invariant, metamorphic, tolerance, reproducibility, and review techniques to a deterministic financial model. Explain what each check establishes and what remains unverified.

Use the following model unless a problem states otherwise:

\[
P(F,r,T)=Fe^{-rT},\qquad
V=\sum_i C_i e^{-rt_i}.
\]

Rates are constant continuously compounded annual rates, times are nonnegative years, and cash flows are signed amounts in a common currency. Assume finite inputs and representable outputs. For numerical work, document the comparison rule and tolerance.

## Part A — Foundations

1. Explain the difference between software verification and model validation.
2. Define an independent known-value benchmark and give one example for \(P\).
3. Derive \(P(F,r,0)\).
4. Derive \(P(F,0,T)\).
5. Evaluate \(P(100,\ln 2,1)\).
6. Explain why a correct formula may still be unsuitable for a business use.
7. State a property that a function returning zero for every input would satisfy.
8. Give a known-value test that exposes that function.
9. Explain the role of a domain in a test specification.
10. Distinguish an authored test from an executed and passing test.

## Part B — Boundaries and invalid inputs

11. Specify whether a negative rate is admissible in the stated continuous model.
12. Specify whether a negative time is admissible under this contract.
13. Explain why NaN should be rejected for financial input.
14. Evaluate the empty cash-flow sum under the convention that it equals zero.
15. Define a test for a time just above zero and explain how to select a representable input.
16. For an annual effective rate model \(F/(1+r)^n\), state the domain of \(r\) when the contract requires a positive accumulation factor.
17. Derive the ordinary annuity value at zero rate for \(n\) payments of \(C\).
18. Explain why the annuity closed form has a removable singularity at zero rate.
19. Describe what should happen when a mathematically valid input causes numerical overflow.
20. Explain why underflow can be material even when no exception is raised.

## Part C — Invariants and metamorphic relations

21. Prove cash-flow linearity in \(F\) for fixed \(r,T\).
22. Show that splitting 100 into 40 and 60 at the same date preserves PV.
23. Explain whether splitting a payment across two different dates generally preserves PV.
24. State the constant-rate discount-factor composition relation.
25. Explain why a time-varying rate needs interval-specific factors.
26. Prove that increasing \(r\) decreases \(P\) for \(F>0,T>0\).
27. State the corresponding result for \(F<0,T>0\).
28. Explain the result when \(T=0\).
29. Describe a correct permutation test for a cash-flow portfolio.
30. Explain why feasibility of portfolio weights does not prove optimality.

## Part D — Numerical reliability

31. Compute absolute and relative error for actual 101 and reference 100.
32. Explain why relative error against a zero reference is undefined.
33. For the rule \(|a-b|\le\mathrm{atol}+\mathrm{rtol}|b|\), decide whether \(a=101,b=100,\mathrm{atol}=0,\mathrm{rtol}=0.01\) passes.
34. Explain how Python's math.isclose rule differs from the preceding asymmetric sum rule.
35. Give a reason to use an absolute tolerance near zero.
36. Explain cancellation in computing \(e^x-1\) for small \(x\), and name a stable alternative.
37. Explain why a small linear-system residual does not guarantee a small solution error.
38. List two factors that can affect floating-point summation order.
39. Explain why tolerances should not be widened solely to make a test pass.
40. Identify the units of an absolute tolerance on dollar-valued PV.

## Part E — Evidence, findings, and decisions

41. List five items needed to reconstruct a test execution.
42. Explain the limits of a random seed for reproducibility.
43. Explain what a SHA-256 digest can establish and what it cannot.
44. Explain why a source commit alone may not identify all executed code.
45. Describe a safe approach for recording evidence involving sensitive data.
46. List the essential fields of a review finding.
47. Distinguish remediation from authorized risk acceptance.
48. Explain why missing evidence differs from a failed test.
49. List three prerequisites for an illustrative model release gate.
50. Explain why an automated checklist cannot itself grant business approval.

## Part F — Applied validation project

51. Build a validation table for \(V\) with at least two independent known values, three boundary cases, and three properties. Include assumptions and tolerances.
52. A developer computes \(100(1.25)=125\) for the present value of a one-period payment under an annual effective rate of 25%. Write a finding with expected value, actual value, impact, remediation, and verification plan.
53. Design a reproducible synthetic test run: record source version, input, environment, command, actual result, exit code, and reviewer status.
54. Construct a scenario in which a metamorphic check passes for an incorrect implementation. Add an independent test that detects the error.
55. Propose a release decision when tests pass but independent mathematical review is pending. Explain the remaining gate.

## Twelve-step lab

1. Write the valuation contract, including units and exclusions.
2. Implement a deterministic cash-flow valuation.
3. Derive independent analytic test values.
4. Test zero rate and zero horizon.
5. Test negative time and non-finite inputs.
6. Check a same-date split.
7. Check paired permutation.
8. Check scaling and conditional rate monotonicity.
9. Document the comparison rule and tolerance.
10. Record the exact source, inputs and environment when running.
11. Record findings and independent review status.
12. Prepare a release recommendation without implying approval.
