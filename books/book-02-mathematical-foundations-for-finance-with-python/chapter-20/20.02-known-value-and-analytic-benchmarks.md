## Learning objectives

Construct independently derived known-value tests, identify analytical oracles, distinguish exact identities from approximate numerical checks, validate financial rate and timing conventions, and recognize ill-conditioned or circular benchmarks.

## 1. What makes a benchmark trustworthy?

A **known-value benchmark** is an input-output pair whose expected result follows from a transparent mathematical argument. An **analytic benchmark** derives the result from a closed-form solution or identity. An **oracle** is the mechanism used to decide whether an implementation is correct.

An oracle should be independent of the implementation being checked. Copying the same faulty expression into the test merely creates correlated errors. Useful sources include hand derivations, algebraically distinct formulas, published mathematical identities, high-precision calculations, and simplified cases with obvious outputs.

An exact mathematical result and its floating-point representation are not identical. Always distinguish the symbolic target from the numerical comparison tolerance.

## 2. Benchmark design: a hierarchy

1. **Degenerate cases:** zero rate, zero horizon, zero payment, identity matrices.
2. **Closed-form cases:** exponential growth, level annuities, linear ODEs.
3. **Structural identities:** linearity, symmetry, conservation, inverse relations.
4. **Independent algorithms:** compare analytical solutions with numerical integration or a separately derived recurrence.
5. **High-precision references:** use independent precision settings and document rounding.
6. **Domain-reviewed examples:** verify timing, units, financial conventions, and intended use.

Prefer simple benchmarks first: their failure modes are easier to diagnose.

## 3. Present-value benchmark

For a deterministic cash flow \(F\) at \(T\ge0\) years under constant continuously compounded rate \(r\),

\[
PV=Fe^{-rT}.
\]

Reference cases include \(PV(F,0,T)=F\), \(PV(F,r,0)=F\), and \(PV(0,r,T)=0\).

For \(F=1000\), \(r=0.05\), \(T=2\), the independent reference is \(1000e^{-0.1}\). A test should document that \(r\) is continuously compounded rather than nominal.

## 4. Rate-conversion benchmark

A nominal annual rate \(j\), compounded \(m\) times annually, corresponds to continuous rate

\[
r_c=m\log(1+j/m),\qquad j=m\operatorname{expm1}(r_c/m),
\]

provided \(1+j/m>0\). For a full year,

\[
(1+j/m)^m=e^{r_c}.
\]

A robust test checks both the independently derived equality and a round trip. A round trip alone can miss two mutually compensating bugs.

Use \`log1p\` and \`expm1\` near zero.

## 5. Geometric cash-flow benchmark

For \(N\) equal end-of-period payments \(C\) and effective periodic discount rate \(i>-1\),

\[
PV=C\sum_{k=1}^{N}(1+i)^{-k}.
\]

For \(i\ne0\), this equals

\[
\boxed{PV=C\frac{1-(1+i)^{-N}}{i}},
\]

while \(i=0\) gives \(PV=CN\). The explicit discounted sum and closed-form expression are algebraically independent reference routes. Near zero, evaluate the numerator with \`-expm1(-N*log1p(i))\` to reduce cancellation.

Payment timing matters: a beginning-of-period annuity differs by a factor \(1+i\) when comparing the same \(N\) payments.

## 6. Continuous cash-flow benchmark

For constant continuous inflow \(u\) and constant continuous rate \(r\),

\[
A'=rA+u,\qquad A(0)=A_0.
\]

The exact balance is

\[
A(T)=A_0e^{rT}+u\frac{\operatorname{expm1}(rT)}{r}
\]

when \(r\ne0\), and \(A_0+uT\) when \(r=0\). Compare this analytic result with a numerical solver, using a tolerance justified by solver order, step size, and roundoff.

## 7. Depletion-time benchmark

For \(A'=rA-w\) with \(A_0,w>0\), the zero-rate depletion time is \(A_0/w\). For \(r\ne0\), when a positive finite crossing exists,

\[
t_*=-\frac{\log(1-rA_0/w)}{r}.
\]

For \(r>0\), a finite crossing exists only if \(w>rA_0\). When \(r<0\), with \(A_0,w>0\), a finite crossing exists. Check the residual \(A(t_*)\) and, independently, the analytic event-time formula. A small residual does not establish correct event ordering when jumps are present.

## 8. Linear-algebra benchmark

For a nonsingular \(2\times2\) matrix

\[
M=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\c&d\end{pmatrix},\quad \det M=ad-bc\ne0,
\]

the analytic inverse is

\[
M^{-1}=\frac1{ad-bc}\begin{pmatrix}d&-b\\-c&a\end{pmatrix}.
\]

For \(M=\begin{pmatrix}2&1\\1&3\end{pmatrix}\) and \(y=(5,7)^T\), the exact solution is \(x=(8/5,9/5)^T\). This provides a small independent reference for later matrix solvers.

Residual checks \(Mx-y\) supplement, but do not replace, a known solution; ill-conditioning can make small residuals misleading.

## 9. Tolerances and benchmark limitations

Use a comparison rule such as

\[
|x_{\mathrm{actual}}-x_{\mathrm{reference}}|
\le \mathrm{atol}+\mathrm{rtol}|x_{\mathrm{reference}}|.
\]

Choose tolerances based on expected numerical error, not to force a pass. Near zero, absolute tolerance matters. For very large magnitudes, relative tolerance is often more informative.

A benchmark may fail because of inconsistent units, rate conventions, rounding, day-count rules, a poor numerical algorithm, or a mistaken oracle. Investigate the reference as carefully as the implementation.

## 10. Twenty worked exercises with solutions

**1.** Define a known-value benchmark.  
*Solution:* An input-output pair with an independently justified expected result.

**2.** Why is comparing a function with itself invalid?  
*Solution:* It cannot detect a shared implementation error.

**3.** Give the zero-rate present value.  
*Solution:* \(PV(F,0,T)=F\).

**4.** Give the zero-horizon present value.  
*Solution:* \(PV(F,r,0)=F\).

**5.** Find \(PV\) for \(F=1000,r=0.05,T=2\).  
*Solution:* \(1000e^{-0.1}\).

**6.** Convert nominal \(j\) to continuous \(r_c\).  
*Solution:* \(r_c=m\log(1+j/m)\).

**7.** Convert continuous \(r_c\) to nominal \(j\).  
*Solution:* \(j=m\operatorname{expm1}(r_c/m)\).

**8.** Why is \`log1p\` helpful?  
*Solution:* It accurately computes \(\log(1+x)\) for small \(x\).

**9.** Write an end-of-period annuity as a sum.  
*Solution:* \(C\sum_{k=1}^{N}(1+i)^{-k}\).

**10.** Give the annuity closed form for \(i\ne0\).  
*Solution:* \(C[1-(1+i)^{-N}]/i\).

**11.** Give its zero-rate limit.  
*Solution:* \(CN\).

**12.** How does beginning-of-period payment timing change the annuity?  
*Solution:* Multiply the otherwise identical end-of-period value by \(1+i\).

**13.** State the continuous-contribution ODE.  
*Solution:* \(A'=rA+u\).

**14.** State its zero-rate solution.  
*Solution:* \(A(T)=A_0+uT\).

**15.** Give the zero-rate depletion time.  
*Solution:* \(A_0/w\).

**16.** When does positive-rate depletion occur?  
*Solution:* When \(w>rA_0\).

**17.** Find the determinant of \(\begin{pmatrix}2&1\\1&3\end{pmatrix}\).  
*Solution:* \(2(3)-1(1)=5\).

**18.** Solve \(2x+y=5,\ x+3y=7\).  
*Solution:* \(x=8/5,\ y=9/5\).

**19.** State a mixed tolerance.  
*Solution:* \(|a-b|\le\mathrm{atol}+\mathrm{rtol}|b|\).

## 12. Applied problems

**A. Annuity oracle:** Compare a 24-payment monthly annuity using explicit discounted payments and a closed-form expression at a 0.5% effective monthly rate.

**B. Rate identity:** Convert a nominal 6% rate compounded monthly to a continuous rate, then compare one-year balances.

**C. Event benchmark:** Derive depletion time for \(A_0=1000,r=0.04,w=100\); explain why a deposit at a later time cannot reverse an earlier terminal event.

**D. Independent linear solver:** Derive a two-equation solution by elimination and compare with a numerical solver.

**E. Oracle audit:** Explain how a benchmark might be wrong because of a beginning-versus-end payment convention.
