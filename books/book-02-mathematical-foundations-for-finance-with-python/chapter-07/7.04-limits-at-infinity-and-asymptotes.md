## Learning objectives

Distinguish limits as an input tends to infinity from unbounded limits at finite inputs; derive horizontal and vertical asymptotes; compare rational function growth; and evaluate long-run financial recurrences and perpetuities.

## Limits at infinity

The statement \(\lim_{x\to\infty}f(x)=L\) means that for any \(\varepsilon>0\), a threshold \(M\) exists such that \(x>M\) implies \(|f(x)-L|<\varepsilon\). A corresponding definition applies as \(x\to-\infty\).

For \(f(x)=3+8/x\), the limit at either infinity is 3, giving the horizontal asymptote \(y=3\). A horizontal asymptote can be crossed: it describes long-run behavior, not an impenetrable boundary.

## Rational functions

Divide by the highest power in the denominator. For example,

\[
\frac{4x^2+3x+1}{2x^2+7}=\frac{4+3/x+1/x^2}{2+7/x^2}\to2
\]

as \(x\to\pm\infty\). Lower numerator degree gives limit zero; equal degrees give the leading-coefficient ratio; higher numerator degree generally yields no finite horizontal asymptote. Polynomial division can reveal slant or higher-degree asymptotes.

## Vertical asymptotes and removable holes

The function \(1/(x-2)\) tends to \(-\infty\) from the left of 2 and \(+\infty\) from the right: \(x=2\) is a vertical asymptote. By contrast, \((x^2-4)/(x-2)=x+2\) for \(x\ne2\), so it has a removable hole at 2, not a vertical asymptote.

## Growth hierarchy

As \(x\to+\infty\), \(\log x/x^p\to0\) for \(p>0\), and \(x^p/a^x\to0\) for \(a>1\). Exponential growth eventually dominates polynomial growth, which eventually dominates logarithmic growth. The statement is asymptotic and need not describe short-horizon observations.

## Financial recurrence and its fixed point

For \(B_{n+1}=qB_n+c\), repeated substitution yields

\[
B_n=q^nB_0+c\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}q^k.
\]

For \(q\ne1\), this becomes

\[
B_n=B_*+q^n(B_0-B_*),\quad B_*=\frac{c}{1-q}.
\]

If \(0\le q<1\), then \(q^n\to0\) and \(B_n\to B_*\). With \(q=0.9,c=50\), the limit is 500 regardless of the finite initial balance. For \(q=1\), \(B_n=B_0+nc\). For \(q>1\), the fixed point is unstable: only starting exactly at \(B_*\) yields a constant sequence.

## Growing perpetuity and tail

If \(r>g>-1\), define \(q=(1+g)/(1+r)\in(0,1)\). The first \(n\) payments have

\[
PV_n=\frac{C_1}{r-g}(1-q^n),\qquad
T_n=\frac{C_1}{r-g}q^n.
\]

Hence \(PV_n+T_n=C_1/(r-g)\) and the tail tends to zero. A nonzero infinite stream with \(r\le g\) is not convergent.

## Numerical verification

For a rational function, normalize before computing with very large inputs to avoid overflow. For the recurrence, the fixed-point form exposes convergence. Sampled values support intuition but do not prove an asymptotic limit.

## Exercises with answers

1. Evaluate \(\lim_{x\to\infty}(3+8/x)\). **Answer:** 3.
2. Evaluate \(\lim_{x\to-\infty}(4x^2+3x+1)/(2x^2+7)\). **Answer:** 2.
3. Is \(x=2\) a vertical asymptote of \(1/(x-2)\)? **Answer:** Yes.
4. Is \(x=2\) a vertical asymptote of \((x^2-4)/(x-2)\)? **Answer:** No, it is removable.
5. Find \(\lim_{x\to\infty}\log(x)/x\). **Answer:** 0.
6. Find the steady state of \(B_{n+1}=0.9B_n+50\). **Answer:** 500.
7. Does \(B_{n+1}=1.05B_n+50\) converge for arbitrary \(B_0\)? **Answer:** No; only the exact fixed point \(-1000\) is constant.
8. Find the growing perpetuity PV for \(C_1=100,r=0.06,g=0.02\). **Answer:** 2500.
9. Find its time-zero tail after ten payments. **Answer:** \(2500(1.02/1.06)^{10}\).
10. Explain why large-input numerical samples do not prove a limit. **Answer:** Finite observations cannot establish behavior for all sufficiently large inputs.
