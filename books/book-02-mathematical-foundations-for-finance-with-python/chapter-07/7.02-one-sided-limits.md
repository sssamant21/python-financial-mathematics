## Learning objectives

Interpret left-hand and right-hand limits, determine whether a two-sided limit exists, distinguish function values from nearby behavior, evaluate piecewise functions at thresholds, and test numerical evidence against mathematical reasoning.

## 1. Direction matters

The **left-hand limit**

\[
\lim_{x\to a^-}f(x)=L_-
\]

describes the behavior of \(f(x)\) as \(x\) approaches \(a\) using inputs strictly less than \(a\). The **right-hand limit**

\[
\lim_{x\to a^+}f(x)=L_+
\]

uses inputs strictly greater than \(a\).

The two-sided finite limit exists and equals \(L\) precisely when both one-sided finite limits exist and equal \(L\):

\[
\boxed{\lim_{x\to a}f(x)=L\iff L_-=L_+=L.}
\]

The value \(f(a)\) may differ from both limits, or may not be defined.

## 2. A step function at a threshold

Consider

\[
f(x)=
\begin{cases}
2,&x<5,\\
7,&x\ge5.
\end{cases}
\]

The left-hand limit at \(5\) is \(2\); the right-hand limit is \(7\). Since they differ, the two-sided limit does not exist. The actual value is \(f(5)=7\).

This distinction is essential in fee schedules, eligibility rules, and contractual thresholds.

## 3. A piecewise function whose sides agree

Let

\[
g(x)=
\begin{cases}
x+1,&x<2,\\
3x-3,&x\ge2.
\end{cases}
\]

Both one-sided limits at \(2\) equal \(3\), so the two-sided limit exists and equals \(3\). Here \(g(2)=3\) as well.

If we changed only \(g(2)\) to \(100\), the limit would remain \(3\), although the function would no longer be continuous at \(2\).

## 4. A function with an isolated hole

For

\[
h(x)=\frac{x^2-9}{x-3},\qquad x\ne3,
\]

factor the numerator: \(h(x)=x+3\) away from \(3\). Thus both one-sided limits are \(6\), and the two-sided limit is \(6\), even though \(h(3)\) is undefined.

## 5. A financial example: threshold-based fees

Suppose a hypothetical transaction fee in dollars depends on transaction amount \(A\):

\[
F(A)=
\begin{cases}
5,&0\le A<1000,\\
10,&A\ge1000.
\end{cases}
\]

At \(A=1000\),

\[
\lim_{A\to1000^-}F(A)=5,\qquad
\lim_{A\to1000^+}F(A)=10.
\]

The fee at the threshold is \(10\), but no two-sided limit exists there. A numerical optimization algorithm assuming smooth fees could make incorrect inferences near this threshold.

This is an illustrative fee model, not a claim about a real provider.

## 6. One-sided limits at domain boundaries

For \(f(x)=\sqrt{x}\), the real-valued domain is \(x\ge0\). The right-hand limit at \(0\) is \(0\). A left-hand limit in the real domain is not defined because there are no domain points to the left of zero arbitrarily near zero.

At an endpoint, a one-sided limit is often the relevant concept. Do not assert a two-sided real limit merely because the available side converges.

## 7. Formal one-sided definitions

The statement \(\lim_{x\to a^-}f(x)=L\) means that for every \(\varepsilon>0\), some \(\delta>0\) ensures

\[
a-\delta<x<a\Longrightarrow |f(x)-L|<\varepsilon.
\]

For the right-hand limit, use \(a<x<a+\delta\).

For the step function above, all values sufficiently close to \(5\) on the left are exactly \(2\), so the left-hand limit is \(2\). On the right they are exactly \(7\), giving the right-hand limit \(7\).

## 8. Numerical investigation

Sample from both sides using \(a\pm h\) for decreasing positive \(h\). Avoid evaluating exactly at \(a\) when investigating a limit, and ensure that floating-point rounding has not made \(a-h=a\) or \(a+h=a\).

A finite table can suggest a limit but cannot prove it. Algebraic simplification and piecewise definitions provide the mathematical justification in these examples.

**3.** Does the two-sided limit in Exercise 2 exist?

**4.** Find the one-sided limits of \(g(x)=x+1\) for \(x<2\), and \(3x-3\) for \(x\ge2\), at \(2\).

**5.** Find the limit of \((x^2-9)/(x-3)\) as \(x\to3\).

**6.** Explain why changing only the value of a function at \(x=a\) need not change its limit there.

**7.** Find the right-hand limit of \(\sqrt{x}\) at zero in the real domain.

**8.** A fee jumps from 5 to 10 at transaction amount 1000. What does the jump imply about continuity and the two-sided limit?
