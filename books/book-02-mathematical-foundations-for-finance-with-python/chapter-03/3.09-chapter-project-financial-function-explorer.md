## Project overview

Build a **Financial Function Explorer** that converts a documented financial model into exact graph coordinates, evaluates break-even quantities, classifies monotonicity and discrete curvature, and identifies feasible profit extrema. The project integrates domain and range (3.1), graph interpretation (3.2), function composition (3.3), inverse reasoning (3.4), piecewise thresholds (3.5), monotonicity and bounds (3.6), convexity and concavity (3.7), and explicit specifications (3.8).

**Business scenario:** A hypothetical business sells whole items. It has a fixed unit selling price, a fixed variable cost per item, and a fixed operating cost. If quantity exceeds a specified threshold, an additional fixed expansion cost applies. All currency values and model parameters are synthetic.

## 1. Model and contracts

Let $q\in\{0,1,\ldots,Q\}$, where $Q$ is a nonnegative integer capacity. Let $p,v,F,E\ge0$ be price, variable cost, base fixed cost, and optional expansion cost. Let $T\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}$ be the expansion threshold, when enabled.

Revenue:

$$R(q)=pq.$$

Cost:

$$C(q)=F+vq+E\mathbf 1_{\{q>T\}},$$

where the indicator is 1 if the condition holds and 0 otherwise. Without expansion, set $E=0$.

Profit is a **composition of transformations**:

$$\Pi(q)=R(q)-C(q).$$

The explorer reports $(q,R(q),C(q),\Pi(q))$ for each feasible quantity. Currency outputs use exact rational values, not floats. The threshold convention is explicit: extra cost begins at $q=T+1$, **not** at $q=T$.

**Inputs:** Nonnegative exact rational price and costs; nonnegative whole-unit capacity and optional threshold. **Outputs:** Exact coordinates, extrema and their quantities, exact zero-profit quantities, nonnegative-profit quantities, first differences, monotonicity classifications, and finite-grid curvature classifications.

**Limitations:** The model does not incorporate demand elasticity, taxes, refunds, variable unit prices, inflation, financing, uncertainty, or rounding rules. The quantity domain is finite. This is an educational model, not a forecast.

## 2. Milestone A — Baseline model

Choose $p=25$, $v=15$, $F=40$, and $Q=8$, with no expansion cost. Then

$$\Pi(q)=10q-40.$$

The exact graph coordinates include:

| Quantity | Revenue | Cost | Profit |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 0 | 0 | 40 | -40 |
| 4 | 100 | 100 | 0 |
| 8 | 200 | 160 | 40 |

The unique feasible break-even quantity is 4. The minimum profit is -40 at zero quantity, and the maximum is 40 at eight items. Profit increases by exactly 10 currency units per additional item. Since its first differences are constant, the sequence is both discretely convex and discretely concave.

**Independent algebraic check:** $q=(\Pi+40)/10$; target profit zero implies $q=4$, consistent with the explorer's reported zero-profit quantity.

## 3. Milestone B — Capacity expansion threshold

Add $T=5$ and $E=30$. Now

$$C(q)=40+15q+\begin{cases}0,&q\le5,\\30,&q>5.\end{cases}$$

Consequently,

$$\Pi(q)=10q-40-\begin{cases}0,&q\le5,\\30,&q>5.\end{cases}$$

At $q=5$, profit is 10. At $q=6$, profit is -10. The one-item profit difference is $-20$, not the usual $+10$: an extra contribution of 10 triggers 30 in expansion cost.

At $q=7$, profit returns to zero. Thus the model has **two feasible break-even quantities**, 4 and 7. This shows why applying a single linear inverse formula across a piecewise function is incorrect.

For $Q=8$, the maximum profit is 10 at both $q=5$ and $q=8$. The finite-grid profit sequence is neither nondecreasing nor globally discretely convex or concave.

## 4. Milestone C — Generate and inspect graph-ready coordinates

Use [section_3_09.py](code/section_3_09.py) to generate complete exact coordinate tables. Each row is a point in the financial graphs. Plot quantity on the horizontal axis and revenue, cost, or profit on the vertical axis, with explicit units.

Because quantity is whole-valued, use discrete markers. Connecting the points visually is optional and must be labeled as interpolation rather than a claim that fractional quantities are feasible.

For each row, check the invariant

$$R(q)-C(q)=\Pi(q).$$

For each neighboring pair, verify

$$\Delta\Pi(q)=\Pi(q+1)-\Pi(q).$$

The implementation computes these differences and reports finite-domain classifications.

## 7. Independent exercises

**1.** Recompute the baseline break-even quantity if fixed cost increases to 50 and all other baseline parameters remain unchanged.

**2.** With the expansion model, compute profit at quantities 5, 6, 7, and 8.

**3.** Why does the expansion model have two zero-profit quantities?

**4.** What is the maximum feasible profit when the expansion model's capacity is reduced from 8 to 6?

**5.** What is the mathematical difference between reporting the maximum profit and reporting the quantity or quantities at which it occurs?

**6.** Why is testing the expansion threshold itself insufficient?

**7.** Explain why passing the explorer's reference checks does not establish that the business's future revenue will match the model.

### Worked solutions

1. $\Pi(q)=10q-50$; break-even $q=5$.
2. Profits: 10, -10, 0, and 10.
3. Profit reaches zero at 4 before expansion; the additional fixed cost creates a loss at 6, and profit reaches zero again at 7.
4. At capacity 6, the maximum is 10 at quantity 5.
5. The maximum is an output (currency); an argmax is an input (items). Several quantities may tie.
6. The cost jump occurs at the first quantity **above** the threshold, so both sides must be tested.
7. Tests establish behavior under the assumed equations; they do not validate real-world demand, costs, or forecasts.
