## Learning objectives

Define the Jacobian of a vector-valued function, distinguish its rows from scalar gradients, check matrix dimensions, use the Jacobian for first-order scenario changes, apply the vector chain rule, and validate financial sensitivities against independent calculations.

## 1. From a gradient to a Jacobian

For a differentiable vector-valued function

\[
F:\mathbb R^n\to\mathbb R^m,\qquad
F(x)=\begin{bmatrix}F_1(x)\\\vdots\\F_m(x)\end{bmatrix},
\]

the **Jacobian** is the \(m\times n\) matrix

\[
\boxed{J_F(x)=\begin{bmatrix}
\partial F_1/\partial x_1&\cdots&\partial F_1/\partial x_n\\
\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
\partial F_m/\partial x_1&\cdots&\partial F_m/\partial x_n
\end{bmatrix}}.
\]

Each row is the transpose of one output's gradient. For \(m=1\), the Jacobian is a row vector while the gradient is conventionally a column vector.

## 2. Linear approximation

For a small displacement \(\Delta x\in\mathbb R^n\),

\[
\boxed{F(x+\Delta x)-F(x)\approx J_F(x)\Delta x}.
\]

The product is an \(m\)-component vector. It estimates **all** output changes under one input scenario. A Jacobian is local: its accuracy deteriorates for sufficiently large shocks or highly nonlinear models.

## 3. Worked algebra example

Let

\[
F(x,y)=\begin{bmatrix}x^2+y\\xy^2\end{bmatrix}.
\]

Then

\[
J_F(x,y)=\begin{bmatrix}2x&1\\y^2&2xy\end{bmatrix}.
\]

At \((x,y)=(2,3)\),

\[
F(2,3)=(7,18)^\mathsf T,\qquad
J_F(2,3)=\begin{bmatrix}4&1\\9&12\end{bmatrix}.
\]

For \(\Delta x=(0.1,-0.2)^\mathsf T\), the first-order output change is

\[
J_F\Delta x=(0.2,-1.5)^\mathsf T.
\]

The exact change is

\[
F(2.1,2.8)-F(2,3)
=(0.21,-1.536)^\mathsf T.
\]

The discrepancy reflects curvature and interaction terms.

## 4. Two financial outputs from two inputs

Consider two hypothetical continuously compounded future-value instruments with different principals and horizons:

\[
G(r,t)=\begin{bmatrix}
1000e^{rt}\\
500e^{2rt}
\end{bmatrix}.
\]

The second instrument's exponent is \(2rt\), which can represent a doubled rate-time exposure for this educational example. It is **not** automatically a realistic bond model.

Its Jacobian is

\[
\boxed{J_G(r,t)=\begin{bmatrix}
1000t e^{rt}&1000r e^{rt}\\
1000t e^{2rt}&1000r e^{2rt}
\end{bmatrix}}.
\]

For the second output, \(\partial(500e^{2rt})/\partial r=1000t e^{2rt}\) and \(\partial/\partial t=1000r e^{2rt}\).

At \(r=0.05,t=2\),

\[
G=\begin{bmatrix}1000e^{0.1}\\500e^{0.2}\end{bmatrix},
\qquad
J_G=\begin{bmatrix}
2000e^{0.1}&50e^{0.1}\\
2000e^{0.2}&50e^{0.2}
\end{bmatrix}.
\]

Both outputs have proportional sensitivities because they depend on the same product \(rt\). The Jacobian has rank one at this point, and in fact throughout the stated domain. Consequently, local changes in \(r\) and \(t\) cannot be uniquely identified from these two output changes alone.

## 5. A rate-and-time scenario

For \(\Delta r=0.01\) and \(\Delta t=0.5\), the Jacobian approximation is

\[
J_G\begin{bmatrix}0.01\\0.5\end{bmatrix}
=\begin{bmatrix}
45e^{0.1}\\45e^{0.2}
\end{bmatrix}.
\]

The exact change is

\[
\begin{bmatrix}
1000(e^{0.15}-e^{0.1})\\
500(e^{0.30}-e^{0.20})
\end{bmatrix}.
\]

Compare each approximation with its own exact revaluation. The first-order estimate is not an exact portfolio P&L forecast.

## 6. Vector chain rule

If \(x=x(s)\in\mathbb R^n\), then

\[
\boxed{\frac{dF(x(s))}{ds}=J_F(x(s))\,x'(s)}.
\]

For a composition \(H=F\circ g\) with \(g:\mathbb R^k\to\mathbb R^n\),

\[
\boxed{J_H(z)=J_F(g(z))J_g(z)}.
\]

The matrix dimensions must align: \((m\times n)(n\times k)=(m\times k)\). This provides a disciplined way to propagate sensitivities through a multi-stage financial model.

## 7. Units and interpretation

A Jacobian entry has units of output \(i\) per unit of input \(j\). Different rows may represent different currencies or risk metrics, and columns may involve rates, time, or prices. Always label inputs and outputs, distinguish decimal-rate shocks from percentage-point shocks, and document which calibration parameters remain fixed.

A rank-deficient Jacobian can indicate that different inputs produce indistinguishable first-order output changes. That is a **local identifiability** issue, not necessarily a software defect.

## 8. Numerical validation

A centered finite-difference approximation for column \(j\) is

\[
J_F(x)_{:,j}\approx
\frac{F(x+h e_j)-F(x-h e_j)}{2h}.
\]

Use separate step sizes suited to each input's units and scale. Reject invalid dimensions and nonfinite values. Check every output component independently, and avoid domain-violating perturbations.

## 10. Twenty worked exercises

**1.** What is a Jacobian?  
*Answer:* The matrix of first partial derivatives of a vector-valued function.

**2.** What is its shape for \(F:\mathbb R^3\to\mathbb R^2\)?  
*Answer:* \(2\times3\).

**3.** What does Jacobian row \(i\) represent?  
*Answer:* The transposed gradient of output \(F_i\).

**4.** Write the linearization of \(F\).  
*Answer:* \(\Delta F\approx J_F\Delta x\).

**5.** Differentiate \(F_1=x^2+y\) with respect to \(x\).  
*Answer:* \(2x\).

**6.** Differentiate \(F_1\) with respect to \(y\).  
*Answer:* \(1\).

**7.** Differentiate \(F_2=xy^2\) with respect to \(x\).  
*Answer:* \(y^2\).

**8.** Differentiate \(F_2\) with respect to \(y\).  
*Answer:* \(2xy\).

**9.** Evaluate \(J_F(2,3)\).  
*Answer:* \(\begin{bmatrix}4&1\\9&12\end{bmatrix}\).

**10.** Compute \(J_F(2,3)(0.1,-0.2)^\mathsf T\).  
*Answer:* \((0.2,-1.5)^\mathsf T\).

**11.** Find the exact change in the first algebraic output.  
*Answer:* \(0.21\).

**12.** Find the exact change in the second algebraic output.  
*Answer:* \(-1.536\).

**13.** What are the two financial outputs?  
*Answer:* \(1000e^{rt}\) and \(500e^{2rt}\).

**14.** Find \(\partial G_1/\partial r\).  
*Answer:* \(1000te^{rt}\).

**15.** Find \(\partial G_1/\partial t\).  
*Answer:* \(1000re^{rt}\).

**16.** Find \(\partial G_2/\partial r\).  
*Answer:* \(1000te^{2rt}\).

**17.** Find \(\partial G_2/\partial t\).  
*Answer:* \(1000re^{2rt}\).

**18.** What is the rank of \(J_G\) at \(r=0.05,t=2\)?  
*Answer:* One.

**19.** State the vector chain rule.  
*Answer:* \(dF/ds=J_F(x(s))x'(s)\).
