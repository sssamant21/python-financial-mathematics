## Learning objectives

Derive the total differential of a scalar multivariable function, use the multivariable chain rule for dependent inputs, distinguish a differential approximation from an exact finite change, propagate financial sensitivities, and document the assumptions behind rate and time shocks.

## 1. Total differentials

For a differentiable function \(f(x,y)\), its differential at a point is

\[
\boxed{df=f_x\,dx+f_y\,dy}.
\]

For \(f(x_1,\ldots,x_n)\),

\[
\boxed{df=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_i}\,dx_i}.
\]

This expression is the linear part of a small change. For finite input increments,

\[
\Delta f\approx\sum_i f_{x_i}\Delta x_i,
\]

with derivatives evaluated at the starting point. The approximation error depends on curvature and the shock size.

## 2. A polynomial worked example

Let

\[
f(x,y)=x^2y+3y^2.
\]

Then

\[
f_x=2xy,\qquad f_y=x^2+6y,
\]

and

\[
df=2xy\,dx+(x^2+6y)\,dy.
\]

At \((x,y)=(2,1)\), \(f_x=4\), \(f_y=10\). For \((dx,dy)=(0.1,-0.05)\),

\[
df=4(0.1)+10(-0.05)=\boxed{-0.1}.
\]

The exact change is

\[
f(2.1,0.95)-f(2,1)
=(2.1)^2(0.95)+3(0.95)^2-7
=\boxed{-0.102}.
\]

Thus the linear approximation differs from the exact change by \(-0.002\).

## 3. Financial example: simultaneous principal, rate, and horizon changes

For continuously compounded value,

\[
V(P,r,t)=Pe^{rt},
\]

Section 15.2 established

\[
V_P=e^{rt},\quad V_r=Pte^{rt},\quad V_t=Pre^{rt}.
\]

The total differential is therefore

\[
\boxed{dV=e^{rt}\,dP+Pte^{rt}\,dr+Pre^{rt}\,dt}.
\]

At \(P=1000\), \(r=0.05\) per year, and \(t=2\) years, consider \(dP=100\), \(dr=0.01\), and \(dt=0.5\) years. Since \(e^{0.1}\approx1.105170186\),

\[
dV\approx(1.105170186)(100)
+(2210.340372)(0.01)
+(55.258509)(0.5)
=\boxed{160.249677}.
\]

The exact finite scenario difference is

\[
\Delta V=1100e^{(0.06)(2.5)}-1000e^{(0.05)(2)}
=1100e^{0.15}-1000e^{0.1}
\approx\boxed{172.893477}.
\]

The differential is an approximation about the base point, not an exact multi-input revaluation.

## 4. The chain rule for a time-dependent input

Suppose \(r=r(s)\) and \(t=t(s)\) are differentiable functions of scenario parameter \(s\), while principal \(P\) is constant. Define

\[
W(s)=V(P,r(s),t(s)).
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{\frac{dW}{ds}=V_r\frac{dr}{ds}+V_t\frac{dt}{ds}}.
\]

If principal also depends on \(s\),

\[
\frac{dW}{ds}=V_P P'(s)+V_r r'(s)+V_t t'(s).
\]

This is the chain rule along a path in input space. The partial derivative \(V_r\) alone does not capture the total rate-linked effect if time or principal also changes with the scenario.

## 5. Worked chain-rule path

Let

\[
P(s)=1000+100s,\quad r(s)=0.05+0.01s,\quad t(s)=2+0.5s.
\]

At \(s=0\), \(P'=100\), \(r'=0.01\), and \(t'=0.5\). The chain rule gives

\[
W'(0)=160.249677\text{ currency units per unit }s.
\]

At \(s=1\), the exact change is the scenario difference from Section 3. This demonstrates how a path derivative captures the *instantaneous* slope of a jointly moving scenario.

## 6. Chain rule for intermediate model parameters

If an instrument value depends on yield \(y\) and yield depends on a market parameter \(z\), then

\[
\frac{dV}{dz}=\frac{\partial V}{\partial y}\frac{dy}{dz},
\]

assuming no other \(z\)-dependence. If there is direct dependence \(V=V(y(z),z)\), then

\[
\frac{dV}{dz}=V_y\,y'(z)+V_z.
\]

Omitting the direct term is a common sensitivity error. Multiple intermediates require summing every path of dependence.

## 7. Total differential of discounted cash flow

For

\[
PV(F,r,t)=Fe^{-rt},
\]

the differential is

\[
\boxed{dPV=e^{-rt}\,dF-Fte^{-rt}\,dr-Fre^{-rt}\,dt}.
\]

At \(F=1000\), \(r=0.05\), \(t=2\), increasing the discount rate alone decreases present value. A simultaneous increase in future cash flow may offset that decrease; the total differential combines the local effects.

## 8. Accuracy, scaling, and model governance

A differential assumes differentiability at the base point and approximates sufficiently small changes. For larger changes, perform a full revaluation and compare with the first-order estimate. Document units: \(dr=0.01\) is one percentage point for a decimal annual rate, and \(dt=0.5\) represents half a year. Economic dependence between risk factors must be specified; mathematical partial derivatives alone do not describe market co-movement. At domain boundaries, two-sided perturbations may be inadmissible.

**1.** State the total differential for \(f(x,y)\).  
*Answer:* \(df=f_xdx+f_ydy\).

**2.** State the \(n\)-input version.  
*Answer:* \(df=\sum_i f_{x_i}dx_i\).

**3.** Differentiate \(f=x^2y+3y^2\) with respect to \(x\).  
*Answer:* \(2xy\).

**4.** Differentiate the same function with respect to \(y\).  
*Answer:* \(x^2+6y\).

**5.** Evaluate \(f_x(2,1)\).  
*Answer:* \(4\).

**6.** Evaluate \(f_y(2,1)\).  
*Answer:* \(10\).

**7.** Find \(df\) for \(dx=0.1,dy=-0.05\).  
*Answer:* \(-0.1\).

**8.** Find the exact polynomial change for those increments.  
*Answer:* \(-0.102\).

**9.** Write \(V(P,r,t)\).  
*Answer:* \(Pe^{rt}\).

**10.** Write \(V_P\).  
*Answer:* \(e^{rt}\).

**11.** Write \(V_r\).  
*Answer:* \(Pte^{rt}\).

**12.** Write \(V_t\).  
*Answer:* \(Pre^{rt}\).

**13.** State \(dV\).  
*Answer:* \(e^{rt}dP+Pte^{rt}dr+Pre^{rt}dt\).

**14.** What is a one-percentage-point rate shock in decimal units?  
*Answer:* \(0.01\).

**15.** Evaluate \(dV\) for the reference multi-input shock.
