## Learning objectives

Compute second and mixed partial derivatives, assemble a Hessian, understand symmetry under regularity assumptions, interpret curvature and interaction effects, classify two-dimensional quadratic curvature, and validate second-order financial sensitivities.

## 1. Second partial derivatives

For a twice differentiable scalar function \(f(x,y)\), the second partials are

\[
f_{xx}=\frac{\partial^2f}{\partial x^2},\quad
f_{yy}=\frac{\partial^2f}{\partial y^2},\quad
f_{xy}=\frac{\partial}{\partial y}\left(\frac{\partial f}{\partial x}\right),\quad
f_{yx}=\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\left(\frac{\partial f}{\partial y}\right).
\]

The first two describe coordinate-wise curvature; the mixed partials describe how a sensitivity to one input changes when another input changes.

## 2. Hessian matrix

For \(f:\mathbb R^n\to\mathbb R\), the Hessian is

\[
\boxed{H_f(x)=\left[\frac{\partial^2 f}{\partial x_i\partial x_j}\right]_{i,j=1}^n}.
\]

For two inputs,

\[
H_f=\begin{bmatrix}f_{xx}&f_{xy}\\f_{yx}&f_{yy}\end{bmatrix}.
\]

When the mixed second partials are continuous in a neighborhood, **Clairaut's theorem** gives \(f_{xy}=f_{yx}\); the Hessian is symmetric. Equality is not guaranteed solely by the existence of both mixed partials at one isolated point.

## 3. Polynomial worked example

Let

\[
f(x,y)=x^2+3xy+2y^2.
\]

The gradient is

\[
\nabla f=(2x+3y,\;3x+4y)^\mathsf T.
\]

The Hessian is constant:

\[
\boxed{H_f=\begin{bmatrix}2&3\\3&4\end{bmatrix}}.
\]

The mixed partials are both \(3\). The determinant is \(2(4)-3^2=-1<0\), so the Hessian is indefinite: the surface has positive and negative curvature directions. A positive diagonal does not by itself imply positive definiteness.

At \((1,2)\), the gradient is \((8,11)\) and \(f(1,2)=15\).

## 4. Second-order Taylor approximation

For a small displacement \(h\),

\[
\boxed{f(x+h)\approx f(x)+\nabla f(x)^\mathsf Th+\frac12h^\mathsf TH_f(x)h}.
\]

For a quadratic polynomial with constant Hessian, this expression is **exact** for every displacement. For a general smooth nonlinear function, it is a local approximation; Section 15.7 develops multivariable Taylor expansions more fully.

With \(h=(0.1,-0.2)\) in the polynomial example,

\[
\nabla f^\mathsf Th=8(0.1)+11(-0.2)=-1.4,
\]

\[
\frac12h^\mathsf TH_fh
=\frac12\left(2(0.1)^2+6(0.1)(-0.2)+4(-0.2)^2\right)=0.03.
\]

Therefore \(f(1.1,1.8)-f(1,2)=-1.37\), exactly.

## 5. Financial example: future-value curvature

For \(V(P,r,t)=Pe^{rt}\), holding \(P\) fixed, the gradient with respect to \((r,t)\) is

\[
\nabla_{r,t}V=\begin{bmatrix}Pte^{rt}\\Pre^{rt}\end{bmatrix}.
\]

Its Hessian is

\[
\boxed{
H_{r,t}V=
Pe^{rt}\begin{bmatrix}
t^2&1+rt\\
1+rt&r^2
\end{bmatrix}
}.
\]

Thus

\[
V_{rr}=Pt^2e^{rt},\quad
V_{tt}=Pr^2e^{rt},\quad
V_{rt}=V_{tr}=Pe^{rt}(1+rt).
\]

At \(P=1000,r=0.05,t=2\), write \(v=1000e^{0.1}\). Then

\[
H_{r,t}V=
\begin{bmatrix}
4v&1.1v\\
1.1v&0.0025v
\end{bmatrix}.
\]

The determinant is \(v^2(0.01-1.21)=-1.2v^2<0\). The Hessian is indefinite: future value is convex in each variable separately for \(P>0\), yet not jointly convex in rate and time. This is a useful warning against interpreting individual positive second derivatives as joint convexity.

## 6. Rate-time interaction and scenario risk

For simultaneous rate and time increments \(h=(\Delta r,\Delta t)\), the second-order correction is

\[
\frac12V_{rr}(\Delta r)^2
+V_{rt}\Delta r\Delta t
+\frac12V_{tt}(\Delta t)^2.
\]

The mixed term is **not halved**: it appears twice in the symmetric quadratic form before multiplication by \(1/2\).

For \(\Delta r=0.01,\Delta t=0.5\), at the reference point:

\[
\text{first order}=v(2(0.01)+0.05(0.5))=0.045v,
\]

\[
\text{second order}=v\left(2(0.01)^2+1.1(0.01)(0.5)+0.00125(0.5)^2\right)
=0.0060125v.
\]

The combined second-order estimate is \(0.0510125v\). The exact change is

\[
1000e^{(0.06)(2.5)}-1000e^{(0.05)(2)}
=1000(e^{0.15}-e^{0.1}).
\]

Compare the first- and second-order approximations with this exact result; neither approximation is guaranteed to remain accurate under arbitrarily large shocks.

## 7. Portfolio variance Hessian

For a symmetric covariance matrix \(C\),

\[
q(w)=w^\mathsf TCw,\qquad \nabla q(w)=2Cw,\qquad
\boxed{H_q=2C}.
\]

For

\[
C=\begin{bmatrix}0.04&0.01\\0.01&0.09\end{bmatrix},
\]

\[
H_q=\begin{bmatrix}0.08&0.02\\0.02&0.18\end{bmatrix}.
\]

Since \(C\) is positive definite, so is \(H_q\); the variance function is strictly convex in unrestricted weights. A fully invested or long-only portfolio still requires feasibility constraints in optimization.

## 8. Numerical verification

For a smooth function, centered second differences estimate diagonal Hessian entries:

\[
f_{xx}(x,y)\approx
\frac{f(x+h,y)-2f(x,y)+f(x-h,y)}{h^2}.
\]

A centered mixed difference estimates

\[
f_{xy}(x,y)\approx
\frac{f(x+h,y+k)-f(x+h,y-k)-f(x-h,y+k)+f(x-h,y-k)}{4hk}.
\]

Choose step sizes relative to each input's scale. Very small steps can amplify cancellation, particularly for second derivatives. Prefer analytical benchmarks when available and check symmetry, dimensions, units, and perturbation-domain validity.

## 10. Twenty worked exercises

**1.** Define \(f_{xy}\).  
*Answer:* Differentiate \(f_x\) with respect to \(y\).

**2.** When can mixed partials be interchanged?  
*Answer:* A sufficient condition is continuity of the mixed second partials in a neighborhood.

**3.** Find \(f_x\) for \(f=x^2+3xy+2y^2\).  
*Answer:* \(2x+3y\).

**4.** Find \(f_y\).  
*Answer:* \(3x+4y\).

**5.** Find \(f_{xx}\).  
*Answer:* \(2\).

**6.** Find \(f_{yy}\).  
*Answer:* \(4\).

**7.** Find \(f_{xy}\) and \(f_{yx}\).  
*Answer:* Both are \(3\).

**8.** Write the polynomial Hessian.  
*Answer:* \(\begin{bmatrix}2&3\\3&4\end{bmatrix}\).

**9.** Classify that Hessian.  
*Answer:* Indefinite, because its determinant is \(-1\).

**10.** Evaluate the polynomial gradient at \((1,2)\).  
*Answer:* \((8,11)\).

**11.** Compute the linear change for \(h=(0.1,-0.2)\).  
*Answer:* \(-1.4\).

**12.** Compute the quadratic correction.  
*Answer:* \(0.03\).

**13.** Compute the exact polynomial change.  
*Answer:* \(-1.37\).

**14.** Find \(V_{rr}\) for \(V=Pe^{rt}\).  
*Answer:* \(Pt^2e^{rt}\).

**15.** Find \(V_{tt}\).  
*Answer:* \(Pr^2e^{rt}\).

**16.** Find \(V_{rt}\).  
*Answer:* \(Pe^{rt}(1+rt)\).

**17.** Is \(V\) jointly convex in \((r,t)\) at the reference point?  
*Answer:* No; the Hessian determinant is negative.

**18.** What is the Hessian of \(w^\mathsf TCw\) for symmetric \(C\)?  
*Answer:* \(2C\).

**19.** Why is a mixed term not halved in a second-order expansion?  
*Answer:* The symmetric quadratic form contains both off-diagonal terms.
