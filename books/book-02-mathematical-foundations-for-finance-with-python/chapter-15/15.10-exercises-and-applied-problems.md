## Chapter assessment objectives

Consolidate the mathematical foundations of functions of several variables, partial derivatives, total differentials, the chain rule, gradients, directional derivatives, Jacobians, Hessians, Taylor approximations, and level sets. Interpret each calculation using financial units, domain restrictions, constraints, and exact-versus-local distinctions.

## Reference models

Use the following models throughout unless an exercise specifies otherwise:

\[
f(x,y)=x^2+3xy+2y^2,\qquad
V(P,r,t)=Pe^{rt},\qquad
D(F,r,t)=Fe^{-rt},
\]

\[
q(w)=0.04w_1^2+0.02w_1w_2+0.09w_2^2,
\qquad
G(r,t)=\begin{bmatrix}1000e^{rt}\\500e^{2rt}\end{bmatrix}.
\]

Rates are decimal annual rates; \(t\) is measured in years. Where a financial interpretation requires positive principal or cash flow, assume it explicitly. The models are educational and omit market calibration and transaction costs.

## Part A — Fundamentals: 20 worked exercises

**1. Identify the domain of \(f(x,y)\).**  
*Solution:* All of \(\mathbb R^2\), because \(f\) is polynomial.

**2. Evaluate \(f(1,2)\).**  
*Solution:* \(1+6+8=15\).

**3. Find \(f_x\).**  
*Solution:* \(2x+3y\).

**4. Find \(f_y\).**  
*Solution:* \(3x+4y\).

**5. Evaluate \(\nabla f(1,2)\).**  
*Solution:* \((8,11)^\mathsf T\).

**6. Find the differential of \(f\) at \((1,2)\).**  
*Solution:* \(df=8\,dx+11\,dy\).

**7. Estimate the change for \((dx,dy)=(0.1,-0.2)\).**  
*Solution:* \(8(0.1)+11(-0.2)=-1.4\).

**8. Find the Hessian of \(f\).**  
*Solution:* \(\begin{bmatrix}2&3\\3&4\end{bmatrix}\).

**9. Find the mixed partials of \(f\).**  
*Solution:* \(f_{xy}=f_{yx}=3\).

**10. Classify the Hessian.**  
*Solution:* Indefinite because its determinant is \(8-9=-1\).

**11. Find the second-order correction for the displacement in Exercise 7.**  
*Solution:* \(\tfrac12(0.02-0.12+0.16)=0.03\).

**12. Find the exact change in \(f\).**  
*Solution:* \(-1.4+0.03=-1.37\); this is exact because \(f\) is quadratic.

**13. Find the gradient of \(V(P,r,t)\).**  
*Solution:* \((e^{rt},Pte^{rt},Pre^{rt})^\mathsf T\).

**14. Find \(V_r\) at \((1000,0.05,2)\).**  
*Solution:* \(2000e^{0.1}\).

**15. Find \(V_t\) at that point.**  
*Solution:* \(50e^{0.1}\).

**16. Find \(D_r\) for \(D(F,r,t)=Fe^{-rt}\).**  
*Solution:* \(-Fte^{-rt}\).

**17. Find \(D_t\).**  
*Solution:* \(-Fre^{-rt}\).

**18. Define a Jacobian for \(F:\mathbb R^n\to\mathbb R^m\).**  
*Solution:* The \(m\times n\) matrix of first partial derivatives.

**19. Define a level set.**  
*Solution:* \(\{x:f(x)=c\}\) for a specified constant \(c\).

**20. State the local tangent condition on a regular level set.**  
*Solution:* \(\nabla f(x)^\mathsf Td=0\).

## Part B — Financial applications: 20 worked exercises

**21. Compute the reference future value.**  
*Solution:* \(V(1000,0.05,2)=1000e^{0.1}\).

**22. Compute the exact future-value change when \(r\) rises to \(0.06\) and \(t\) rises to \(2.5\), with \(P=1000\).**  
*Solution:* \(1000(e^{0.15}-e^{0.1})\).

**23. Find the first-order estimate for that rate–time shock.**  
*Solution:* \(1000e^{0.1}(2(0.01)+0.05(0.5))=45e^{0.1}\).

**24. Find the rate–time Hessian of \(V\) with principal fixed.**  
*Solution:* \(Pe^{rt}\begin{bmatrix}t^2&1+rt\\1+rt&r^2\end{bmatrix}\).

**25. Evaluate its mixed partial at the reference point.**  
*Solution:* \(1100e^{0.1}\).

**26. Find the second-order correction for the shock in Exercise 22.**  
*Solution:* \(0.0060125(1000e^{0.1})\).

**27. Find the second-order estimated change.**  
*Solution:* \(0.0510125(1000e^{0.1})\).

**28. Write the signed second-order approximation error.**  
*Solution:* \(0.0510125(1000e^{0.1})-1000(e^{0.15}-e^{0.1})\).

**29. Find the fixed-\(V\) rate–time slope.**  
*Solution:* \(dt/dr=-t/r\) for \(r\ne0\).

**30. Evaluate that slope at \(r=0.05,t=2\).**  
*Solution:* \(-40\) years per unit decimal rate.

**31. Find the exact maturity that preserves \(rt=0.1\) after a rate rise of \(0.001\).**  
*Solution:* \(t_{\rm new}=0.1/0.051\).

**32. Find the gradient of \(q(w)\).**  
*Solution:* \((0.08w_1+0.02w_2,\;0.02w_1+0.18w_2)^\mathsf T\).

**33. Evaluate the variance at \(w=(0.6,0.4)\).**  
*Solution:* \(0.0144+0.0048+0.0144=0.0336\).

**34. Evaluate the variance gradient there.**  
*Solution:* \((0.056,0.084)^\mathsf T\).

**35. Find the unconstrained iso-variance slope.**  
*Solution:* \(dw_2/dw_1=-0.056/0.084=-2/3\).

**36. Find the feasible variance derivative under \(w_1+w_2=1\).**  
*Solution:* \(dq/dw_1=0.056-0.084=-0.028\).

**37. Find the portfolio variance Hessian.**  
*Solution:* \(\begin{bmatrix}0.08&0.02\\0.02&0.18\end{bmatrix}\).

**38. Find the Jacobian of \(G(r,t)\).**  
*Solution:* \(\begin{bmatrix}1000te^{rt}&1000re^{rt}\\1000te^{2rt}&1000re^{2rt}\end{bmatrix}\).

**39. What is the rank of this Jacobian at \(r=0.05,t=2\)?**  
*Solution:* One, because the second row is proportional to the first.

**40. Why is the Jacobian rank relevant?**  
*Solution:* Its rank determines whether independent local input movements can be identified from the observed output changes.

## Part C — Integrated project exercises: 10 worked exercises

Use the Section 15.9 engine

\[
F(P,r,t)=\begin{bmatrix}Pe^{rt}\\0.5Pe^{-rt}\end{bmatrix}
\]

with base \(x=(1000,0.05,2)\) and shock \(h=(100,0.01,0.5)\).

**41. Compute the base outputs.**  
*Solution:* \((1000e^{0.1},500e^{-0.1})^\mathsf T\).

**42. Compute the shocked outputs.**  
*Solution:* \((1100e^{0.15},550e^{-0.15})^\mathsf T\).

**43. Compute the exact first-output change.**  
*Solution:* \(1100e^{0.15}-1000e^{0.1}\).

**44. Compute the exact second-output change.**  
*Solution:* \(550e^{-0.15}-500e^{-0.1}\).

**45. Compute the first-output gradient at the base point.**  
*Solution:* \(e^{0.1}(1,2000,50)^\mathsf T\).

**46. Compute the second-output gradient at the base point.**  
*Solution:* \(e^{-0.1}(0.5,-1000,-25)^\mathsf T\).

**47. Compute the first-order first-output change.**  
*Solution:* \(145e^{0.1}\).

**48. Compute the first-order second-output change.**  
*Solution:* \(27.5e^{-0.1}\).

**49. Compute the total portfolio first-order change.**  
*Solution:* \(145e^{0.1}+27.5e^{-0.1}\).

**50. Explain why first-order estimates can differ from exact changes.**  
*Solution:* They omit Hessian and higher-order terms, including nonlinear rate–time and principal interactions.
