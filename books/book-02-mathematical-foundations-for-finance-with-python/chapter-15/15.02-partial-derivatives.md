## Learning objectives

Define first-order partial derivatives, calculate them analytically, interpret financial sensitivities with units, compare exact changes with local linear approximations, use finite differences responsibly, and identify limits of a ceteris-paribus interpretation.

## 1. Definition and notation

For \(f(x,y)\), the partial derivative with respect to \(x\) at \((x,y)\) holds \(y\) fixed:

\[
\frac{\partial f}{\partial x}(x,y)
=\lim_{h\to0}\frac{f(x+h,y)-f(x,y)}{h},
\]

when the limit exists. Similarly,

\[
\frac{\partial f}{\partial y}(x,y)
=\lim_{h\to0}\frac{f(x,y+h)-f(x,y)}{h}.
\]

Other notations include \(f_x,f_y\). A partial derivative measures **local change per unit of the selected input**, not the total effect of simultaneous input movements.

## 2. Basic differentiation rules

Treat all other independent inputs as constants. For

\[
f(x,y)=3x^2y+5y^2-4x,
\]

the partial derivatives are

\[
f_x=6xy-4,\qquad f_y=3x^2+10y.
\]

At \((x,y)=(2,1)\), these evaluate to \(f_x=8\) and \(f_y=22\).

The familiar sum, product, quotient, and chain rules apply within each single-input slice. Domain constraints remain relevant.

## 3. Financial example: continuously compounded future value

For

\[
V(P,r,t)=Pe^{rt},
\]

the three first-order partial derivatives are

\[
\boxed{V_P=e^{rt}},\qquad
\boxed{V_r=Pte^{rt}},\qquad
\boxed{V_t=Pre^{rt}}.
\]

At \(P=1000\), \(r=0.05\) per year, and \(t=2\) years:

\[
V\approx1105.170186,
\]

\[
V_P=e^{0.1}\approx1.105170186,
\]

\[
V_r=2000e^{0.1}\approx2210.340372,
\]

\[
V_t=50e^{0.1}\approx55.258509.
\]

**Units:** \(V_P\) is currency per currency; \(V_r\) is currency per unit change in the decimal annual rate; \(V_t\) is currency per year. A one-percentage-point rate move is \(\Delta r=0.01\), not \(1\).

For a one-percentage-point rate increase, the first-order rate approximation is

\[
\Delta V\approx V_r\Delta r
=2210.340372(0.01)
\approx22.103404.
\]

The exact change is

\[
1000e^{0.12}-1000e^{0.1}\approx22.326666.
\]

The difference reflects curvature, not a coding error.

## 4. Present-value sensitivities

For a single deterministic future cash flow,

\[
PV(F,r,t)=Fe^{-rt},
\]

\[
\boxed{PV_F=e^{-rt}},\quad
\boxed{PV_r=-Fte^{-rt}},\quad
\boxed{PV_t=-Fre^{-rt}}.
\]

At \(F=1000\), \(r=0.05\), \(t=2\), present value is about \(904.837418\). Its rate sensitivity is \(-1809.674836\) currency per unit decimal rate. A rate increase reduces this discounted value, holding cash flow and maturity fixed.

For a zero maturity \(t=0\), the rate partial derivative is zero. For a zero cash flow, all three displayed partial derivatives except \(PV_F\) vanish.

## 5. Geometric interpretation

For a surface \(z=f(x,y)\), the partial derivatives are slopes along coordinate-aligned slices. The \(x\)-slope keeps \(y\) fixed; the \(y\)-slope keeps \(x\) fixed. A direction in which both inputs move requires combining sensitivities; gradients and directional derivatives are developed in Section 15.4.

## 6. Numerical finite differences

A forward difference estimates

\[
f_x(x,y)\approx\frac{f(x+h,y)-f(x,y)}{h},
\]

with truncation error generally \(O(h)\) for sufficiently smooth functions.

A centered difference estimates

\[
f_x(x,y)\approx\frac{f(x+h,y)-f(x-h,y)}{2h},
\]

with truncation error generally \(O(h^2)\) when sufficient derivatives exist.

Neither formula benefits from making \(h\) arbitrarily tiny: subtractive cancellation and floating-point roundoff eventually dominate. Choose \(h\) relative to input scale, check stability across step sizes, and compare against analytical results when available. Keep every perturbed point inside the function's domain.

## 7. Mixed and second partial derivatives: preview

For \(V=Pe^{rt}\),

\[
V_{rr}=Pt^2e^{rt},\qquad
V_{rt}=Pe^{rt}(1+rt).
\]

Under suitable continuity assumptions, \(V_{rt}=V_{tr}\). Section 15.6 develops Hessians and mixed partials systematically. A first-order rate sensitivity alone does not fully describe a large rate shock.

## 8. Sensitivity governance

Partial derivatives assume the other variables are held fixed. In markets, rate, time, volatility, spreads, and cash flows may move together. A sensitivity can be numerically correct but economically misleading if the model omits relevant drivers or if calibration parameters change with the shocked input. Report the held-fixed variables, input units, evaluation point, and shock convention.

## 10. Twenty worked exercises

**1.** Define \(f_x(x,y)\).  
*Answer:* \(\lim_{h\to0}[f(x+h,y)-f(x,y)]/h\), if it exists.

**2.** What is held fixed when finding \(f_x\)?  
*Answer:* All independent inputs except \(x\).

**3.** Find \(\partial(x^2y)/\partial x\).  
*Answer:* \(2xy\).

**4.** Find \(\partial(x^2y)/\partial y\).  
*Answer:* \(x^2\).

**5.** Find \(f_x\) for \(f=3x^2y+5y^2-4x\).  
*Answer:* \(6xy-4\).

**6.** Find \(f_y\) for the same \(f\).  
*Answer:* \(3x^2+10y\).

**7.** Evaluate \(f_x(2,1)\).  
*Answer:* \(8\).

**8.** Evaluate \(f_y(2,1)\).  
*Answer:* \(22\).

**9.** Differentiate \(V=Pe^{rt}\) with respect to \(P\).  
*Answer:* \(e^{rt}\).

**10.** Differentiate \(V\) with respect to \(r\).  
*Answer:* \(Pte^{rt}\).

**11.** Differentiate \(V\) with respect to \(t\).  
*Answer:* \(Pre^{rt}\).

**12.** Evaluate \(V_r(1000,0.05,2)\).  
*Answer:* \(2000e^{0.1}\approx2210.340372\).

**13.** What is a one-percentage-point shock in decimal-rate units?  
*Answer:* \(0.01\).

**14.** Approximate the value increase for that rate shock.  
*Answer:* \(V_r(0.01)\approx22.103404\).

**15.** Differentiate \(PV=Fe^{-rt}\) with respect to \(r\).  
*Answer:* \(-Fte^{-rt}\).

**16.** Evaluate \(PV_r(1000,0.05,2)\).  
*Answer:* \(-2000e^{-0.1}\approx-1809.674836\).

**17.** What is \(V_r\) at \(t=0\)?  
*Answer:* \(0\).

**18.** Write the centered difference for \(f_x\).  
*Answer:* \([f(x+h,y)-f(x-h,y)]/(2h)\).

**19.** Why can very small finite-difference steps be inaccurate?  
*Answer:* Subtractive cancellation and roundoff.

## 11. Applied assessment

At \(P=1000,r=0.05,t=2\), compute all three future-value partial derivatives. Estimate the impact of a one-percentage-point rate shock, compare it with the exact scenario difference, and validate \(V_r\) with a centered difference using \(h=10^{-5}\). Repeat for present value and explain the opposite sign of the rate sensitivity.

**Acceptance:** analytical formulas correct, consistent units, documented held-fixed assumptions, finite-difference agreement within an explicitly selected tolerance, and clear distinction between exact and first-order scenario changes.
