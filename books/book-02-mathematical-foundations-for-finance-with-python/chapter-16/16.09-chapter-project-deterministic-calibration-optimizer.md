## Project objective

Build a transparent, deterministic calibration optimizer that turns a small synthetic financial dataset into a fitted annual rate, with an analytical reference solution, documented numerical safeguards, independent holdout evaluation, and reproducible evidence. The project combines objective design (16.1), derivatives (16.2), curvature (16.3), convexity (16.4), gradient descent (16.5), Newton's method (16.6), scaling (16.7), and residual validation (16.8).

## 1. Business problem and scope

Suppose a synthetic pricing model predicts

\[
P_i(r)=1000(1+t_i r),
\]

where \(t_i\) is a year fraction and \(r\) is a decimal annual rate. The training observations are \((t_i,P_i^{obs})=(1,1050),(2,1100),(3,1150)\); the untouched holdout is \((4,1201)\). These are pedagogical observations, not tradable market quotes. No compounding, discounting, arbitrage constraints, or market microstructure is implied.

The target is a deterministic, auditable estimate of \(r\), not an assertion that this toy model is production-ready.

## 2. Objective and units

Define the normalized residual

\[
e_i(r)=\frac{P_i(r)-P_i^{obs}}{1000}.
\]

This residual is dimensionless; the raw price residual has currency units. Minimize

\[
\boxed{L(r)=\tfrac12\sum_{i=1}^3e_i(r)^2}.
\]

With \(b_i=(P_i^{obs}-1000)/1000\), the residual is \(e_i(r)=t_i r-b_i\). Consequently,

\[
L'(r)=\sum_i t_i(t_i r-b_i),\qquad
L''(r)=\sum_i t_i^2=14.
\]

Because \(L''>0\), the objective is strongly convex and has a unique global minimizer:

\[
\boxed{r^*=\frac{\sum_i t_i b_i}{\sum_i t_i^2}=0.05}.
\]

The unnormalized raw price loss has the same minimizer but a differently scaled gradient and Hessian.

## 3. Optimization engines

**Analytical benchmark:** compute the closed-form weighted linear least-squares solution, independent of any iterative optimizer.

**Gradient descent:** apply \(r_{k+1}=r_k-\alpha L'(r_k)\). Since the curvature is \(14\), constant-step convergence requires \(0<\alpha<2/14\). The example uses \(\alpha=0.05\), within that range.

**Newton:** apply \(r_{k+1}=r_k-L'(r_k)/L''(r_k)\). Exact quadratic curvature gives the minimizer in one full step from any finite starting point, barring numerical overflow or loss of precision.

Both engines must retain iteration counts, termination reason, objective, gradient, and an auditable bounded history.

## 4. Deterministic starts and model comparison

Use the fixed initial guesses \((-0.10,0,0.10)\) for both iterative methods. The project is convex, so these starts should converge to the same unique minimizer under suitable solver settings. The multiple starts here test numerical reproducibility and algorithm behavior, not competing local minima.

Compare each fitted rate against the analytical reference and recompute all final residuals from the original observations. Do not accept a solver's self-reported success as sufficient evidence.

## 5. Holdout validation

At \(r^*=0.05\), the model predicts \(P(4)=1200\) while the holdout observation is \(1201\). Thus the holdout signed price residual is \(-1\) dollar and the absolute holdout error is \(1\) dollar.

This intentionally separates zero training error from nonzero holdout error. The holdout is evaluated only after fitting; it is not used to choose the fitted parameter, algorithm, or thresholds.

## 6. Acceptance contract

A reference implementation should meet the following **provisional manuscript criteria**, subject to review:

- Every input, output, objective value, gradient, and update must be finite.
- Training data and holdout data must remain separate.
- Analytical, gradient-descent, and Newton solutions should agree to an absolute rate tolerance of \(10^{-8}\) under the documented configuration.
- The final gradient magnitude should be at most \(10^{-8}\) for a successful iterative run.
- Training price MAE and RMSE should be at most \(10^{-5}\) dollars.
- Holdout price residual should be \(-1\) dollar within a \(10^{-5}\)-dollar numerical tolerance.
- Failed iterations, invalid inputs, and exhausted iteration budgets must be distinguishable from convergence.
- All claims must distinguish draft reference checks from independent runtime certification.

Each iterative run now records its own `training` and `holdout` price metrics, `recomputed_loss`, `recomputed_gradient` and `provisional_acceptance`. The reported gradient and the gradient recomputed from the candidate must both meet the threshold. Overall acceptance requires all six candidates to pass, alongside the analytical benchmark checks. Top-level `training` and `holdout` remain metrics at the analytical benchmark; they must not be substituted for a candidate's metrics.

These thresholds are illustrative for this exact synthetic example; they are not universal calibration standards.

## 9. Troubleshooting

**Gradient descent oscillates or diverges:** check that the learning rate is below \(1/7\) for this objective, and that the gradient corresponds to the normalized loss.

**Newton returns an invalid step:** verify curvature is positive and finite; reject singular or nonfinite updates.

**Solutions disagree:** compare parameter units, objective scaling, start points, and stopping tolerances; independently recompute loss and gradient.

**Training error is zero but holdout error is not:** this is expected for the deliberately perturbed holdout; do not refit on it.

**An apparently successful solver has a large gradient:** treat the numerical status as inadequate and inspect the termination reason.

## Normalized arithmetic contract — validation follow-up

The analytical-rate quotient and curvature retain exact expressions of represented observations until final rounding. The normalized curvature is `sum(t*t)`, one millionth of the raw-price curvature in Section 16.8; an already-rounded raw result is not used to compute it. Gradient aggregation preserves this section's existing rounded residual convention (Section 16.9 rounds normalized residuals first; Section 16.10 aggregates raw residuals before the factor of 1000). Thus last-bit gradient differences can remain. Nonzero results outside finite nonzero float range raise ValueError; true zero is accepted. Analytical fitting can return a finite quotient even when direct curvature is out of range. Price/raw-residual and solver-update range remain limited; selected normalized objective/loss range is addressed by the later note.
