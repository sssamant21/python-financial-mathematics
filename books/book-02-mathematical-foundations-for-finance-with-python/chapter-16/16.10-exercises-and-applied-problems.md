## 1. Mathematical reference sheet

For differentiable \(f:\mathbb R^n\to\mathbb R\), a stationary point satisfies \(\nabla f(x^*)=0\). At a stationary point, a positive-definite Hessian is sufficient for a strict local minimum; a negative-definite Hessian is sufficient for a strict local maximum; an indefinite Hessian indicates a saddle. Semidefinite Hessians can be inconclusive.

On an open convex domain, twice-differentiable \(f\) is convex exactly when \(H_f(x)\succeq0\) everywhere. For convex differentiable \(f\), a stationary point is globally minimizing. Strict convexity ensures at most one minimizer, not necessarily existence.

Gradient descent is \(x_{k+1}=x_k-\alpha_k\nabla f(x_k)\). Newton's direction solves \(H(x_k)d_k=-\nabla f(x_k)\). Both require numerical safeguards and honest termination reporting.

## 2. Twenty worked exercises

**Exercise 1 — Financial objective.** Define the squared normalized price residual for \(P(r)=1000(1+2r)\) and observed price \(1100\).

*Solution:* \(e(r)=[P(r)-1100]/1000=2r-0.1\), so \(L(r)=\tfrac12(2r-0.1)^2\). The rate is dimensionless per year.

**Exercise 2 — Gradient.** Differentiate Exercise 1.

*Solution:* \(L'(r)=2(2r-0.1)=4r-0.2\).

**Exercise 3 — Stationary point.** Solve \(L'(r)=0\).

*Solution:* \(r=0.05\).

**Exercise 4 — Second derivative.** Classify the stationary point.

*Solution:* \(L''(r)=4>0\), so \(0.05\) is a strict local minimum.

**Exercise 5 — Globality.** Prove that the minimizer is global.

*Solution:* \(L''(r)=4\) everywhere on \(\mathbb R\), so the objective is strongly convex and \(0.05\) is its unique global minimizer.

**Exercise 6 — Convex set.** Is the long-only, fully invested two-asset feasible set convex?

*Solution:* Yes. \(w_1,w_2\ge0\) and \(w_1+w_2=1\) are preserved by convex combinations.

**Exercise 7 — Nonconvex set.** Is \(\{-2,2\}\) convex?

*Solution:* No. Its midpoint zero is absent.

**Exercise 8 — Jensen inequality.** Verify convexity of \(f(x)=x^2\) using \(x=-1\), \(y=3\), and \(\lambda=1/2\).

*Solution:* \(f(1)=1\le[1+9]/2=5\).

**Exercise 9 — Hessian matrix.** Find the Hessian of \(f(x,y)=2(x-1)^2+3(y+2)^2\).

*Solution:* \(H=\operatorname{diag}(4,6)\), which is positive definite.

**Exercise 10 — Two-variable minimizer.** Find the global minimizer of Exercise 9.

*Solution:* \((1,-2)\), uniquely.

**Exercise 11 — Gradient descent.** For \(f(x)=\tfrac12(x-3)^2\), start at \(x_0=0\) with \(\alpha=0.5\). Find \(x_1\) and \(x_2\).

*Solution:* \(x_1=1.5\), \(x_2=2.25\).

**Exercise 12 — Stability.** Give the stable fixed-step interval for Exercise 11.

*Solution:* \(0<\alpha<2\), since the quadratic curvature is one.

**Exercise 13 — Newton.** Apply full Newton to Exercise 11 from \(x_0=0\).

*Solution:* \(x_1=0-(-3)/1=3\), the minimizer.

**Exercise 14 — Indefinite Hessian.** Classify the stationary point of \(f(x,y)=x^2-y^2\).

*Solution:* \((0,0)\) is a saddle because \(H=\operatorname{diag}(2,-2)\) is indefinite.

**Exercise 15 — Scaling.** Let \(f(x,y)=\tfrac12[10^6(x-0.02)^2+(y-3)^2]\). Set \(u=1000(x-0.02)\), \(v=y-3\). Find the transformed objective.

*Solution:* \(\tfrac12(u^2+v^2)\); the transformed Hessian is \(I\).

**Exercise 16 — Objective scaling.** Does multiplying a differentiable objective by a positive constant change the exact Newton direction?

*Solution:* No, provided gradient and Hessian are scaled consistently and the Newton system is solvable.

**Exercise 17 — Calibration.** Fit \(P_i(r)=1000(1+t_i r)\) to \((t_i,P_i^{obs})=(1,1050),(2,1100),(3,1150)\).

*Solution:* With \(b_i=(P_i^{obs}-1000)/1000\), \(r^*=\sum t_i b_i/\sum t_i^2=0.7/14=0.05\).

**Exercise 18 — Residual metrics.** Find MAE, RMSE, and bias for residuals \((-2,0,2)\) dollars.

*Solution:* MAE \(=4/3\) dollars, RMSE \(=\sqrt{8/3}\) dollars, bias \(=0\).

**Exercise 19 — Holdout.** Using Exercise 17's rate, predict price at \(t=4\), with observed price \(1201\).

*Solution:* Predicted price \(1200\); signed residual \(-1\) dollar.

**Exercise 20 — Identifiability.** Why can observations of \(m(a,b)=a+b\) not uniquely determine both parameters?

*Solution:* Every pair with the same sum gives the same model output; the Jacobian columns are identical and its rank is one.

## 3. Applied problems

### Problem A — Method comparison

Use the objective \(L(r)=\tfrac12\sum_i(t_i r-b_i)^2\) from Exercise 17. Derive its gradient \(14r-0.7\) and Hessian \(14\). Compare fixed-step gradient descent at \(\alpha=0.05\) with full Newton from initial rates \(-0.1\), \(0\), and \(0.1\). Report objective values, gradient norms, iteration counts, and termination reasons. Confirm that the converged solutions agree with the analytical rate to a declared tolerance.

### Problem B — Convexity and local methods

Analyze \(g(x)=(x^2-1)^2+0.2x\). Derive \(g'(x)=4x(x^2-1)+0.2\) and \(g''(x)=12x^2-4\). Explain why different initial guesses may converge to different local minima, and why a finite multistart experiment cannot prove global optimality.

### Problem C — Portfolio risk

For

\[
\Sigma=\begin{bmatrix}0.04&0.01\\0.01&0.09\end{bmatrix},
\]

show that \(\Sigma\) is positive definite using its leading principal minors. Calculate \(w^\mathsf T\Sigma w\) at \(w=(0.6,0.4)\), giving \(0.0336\). Explain why the unconstrained zero-weight minimizer is not a feasible fully invested portfolio.

### Problem D — Independent calibration acceptance

Recompute the training residuals and holdout residual from the original observations rather than trusting optimizer output. Specify a numerical acceptance gate and separate it from financial model validation. Record whether the checks were executed, which data were used, and whether an independent review occurred.

## 5. Troubleshooting and common misconceptions

- A zero gradient is not automatically a minimum; inspect curvature and domain assumptions.
- Positive curvature at a single point does not establish global convexity.
- A convex objective does not make a nonconvex feasible region convex.
- A small solver step can indicate stagnation rather than convergence.
- A good training fit does not guarantee holdout accuracy.
- Parameter scaling changes numerical coordinates; report converted financial units.
- Multiple successful starts are evidence of reproducibility, not a global optimality certificate.
- A test script in the repository is not evidence that it has been executed.

The analytical-rate quotient and curvature retain exact expressions of represented observations until final rounding. The normalized curvature is `sum(t*t)`, one millionth of the raw-price curvature in Section 16.8; an already-rounded raw result is not used to compute it.
