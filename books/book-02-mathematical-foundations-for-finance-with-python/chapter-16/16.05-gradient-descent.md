## Learning objectives

Explain the negative-gradient direction, implement gradient descent, select a step size, distinguish convergence from mere iteration, understand conditioning and stopping criteria, and use the method for a synthetic financial calibration.

## 1. From calculus to an algorithm

For a differentiable scalar objective \(f:\mathbb R^n\to\mathbb R\), the gradient \(\nabla f(x)\) points in the direction of greatest first-order increase in the Euclidean coordinate metric. To seek a smaller objective, use the negative gradient:

\[
\boxed{x_{k+1}=x_k-\alpha_k\nabla f(x_k)}
\]

where \(\alpha_k>0\) is the **step size** or learning rate. This is a local method: without convexity or other assumptions, it does not guarantee a global optimum.

For small \(\alpha>0\),

\[
f(x-\alpha\nabla f(x))
=f(x)-\alpha\|\nabla f(x)\|_2^2+O(\alpha^2).
\]

Thus \(-\nabla f\) is a descent direction whenever the gradient is nonzero, but a large step can increase the objective.

## 2. A solvable quadratic

Let

\[
f(x)=\tfrac12 a(x-b)^2,\qquad a>0.
\]

Then \(f'(x)=a(x-b)\), and gradient descent becomes

\[
x_{k+1}=x_k-\alpha a(x_k-b).
\]

With error \(e_k=x_k-b\),

\[
\boxed{e_{k+1}=(1-\alpha a)e_k}.
\]

Therefore \(x_k\to b\) for every initial point if

\[
\boxed{0<\alpha<\frac2a}.
\]

At \(\alpha=1/a\), the exact quadratic minimizer is reached in one step. At \(\alpha=2/a\), errors alternate without decreasing; larger positive steps diverge for nonzero initial error. The special choice \(\alpha=1/a\) is not a universal learning rate.

## 3. Financial rate calibration and scaling

The synthetic loss from Section 16.1 is

\[
L(r)=[1000(1+2r)-1100]^2
=4{,}000{,}000(r-0.05)^2.
\]

Its gradient is \(L'(r)=8{,}000{,}000(r-0.05)\). Gradient descent in the raw rate coordinate converges for a constant step size satisfying

\[
0<\alpha<\frac{2}{8{,}000{,}000}=2.5\times10^{-7}.
\]

The small permissible step is caused by objective scaling, not by any universal property of interest rates. Define a normalized loss

\[
\boxed{\ell(r)=\left(\frac{1000(1+2r)-1100}{2000}\right)^2=(r-0.05)^2}.
\]

Now \(\ell'(r)=2(r-0.05)\), and a constant step converges for \(0<\alpha<1\). At \(\alpha=0.25\),

\[
r_{k+1}=r_k-0.5(r_k-0.05),
\]

so the error halves each iteration. Starting at \(r_0=0\), the sequence is

\[
0,\ 0.025,\ 0.0375,\ 0.04375,\ 0.046875,\ldots
\]

The optimizer's reported rate is dimensionless per year; the loss and its gradient use the normalized objective's units.

## 4. Multivariable quadratic and conditioning

For

\[
f(x)=\tfrac12(x-x^*)^\mathsf TH(x-x^*),
\]

with symmetric positive definite \(H\), the iteration error obeys

\[
e_{k+1}=(I-\alpha H)e_k.
\]

A constant step converges if \(0<\alpha<2/\lambda_{\max}(H)\). Its slowest mode depends on the smallest eigenvalue. A large condition number \(\kappa(H)=\lambda_{\max}/\lambda_{\min}\) makes gradient descent slow even when the objective is strictly convex.

The optimal constant step for worst-case contraction on a positive-definite quadratic is

\[
\alpha_{\mathrm{opt}}=\frac{2}{\lambda_{\min}+\lambda_{\max}},
\]

with spectral contraction factor \((\kappa-1)/(\kappa+1)\).

## 5. Fixed step versus line search

A **fixed step** is simple and reproducible when curvature bounds are known. A **backtracking line search** starts with a trial step and reduces it until a sufficient-decrease rule is satisfied. The Armijo condition for descent direction \(d\) is

\[
\boxed{f(x+\alpha d)\le f(x)+c\alpha\nabla f(x)^\mathsf Td},
\qquad 0<c<1.
\]

For \(d=-\nabla f(x)\), the right-hand side becomes \(f(x)-c\alpha\|\nabla f(x)\|^2\). Backtracking does not guarantee convergence for arbitrary nonconvex functions or invalid model domains; safeguards and assumptions still matter.

## 6. Stopping and diagnostics

Record at least the objective, gradient norm, step norm, iteration count, and termination reason. Useful stopping criteria include:

- Gradient norm below a scale-aware tolerance.
- Small parameter change **and** small objective improvement.
- Maximum iteration budget or line-search failure.
- Nonfinite objective, gradient, or proposed update.

A tiny parameter step alone does not certify optimality: it can result from a tiny learning rate or numerical stagnation. Likewise, a low gradient norm at a saddle does not certify a minimum.

## 9. Twenty worked exercises

**1.** Write the gradient descent update.  
*Answer:* \(x_{k+1}=x_k-\alpha_k\nabla f(x_k)\).

**2.** Why use the negative gradient?  
*Answer:* It is a local descent direction when the gradient is nonzero.

**3.** Differentiate \(\tfrac12a(x-b)^2\).  
*Answer:* \(a(x-b)\).

**4.** Find the quadratic minimizer for \(a>0\).  
*Answer:* \(x^*=b\).

**5.** Derive the error recurrence.  
*Answer:* \(e_{k+1}=(1-\alpha a)e_k\).

**6.** Give the scalar stability interval.  
*Answer:* \(0<\alpha<2/a\).

**7.** What happens at \(\alpha=1/a\)?  
*Answer:* One-step convergence for this exact quadratic.

**8.** What happens at \(\alpha=2/a\)?  
*Answer:* The nonzero error alternates without shrinking.

**9.** What happens for \(\alpha>2/a\)?  
*Answer:* Nonzero errors diverge in magnitude.

**10.** Find the normalized calibration minimizer.  
*Answer:* \(r^*=0.05\).

**11.** Differentiate \((r-0.05)^2\).  
*Answer:* \(2(r-0.05)\).

**12.** Starting at zero with \(\alpha=0.25\), find \(r_1\).  
*Answer:* \(0.025\).

**13.** Find \(r_2\).  
*Answer:* \(0.0375\).

**14.** Find \(r_3\).  
*Answer:* \(0.04375\).

**15.** Find \(r_4\).  
*Answer:* \(0.046875\).

**16.** Find the gradient magnitude at \(r_2\).  
*Answer:* \(0.025\).

**17.** State the multivariable quadratic constant-step condition.  
*Answer:* \(0<\alpha<2/\lambda_{\max}(H)\).

**18.** What is the effect of a large condition number?  
*Answer:* Slower convergence along poorly conditioned directions.

**19.** Why is a small parameter step not sufficient to claim convergence?  
*Answer:* It may reflect an excessively small learning rate or stagnation.
