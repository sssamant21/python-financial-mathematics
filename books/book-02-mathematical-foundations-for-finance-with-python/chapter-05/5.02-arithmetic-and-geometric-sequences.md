## Learning objectives

## 1. Arithmetic sequences: constant differences

A sequence \((a_n)_{n=0}^N\) is arithmetic when successive terms differ by a constant \(d\):

\[
a_{n+1}-a_n=d.
\]

Repeated substitution gives

\[
\boxed{a_n=a_0+nd}.
\]

For example, a hypothetical monthly contribution schedule that starts at 100 and increases by 10 each month has payments \(C_k=100+10(k-1)\), for \(k=1,2,\ldots\). The first payment is \(C_1=100\), not \(C_0\).

### Finite arithmetic sums

Pair the first and last terms:

\[
S_N=\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}(a_0+nd)
=\boxed{\frac{N}{2}\left(2a_0+(N-1)d\right)}.
\]

The sum contains **N terms**, indexed 0 through \(N-1\). For payments \(100,110,120,130\), the total is \(460\).

The formula remains valid when \(d=0\), when all terms are equal.

## 2. Geometric sequences: constant ratios

A sequence is geometric when every term is multiplied by a fixed factor \(q\):

\[
g_{n+1}=qg_n,\qquad \boxed{g_n=g_0q^n}.
\]

When \(g_n\ne0\), the ratio \(g_{n+1}/g_n=q\). A zero starting value produces an all-zero sequence, so a ratio inferred from its terms is not defined even though the recurrence is.

For a balance with a constant periodic return \(i>-1\), \(q=1+i>0\), giving

\[
B_n=B_0(1+i)^n.
\]

If \(i=0.02\), then \(q=1.02\): each completed period multiplies the balance by 1.02.

### Finite geometric sums

For \(q\ne1\),

\[
S_N=\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}g_0q^n
=\boxed{g_0\frac{q^N-1}{q-1}}.
\]

To derive it, subtract \(S_N\) from \(qS_N\); the intermediate terms cancel:

\[
(q-1)S_N=g_0(q^N-1).
\]

For \(q=1\), every term is \(g_0\), so \(\boxed{S_N=Ng_0}\).

The finite formula is valid for any real \(q\), including zero and negative ratios. A financial *positive growth factor* model, however, normally restricts \(q>0\).

## 3. Financial applications

**Constant deposits without interest:** A deposit sequence of 100, 110, 120, and 130 has an arithmetic pattern and total deposits of 460.

**Compounding without additional cash flows:** A balance of 1000 earning 5% per completed period follows a geometric pattern:

\[
B_0=1000,\ B_1=1050,\ B_2=1102.50,\ B_3=1157.625.
\]

**Fixed end-of-period deposits with interest:** Suppose \(C\) is deposited at the end of each of \(N\) periods and the per-period rate is \(i>-1\). The accumulated value immediately after payment \(N\) is

\[
FV_N=C\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}(1+i)^k.
\]

For \(i\ne0\),

\[
\boxed{FV_N=C\frac{(1+i)^N-1}{i}},
\]

and for \(i=0\), \(\boxed{FV_N=CN}\).

This formula assumes the first contribution earns interest for \(N-1\) periods and the final contribution earns no interest before the measurement point. Deposits made at the beginning of each period have a different accumulation factor.

## 4. Numerical stability for small rates

The formula \(((1+i)^N-1)/i\) can lose precision when \(i\) is close to zero. Use

\[
\boxed{\frac{\operatorname{expm1}(N\operatorname{log1p}(i))}{i}}
\]

when \(i\ne0\) and \(i>-1\). At exactly zero, use \(N\). The mathematical limit as \(i\to0\) is \(N\).

For a geometric sum with a positive ratio \(q\) near one, set \(i=q-1\). Note that converting a previously rounded \(q\) back into \(i\) cannot recover precision already lost in the input.

## 5. Indexing and common errors

- \(a_n=a_0+nd\) is zero-based; a one-based sequence is \(a_k=a_1+(k-1)d\).
- \(\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\) has \(N\) terms; \(\sum_{n=0}^{N}\) has \(N+1\) terms.
- A sequence with constant **absolute** changes is arithmetic; one with constant **multiplicative** changes is geometric.
- The final balance after \(N\) completed periods is not the same quantity as the sum of the balances observed over those periods.
- The accumulation of fixed end-of-period deposits is a geometric sum of **cash-flow contributions**, not a simple geometric balance path when contributions are present.

## 7. Exercises

**1.** Find the fifth term (index 4) of an arithmetic sequence with \(a_0=7\) and \(d=3\).

**2.** Find the sum of the first 10 terms of \(a_n=5+2n\), starting at \(n=0\).

**3.** Find \(g_4\) if \(g_0=100\) and \(q=1.05\).

**4.** Derive the finite geometric-sum formula by multiplying \(S_N\) by \(q\) and subtracting.

**5.** Find the sum of four terms \(3,6,12,24\).

**6.** Calculate the future value immediately after three end-of-period deposits of 100 at 10% per period.

**7.** What is the future value of three deposits of 100 if the periodic rate is zero?

**8.** Explain why \`expm1(N*log1p(i))/i\` is useful for very small nonzero \(i\).

### Worked solutions

1. \(a_4=7+4(3)=19\).
2. \(S_{10}=10[2(5)+9(2)]/2=140\).
3. \(g_4=100(1.05)^4\approx121.550625\).
4. \((q-1)S_N=g_0(q^N-1)\), so \(S_N=g_0(q^N-1)/(q-1)\) for \(q\ne1\).
5. \(3+6+12+24=45\).
6. \(100(1.1^2+1.1+1)=331\).
7. \(100(3)=300\).
8. It avoids cancellation in \(q^N-1\) when \(q=1+i\) is close to one, and preserves accuracy for small changes.
