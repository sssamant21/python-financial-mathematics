## Learning objectives

## 1. The variable-rate recurrence

Let \(B_{n-1}\) denote the opening balance, \(i_n>-1\) the return for period \(n\), and \(C_n\) the signed cash flow. Deposits are positive; withdrawals are negative. If the cash flow arrives **at the end** of the period,

\[
\boxed{B_n=(1+i_n)B_{n-1}+C_n.}
\]

Set \(q_n=1+i_n\). Expanding two periods:

\[
B_1=q_1B_0+C_1,\qquad
B_2=q_2q_1B_0+q_2C_1+C_2.
\]

Rates must match the modeled period duration. A quoted annual rate is not automatically a monthly rate.

## 2. General weighted-cash-flow formula

Repeated substitution yields

\[
\boxed{B_N=B_0\prod_{j=1}^{N}q_j+
\sum_{k=1}^{N}C_k\prod_{j=k+1}^{N}q_j.}
\]

An empty product is one. The final end-of-period payment \(C_N\) earns no additional return before \(B_N\) is measured. Earlier cash flows experience only the returns credited after their arrival.

For **beginning-of-period** payments, the recurrence and its expansion become

\[
B_n=(B_{n-1}+C_n)q_n,
\]

\[
\boxed{B_N=B_0\prod_{j=1}^{N}q_j+
\sum_{k=1}^{N}C_k\prod_{j=k}^{N}q_j.}
\]

Here each payment earns the return of its own period.

## 3. Worked ledger

Starting from \(B_0=1000\), use rates \(2\%,-1\%,3\%\) and end-of-period cash flows \(+100,-50,0\).

| Period | Opening | Rate | Interest | Cash flow | Closing |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | 1000.000 | 2% | 20.000 | +100 | 1120.000 |
| 2 | 1120.000 | -1% | -11.200 | -50 | 1058.800 |
| 3 | 1058.800 | 3% | 31.764 | 0 | 1090.564 |

Thus \(B_3=1090.564\). For beginning-of-period flows with identical rates and cash amounts, the balances are \(1122\), \(1061.28\), and \(1093.1184\). The difference is solely the payment timing convention.

## 4. Reconciliation

Define period interest \(I_n=i_nB_{n-1}\) for end-of-period flows and \(I_n=i_n(B_{n-1}+C_n)\) for beginning-of-period flows. Both models satisfy

\[
\boxed{B_N-B_0=\sum_{n=1}^{N}I_n+\sum_{n=1}^{N}C_n.}
\]

In the worked end-of-period ledger, net cash flow is \(50\), total interest is \(40.564\), and balance change is \(90.564\). This identity is useful for audit and debugging; floating-point implementations should compare with a numerical tolerance.

## 5. Scheduling choices and boundaries

A schedule can represent changing monthly contributions, seasonal withdrawals, rate scenarios, or a planned contribution holiday. The model assumes abstract equal-length periods and period-specific effective rates. Actual dates, day-count conventions, compounding frequencies, transaction-level rounding, taxes, and fees require additional rules.

A negative computed balance is mathematically valid but may violate account restrictions. Decide explicitly whether to permit borrowing or reject an overdraft. The Python lab supports an optional no-negative-balance constraint.

Do not infer investment returns from changes in the balance alone: external deposits and withdrawals are separate components. An arithmetic mean of rates generally cannot reproduce a variable-rate path with irregular cash flows, because the order of returns matters.
