## Project objective

Build and verify a deterministic balance engine that handles variable periodic rates, signed contributions and withdrawals, cash-flow timing, liquidity constraints, and auditable period-by-period reconciliation.

This project integrates Sections 5.1–5.8. It is an educational calculation engine, **not** a banking ledger, investment forecast, or production financial system.

## 1. Requirements and assumptions

Each period is represented by a rate \(i_n>-1\) and signed external cash flow \(C_n\), with positive values for deposits and negative values for withdrawals. All periods have the same abstract duration. One timing convention applies to the whole schedule:

- **End:** \(B_n=(1+i_n)B_{n-1}+C_n\).
- **Beginning:** \(B_n=(B_{n-1}+C_n)(1+i_n)\).

The engine records opening balance, rate, cash flow requested and applied, interest, closing balance, and payment status. It must reject nonfinite numbers, mismatched schedules, invalid timing, and rates at or below -100%.

A policy flag selects whether negative balances are allowed. When they are forbidden, the project uses a **reject-and-stop** policy: an infeasible withdrawal is not applied, and no later periods are processed. Under end-of-period timing, interest for the failed period is credited before the rejected withdrawal; under beginning-of-period timing, the rejected withdrawal leaves the opening balance unchanged and no growth is processed for that failed period. The ledger records this convention explicitly.

The engine does not implement fees, taxation, day-count conventions, currency rounding, borrowing costs, or probabilistic returns.

## 2. Mathematical foundation

For end-of-period cash flows, write \(q_n=1+i_n\). Then

\[
B_N=B_0\prod_{j=1}^{N}q_j+
\sum_{k=1}^{N}C_k\prod_{j=k+1}^{N}q_j.
\]

For beginning-of-period cash flows,

\[
B_N=B_0\prod_{j=1}^{N}q_j+
\sum_{k=1}^{N}C_k\prod_{j=k}^{N}q_j.
\]

These expressions provide independent final-balance checks for schedules **without rejected payments**. When a payment is rejected, the original requested schedule is no longer the executed schedule; verification must use the applied flows and actual processed periods.

For every processed ledger row, define interest according to its timing convention. Then the telescoping reconciliation identity is

\[
\boxed{B_{\mathrm{final}}-B_0=
\sum \mathrm{interest}+\sum \mathrm{applied\ cash\ flow}.}
\]

The right side must use **applied**, not merely requested, payments.

## 3. Engine architecture

The companion [Python implementation](code/section_5_09.py) provides:

1. A \`Period\` data class with \`rate\` and \`cash_flow\`.
2. An \`EngineResult\` containing opening and closing balances, processed ledger rows, and the first rejected period.
3. \`run_engine(...)\` for end/begin timing and optional no-overdraft enforcement.
4. \`reconcile(...)\` for the ledger accounting identity.
5. \`closed_form_reference(...)\` for an independent product/sum calculation on an unrestricted schedule.

The reference implementation uses standard-library \`math\` and \`dataclasses\`. All numeric calculations use floating point. Real currency systems need an explicit decimal and rounding policy.

## 4. Scenario A — contributions with changing rates

Start with 1000, end-of-period rates \((0.02,-0.01,0.03)\), and signed cash flows \((100,-50,0)\).

| Period | Opening | Interest | Applied flow | Closing |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | 1000.000 | 20.000 | +100 | 1120.000 |
| 2 | 1120.000 | -11.200 | -50 | 1058.800 |
| 3 | 1058.800 | 31.764 | 0 | 1090.564 |

Reconciliation:

\[
1090.564-1000=90.564=(20-11.2+31.764)+(100-50).
\]

The product/sum reference must independently yield \(1090.564\).

## 5. Scenario B — first infeasible withdrawal

Start with 500, rates of 1% for three periods, and end-of-period withdrawals of 200 each.

- Period 1: \(500(1.01)-200=305\).
- Period 2: \(305(1.01)-200=108.05\).
- Period 3: available funds after interest are \(108.05(1.01)=109.1305\), so a withdrawal of 200 is infeasible.

Under the project’s reject-and-stop convention, the third withdrawal is rejected, the final balance is \(109.1305\), and the first rejected period is 3. Interest of \(1.0805\) for the third period is included in the ledger; no withdrawal is applied.

## 6. Scenario C — timing sensitivity

Starting with 100 and a 10% rate, request a withdrawal of 105.

- **End-of-period:** 110 is available after growth; payment is feasible and the closing balance is 5.
- **Beginning-of-period:** only 100 is available before growth; payment is rejected immediately.

This demonstrates why a financial model must make payment timing explicit.

## 7. Independent checks and acceptance criteria

The [reference checks](code/check_section_5_09.py) must verify:

- Constant and variable rates and signed cash flows.
- Both payment timing conventions.
- Agreement with the independent product/sum formula when all flows execute.
- Reconciliation of each completed ledger, including rejected-payment cases.
- First infeasible period, no-overdraft enforcement, and reject-and-stop behavior.
- Empty schedules, zero interest, exact exhaustion, and invalid input handling.

A draft passes **source-completeness review** when all of these cases are represented in code. **Runtime validation** requires actually executing the checks, recording the interpreter/version and results, and resolving any failures. **Canonical approval** additionally requires independent mathematical and editorial review.
