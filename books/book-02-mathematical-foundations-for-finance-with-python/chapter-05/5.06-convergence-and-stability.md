## Learning objectives

Classify convergence of affine recurrences; quantify error decay or amplification; derive a time-to-tolerance bound; analyze sensitivity to initial balances and one-time disturbances; distinguish mathematical stability from financial risk; and implement numerical verification.

## 1. Convergence of a sequence

A sequence \((B_n)\) converges to \(L\) if, for every tolerance \(\varepsilon>0\), there is an index \(N_\varepsilon\) such that \(|B_n-L|<\varepsilon\) for every \(n\ge N_\varepsilon\).

Consider the affine recurrence

\[
B_{n+1}=qB_n+C.
\]

If \(q\ne1\), its fixed point is \(B^*=C/(1-q)\). Subtracting the fixed-point identity gives

\[
e_{n+1}=qe_n,\qquad e_n=B_n-B^*,
\]

so

\[
\boxed{e_n=q^n e_0},\qquad
\boxed{B_n=B^*+q^n(B_0-B^*)}.
\]

Convergence is therefore governed by the magnitude of \(q\), not merely by the sign of the cash flow.

## 2. Stability classifications

For the general real-valued affine recurrence:

| Factor | Behavior for a nonzero initial deviation |
|---|---|
| \(|q|<1\) | Converges geometrically to the unique fixed point |
| \(q=1,C=0\) | Every balance is constant; neutral, not attracting |
| \(q=1,C\ne0\) | No fixed point; linear drift |
| \(q=-1\) | Alternates around the fixed point without converging, unless initially fixed |
| \(|q|>1\) | Deviations grow in magnitude; fixed point is unstable |

In the financial accumulation model \(q=1+i>0\), the relevant attracting case is \(-1<i<0\), giving \(0<q<1\). Positive periodic rates \(i>0\) give \(q>1\) and an unstable fixed point, even if the balance is exactly constant when initialized at equilibrium.

**Stability is a property of perturbations**, not simply whether one carefully selected path stays unchanged.

## 3. Convergence speed and half-life

When \(0<|q|<1\), the deviation magnitude after \(n\) periods is

\[
|e_n|=|q|^n|e_0|.
\]

For a target absolute tolerance \(\varepsilon>0\) with \(|e_0|>\varepsilon\), the condition \(|e_n|\le\varepsilon\) is equivalent to

\[
n\ge\frac{\ln(\varepsilon/|e_0|)}{\ln|q|}.
\]

Because \(\ln|q|<0\), the inequality reverses when dividing. The first integer index satisfying the non-strict tolerance is

\[
\boxed{n_{\min}=\left\lceil
\frac{\ln(\varepsilon/|e_0|)}{\ln|q|}
\right\rceil.}
\]

If \(|e_0|\le\varepsilon\), then \(n_{\min}=0\). If \(q=0\), any nonzero deviation disappears after one step.

The deviation half-life (real-valued number of periods) is

\[
\boxed{h=\frac{\ln(1/2)}{\ln|q|}},\qquad 0<|q|<1.
\]

For \(q=0.9\), \(h\approx6.58\) periods.

## 4. Worked example: approaching equilibrium

Let \(B_{n+1}=0.9B_n+50\), \(B_0=100\). The fixed point is \(B^*=500\), and

\[
B_n=500-400(0.9)^n.
\]

The initial deviation magnitude is 400. To get within 10 units of equilibrium, require

\[
400(0.9)^n\le10,
\]

giving \(n\ge\ln(10/400)/\ln(0.9)\approx35.01\). Therefore the first whole period within the tolerance is **36**.

## 5. Sensitivity to initial conditions

Consider two paths with the same \(q,C\) but different initial balances \(B_0\) and \(\widetilde B_0\). Their difference satisfies

\[
\boxed{B_n-\widetilde B_n=q^n(B_0-\widetilde B_0)}.
\]

Thus stable recurrences forget initial differences over time, while unstable recurrences amplify them. This statement assumes identical subsequent rates and cash flows; it is not a guarantee of robustness to all model errors.

## 6. One-time disturbances

Suppose an unexpected extra cash flow \(\delta\) is applied at the end of period \(k\). If the subsequent factor is constant \(q\), the resulting difference at time \(n\ge k\) is

\[
\boxed{\Delta B_n=q^{n-k}\delta}.
\]

For \(n<k\), the difference is zero. This expression isolates the effect of a single disturbance and is useful in stress testing and reconciliation.

For varying factors \(q_j\), the effect is instead \(\delta\prod_{j=k+1}^{n}q_j\).

## 7. Mathematical stability versus financial risk

A recurrence with \(0<q<1\) can converge mathematically even when the financial arrangement is unacceptable due to fees, inflation, liquidity constraints, or depletion risk. Conversely, a positive-return savings account may have \(q>1\), making a particular fixed point unstable while the balance-growth model itself is economically meaningful.

Practical financial decisions also depend on stochastic returns, variable contributions, taxation, withdrawals, transaction timing, and policy constraints. Constant-rate deterministic stability is a **model diagnostic**, not an investment recommendation.

## 8. Numerical concerns

For \(q\) extremely close to one, convergence may be slow. The ratio \(\ln(\varepsilon/|e_0|)/\ln|q|\) can become very large, and the fixed point \(C/(1-q)\) may be ill-conditioned. When working with \(q=1+i\), use \`log1p(i)\` for stable evaluation of \(\ln q\) if \(i\) is supplied directly.

An analytic tolerance estimate should be checked against the discrete inequality when floating-point rounding places the estimate close to an integer boundary.

## 10. Exercises

**1.** Define convergence of a real sequence.

**2.** Derive \(e_n=q^ne_0\) for an affine recurrence with a unique fixed point.

**3.** Classify \(q=0.9\), \(q=1.1\), and \(q=-1\).

**4.** For \(B_{n+1}=0.9B_n+50\) and \(B_0=100\), find \(B^*\) and \(B_3\).

**5.** Determine the first period when the path in Exercise 4 is within 10 units of equilibrium.

**6.** Calculate the deviation half-life for \(q=0.9\).

**7.** If two paths differ initially by 20 units and \(q=0.8\), how far apart are they after five periods?

**8.** A one-time cash-flow shock of 100 occurs at the end of period 2. What is its effect at the end of period 5 if \(q=1.02\)?

### Worked solutions

1. For each \(\varepsilon>0\), eventually every term lies within \(\varepsilon\) of the limit.
2. Subtract \(B^*=qB^*+C\) from \(B_{n+1}=qB_n+C\), then iterate.
3. \(q=0.9\) attracts; \(q=1.1\) repels; \(q=-1\) oscillates without convergence except at equilibrium.
4. \(B^*=500\), \(B_3=500-400(0.9)^3=208.4\).
5. 36 periods.
6. \(h=\ln(0.5)/\ln(0.9)\approx6.58\) periods.
7. \(20(0.8)^5=6.5536\) units.
8. \(100(1.02)^3=106.1208\).
