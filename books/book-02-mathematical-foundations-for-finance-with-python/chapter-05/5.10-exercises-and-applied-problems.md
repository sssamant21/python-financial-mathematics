## Chapter assessment

This cumulative problem set consolidates Sections 5.1–5.9: sequences and time indexing, arithmetic and geometric progressions, first-order balance recurrences, closed forms, fixed points, convergence, variable-rate schedules, withdrawal boundaries, and the chapter project.

**Conventions:** \(B_0\) is the initial balance, \(B_n\) the balance after \(n\) periods, \(i\) an effective per-period rate, and signed \(C\) a cash flow (positive deposit, negative withdrawal). Unless stated otherwise, payments occur at the **end** of each period. All computations are deterministic and ignore fees, taxes, inflation, rounding policies, and real-world return uncertainty.

## Part A — Mathematical foundations

**1. Sequence indexing.** Let \(B_0=100\) and \(B_{n+1}=B_n+25\). List \(B_0\) through \(B_4\), and derive \(B_n\).

**2. Geometric growth.** Let \(B_0=500\) and \(B_{n+1}=1.04B_n\). Derive \(B_n\) and compute \(B_3\).

**3. Affine recurrence.** Starting at \(B_0=1000\), earn 2% per period and contribute 100 at each period's end. Compute \(B_1,B_2\) by iteration.

**4. Closed form.** Derive \(B_n=q^nB_0+C(q^n-1)/(q-1)\) for \(q\ne1\), and explain the \(q=1\) case.

**5. Timing.** With \(B_0=1000\), \(i=0.05\), and \(C=100\), compute the one-period balance for beginning- and end-of-period contributions.

## Part B — Equilibria and convergence

**6. Withdrawal equilibrium.** For \(B_{n+1}=1.02B_n-100\), compute the fixed point and classify its stability.

**7. Stable equilibrium.** For \(B_{n+1}=0.9B_n+50\), find the fixed point and express \(B_n\) given \(B_0=100\).

**8. Tolerance.** For Exercise 7, find the first integer \(n\) satisfying \(|B_n-500|\le10\).

**9. Sensitivity.** Two paths share \(q=0.8\) and identical cash flows, but their initial balances differ by 20. Find the difference after five periods.

**10. One-time shock.** A deposit of 100 is added at the end of period 2 to an otherwise unchanged schedule with \(q=1.02\). What difference does this create at the end of period 5?

## Part C — Variable schedules and liquidity

**11. Variable-rate ledger.** Starting from 1000, use rates \(0.02,-0.01,0.03\) and end-of-period cash flows \(100,-50,0\). Compute the final balance and reconcile interest and cash flows.

**12. Beginning-of-period schedule.** Repeat Exercise 11 with all flows at period beginnings.

**13. Zero-rate depletion.** Starting with 500 and withdrawing 200 at the end of each period, find the first infeasible full withdrawal under a no-overdraft rule.

**14. Interest and depletion.** Starting with 500, earning 1% each period, and withdrawing 200 at each period end, find the first infeasible withdrawal and the available balance immediately before it.

**15. Exact exhaustion.** Starting with 1000 at zero interest and withdrawing 250 per period, how many full withdrawals are feasible? When does the next withdrawal fail?

**16. Timing and liquidity.** Starting with 100 and a 10% periodic return, determine whether a withdrawal of 105 is feasible at the beginning and at the end of the period.

## Part D — Python capstone and engineering judgment

**17. Engine reconciliation.** Explain why the reconciliation identity must use **applied** cash flows rather than **requested** cash flows when a withdrawal is rejected.

**18. Closed-form cross-check.** For end-of-period rates \(q_1,q_2\) and flows \(C_1,C_2\), derive \(B_2\) and identify which cash flow earns the second period's return.

**19. Input validation.** State four input conditions that the recurrence engine should reject and why.

**20. Modeling critique.** Explain why an algebraic equilibrium \(B^*=W/i\) for a positive constant return does not prove that a withdrawal plan is sustainable in practice.

## Worked solutions

**1.** \(100,125,150,175,200\). In general \(B_n=100+25n\).

**2.** \(B_n=500(1.04)^n\); \(B_3=562.432\).

**3.** \(B_1=1000(1.02)+100=1120\). \(B_2=1120(1.02)+100=1242.4\).

**4.** Substitute recursively to obtain \(B_n=q^nB_0+C\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}q^k\); evaluate the finite geometric sum. At \(q=1\), \(B_n=B_0+nC\).

**5.** End: \(1000(1.05)+100=1150\). Beginning: \((1000+100)(1.05)=1155\).

**6.** \(B^*=100/0.02=5000\); unstable because \(q=1.02>1\).

**7.** \(B^*=50/(1-0.9)=500\), and \(B_n=500-400(0.9)^n\); attracting because \(|q|<1\).

**8.** Solve \(400(0.9)^n\le10\). The first integer is \(n=36\).

**9.** \(20(0.8)^5=6.5536\).

**10.** \(100(1.02)^3=106.1208\).

**11.** Period balances: \(1120\), \(1058.8\), \(1090.564\). Total interest \(20-11.2+31.764=40.564\); net external cash flow \(50\); change \(90.564\).

**12.** Period balances: \(1122\), \(1061.28\), \(1093.1184\).

**13.** Period 3; only 100 remains after two full withdrawals.

**14.** Period 3; available after interest is \(108.05(1.01)=109.1305\), less than 200.

**15.** Four full withdrawals, ending exactly at zero; period 5 fails.

**16.** Beginning: infeasible because 100 is available. End: feasible because 110 is available; closing balance is 5.

**17.** Rejected requests do not change the executed balance. Only applied flows enter the telescoping identity \(B_N-B_0=\sum I_n+\sum C_n^{\mathrm{applied}}\).

**18.** \(B_2=q_2q_1B_0+q_2C_1+C_2\). The first period's end-of-period cash flow \(C_1\) earns the second period's return; \(C_2\) does not.

**19.** Examples: nonfinite values; nonnumeric or boolean amounts; rates \(\le-1\) when positive growth factors are required; invalid payment timing; negative initial balance under no-overdraft policy; mismatched rate and flow schedule lengths.

**20.** Real returns vary; timing, inflation, fees, taxes, liquidity restrictions, and sequence-of-returns risk are omitted. The constant-rate equilibrium is unstable for \(i>0\).
