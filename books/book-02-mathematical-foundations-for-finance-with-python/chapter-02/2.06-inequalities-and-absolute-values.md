## 2.6.1 Learning Objectives

After this section, you should be able to express an inequality's solution as a set, preserve or reverse its direction correctly during rearrangement, translate absolute-value conditions into intervals or unions, and use sign analysis for factored quadratic and rational expressions. You should also be able to intersect mathematical solutions with financial constraints and verify strict and non-strict boundaries exactly.

Section 2.5 found values where a quadratic equals zero. Here those values help divide the real line into regions where an expression has a particular sign. The question changes from “which values make equality hold?” to “which values satisfy a bound?”

Chapter 01 introduced constraints and intervals. This section develops the algebra needed to solve them systematically. Our examples use synthetic quantities and exact rational thresholds; they do not supply a contractual tolerance or trading policy.

## 2.6.2 Report a Solution Set, Not Just a Boundary

The inequality \(x\leq5\) has infinitely many real solutions. The boundary value 5 is only one of them. Write the solution as \(( -\infty,5]\) or \(\{x\in\mathbb R:x\leq5\}\).

A square bracket includes a finite endpoint; a parenthesis excludes it. Infinity is not a real endpoint and is never included. Thus \([3,+\infty)\) includes 3 but does not claim to include a number called infinity.

The difference between \(x<5\) and \(x\leq5\) is a policy distinction as well as a notation distinction. A maximum permitted amount often includes its boundary, while a condition stated as “strictly below” does not.

A singleton can be written \([3,3]=\{3\}\). The interval \((3,3)\) is empty. Keep the empty set separate from the singleton zero and from the whole real line; each represents a different result.

## 2.6.3 Rearrange Linear Inequalities by Sign

Adding or subtracting the same expression on both sides preserves an inequality's direction. Multiplying or dividing by a positive quantity preserves direction; multiplying or dividing by a negative quantity reverses it.

For example,

\[
-2x+10\leq4
\iff-2x\leq-6
\iff x\geq3.
\]

The solution is \([3,+\infty)\). For a strict original inequality, the final bound is also strict and the solution is \((3,+\infty)\).

More generally, solving \(ax+b\leq L\) requires three coefficient cases:

| Coefficient | Result |
|---|---|
| \(a>0\) | \(x\leq(L-b)/a\) |
| \(a<0\) | \(x\geq(L-b)/a\) |
| \(a=0\) | All reals if \(b\leq L\), otherwise empty |

Do not divide by a parameter before deciding its sign and whether it can be zero. The zero-coefficient branch is a valid mathematical outcome, not a numerical exception to hide.

## 2.6.4 Combine Conditions with Intersection or Union

A conjunction requires every condition, so its solution is an intersection. If \(x\geq2\) and \(x<7\), the solution is \([2,7)\). An alternative joined by “or” gives a union: \(x\leq2\) or \(x>7\) gives \(( -\infty,2]\cup(7,+\infty)\).

When intervals meet at an endpoint, check both inclusion rules. The intersection of \([0,1/2]\) and \([1/2,1]\) is the singleton \(\{1/2\}\). Replace the first interval with \([0,1/2)\), and the intersection becomes empty.

A financial domain is another set to intersect. If a calculation gives \(x\geq-3\) but the transaction amount must be nonnegative, the admissible result is \([0,+\infty)\). The algebraic set has not become wrong; it has been restricted by another requirement.

An intersection does not average conflicting bounds. If a minimum transfer is 500 and an independent cap is 400, their conjunction is empty. Returning 450 would satisfy neither requirement and has no justification as a compromise solution.

## 2.6.5 Absolute Value as Distance Within a Band

For a nonnegative threshold \(d\),

\[
|u|\leq d\iff-d\leq u\leq d.
\]

For a positive threshold, the strict version is \(|u|<d\iff-d<u<d\). The quantity \(|x-a|\) is distance from the center \(a\), so

\[
|x-a|\leq d\iff x\in[a-d,a+d].
\]

A weight target of 0.50 with absolute tolerance 0.10 gives interval \([0.40,0.60]\). Here tolerance means an explicit policy band in weight units, not a floating-point comparison tolerance. A width of 0.10 means ten percentage points of weight, not a relative 10% of the target.

At \(d=0\), \(|u|\leq0\) requires \(u=0\), while \(|u|<0\) is impossible. At \(d<0\), either “less than” version is impossible because absolute values are nonnegative. The algebra solver in the lab returns an empty set for these negative-threshold questions; a separate configuration interface might instead reject a negative policy tolerance.

## 2.6.6 Absolute Value Outside a Band

For \(d>0\),

\[
|u|\geq d\iff u\leq-d\ \text{or}\ u\geq d,
\]

and

\[
|u|>d\iff u<-d\ \text{or}\ u>d.
\]

The result is a union of outside regions, not the interval between the bounds. For example, \(|x-5|>2\) gives \(x<3\) or \(x>7\).

At zero threshold, \(|u|\geq0\) holds for all real values, while \(|u|>0\) excludes exactly those inputs where \(u=0\). With a negative threshold, both outside conditions hold for every real \(u\).

Negation can help verify boundary choices. The complement of \(|x-a|\leq d\) is \(|x-a|>d\); the boundary is excluded from the complement because it was included in the original set. Do not swap “and” and “or” without checking whether you are describing the inside band, the outside band, or its complement.

## 2.6.7 Solve an Absolute Affine Inequality

For \(|ax+b|\leq d\) with \(d\geq0\), write

\[
-d\leq ax+b\leq d.
\]

If \(a\ne0\), solve both endpoint equations and put the results in increasing order. For example,

\[
|-2x+4|\leq2
\iff-2\leq-2x+4\leq2
\iff1\leq x\leq3.
\]

The negative coefficient reverses the inequalities when dividing. Sorting the two derived endpoints is a convenient implementation technique after the algebra has established that the result is an inside interval.

If \(a=0\), the condition no longer depends on \(x\): check \(|b|\leq d\) and return all reals or empty. For strict conditions, use strict comparisons and open finite endpoints. The case \(|0x+0|<0\) is empty; \(|0x+0|\leq0\) is all reals.

These distinctions are easy to miss if a function assumes every band has positive width and a nonzero coefficient. The lab handles them explicitly and preserves exact rational endpoints.

## 2.6.8 Use Quadratic Roots to Analyze Signs

Revisit the synthetic profit model from Section 2.5:

\[
\pi(q)=-0.1(q-20)(q-80).
\]

The roots divide the real line into three open regions:

| Region | \(q-20\) | \(q-80\) | Profit sign |
|---|---|---|---|
| \(q<20\) | Negative | Negative | Negative |
| \(20<q<80\) | Positive | Negative | Positive |
| \(q>80\) | Positive | Positive | Negative |

Therefore \(\pi(q)\geq0\) gives \([20,80]\), while \(\pi(q)>0\) gives \((20,80)\). A capacity cap \(q\leq60\) restricts the first set to \([20,60]\). The model's nonnegative quantity and price requirements must also be retained; here they allow \(0\leq q\leq120\), which does not further narrow that intersection.

A repeated root does not necessarily change sign across its location. The expression \((x-3)^2\) is nonnegative on both sides of 3. Thus \((x-3)^2\leq0\) gives only \(\{3\}\), while the strict inequality \((x-3)^2<0\) has no real solutions. Use factor signs and multiplicities rather than assuming every root reverses the sign.

## 2.6.9 Rational Inequalities Retain Poles

Consider

\[
\frac{x-2}{x-5}\geq0,\qquad x\ne5.
\]

Critical values are numerator zero 2 and denominator zero 5. Below 2, both factors are negative, so their quotient is positive. Between 2 and 5 the quotient is negative. Above 5 it is positive. The solution is

\[
(-\infty,2]\cup(5,+\infty).
\]

Include 2 because the quotient is defined and equals zero. Exclude 5 because the original expression is undefined, even though the inequality is non-strict. For the strict version, exclude 2 as well.

Multiplying by \(x-5\) without its sign is invalid: it is positive on one region and negative on another. Either split the domain into sign cases or use a sign analysis of the original quotient. Denominator exclusions are not endpoints that become admissible merely by choosing a square bracket.

For a financial fee condition \(F/P+k\leq q\) with \(P>0\), multiplying by principal preserves direction because its sign is already known. That justification comes from the financial domain; it cannot be reused for a generic denominator of unknown sign.

## 2.6.10 Intersect a Weight Band with a Strict Cap

Let a single long-only weight \(w\) satisfy

\[
0\leq w\leq1,\qquad |w-0.50|\leq0.10,
\qquad w<0.55.
\]

The absolute band is \([0.40,0.60]\). Intersecting with the long-only domain and strict cap gives

\[
w\in[0.40,0.55).
\]

Weight 0.40 is allowed; 0.55 is excluded. A value of 0.5499 passes all three conditions, while 0.60 fails the cap despite meeting the band boundary. The cap cannot be ignored because the band itself was satisfied.

This is a scalar constraint example, not a full portfolio validation. Multiple weights may also need to sum to one, share consistent labels, and satisfy joint exposure requirements. The scalar intersection does not establish those additional properties.

All thresholds in the lab are exact rationals. It does not round a value back inside a strict cap or substitute numerical closeness for a policy inequality. Contractual rounding or increments would be additional rules to specify.

## 2.6.11 Worked Python Lab — Exact Interval Solutions

Save as [code/section_2_06.py](code/section_2_06.py). A dictionary represents one interval; `None` represents the empty set. An absent lower or upper endpoint inside an interval dictionary means unboundedness, not missing data. Infinite endpoints are always open.

```python
"""Exact linear bounds, absolute affine bands, and interval intersections."""
from fractions import Fraction


def rational(value):
    if type(value) not in (int, str, Fraction):
        raise TypeError("use an integer, rational string, or Fraction")
    return Fraction(value)


def interval(lower=None, upper=None, *, left_closed=True, right_closed=True):
    if type(left_closed) is not bool or type(right_closed) is not bool:
        raise TypeError("endpoint flags must be Boolean")
    lo = None if lower is None else rational(lower)
    hi = None if upper is None else rational(upper)
    lc, rc = left_closed and lo is not None, right_closed and hi is not None
    if lo is not None and hi is not None:
        if lo > hi or (lo == hi and not (lc and rc)):
            return None
    return {"lower": lo, "upper": hi, "left_closed": lc, "right_closed": rc}


def contains(region, value):
    x = rational(value)
    if region is None:
        return False
    lo, hi = region["lower"], region["upper"]
    left = lo is None or x > lo or (x == lo and region["left_closed"])
    right = hi is None or x < hi or (x == hi and region["right_closed"])
    return left and right


def intersect(left, right):
    if left is None or right is None:
        return None
    regions = (left, right)
    lowers = [r["lower"] for r in regions if r["lower"] is not None]
    uppers = [r["upper"] for r in regions if r["upper"] is not None]
    lo, hi = max(lowers) if lowers else None, min(uppers) if uppers else None
    lc = all(r["lower"] != lo or r["left_closed"] for r in regions)
    rc = all(r["upper"] != hi or r["right_closed"] for r in regions)
    return interval(lo, hi, left_closed=lc, right_closed=rc)


def linear_upper(*, coefficient, constant, limit, strict=False):
    if type(strict) is not bool:
        raise TypeError("strict must be Boolean")
    a, b, bound = map(rational, (coefficient, constant, limit))
    if a == 0:
        holds = b < bound if strict else b <= bound
        return interval() if holds else None
    endpoint = (bound - b) / a
    if a > 0:
        return interval(upper=endpoint, right_closed=not strict)
    return interval(lower=endpoint, left_closed=not strict)


def absolute_band(*, coefficient, constant, threshold, strict=False):
    if type(strict) is not bool:
        raise TypeError("strict must be Boolean")
    a, b, width = map(rational, (coefficient, constant, threshold))
    if width < 0:
        return None
    if a == 0:
        holds = abs(b) < width if strict else abs(b) <= width
        return interval() if holds else None
    lo, hi = sorted(((-width - b) / a, (width - b) / a))
    return interval(lo, hi, left_closed=not strict, right_closed=not strict)


def display(region):
    if region is None:
        return "empty"
    lo = "-inf" if region["lower"] is None else str(region["lower"])
    hi = "+inf" if region["upper"] is None else str(region["upper"])
    return ("[" if region["left_closed"] else "(") + lo + ", " + hi + ("]" if region["right_closed"] else ")")


def main():
    bound = linear_upper(coefficient=-2, constant=10, limit=4)
    band = absolute_band(coefficient=-2, constant=4, threshold=2)
    weights = absolute_band(coefficient=1, constant="-0.5", threshold="0.1")
    cap = linear_upper(coefficient=1, constant=0, limit="0.55", strict=True)
    feasible = intersect(intersect(weights, interval(0, 1)), cap)
    print("Negative-coefficient bound:", display(bound))
    print("Absolute affine band:", display(band))
    print("Weight band and strict cap:", display(feasible))
    print("Upper weight boundary accepted:", contains(feasible, "0.55"))
    print("Strict zero-width band:", display(absolute_band(
        coefficient=1, constant=0, threshold=0, strict=True)))
    # Profit roots and sign analysis were derived in the manuscript.
    profit_region = interval(20, 80)
    print("Nonnegative profit with capacity 60:", display(intersect(profit_region, interval(0, 60))))


if __name__ == "__main__":
    main()
```

```text
Absolute affine band: [1, 3]
Weight band and strict cap: [2/5, 11/20)
Upper weight boundary accepted: False
Strict zero-width band: empty
Nonnegative profit with capacity 60: [20, 60]
```

## 2.6.12 Practice Problems

1. Solve \(5-3x<11\) over the reals, then impose \(x\geq0\). Check the algebraic boundary and one point on each side.
2. Solve \(|2x-6|\leq4\), then intersect with \(x<4\). State the strictness of every final endpoint.
3. Solve \(|x-5|>2\), \(|x-5|\leq0\), and \(|x-5|<0\). Explain why the first is a union and the other two are not the same set.
4. For \(\pi(q)=-0.1(q-20)(q-80)\), find the set where profit is strictly positive under nonnegative quantity, nonnegative price \(12-0.1q\), and capacity \(q\leq60\). Are 20 and 60 included?
5. Solve \((x-2)/(x-5)\geq0\) and the corresponding strict inequality. Identify the numerator zero and the pole, and explain their different endpoint rules.
6. A long-only weight has target 0.60, absolute band width 0.15, and cap \(w\leq0.70\). Find the feasible interval. What happens if an added floor is \(w>0.70\), or instead \(w\geq0.70\)?

## 2.6.13 Worked Answers

1. Subtract five and divide by −3, reversing the inequality: \(x>-2\). At −2 the original sides are equal, so the strict condition fails; −3 fails and −1 passes. Intersecting with nonnegative inputs gives \([0,+\infty)\).
2. Rewrite \(-4\leq2x-6\leq4\), giving \(1\leq x\leq5\). The strict cap gives final interval \([1,4)\): the lower endpoint is included, the upper excluded.
3. The outside condition gives \(( -\infty,3)\cup(7,+\infty)\). Distance is greater than two on either outside region. The non-strict zero band gives the singleton \(\{5\}\); the strict zero band is empty because absolute value cannot be negative.
4. Strict positive profit gives \((20,80)\). Nonnegative price gives \(q\leq120\), and the capacity cap gives final set \((20,60]\). Quantity 20 is excluded because profit is zero there. Quantity 60 is included because profit is positive and capacity equality is allowed.
5. The non-strict set is \(( -\infty,2]\cup(5,+\infty)\). The strict set is \(( -\infty,2)\cup(5,+\infty)\). Numerator zero 2 is a defined zero, so it is included only in the non-strict case. Pole 5 is undefined and excluded from both.
6. The band is \([0.45,0.75]\). Intersecting with long-only and cap gives \([0.45,0.70]\). Adding \(w>0.70\) makes the conjunction empty; adding \(w\geq0.70\) leaves only \(\{0.70\}\). No averaging or rounding of conflicting constraints is justified.

## 2.6.14 Common Mistakes, Summary, and Sources

Common mistakes include forgetting a sign reversal, ignoring the zero-coefficient case, using “and” for an outside absolute-value region, replacing a strict boundary with a closed one, and including a rational pole because the inequality is non-strict. Another is assuming every quadratic root changes sign or that a scalar weight check validates a whole portfolio.

Solve on the original mathematical domain, identify boundaries and excluded points, and combine conditions with the correct set operation. Verify endpoints as well as representative interior points. Keep exact policy constraints distinct from numerical comparison tolerances and contractual rounding rules.
