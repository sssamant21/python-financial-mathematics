Unless specified otherwise, use exact rational arithmetic, clearly label units, and substitute each candidate into the **original** equation.

## 2.10.1 Learning outcomes and assessment rules

After completing these problems, you should be able to rearrange and validate algebraic expressions; track excluded denominator values; apply powers, roots, and sign restrictions; solve quadratic and simultaneous equations; interpret absolute values and inequality boundaries; and distinguish exact break-even equality from minimum feasible integer quantities.

**Rules:** Show intermediate reasoning; identify the domain before division or squaring; classify zero-denominator and degenerate cases; and avoid silently rounding an equality solution. For whole-item problems, quantities must be nonnegative integers. Monetary examples use one unspecified currency unit and no taxes or compounding unless explicitly stated.

## 2.10.2 Core exercises

**E01 — Equivalent forms.** Expand and simplify $3(2x-5)-2(x+4)$. Verify your result at $x=-2$ and explain why testing one point does not prove equivalence.

**E02 — Linear equation.** Solve $5(2x-3)=3x+20$ and check by substitution.

**E03 — Rational equation and exclusions.** Solve $(x+1)/(x-2)=3$. State the excluded input and check the candidate.

**E04 — Powers and roots.** Solve $x^2=49$ over (a) all real numbers and (b) nonnegative real numbers. Explain why the principal square root is not the entire real solution set.

**E05 — Fractional exponent.** Solve $x^{3/2}=27$ over the real domain. State why $x$ must be nonnegative.

**E06 — Quadratic equation.** Solve $q^2-7q+12=0$. If $q$ represents a quantity, are both roots feasible under $q\ge0$?

**E07 — Quadratic with no real roots.** Classify $x^2+4x+8=0$ over the reals using the discriminant.

**E08 — Linear inequality.** Solve $-3x+12\ge0$. Explain why the inequality direction changes when dividing by a negative number.

**E09 — Absolute-value tolerance.** Solve $|p-100|\le5$. Interpret the result as a permissible price interval.

**E10 — Two-equation system.** Solve $x+y=50$ and $2x+3y=130$. Check both equations.

**E11 — Singular system.** Classify $x+y=10$, $2x+2y=20$, and then replace the second right-hand side by $21$. Explain why a zero determinant alone is not enough to classify a system.

**E12 — Algebraic versus feasible allocation.** Solve $x+y=100$, $0.04x+0.06y=7$. Check the equations and determine whether $x,y\ge0$ is satisfied.

## 2.10.3 Financial applications

**A01 — Net revenue and unit analysis.** Net revenue is $R=pq-F$. Given $p=12$ currency/item, $q=100$ items, and $F=50$ currency, compute $R$ and identify the units of each term.

**A02 — Exact break-even.** A synthetic product has price $25$, variable cost $15$, and fixed cost $1200$. Find its exact break-even quantity and verify it in the original profit equation.

**A03 — Whole-unit profit threshold.** For the same product, find the minimum whole-item quantity needed for profit of **at least** $255$. Verify both the chosen quantity and one fewer item. Is there a whole-unit solution for profit **exactly** equal to $255$?

**A04 — Inverse price.** At quantity $150$, variable cost $15$ per item, fixed cost $1200$, and target profit $300$, solve for the unit price. Check it.

**A05 — Zero margin.** If price equals variable cost and fixed cost is $100$, classify exact break-even quantities. What if the target profit is $-100$?

**A06 — Sensitivity to equation scaling.** For $2x+3y=13$, compute the signed residual at $(0,0)$, then multiply the entire equation by $1/1000$ and compute the new residual. Explain why the candidate did not improve.

**A07 — Extraneous root.** Starting from $x=4$, multiply both sides by $x-2$. Solve the transformed equation and identify the introduced candidate.

**A08 — Domain-aware rearrangement.** Solve $pq-F=T$ for $p$ when $q>0$. Classify the special case $q=0$ without dividing by zero.

## 2.10.4 Integrated mini-case: target profit and independent verification

A hypothetical one-product operation has fixed cost $F=900$ currency, price $p=18$ currency/item, and variable cost $v=12$ currency/item. Assume quantity is a nonnegative integer.

1. Derive profit as a function of quantity.
2. Find exact break-even quantity and check the original equation.
3. Find the minimum whole-unit quantity needed to earn at least $125$ profit.
4. Evaluate profit at that quantity and one item below.
5. Solve for the selling price that yields profit $300$ at exactly $200$ items.
6. If variable cost rises to $18$ per item while price stays $18$, explain whether break-even is possible with fixed cost $900$.
7. List two model assumptions whose violation would limit the business interpretation of these results.

## 2.10.5 Companion solutions

The following solutions show the key derivation and verification. Attempt the problems before reading them.

|---|---|---|
| E01 | $4x-23$ | $6x-15-2x-8=4x-23$; at $x=-2$, both give $-31$ |
| E02 | $x=5$ | $10x-15=3x+20\Rightarrow7x=35$; both sides $35$ |
| E03 | $x=7/2$ | $x\ne2$; $x+1=3x-6\Rightarrow x=7/2$; ratio $3$ |
| E04 | $x=\pm7$ over reals; $x=7$ over $x\ge0$ | Squaring loses sign information |
| E05 | $x=9$ | For $x\ge0$, $(\sqrt{x})^3=27$, so $\sqrt{x}=3$ |
| E06 | $q=3$ or $4$ | $(q-3)(q-4)=0$; both nonnegative |
| E07 | No real solution | Discriminant $4^2-4(1)(8)=-16<0$ |
| E08 | $x\le4$ | Divide $-3x\ge-12$ by $-3$ and reverse direction |
| E09 | $95\le p\le105$ | Absolute distance from 100 is at most 5 |
| E10 | $(x,y)=(20,30)$ | $20+30=50$, $40+90=130$ |
| E11 | A line; then no solution | The second equation is respectively redundant or contradictory |
| E12 | $(x,y)=(-50,150)$ | Sum $100$, interest $-2+9=7$; fails nonnegativity |

### Financial application solutions

**A01.** $R=12(100)-50=1150$ currency units. Price times quantity and fixed cost are both currency, so subtraction is dimensionally valid.

**A02.** Margin $m=25-15=10$ currency/item. Break-even $q=1200/10=120$ items. Check: $(25-15)(120)-1200=0$.

**A03.** $10q-1200\ge255$ gives $q\ge145.5$. Minimum whole quantity is $146$; profit at $145$ is $250<255$, and at $146$ is $260\ge255$. No whole-item quantity gives profit exactly $255$.

**A04.** $p=15+(1200+300)/150=25$. Check: $(25-15)(150)-1200=300$.

**A05.** When margin is zero, profit is $-100$ for every quantity. Break-even $T=0$ is impossible; exact target $T=-100$ holds for all nonnegative quantities.

**A06.** Original residual $-13$; scaled residual $-13/1000$. The solution set and candidate are unchanged. Raw residual magnitude is scale-dependent.

**A07.** $(x-4)(x-2)=0$ has roots $4$ and $2$. Only $4$ solves the original $x=4$; $2$ is extraneous.

**A08.** For $q>0$, $p=(T+F)/q$. If $q=0$, the original equation is $-F=T$ and every permitted price works when that equality holds; otherwise no price works.

### Integrated mini-case solutions

1. $\Pi(q)=(18-12)q-900=6q-900$.
2. $q=900/6=150$ items; check $6(150)-900=0$.
3. $6q-900\ge125$ implies $q\ge1025/6=170+5/6$, so minimum whole quantity is $171$.
4. $\Pi(171)=126\ge125$, while $\Pi(170)=120<125$.
5. $p=12+(900+300)/200=18$ currency/item; check $(18-12)(200)-900=300$.
6. Margin becomes zero, profit remains $-900$ at every quantity; break-even is impossible.
7. Examples: constant unit price and cost; every item produced is sold; no taxes; no capacity limit. Any two properly explained are acceptable.
