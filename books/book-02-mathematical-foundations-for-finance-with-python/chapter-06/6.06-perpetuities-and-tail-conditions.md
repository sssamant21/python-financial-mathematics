## Learning objectives

## 1. Perpetuities as infinite discounted sums

A perpetuity is an idealized payment stream that continues indefinitely. Its value is a limit of finite discounted sums, **not** an assumption that an issuer can actually pay forever.

For a constant payment \(C\) at the end of periods \(1,2,\ldots\) and an effective discount rate \(r>0\),

\[
PV=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{C}{(1+r)^k}.
\]

With \(q=(1+r)^{-1}\in(0,1)\), the series is geometric:

\[
\boxed{PV_{\mathrm{level}}=\frac{C}{r}.}
\]

For payments beginning immediately at time zero, the value is

\[
\boxed{PV_{\mathrm{due}}=\frac{C(1+r)}{r}.}
\]

The difference between these two values is the immediate payment \(C\).

## 2. Growing perpetuity

Let the first end-of-period payment be \(C_1\) and let subsequent payments grow at a constant effective rate \(g>-1\):

\[
C_k=C_1(1+g)^{k-1}.
\]

Discount at a constant effective rate \(r>-1\). The present value is

\[
PV=\frac{C_1}{1+r}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}
\left(\frac{1+g}{1+r}\right)^j.
\]

For nonzero \(C_1\), the series converges precisely when

\[
\left|\frac{1+g}{1+r}\right|<1.
\]

Since both growth factors are positive, this reduces to \(r>g\). Thus

\[
\boxed{PV_{\mathrm{growing}}=\frac{C_1}{r-g}}
\quad(r>g>-1).
\]

If \(r=g\), the discounted payments are constant and the infinite sum diverges. If \(r<g\), discounted payments grow and the series diverges. A zero first payment gives an identically zero stream; the usual nonzero-payment convergence condition is then unnecessary.

## 3. Deferred level perpetuity

Suppose a level payment \(C\) starts at the **end of period \(m+1\)**, with no payments in periods \(1,\ldots,m\). Its value at time \(m\) is \(C/r\), so its time-zero value is

\[
\boxed{PV_{\mathrm{deferred}}=\frac{C}{r}(1+r)^{-m}},
\]

where \(m\) is a nonnegative integer and \(r>0\). For \(m=0\), the first payment occurs at period 1 and the formula reduces to an ordinary perpetuity.

Be careful with language: “first payment at time \(m\)” and “deferred by \(m\) full periods” do **not** mean the same thing.

## 4. Deferred growing perpetuity

If the first payment \(C_{m+1}\) occurs at time \(m+1\), then subsequent payments grow at rate \(g\), and \(r>g>-1\),

\[
\boxed{PV_0=\frac{C_{m+1}}{r-g}(1+r)^{-m}}.
\]

The payment symbol refers to the **first actual payment after deferral**, not necessarily to an amount that would have been paid at time 1.

## 5. Exact tail after \(N\) payments

For a level perpetuity, the present value of payments at times \(N+1,N+2,\ldots\) is

\[
\boxed{T_N^{\mathrm{level}}=\frac{C}{r}(1+r)^{-N}}.
\]

For a growing perpetuity whose first payment is \(C_1\), the first omitted payment is \(C_{N+1}=C_1(1+g)^N\). The tail value at time \(N\) is \(C_{N+1}/(r-g)\), so

\[
\boxed{T_N^{\mathrm{growing}}=
\frac{C_1}{r-g}
\left(\frac{1+g}{1+r}\right)^N}.
\]

In both cases,

\[
PV_{\infty}=PV_{\text{first }N\text{ payments}}+T_N.
\]

The tail formula is an **exact identity** under the assumed model, not an empirical estimate.

## 6. Worked example: level perpetuity and deferral

For \(C=100\), \(r=0.05\), the ordinary perpetuity is worth

\[
PV=\frac{100}{0.05}=2000.
\]

If payments begin at the end of period 4, then \(m=3\) and

\[
PV_{\mathrm{deferred}}=2000(1.05)^{-3}
\approx1727.6752.
\]

After the first ten payments of the nondeferred stream, the remaining time-zero tail is

\[
T_{10}=2000(1.05)^{-10}\approx1227.8265.
\]

The first ten payments have present value \(2000-T_{10}\approx772.1735\).

## 7. Worked example: growing tail

Let \(C_1=100\), \(g=0.02\), \(r=0.05\). Since \(r>g\),

\[
PV_{\infty}=\frac{100}{0.03}\approx3333.3333.
\]

After \(N=5\) payments, the exact remaining present value is

\[
T_5=\frac{100}{0.03}\left(\frac{1.02}{1.05}\right)^5.
\]

The omitted tail is discounted to **time zero**, not to time five. The latter value is \(C_6/(r-g)\); multiplying by \((1+r)^{-5}\) converts it to time zero.

## 8. Convergence, horizon, and risk

A finite sum of discounted payments can be computed for any finite horizon with valid rates, even if the corresponding infinite sum diverges. A financial decision should not extrapolate a finite-horizon model to infinity without examining growth, discounting, payment sustainability, default, liquidity, and uncertainty.

A small positive difference \(r-g\) produces a large growing-perpetuity value and high sensitivity to parameter changes. This is a modeling warning, not a guarantee of value.
