## Learning objectives

Distinguish ordinary annuities from annuities due, derive present and future value formulas from finite geometric sums, handle zero rates, reconcile present and future values, and validate calculations in Python.

## 1. Payment timing

An **ordinary annuity** pays a fixed signed amount \(C\) at the **end** of each of \(n\) periods, at times \(1,\ldots,n\). An **annuity due** pays at the **beginning** of each period, at times \(0,\ldots,n-1\). The periodic effective rate \(i>-1\) is constant, and \(q=1+i>0\). Deposits or receipts are positive under the selected cash-flow perspective.

The valuation date is time zero for present value and time \(n\) for future value. These definitions assume regular periods, no fees, no default, and no intervening payment changes.

## 2. Ordinary annuity: future value

A payment made at time \(k\) accumulates for \(n-k\) periods. Hence

\[
FV_{\mathrm{ord}}=C\sum_{k=1}^{n}q^{n-k}
=C\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}q^j.
\]

Using the finite geometric-sum identity,

\[
\boxed{FV_{\mathrm{ord}}=C\frac{q^n-1}{i}}
\qquad (i\ne0).
\]

For \(i=0\), \(FV_{\mathrm{ord}}=nC\). An initial balance \(B_0\) adds \(B_0q^n\) to the total final balance.

## 3. Ordinary annuity: present value

Discount each end-of-period payment back to time zero:

\[
PV_{\mathrm{ord}}=C\sum_{k=1}^{n}q^{-k}.
\]

The finite geometric sum gives

\[
\boxed{PV_{\mathrm{ord}}=C\frac{1-q^{-n}}{i}}
\qquad (i\ne0).
\]

For \(i=0\), \(PV_{\mathrm{ord}}=nC\). The two ordinary-annuity values satisfy

\[
\boxed{FV_{\mathrm{ord}}=PV_{\mathrm{ord}}q^n.}
\]

This is a useful independent cross-check when discounting and compounding use the same rate and horizon.

## 4. Annuity due: present and future values

Each annuity-due payment occurs one period earlier than the corresponding ordinary-annuity payment, so it accumulates one extra period to time \(n\) and is discounted one fewer period to time zero:

\[
\boxed{FV_{\mathrm{due}}=q\,FV_{\mathrm{ord}}},\qquad
\boxed{PV_{\mathrm{due}}=q\,PV_{\mathrm{ord}}}.
\]

For \(i=0\), \(q=1\) and the timing difference disappears. For positive rates and positive payments, annuity-due values exceed ordinary-annuity values.

## 5. Worked example: savings contributions

Contribute \(C=100\) per period for \(n=3\) periods at an effective rate of \(i=0.02\). With \(q=1.02\),

\[
FV_{\mathrm{ord}}=100(1+1.02+1.02^2)
=\boxed{306.04}.
\]

For beginning-of-period contributions,

\[
FV_{\mathrm{due}}=306.04(1.02)=\boxed{312.1608}.
\]

The present values are

\[
PV_{\mathrm{ord}}=\frac{306.04}{1.02^3}
\approx288.3884,
\]

\[
PV_{\mathrm{due}}=1.02\,PV_{\mathrm{ord}}
\approx294.1562.
\]

Check the exact values with the Python lab rather than treating displayed rounding as authoritative.

## 6. Worked example: loan-payment valuation

Suppose a loan requires three end-of-period payments of 100 and has a periodic effective discount rate of 2%. Its time-zero payment-stream value is

\[
PV=100\left(\frac1{1.02}+\frac1{1.02^2}+\frac1{1.02^3}\right).
\]

The ordinary-annuity formula yields the same value. A loan's actual contractual payment may also include fees, insurance, different day-count conventions, or rounding; these are not part of this simplified valuation.

## 7. Payment solving

For a specified ordinary-annuity present value \(P\), the level end-of-period payment is

\[
\boxed{C=P\frac{i}{1-q^{-n}}}\quad(i\ne0,\ n>0).
\]

For \(i=0\), \(C=P/n\). For an annuity due, the payment required for the same \(P\) is \(C_{\mathrm{due}}=C_{\mathrm{ord}}/q\). If \(n=0\), there are no payments and the inverse payment problem is undefined for a nonzero target.

## 8. Numerical reliability

When \(i\) is near zero, direct evaluation of \(q^n-1\) and \(1-q^{-n}\) can suffer cancellation. Use \`log1p\` and \`expm1\`:

\[
FV_{\mathrm{ord}}=C\frac{\operatorname{expm1}(n\operatorname{log1p}(i))}{i},
\]

\[
PV_{\mathrm{ord}}=C\frac{-\operatorname{expm1}(-n\operatorname{log1p}(i))}{i}.
\]

At \(i=0\), return \(nC\). The companion lab checks finite inputs and outputs, rejects rates at or below -100%, and distinguishes zero payments from an invalid horizon.

## 10. Exercises

**1.** State the payment times for a three-period ordinary annuity and annuity due.

**2.** Derive the ordinary-annuity future value using a geometric sum.

**3.** Derive the ordinary-annuity present value.

**4.** Compute the future value of three end-of-period payments of 100 at 2%.

**5.** Compute the corresponding annuity-due future value.

**6.** What are both future values if the periodic rate is zero?

**7.** Show that \(FV_{\mathrm{ord}}=PV_{\mathrm{ord}}(1+i)^n\).

**8.** Derive the ordinary-annuity level payment from a given present value.

### Worked solutions

1. Ordinary: \(1,2,3\); due: \(0,1,2\).
2. \(C\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}q^j=C(q^n-1)/i\) for \(i\ne0\).
3. \(C\sum_{k=1}^nq^{-k}=C(1-q^{-n})/i\) for \(i\ne0\).
4. \(306.04\).
5. \(312.1608\).
6. Both \(300\).
7. Multiply \(C(1-q^{-n})/i\) by \(q^n\) to obtain \(C(q^n-1)/i\).
8. Rearrange \(P=C(1-q^{-n})/i\) to obtain \(C=Pi/(1-q^{-n})\), with \(C=P/n\) for \(i=0\).
