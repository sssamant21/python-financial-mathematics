## Learning objectives

Read and manipulate finite sums and products; distinguish indices and bounds; apply index shifts, splitting, and factorization; translate recurring cash flows and compounded returns into mathematical notation; and verify calculations in Python.

## 1. Why compact notation matters

Financial models involve repeated operations over periods. Instead of writing \(C_1+C_2+\cdots+C_N\), write

\[
\boxed{\sum_{k=1}^{N}C_k}.
\]

The symbol \(\sum\) means **add** the terms obtained by substituting every integer index from the lower bound through the upper bound. The index \(k\) is a *dummy variable*: \(\sum_{k=1}^{N}C_k=\sum_{j=1}^{N}C_j\), provided the indexed terms are renamed consistently.

The product symbol \(\prod\) means **multiply**:

\[
\boxed{\prod_{k=1}^{N}(1+i_k)
=(1+i_1)(1+i_2)\cdots(1+i_N).}
\]

Unlike addition, multiplication compounds returns across periods.

## 2. Index bounds and empty ranges

A finite sum includes both bounds. For example,

\[
\sum_{k=2}^{4}k^2=2^2+3^2+4^2=29.
\]

A product likewise includes both bounds:

\[
\prod_{k=2}^{4}k=2\cdot3\cdot4=24.
\]

An **empty sum** is defined as zero; an **empty product** is defined as one. These conventions make formulas work at \(N=0\) and simplify cash-flow expressions when a final payment has no subsequent periods to accumulate.

For \(N=0\),

\[
\sum_{k=1}^{0}C_k=0,\qquad
\prod_{k=1}^{0}(1+i_k)=1.
\]

## 3. Rules for finite sums

For constants \(a,b\),

\[
\sum_{k=m}^{n}(a x_k+b y_k)
=a\sum_{k=m}^{n}x_k+b\sum_{k=m}^{n}y_k.
\]

A constant summed over \(N\) periods contributes \(Nc\):

\[
\sum_{k=1}^{N}c=Nc.
\]

Adjacent ranges may be split:

\[
\sum_{k=1}^{N}x_k=
\sum_{k=1}^{M}x_k+\sum_{k=M+1}^{N}x_k
\quad(0\le M\le N).
\]

Reindexing preserves a sum when terms and bounds are shifted together:

\[
\sum_{k=1}^{N}x_k=\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}x_{j+1}.
\]

A common mistake is changing the index without updating the term or bounds.

## 4. Rules for finite products

Products of nonzero factors can be split into adjacent ranges:

\[
\prod_{k=1}^{N}q_k=
\left(\prod_{k=1}^{M}q_k\right)
\left(\prod_{k=M+1}^{N}q_k\right).
\]

For a constant factor \(q\),

\[
\prod_{k=1}^{N}q=q^N.
\]

For positive factors, logarithms turn products into sums:

\[
\ln\left(\prod_{k=1}^{N}q_k\right)=\sum_{k=1}^{N}\ln q_k.
\]

This identity is useful for analyzing long return sequences. It requires \(q_k>0\) for real logarithms.

**Do not distribute a product over a sum:** generally \(\prod_k(a_k+b_k)\ne\prod_k a_k+\prod_k b_k\).

## 5. Financial application: cash-flow totals

Let \(C_k\) be a signed end-of-period cash flow, positive for deposits and negative for withdrawals. The net external flow over \(N\) periods is

\[
\boxed{C_{\mathrm{net}}=\sum_{k=1}^{N}C_k}.
\]

For \((C_1,C_2,C_3)=(100,-40,60)\), the net flow is \(120\). This does **not** equal the final balance change if interest is earned or charged; interest must be reconciled separately.

## 6. Financial application: variable-rate growth

For a balance with no external flows, \(B_k=(1+i_k)B_{k-1}\), hence

\[
\boxed{B_N=B_0\prod_{k=1}^{N}(1+i_k)}.
\]

Starting with \(1000\), rates \(2\%,-1\%,3\%\) produce

\[
B_3=1000(1.02)(0.99)(1.03)=1040.094.
\]

The order of returns does not affect this **no-cash-flow** product, but it generally matters when cash flows are inserted between periods.

## 7. Financial application: future value of deposits

For signed cash flows arriving at period ends and \(q_j=1+i_j\),

\[
\boxed{B_N=B_0\prod_{j=1}^{N}q_j+
\sum_{k=1}^{N}C_k\prod_{j=k+1}^{N}q_j.}
\]

The outer sum combines each payment's future value; the inner product compounds that payment only over subsequent periods. The empty-product convention gives the final payment a weight of one.

For \(B_0=1000\), rates \((0.02,-0.01)\), and end-of-period flows \((100,-50)\),

\[
B_2=1000(1.02)(0.99)+100(0.99)-50=1058.8.
\]

This formula bridges Chapter 05 recurrences and the geometric-series derivations ahead.

## 8. Numerical implementation

Python's built-in \`sum\` and \`math.prod\` directly express finite sums and products. \`math.fsum\` improves floating-point summation for mixed-magnitude or canceling terms. These functions do not make floating-point arithmetic exact; real accounting requires an explicit decimal and rounding convention.

**3.** State the values of an empty sum and empty product.

**4.** Reindex \(\sum_{k=1}^{N}C_k\) so the index begins at zero.

**5.** Compute the net flow for \((100,-40,60)\).

**6.** Compute the growth factor for rates \((0.02,-0.01,0.03)\).

**7.** Compute the no-flow ending balance from 1000 using Exercise 6.

2. \(1\cdot2\cdot3\cdot4=24\).
