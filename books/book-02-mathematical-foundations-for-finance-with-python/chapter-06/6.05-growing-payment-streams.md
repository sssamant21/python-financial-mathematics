## Learning objectives

Model geometrically growing payments; derive finite-horizon present and future values; handle the equal-growth-and-discount-rate limit; compare beginning- and end-of-period timing; and independently validate formulas in Python.

## 1. Define the payment schedule

Let the first payment be \(C_1\) at the **end of period 1**. Subsequent payments grow at a constant rate \(g>-1\):

\[
C_k=C_1(1+g)^{k-1},\qquad k=1,\ldots,n.
\]

Let the constant effective discount rate be \(r>-1\), and define \(d=1+r>0\), \(h=1+g>0\). Positive \(C_1\) represents a receipt under the chosen perspective; negative values represent outflows. A finite stream may be valued even if \(g>r\); the usual infinite growing-perpetuity formula has stricter conditions.

## 2. Present value of a finite growing stream

Each payment is discounted to time zero:

\[
PV_n=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{C_1h^{k-1}}{d^k}
=\frac{C_1}{d}\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\left(\frac{h}{d}\right)^j.
\]

This is a finite geometric sum with ratio \(h/d\). If \(r\ne g\),

\[
\boxed{PV_n=C_1\frac{1-\left(\frac{1+g}{1+r}\right)^n}{r-g}.}
\]

The expression is valid for finite \(n\ge0\), \(r,g>-1\), and \(r\ne g\). For \(n=0\), the present value is zero.

## 3. The equal-rate case

If \(r=g\), then \(h/d=1\). Every payment has the same present value \(C_1/d\), so

\[
\boxed{PV_n=\frac{nC_1}{1+r}}\qquad(r=g).
\]

This is the continuous limit of the unequal-rate formula as \(g\to r\). Dividing by \(r-g\) when they are equal is an algebraic error, not evidence of an infinite present value.

## 4. Future value

The future value at time \(n\) is the present value compounded for \(n\) periods:

\[
FV_n=d^nPV_n
=\sum_{k=1}^{n}C_1h^{k-1}d^{n-k}.
\]

Therefore, for \(r\ne g\),

\[
\boxed{FV_n=C_1\frac{d^n-h^n}{r-g}.}
\]

For \(r=g\),

\[
\boxed{FV_n=nC_1d^{n-1}}\quad(n\ge1),
\]

and \(FV_0=0\). The formulas can be checked by directly iterating the payment schedule.

## 5. Worked example: salary-linked contributions

Suppose the first end-of-period contribution is 100, payments increase 3% each period, and the discount rate is 5%, over three periods. The payments are

\[
100,\quad103,\quad106.09.
\]

The present value is

\[
PV_3=\frac{100}{1.05}+\frac{103}{1.05^2}+\frac{106.09}{1.05^3}
\approx280.3067.
\]

The future value is \(FV_3=PV_3(1.05)^3\approx324.49\).

Both results should agree with the geometric formula and an independent cash-flow loop.

## 6. Beginning-of-period growing payments

For a beginning-of-period stream, the first payment occurs at time zero, with subsequent payments at times \(1,\ldots,n-1\). Relative to the end-of-period schedule, every payment occurs one period earlier. Hence

\[
\boxed{PV_{\mathrm{begin}}=d\,PV_{\mathrm{end}}},\qquad
\boxed{FV_{\mathrm{begin}}=d\,FV_{\mathrm{end}}}.
\]

This timing relationship holds for the same \(n\) payment amounts, growth rate, discount rate, and valuation dates.

## 7. Growing perpetuity boundary

For \(C_1\ne0\), the infinite present value exists only if

\[
\left|\frac{1+g}{1+r}\right|<1.
\]

With \(r,g>-1\), both factors are positive, so this reduces to \(r>g\). In that case,

\[
\boxed{PV_\infty=\frac{C_1}{r-g}}.
\]

If \(r=g\), each discounted payment is constant and the series diverges for nonzero \(C_1\). If \(r<g\), discounted payments increase and the series diverges. Do not apply the growing-perpetuity formula to such cases.

## 8. Numerical stability near \(r=g\)

The quotient \((1-(h/d)^n)/(r-g)\) can lose precision when \(r\) and \(g\) are close. Define

\[
x=\log(1+g)-\log(1+r)
=\operatorname{log1p}(g)-\operatorname{log1p}(r).
\]

Then

\[
PV_n=\frac{C_1}{1+r}\,
\frac{\operatorname{expm1}(nx)}{\operatorname{expm1}(x)}
\]

when \(x\ne0\), with the ratio replaced by \(n\) when \(x=0\). This formulation is usually more stable than subtracting nearly equal large powers, though extreme inputs can still overflow.

## 10. Exercises

**1.** List the first four payments when \(C_1=100\) and \(g=0.02\).

**2.** Derive the finite growing-stream present-value formula from a geometric sum.

**3.** Compute \(PV_3\) when \(C_1=100\), \(g=0.03\), \(r=0.05\).

**4.** Find \(FV_3\) for Exercise 3.

**5.** Derive the equal-rate formula for \(r=g\).

**6.** Find the present value of three end-of-period payments when \(C_1=100\), \(r=g=0.04\).

**7.** Convert an end-of-period growing stream to beginning-of-period timing.

**8.** State the convergence condition for a growing perpetuity with \(r,g>-1\) and nonzero first payment.

### Worked solutions

1. \(100,102,104.04,106.1208\).
2. Factor out \(C_1/(1+r)\) and sum \(\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}((1+g)/(1+r))^j\).
3. \(100/1.05+103/1.05^2+106.09/1.05^3\approx280.3067\).
4. \(PV_3(1.05)^3\approx324.49\).
5. Each discounted payment equals \(C_1/(1+r)\), so \(PV_n=nC_1/(1+r)\).
6. \(3(100)/1.04\approx288.4615\).
7. Multiply either the end-of-period present value or future value by \(1+r\).
8. \(r>g\); otherwise the infinite discounted series diverges.
