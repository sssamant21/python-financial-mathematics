## Project objective

Build a standard-library Python valuation engine that derives and independently checks finite cash-flow series. The engine must handle level and growing payments, beginning- and end-of-period timing, zero and negative finite discount rates above -100%, and convergent infinite streams.

This is a mathematical verification project, not a trading, lending, or actuarial pricing system. It assumes constant per-period rates and regular payment intervals.

## 1. Requirements and conventions

Let \(C_1\) denote the first payment, \(g>-1\) the effective per-period payment growth rate, \(r>-1\) the effective per-period discount rate, and \(n\ge0\) the integer number of payments.

For end-of-period timing, payment \(k\) occurs at time \(k\), for \(k=1,\ldots,n\). For beginning-of-period timing, payment \(k\) occurs at time \(k-1\). In both cases, future value is measured at time \(n\).

A signed first payment is allowed. All input rates and payments must be finite. Finite values exist for every valid finite horizon; infinite values require separate convergence checks.

## 2. Mathematical derivation

For end-of-period growing payments,

\[
PV_n=\sum_{k=1}^n \frac{C_1(1+g)^{k-1}}{(1+r)^k}.
\]

If \(r\ne g\),

\[
\boxed{PV_n=C_1\frac{1-\left(\frac{1+g}{1+r}\right)^n}{r-g}}.
\]

If \(r=g\), the finite-sum limit is

\[
\boxed{PV_n=\frac{nC_1}{1+r}}.
\]

For both cases, \(FV_n=PV_n(1+r)^n\). Beginning-of-period payments have \(PV_{\rm begin}=(1+r)PV_{\rm end}\) and \(FV_{\rm begin}=(1+r)FV_{\rm end}\). A zero-payment horizon gives zero PV and FV.

A level annuity is the special case \(g=0\). At \(r=g=0\), both values are \(nC_1\).

For a nonzero infinite growing stream, convergence requires \(r>g>-1\), and

\[
\boxed{PV_\infty=\frac{C_1}{r-g}}.
\]

The exact time-zero tail after the first \(n\) end-of-period payments is

\[
\boxed{T_n=\frac{C_1}{r-g}
\left(\frac{1+g}{1+r}\right)^n}.
\]

The reconciliation identity is \(PV_\infty=PV_n+T_n\). An infinite level perpetuity requires \(r>0\) for nonzero payments.

## 3. Architecture

The companion [valuation engine](code/section_6_09.py) separates the analytic formula from an independently enumerated cash-flow reference:

- \`closed_form\`: stable geometric-factor calculation using \`log1p\` and \`expm1\`.
- \`direct_sum\`: explicit payment-by-payment discounting or compounding using \`math.fsum\`.
- \`verify\`: compare the two paths with combined absolute and relative tolerances, and verify the PV/FV relationship.
- \`perpetuity\` and \`tail\`: enforce convergence and reconcile a finite stream with its infinite counterpart.
- \`sample_report\`: print a compact, reproducible validation record.

The independent path deliberately enumerates the schedule rather than reusing the geometric closed form. The two implementations still share rate and timing assumptions; reviewers must separately inspect those assumptions.

## 5. Acceptance criteria

1. The engine accepts valid finite numeric inputs and nonnegative integer payment counts; it rejects booleans, nonfinite numbers, and invalid conventions.
2. It returns zero for an empty finite stream.
3. It agrees with independent cash-flow enumeration to specified numerical tolerances.
4. It preserves the time-\(n\) future-value convention for both timing modes.
5. It handles \(r=0\) and \(r=g\) without division by zero.
6. It rejects nonconvergent nonzero perpetuities.
7. It reconciles the first \(n\) payments plus the exact tail with the infinite value.
8. It reports unresolved runtime, review, and model-assumption limitations accurately.

## 6. Failure investigation guide

**Formula differs from direct sum:** check whether the first payment occurs at time zero or one, whether the first payment is grown prematurely, and whether the same rate is used for every period.

**PV/FV identity fails:** check the valuation horizon. Both ordinary and due future values in this project are evaluated at time \(n\).

**NaN or overflow:** inspect the growth and discount rates, horizon, and intermediate exponentials. Extreme values can exceed floating-point range even when the symbolic expression is meaningful.

**A perpetuity is rejected:** check the convergence condition \(r>g\). A finite stream remains valid even if its infinite extension is divergent.

**Close-but-not-identical outputs:** compare against a justified absolute/relative tolerance; do not require bit-for-bit equality between algebraically equivalent floating-point algorithms.

**8.** Write a reviewer checklist covering payment timing, compounding frequency, rate basis, sign convention, and economic assumptions.

### Solution guidance

1. Serialize only finite outputs and include explicit verification tolerances.
2. Fix the random seed and reject cases where intermediate values exceed float range.
3. Use actual payment times \(t_k\) and evaluate \(\sum C_k(1+r)^{-t_k}\) with a clearly stated effective rate basis.
4. Solve \(|C_1/(r-g)|q^n\le\delta\) for \(n\), where \(q=(1+g)/(1+r)\in(0,1)\).
5. Use the linearity of summation.
6. The formula is linear in the signed first payment.
7. Compare both methods with an analytic benchmark.
8. Reject mismatched period units before performing any numerical comparison.
