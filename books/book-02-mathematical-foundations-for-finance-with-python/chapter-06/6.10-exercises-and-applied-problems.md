## Chapter assessment

This final section integrates Sections 6.1–6.9: summation notation, finite and infinite geometric series, ordinary and due annuities, growing streams, perpetuities, exact tails, truncation budgets, zero-rate limits, and independent numerical verification.

**Conventions.** Unless stated otherwise, rates are effective per payment period, first payments are end-of-period at time 1, and present value is measured at time 0. Future value of an n-payment stream is measured at time n. Amounts may be signed; r and g must exceed -1 for finite growing streams. Show equations, substitution, units, and an independent check.

## Part A — Derivation and fundamentals

### Problem 1 — Summation notation
Expand \(\sum_{k=1}^{4} 50(1.02)^{k-1}\) and identify the payment at time 3.

**Solution.** The terms are \(50+51+52.02+53.0604=206.0804\). The third payment is 52.02.

### Problem 2 — Finite geometric series
Derive \(\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}aq^k=a(1-q^n)/(1-q)\) for \(q\ne1\), and state the value when \(q=1\).

**Solution.** Multiply \(S_n\) by \(q\), subtract, and obtain \((1-q)S_n=a(1-q^n)\). When \(q=1\), all n terms equal a, giving \(S_n=na\).

### Problem 3 — Infinite geometric series
Find the sum of \(\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}120(0.75)^k\). Explain why the limit exists.

**Solution.** The ratio has magnitude below one, so the sum converges to \(120/(1-0.75)=480\).

### Problem 4 — Divergence boundary
Does \(\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}10(1.01)^k\) converge?

**Solution.** No. Its terms do not approach zero; the ratio exceeds one.

## Part B — Annuity calculations

### Problem 5 — Ordinary annuity PV
Find the present value of three payments of 100 at a 5% discount rate.

**Solution.**

\[
PV=\frac{100}{1.05}+\frac{100}{1.05^2}+\frac{100}{1.05^3}
\approx272.3248.
\]

### Problem 6 — Ordinary annuity FV
Find the future value of the same payments at time 3.

**Solution.** \(FV=100(1.05^2+1.05+1)=315.25\). Check that \(PV(1.05)^3=315.25\).

### Problem 7 — Annuity due
Shift the three payments in Problem 5 to times 0, 1, and 2. Find PV and FV at time 3.

**Solution.** \(PV_{\rm due}=1.05PV_{\rm ordinary}\approx285.9410\). \(FV_{\rm due}=1.05(315.25)=331.0125\).

### Problem 8 — Zero interest
Find PV and FV of twelve payments of 80 when \(r=0\), for both ordinary and due timing.

**Solution.** All four values are \(12(80)=960\). The timing shift has no effect at zero rate.

## Part C — Growing cash flows

### Problem 9 — Growing annuity
Value end-of-period payments \(100,103,106.09\) at a 5% discount rate.

**Solution.**

\[
PV=\frac{100}{1.05}+\frac{103}{1.05^2}+\frac{106.09}{1.05^3}.
\]

The equivalent closed form is \(100[1-(1.03/1.05)^3]/(0.05-0.03)\). Calculate numerically and reconcile both methods in the lab.

### Problem 10 — Equal growth and discount
Find the present value of five end-of-period payments with \(C_1=100\) and \(g=r=0.04\).

**Solution.** Every payment has discounted value \(100/1.04\), so \(PV=5(100)/1.04\approx480.7692\).

### Problem 11 — Growth greater than discount
For \(C_1=100\), \(g=0.08\), \(r=0.03\), and \(n=4\), does a finite present value exist?

**Solution.** Yes. The finite sum of four discounted payments is well defined. The corresponding infinite stream would diverge because \(g>r\).

### Problem 12 — Negative discount rate
Explain whether a finite stream can be valued at \(r=-0.02\).

**Solution.** Yes, since \(1+r=0.98>0\). Payments further in the future have a larger present-value multiplier, and a separate economic justification for the negative rate is needed.

## Part D — Perpetuities and tails

### Problem 13 — Level perpetuity
Find the value of a level perpetuity paying 150 per period at \(r=0.06\).

**Solution.** \(PV=150/0.06=2500\).

### Problem 14 — Growing perpetuity
Find the value of a perpetuity with first payment 150, growth 2%, and discount 6%.

**Solution.** Because \(0.06>0.02\), \(PV=150/(0.06-0.02)=3750\).

### Problem 15 — Deferred perpetuity
The first level payment of 150 occurs at the end of period 4, with \(r=0.06\). Find its value at time zero.

**Solution.** The value at time 3 is \(150/0.06=2500\). Discount three periods: \(PV_0=2500/(1.06)^3\).

### Problem 16 — Exact growing tail
For Problem 14, calculate the time-zero value of payments beginning at period 6.

**Solution.** Five payments have been completed. The exact tail is

\[
T_5=3750\left(\frac{1.02}{1.06}\right)^5.
\]

The tail plus the first-five-payment present value must equal 3750.

## Part E — Error budgets and verification

### Problem 17 — Relative truncation error
For a geometric series with ratio \(q=0.8\), determine the relative error after ten terms.

**Solution.** The relative error is \(|q|^{10}=0.8^{10}\approx0.1073742\), or 10.73742%.

### Problem 18 — Minimum horizon
For the same series, determine the smallest integer number of terms yielding relative error at most 1%.

**Solution.** Solve \(0.8^n\le0.01\). Thus \(n\ge\ln(0.01)/\ln(0.8)\approx20.64\), so **21 terms** are required.

### Problem 19 — Verification design
Specify three checks for an implementation of growing-annuity PV.

**Solution.** (1) Compare with independently enumerated discounted payments. (2) Verify \(FV=PV(1+r)^n\). (3) Test exact \(r=g\), exact \(r=0\), and small rate differences without division by zero.

### Problem 20 — Model-risk review
Explain why a mathematically convergent perpetuity is not necessarily a realistic valuation.

**Solution.** Convergence does not establish payment sustainability, creditworthiness, stable growth and discount rates, or the absence of default, tax, inflation, and liquidity risks.

## Applied capstone — Savings and long-horizon valuation

A hypothetical fund receives a first end-of-year contribution of 1,000, growing by 3% annually for 10 years. Its effective annual return is 5%.

**Tasks.** (a) Enumerate the ten contributions. (b) Compute their PV and time-10 FV with a closed form. (c) Recalculate with a payment-by-payment loop. (d) Shift the same ten payments to beginning-of-year timing and reconcile by a factor of 1.05. (e) Explain why the infinite extension is mathematically convergent at these rates, while the ten-year fund is finite. (f) Compute the infinite extension's exact tail after ten payments and verify the reconciliation identity.

**Solution outline.** With \(C_1=1000\), \(g=0.03\), \(r=0.05\), and \(n=10\),

\[
PV_{10}=1000\frac{1-(1.03/1.05)^{10}}{0.05-0.03},
\quad FV_{10}=PV_{10}(1.05)^{10}.
\]

The due values are 1.05 times their ordinary counterparts. The infinite PV is \(1000/0.02=50000\), and its exact omitted time-zero tail is \(50000(1.03/1.05)^{10}\). The finite PV plus this tail equals 50000.
