## Learning objectives

Compute second and higher derivatives, interpret concavity and curvature, distinguish candidate inflection points from actual changes in concavity, analyze marginal cost and profit, and calculate the second-order rate sensitivity of discounted cash flows.

## 1. Higher derivatives

If \(f'\) is differentiable, its derivative is the **second derivative**, written \(f''(x)\) or \(d^2f/dx^2\). Repeated differentiation gives \(f^{(3)}(x)\), \(f^{(4)}(x)\), and so on.

For \(f(x)=3x^4-2x^3+5x-7\),

\[
f'(x)=12x^3-6x^2+5,\quad
f''(x)=36x^2-12x,\quad
f^{(3)}(x)=72x-12,\quad
f^{(4)}(x)=72.
\]

The first derivative describes a local slope; the second describes how that slope changes.

## 2. Concavity and inflection points

On an interval where \(f''\) exists:

- \(f''(x)>0\) throughout the interval implies the graph is **concave up** there.
- \(f''(x)<0\) throughout the interval implies **concave down**.
- A point is an **inflection point** when concavity changes across it.

A zero second derivative is only a candidate for an inflection point, not a guarantee. For \(f(x)=x^4\), \(f''(0)=0\) but \(f''(x)=12x^2\ge0\) on both sides, so zero is not an inflection point. For \(f(x)=x^3\), \(f''(x)=6x\) changes sign at zero, so zero is an inflection point.

A function may have an inflection point where its second derivative does not exist; the defining condition is the change in concavity.

## 3. Marginal cost and profit

Let total cost be

\[
C(q)=1000+20q+0.05q^2,\qquad q\ge0.
\]

Then

\[
C'(q)=20+0.1q,\qquad C''(q)=0.1>0.
\]

The marginal cost increases with quantity. If revenue is \(R(q)=100q-0.2q^2\), then profit is

\[
\Pi(q)=R(q)-C(q)=-1000+80q-0.25q^2.
\]

Hence

\[
\Pi'(q)=80-0.5q,\qquad \Pi''(q)=-0.5<0.
\]

The profit function is strictly concave. The stationary point \(\Pi'(q)=0\) occurs at \(q=160\), which is the unique global maximum on the model's feasible range \(0\le q\le500\). The result is conditional on the stylized revenue/cost model and constraints.

## 4. Second-order rate sensitivity of a cash flow

For \(C\ge0\), \(t\ge0\), and continuously compounded rate \(r\),

\[
PV(r)=Ce^{-rt}.
\]

Differentiate with respect to \(r\), holding \(C,t\) fixed:

\[
\boxed{PV'(r)=-tPV(r)},\qquad
\boxed{PV''(r)=t^2PV(r)}.
\]

For \(C>0,t>0\), \(PV''(r)>0\): the present-value curve is convex in the continuously compounded rate.

A second-order local approximation for a rate shock \(h\) is

\[
\boxed{PV(r+h)\approx PV(r)+PV'(r)h+\tfrac12PV''(r)h^2.}
\]

Equivalently,

\[
\frac{\Delta PV}{PV}\approx-th+\frac{t^2h^2}{2}.
\]

The exact relative change is \(\operatorname{expm1}(-th)\). For a single cash flow, \(PV''/PV=t^2\) is the normalized second rate derivative. This is a continuous-rate sensitivity measure; standard market bond convexity conventions may use different yield and compounding definitions.

## 5. Numerical worked example

Take \(C=10{,}000\), \(r=0.04\), and \(t=5\). Then

\[
PV=10000e^{-0.2}\approx8187.307531,
\]
\[
PV'=-5PV\approx-40936.537655,\qquad
PV''=25PV\approx204682.688274.
\]

For a 100-basis-point increase, \(h=0.01\), the first-order approximation is

\[
\Delta PV_1=PV'h\approx-409.365377.
\]

The second-order approximation adds

\[
\tfrac12PV''h^2\approx10.234134,
\]

giving \(\Delta PV_2\approx-399.131242\). The exact change is

\[
\Delta PV_{\mathrm{exact}}=PV\operatorname{expm1}(-0.05)\approx-399.29924.
\]

The second-order estimate is much closer for this example, but this does not guarantee superiority for every model and shock size.

## 6. Multiple cash flows and convexity

For positive payments \(C_i\) at horizons \(t_i\ge0\),

\[
V(r)=\sum_{i=1}^n C_i e^{-rt_i}.
\]

Term-by-term differentiation of the finite sum gives

\[
V'(r)=-\sum_i t_i C_i e^{-rt_i},
\qquad
V''(r)=\sum_i t_i^2 C_i e^{-rt_i}\ge0.
\]

If at least one positive payment occurs at a strictly positive horizon, then \(V''(r)>0\). With negative or signed cash flows, the second derivative need not be positive.

## 7. Numerical second derivatives

The centered second-difference approximation is

\[
D_h^{(2)}f(x)=\frac{f(x+h)-2f(x)+f(x-h)}{h^2}.
\]

For smooth functions and moderate \(h\), its truncation error is typically \(O(h^2)\). Very small \(h\) can amplify floating-point cancellation because the numerator subtracts nearly equal values and divides by \(h^2\). Numerical agreement is evidence, not proof.

## Local Taylor and exact-shock numerical contracts

For represented inputs, the first Taylor change is -C·t·h·exp(-r·t), and the second is C·[-t·h+(t·h)²/2]·exp(-r·t). The implementation combines the complete rational polynomial coefficient before final scaling and conversion. It does not require representable standalone derivatives or separate Taylor terms. Exact cancellation at t·h=2 returns positive zero; this computational identity does not make a large-shock Taylor approximation accurate.

The exact change is C·[exp(-(r+h)·t)-exp(-r·t)]. The helper factors the larger complete price and a nonnegative exponent gap. Products and the shifted rate r+h use exact arithmetic on represented inputs, without requiring a finite intermediate shifted-rate float or base PV.

Shock-before-years prevalidation is retained. Taylor order must be a built-in integer 1 or 2, checked before payment/rate/year validation and nonnegative payment/horizon domains. Exact shocks use the same domain checks without an order parameter. After validation, zero factors use signed algebraic identities: first-order and exact changes have the sign of -C·t·h; second-order zero-factor cases add that first-order signed zero to a zero with C's sign. Finite underflow is permitted; nonfinite final results raise ValueError. Ordinary finite roundings may change under complete-expression evaluation.

## Scoped polynomial output contract (V-125)

`polynomial` first consumes and validates coefficients once, rejects an empty sequence, then validates x. Built-in finite integers/floats excluding bool are converted to binary64 by the shared `finite` helper. Each represented coefficient and x is then interpreted exactly; rational Horner evaluation rounds the complete polynomial once to binary64. This deliberately strengthens the former ordinary-Horner rounding convention: it recovers a finite result when intermediate floating products overflow and avoids intermediate subnormal loss. A true output beyond finite binary64 raises ValueError. Signed nonzero underflow may round to signed zero; exact cancellation returns positive zero. Ordinary roundoff-dependent outputs may change.

`derivative_coefficients` validates the coefficient sequence before order. Order must be a nonnegative built-in int (bool excluded). Order0 returns the validated coefficient tuple, retaining signed zero. Order at least the coefficient count returns `(0.0,)` directly, without an order-sized loop. Other orders use exact integer falling-factorial scaling of the original represented coefficients, round each final coefficient once, and reject nonfinite final coefficients with ValueError. Zero leading terms remain represented in the returned tuple; input is consumed once.

These are educational complete-expression contracts, not a high-throughput polynomial library. Exact rational intermediate sizes can grow with degree and input bit lengths; no bound on arbitrary coefficient-count/degree resource use is established. They do not change the separate sampled-second-difference or signed cash-flow contracts. See [V-125 evidence](../validation/chapter-08-section-06-polynomial-followup.md).


## Scoped sampled-second-difference contract (V-126)

`centered_second_difference` checks callable status before numeric controls, then validates finite x/nonzero finite h and represented finite, distinct x+h and x-h before invoking callbacks. It samples exactly in order `(x+h, x, x-h)`, validating each return before the next call; callback exceptions propagate. A nonfinite/invalid early sample prevents later calls.

The reported quotient is the complete `(f(x+h)-2*f(x)+f(x-h))/h²` of the three represented binary64 sample values, with exact rational arithmetic and one final binary64 rounding. The squared step is evaluated exactly: h² need not itself fit a nonzero finite float. This deliberately extends the former squared-step guard domain. True nonfinite final outputs raise ValueError, exact cancellation returns positive zero, and nonzero negative underflow may return negative zero. Default step1e-3 and signed nonzero steps remain supported.

The callback has already rounded its output; exact quotient arithmetic cannot recover information lost in its evaluation. Rounded perturbations need not form a perfectly symmetric grid. These contracts do not establish the analytical second derivative, convergence, noise robustness, optimal step choice or platform acceptance. Other chapter helper copies retain their separately documented controls. See [V-126 evidence](../validation/chapter-08-section-06-centered-followup.md).
