## Chapter assessment

This capstone consolidates Sections 8.1–8.9: derivative limits, differentiation rules, composite functions, exponential and logarithmic derivatives, higher derivatives, implicit differentiation, and numerical sensitivity estimation.

**Learning outcomes:** derive and interpret financial sensitivities; distinguish partial from total derivatives; identify domain and unit restrictions; compare analytic and numerical methods; and explain limitations of numerical evidence.

### Part A — Foundations and differentiation rules

**1. Definition.** State the limit definition of \(f'(x)\), and derive the derivative of \(f(x)=x^2\).

**2. Polynomial.** Differentiate \(f(x)=4x^5-3x^3+7x-9\).

**3. Product rule.** Differentiate \(f(x)=(x^2+1)e^{2x}\).

**4. Quotient rule.** Differentiate \(f(x)=(2x+1)/(x^2+4)\).

**5. Chain rule.** Differentiate \(f(x)=(1+3x^2)^{-4}\).

**6. Nested exponential.** Differentiate \(f(x)=e^{-0.5x^2}\).

**7. Logarithm.** Differentiate \(f(x)=\ln(1+4x)\), and state the domain.

### Part B — Financial applications

**8. Marginal profit.** A firm's profit is \(\Pi(q)=-600+50q-0.2q^2\), \(0\le q\le200\). Find its stationary point and classify it.

**9. Continuous accumulation.** With \(A(t)=2500e^{0.06t}\), calculate \(A'(t)\), \(A''(t)\), and \(A'(3)\).

**10. Discount-rate sensitivity.** For \(PV(r)=10000e^{-5r}\), derive \(PV'\) and \(PV''\), and evaluate both at \(r=0.04\).

**11. Rate-shock approximation.** For the model in Exercise 10, approximate the change in present value when the decimal rate rises by \(h=0.001\), using first and second order Taylor approximations. Compare with the exact change.

**12. Log return.** A price rises from 80 to 84. Compute the simple return and the logarithmic return. Explain why they differ.

**13. Elasticity.** If demand is \(Q(p)=400p^{-1.5}\), \(p>0\), calculate point price elasticity \(pQ'(p)/Q(p)\).

**14. Break-even sensitivity.** For \((p-15)q=900\), \(p>15\), find \(dq/dp\) and evaluate at \(p=45\).

**15. Bond yield.** A one-period cash flow of 110 is priced at 100, so \(P(y)=110/(1+y)\). Find \(y\), \(dP/dy\), and \(dy/dP\) at the quoted price.

### Part C — Higher derivatives and numerical methods

**16. Concavity.** Find concavity intervals and inflection points for \(f(x)=x^3-6x^2+4\).

**17. Inflection counterexample.** Show that \(f''(0)=0\) is not sufficient for an inflection point using \(f(x)=x^4\).

**18. Finite differences.** For \(f(x)=x^3\), \(x=2\), and \(h=0.1\), calculate forward, backward, and centered first differences. Compare with \(f'(2)\).

**19. Second difference.** Apply \([f(x+h)-2f(x)+f(x-h)]/h^2\) to \(f(x)=x^2\), and explain the result.

**20. Richardson extrapolation.** Derive the combination \((4D^0_{h/2}-D^0_h)/3\), assuming the leading centered-difference error is proportional to \(h^2\).

**21. Nonsmooth warning.** Explain why the centered difference of \(f(x)=|x|\) at zero cannot establish a derivative.

**22. Step selection.** State the heuristic powers of machine epsilon for forward first, centered first, and centered second derivatives. Explain why they are not universally optimal.

### Part D — Integrated applied assessment

**23. Multi-cash-flow present value.** Consider \(V(r)=100e^{-r}+200e^{-2r}+300e^{-3r}\). Derive \(V'(r)\) and \(V''(r)\). State the sign of each for real \(r\).

**24. Rate sensitivity units.** For \(V(r)=Ce^{-tr}\), explain the difference between \(V'(r)\) and the approximate value change from a 1-basis-point increase.

**25. Comparator acceptance.** Design a verification protocol for an analytic first derivative and a centered numerical estimate. Include at least three step sizes, absolute and relative tolerances, domain checks, and recorded evidence.

## Worked solutions

**1.** \(f'(x)=\lim_{h\to0}[f(x+h)-f(x)]/h\). For \(x^2\), the numerator is \(2xh+h^2\), so the quotient is \(2x+h\) and the limit is \(2x\).

**2.** \(f'(x)=20x^4-9x^2+7\).

**3.** \(f'(x)=2xe^{2x}+2(x^2+1)e^{2x}=e^{2x}(2x^2+2x+2)\).

**4.** \(f'(x)=[2(x^2+4)-(2x+1)2x]/(x^2+4)^2=(-2x^2-2x+8)/(x^2+4)^2\).

**5.** \(f'(x)=-24x(1+3x^2)^{-5}\).

**6.** \(f'(x)=-xe^{-0.5x^2}\).

**7.** \(f'(x)=4/(1+4x)\), valid for \(x>-1/4\).

**8.** \(\Pi'(q)=50-0.4q\), so \(q=125\). Since \(\Pi''=-0.4<0\), this is the unique maximizer on the feasible interval.

**9.** \(A'(t)=150e^{0.06t}\), \(A''(t)=9e^{0.06t}\), and \(A'(3)=150e^{0.18}\approx179.5826\).

**10.** \(PV'(r)=-50000e^{-5r}\), \(PV''(r)=250000e^{-5r}\). At \(r=0.04\), \(PV'=-50000e^{-0.2}\approx-40936.5377\), \(PV''=250000e^{-0.2}\approx204682.6883\).

**11.** With \(PV_0=10000e^{-0.2}\), first-order change is \(-5PV_0(0.001)\approx-40.9365\). Second-order change is \(-5PV_0(0.001)+\tfrac12(25PV_0)(0.001)^2\approx-40.8342\). Exact change is \(PV_0(e^{-0.005}-1)\approx-40.8344\). Numerical rounding may vary.

**12.** Simple return \(=(84-80)/80=0.05\). Log return \(=\ln(84/80)=\ln(1.05)\approx0.0487902\). The logarithm is nonlinear; the two coincide only to first order for small returns.

**13.** \(Q'(p)=-600p^{-2.5}\), so \(pQ'/Q=-1.5\).

**14.** \(q=900/(p-15)\), \(dq/dp=-900/(p-15)^2\). At \(p=45\), \(q=30\) and \(dq/dp=-1\) unit per currency-unit increase.

**15.** \(y=110/100-1=0.1\); \(dP/dy=-110/(1.1)^2=-1000/11\approx-90.9091\); \(dy/dP=-11/1000=-0.011\) per currency unit.

**16.** \(f''(x)=6x-12\). Concave down for \(x<2\), up for \(x>2\); inflection point \((2,-12)\).

**17.** For \(x^4\), \(f''(x)=12x^2\ge0\) on both sides of zero, so concavity does not change.

**18.** \(f'(2)=12\). Forward \(=12.61\), backward \(=11.41\), centered \(=12.01\).

**19.** The second difference of \(x^2\) equals \(2\) for any nonzero \(h\) in exact arithmetic, matching \(f''(x)=2\).

**20.** If \(D_h^0=f'+ch^2+O(h^4)\), then \(4D^0_{h/2}-D_h^0=3f'+O(h^4)\); divide by 3.

**21.** The left slope is \(-1\) and the right slope \(+1\). The symmetric difference is zero by cancellation, despite the derivative not existing.

**22.** Forward first: \(\epsilon^{1/2}\); centered first: \(\epsilon^{1/3}\); centered second: \(\epsilon^{1/4}\), each multiplied by an appropriate scale. The optimum also depends on smoothness, cancellation, noise, and domain constraints.

**23.** \(V'=-100e^{-r}-400e^{-2r}-900e^{-3r}<0\); \(V''=100e^{-r}+800e^{-2r}+2700e^{-3r}>0\) for real \(r\).

**24.** \(V'(r)=-tV(r)\) is a derivative per unit of decimal rate. A 1-basis-point change is \(h=0.0001\), so \(\Delta V\approx-0.0001\,tV(r)\), not \(V'(r)\) itself.

**25.** Derive an analytic formula independently; evaluate centered differences at, for example, \(h=10^{-2},10^{-3},10^{-4}\) when all perturbed points are valid; compare \(|D_h-f'|\) with \(a_{\mathrm{tol}}+r_{\mathrm{tol}}|f'|\); log variable units, domain, step sizes, Python version, output, and failures; investigate nonconvergence and do not treat agreement as proof.
