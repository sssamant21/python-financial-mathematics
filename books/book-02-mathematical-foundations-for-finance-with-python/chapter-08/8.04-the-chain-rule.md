## Learning objectives

Identify composite functions, derive the chain rule, differentiate nested powers and exponentials, and apply the result to financial growth, discounting, and sensitivity analysis.

## 1. Composite functions

A composite function is \(y=f(g(x))\). The inner function \(g\) transforms the input, and the outer function \(f\) transforms the result. If \(g\) is differentiable at \(x\) and \(f\) is differentiable at \(g(x)\), then

\[
\boxed{\frac{d}{dx}f(g(x))=f'(g(x))g'(x).}
\]

In Leibniz notation, if \(u=g(x)\) and \(y=f(u)\),

\[
\frac{dy}{dx}=\frac{dy}{du}\frac{du}{dx}.
\]

This notation expresses a composition of local rates of change; it does not require treating the differentials as ordinary fractions.

## 2. Why the rule works

Let \(u=g(x)\), and write the increment in the inner function as \(\Delta u=g(x+h)-g(x)\). Differentiability of \(f\) means there is a function \(\varepsilon(\Delta u)\to0\) such that

\[
f(u+\Delta u)-f(u)=f'(u)\Delta u+\varepsilon(\Delta u)\Delta u.
\]

For \(h\ne0\),

\[
\frac{f(g(x+h))-f(g(x))}{h}
=[f'(u)+\varepsilon(\Delta u)]\frac{g(x+h)-g(x)}{h}.
\]

As \(h\to0\), the second factor tends to \(g'(x)\), and the first tends to \(f'(u)\). The argument also covers increments where \(\Delta u=0\), by defining the remainder factor continuously at zero. This proves the chain rule.

## 3. Nested powers

For \(y=[g(x)]^n\), with \(n\) a positive integer,

\[
\boxed{y'=n[g(x)]^{n-1}g'(x).}
\]

**Example.** Let \(y=(3x^2+2)^4\). Then

\[
y'=4(3x^2+2)^3(6x)=24x(3x^2+2)^3.
\]

A common mistake is to omit the inner derivative \(6x\).

## 4. Exponential and logarithmic compositions

For differentiable \(g\),

\[
\frac{d}{dx}e^{g(x)}=e^{g(x)}g'(x).
\]

For \(g(x)>0\),

\[
\frac{d}{dx}\ln(g(x))=\frac{g'(x)}{g(x)}.
\]

The logarithm requires a positive real argument; the derivative formula must not be used where that domain condition fails.

## 5. Financial application: continuous discounting

A cash flow \(C>0\) payable in \(t\ge0\) years has present value

\[
PV(r)=Ce^{-rt}.
\]

Holding \(C\) and \(t\) constant and differentiating with respect to the continuously compounded annual rate \(r\),

\[
\boxed{\frac{dPV}{dr}=-tCe^{-rt}=-tPV(r).}
\]

If the rate depends on a market factor \(z\), with \(r=r(z)\), then

\[
\boxed{\frac{dPV}{dz}=-tPV(r(z))r'(z).}
\]

This separates sensitivity to the discount rate from sensitivity of the rate to the underlying factor.

**Worked example.** Let \(C=1000\), \(t=4\), and \(r(z)=0.03+0.002z\). At \(z=1\), \(r=0.032\) and \(r'(z)=0.002\). Thus

\[
\frac{dPV}{dz}=-4(1000)e^{-0.128}(0.002)=-8e^{-0.128}\approx-7.039.
\]

The derivative is currency units per unit of \(z\), not currency units per one percentage-point change in the interest rate.

## 6. Financial application: discrete compounding with a changing rate

For an integer \(n\ge1\), horizon \(t\ge0\), and nominal annual rate \(j(z)\), define

\[
FV(z)=P\left(1+\frac{j(z)}n\right)^{nt},
\]

where \(P\ge0\) and \(1+j(z)/n>0\). Differentiation gives

\[
\boxed{\frac{dFV}{dz}
=Pt\left(1+\frac{j(z)}n\right)^{nt-1}j'(z).}
\]

The exponent \(nt\) is held constant while differentiating with respect to \(z\). This formula also applies when \(nt\) is not an integer, provided the positive-base domain is maintained.

For \(j(z)=0.04+0.001z\), \(P=2000\), \(n=12\), and \(t=2\), the derivative measures the response of accumulated value to a unit change in \(z\).

## 7. Financial application: log returns and transformations

For a differentiable positive price \(S(t)\), the instantaneous logarithmic growth rate is

\[
\boxed{\frac{d}{dt}\ln S(t)=\frac{S'(t)}{S(t)}.}
\]

For a positive price path \(S(t)=100(1+0.02t)^3\) on \(t>-50\),

\[
\frac{d}{dt}\ln S(t)=\frac{0.06}{1+0.02t}.
\]

This is an instantaneous log-growth rate; it need not equal a discrete-period simple return.

## 8. Numerical verification and safety

Use centered differences only when both \(z-h\) and \(z+h\) lie inside the model's domain:

\[
D_hF(z)=\frac{F(z+h)-F(z-h)}{2h}.
\]

For very small \(h\), floating-point cancellation may dominate. A numerical match supports an implementation but does not replace the mathematical derivation.

## Callback and finite-difference contract

The general `chain_derivative` helper invokes inner(x), outer_derivative(inner_value), and inner_derivative(x) once each, in that order. It validates finite built-in numeric outputs after each call, propagates callback exceptions, and raises ValueError if the final product is nonfinite. Signed zero and representable underflow remain valid outputs. This guard does not alter the financial helper formulas or make their range validation complete.

The Section 8.4 centered-difference entry point delegates to Section 8.1's guarded helper, retaining the default step 1e-4 and callable-before-controls validation precedence. Invalid, nonfinite or collapsed perturbations reject before callbacks. Finite represented samples are combined as a complete sampled quotient so overflowing intermediate subtraction or denominator products do not discard a finite result. ## Variable-rate present-value range contract

The variable-rate PV functions retain the public `rate_from_factor` calculation: multiply the represented 0.002 by the factor and round, then add the represented 0.03 and round. They do not replace this with an exact affine-rate sum. After that rate is fixed, complete PV and factor-sensitivity coefficients and the horizon-rate product are combined before final output rounding.

For finite nonnegative amount and horizon, zero amount and zero horizon use algebraic identities after validating all inputs, including the factor. Signed zeros are retained. A nonfinite final financial result raises ValueError; finite values are preserved even when a separately evaluated exponential or sensitivity coefficient would overflow or underflow. The sensitivity remains the model expression -t·0.002·PV with the represented coefficient; it is not a derivative of binary64 rounding discontinuities.

[Variable-rate PV reference checks and scope](../validation/chapter-08-variable-rate-pv-followup.md) distinguish complete-expression rounding from the previous intermediate-rounding results. Discrete compounding, other helper ranges, independent review and release acceptance remain pending.

## Discrete-compounding range and exponent contract

The discrete future-value functions retain the rounded nominal-rate, division and base-addition operations that produce b = 1 + nominal_rate(factor)/periods_per_year. After that positive represented base is fixed, the model exponent n·t is formed exactly from the integer period count and represented horizon; the sensitivity exponent n·t−1 also subtracts one exactly. Values and sensitivities combine their complete coefficients and powers before output rounding.

Zero principal and zero horizon use signed algebraic identities after all input and base checks. True nonfinite final results raise ValueError. These changes establish a complete-expression contract rather than preserving each old log/product/power rounding. The factor sensitivity remains p·t·represented(0.001)·b^(n·t−1), not a derivative of machine-rounding discontinuities.

## Large integer period-count conversion

A positive built-in integer count can exceed float conversion range while still producing a finite period-rate quotient. The base builder first uses the existing float/int division. Only if that operation raises OverflowError does it divide the represented finite nominal rate by the exact integer count and round the quotient. Existing working counts therefore retain their original rounded bases, including the 2^53+1 boundary where a global exact-division replacement would change the model.

[Runnable conversion-boundary evidence](../validation/chapter-08-large-period-count-followup.md) covers the overflow midpoint above maximum float, huge counts with nonzero quotients, zero identities and invalid inputs. A base that rounds to one follows this rounded-base model; it is not evidence of the continuous-compounding limit. Computation costs grow with integer bit length. Independent review and supported-platform/release acceptance remain pending.
