## Learning objectives

Identify composite functions, derive the chain rule, differentiate nested powers and exponentials, and apply the result to financial growth, discounting, and sensitivity analysis.

## 1. Composite functions

A composite function is \(y=f(g(x))\). The inner function \(g\) transforms the input, and the outer function \(f\) transforms the result. If \(g\) is differentiable at \(x\) and \(f\) is differentiable at \(g(x)\), then

\[
\boxed{\frac{d}{dx}f(g(x))=f'(g(x))g'(x).}
\]

In Leibniz notation, if \(u=g(x)\) and \(y=f(u)\),

\[
\frac{dy}{dx}=\frac{dy}{du}\frac{du}{dx}.
\]

This notation expresses a composition of local rates of change; it does not require treating the differentials as ordinary fractions.

## 2. Why the rule works

Let \(u=g(x)\), and write the increment in the inner function as \(\Delta u=g(x+h)-g(x)\). Differentiability of \(f\) means there is a function \(\varepsilon(\Delta u)\to0\) such that

\[
f(u+\Delta u)-f(u)=f'(u)\Delta u+\varepsilon(\Delta u)\Delta u.
\]

For \(h\ne0\),

\[
\frac{f(g(x+h))-f(g(x))}{h}
=[f'(u)+\varepsilon(\Delta u)]\frac{g(x+h)-g(x)}{h}.
\]

As \(h\to0\), the second factor tends to \(g'(x)\), and the first tends to \(f'(u)\). The argument also covers increments where \(\Delta u=0\), by defining the remainder factor continuously at zero. This proves the chain rule.

## 3. Nested powers

For \(y=[g(x)]^n\), with \(n\) a positive integer,

\[
\boxed{y'=n[g(x)]^{n-1}g'(x).}
\]

**Example.** Let \(y=(3x^2+2)^4\). Then

\[
y'=4(3x^2+2)^3(6x)=24x(3x^2+2)^3.
\]

A common mistake is to omit the inner derivative \(6x\).

## 4. Exponential and logarithmic compositions

For differentiable \(g\),

\[
\frac{d}{dx}e^{g(x)}=e^{g(x)}g'(x).
\]

For \(g(x)>0\),

\[
\frac{d}{dx}\ln(g(x))=\frac{g'(x)}{g(x)}.
\]

The logarithm requires a positive real argument; the derivative formula must not be used where that domain condition fails.

## 5. Financial application: continuous discounting

A cash flow \(C>0\) payable in \(t\ge0\) years has present value

\[
PV(r)=Ce^{-rt}.
\]

Holding \(C\) and \(t\) constant and differentiating with respect to the continuously compounded annual rate \(r\),

\[
\boxed{\frac{dPV}{dr}=-tCe^{-rt}=-tPV(r).}
\]

If the rate depends on a market factor \(z\), with \(r=r(z)\), then

\[
\boxed{\frac{dPV}{dz}=-tPV(r(z))r'(z).}
\]

This separates sensitivity to the discount rate from sensitivity of the rate to the underlying factor.

**Worked example.** Let \(C=1000\), \(t=4\), and \(r(z)=0.03+0.002z\). At \(z=1\), \(r=0.032\) and \(r'(z)=0.002\). Thus

\[
\frac{dPV}{dz}=-4(1000)e^{-0.128}(0.002)=-8e^{-0.128}\approx-7.039.
\]

The derivative is currency units per unit of \(z\), not currency units per one percentage-point change in the interest rate.

## 6. Financial application: discrete compounding with a changing rate

For an integer \(n\ge1\), horizon \(t\ge0\), and nominal annual rate \(j(z)\), define

\[
FV(z)=P\left(1+\frac{j(z)}n\right)^{nt},
\]

where \(P\ge0\) and \(1+j(z)/n>0\). Differentiation gives

\[
\boxed{\frac{dFV}{dz}
=Pt\left(1+\frac{j(z)}n\right)^{nt-1}j'(z).}
\]

The exponent \(nt\) is held constant while differentiating with respect to \(z\). This formula also applies when \(nt\) is not an integer, provided the positive-base domain is maintained.

For \(j(z)=0.04+0.001z\), \(P=2000\), \(n=12\), and \(t=2\), the derivative measures the response of accumulated value to a unit change in \(z\).

## 7. Financial application: log returns and transformations

For a differentiable positive price \(S(t)\), the instantaneous logarithmic growth rate is

\[
\boxed{\frac{d}{dt}\ln S(t)=\frac{S'(t)}{S(t)}.}
\]

For a positive price path \(S(t)=100(1+0.02t)^3\) on \(t>-50\),

\[
\frac{d}{dt}\ln S(t)=\frac{0.06}{1+0.02t}.
\]

This is an instantaneous log-growth rate; it need not equal a discrete-period simple return.

## 8. Numerical verification and safety

Use centered differences only when both \(z-h\) and \(z+h\) lie inside the model's domain:

\[
D_hF(z)=\frac{F(z+h)-F(z-h)}{2h}.
\]

For very small \(h\), floating-point cancellation may dominate. A numerical match supports an implementation but does not replace the mathematical derivation.

**2.** Differentiate \((2x+1)^5\).

**3.** Differentiate \((3x^2+2)^4\).

**4.** Differentiate \(e^{-3x^2}\).

**5.** Differentiate \(\ln(1+x^2)\).

**6.** Derive \(dPV/dr\) for \(PV(r)=Ce^{-rt}\).

**7.** Derive \(dPV/dz\) when \(r=r(z)\).

**8.** Evaluate \(dPV/dz\) at \(z=1\) for \(C=1000\), \(t=4\), and \(r(z)=0.03+0.002z\).

**9.** Differentiate \(P(1+j(z)/n)^{nt}\) with respect to \(z\), stating its domain.

**10.** For \(S(t)=100(1+0.02t)^3\), find \(d(\ln S)/dt\).

1. \(f'(g(x))g'(x)\).
2. \(10(2x+1)^4\).
3. \(24x(3x^2+2)^3\).
4. \(-6xe^{-3x^2}\).
5. \(2x/(1+x^2)\).
6. \(-tCe^{-rt}\).
7. \(-tCe^{-tr(z)}r'(z)\).
8. \(-8e^{-0.128}\approx-7.039\) currency units per unit of \(z\).
