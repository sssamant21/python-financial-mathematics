## Learning objectives

Derive the derivatives of exponential and logarithmic functions; apply the chain rule to composite growth models; calculate continuous-time accumulation, discounting, log-return and elasticity sensitivities; and validate numerical derivatives safely.

## 1. The natural exponential derivative

The exponential function is distinguished by its instantaneous rate of change:

\[
\boxed{\frac{d}{dx}e^x=e^x}.
\]

From the definition,

\[
\frac{d}{dx}e^x
=\lim_{h\to0}\frac{e^{x+h}-e^x}{h}
=e^x\lim_{h\to0}\frac{e^h-1}{h}=e^x.
\]

The final limit is the small-change exponential limit studied in Chapter 07. For a differentiable inner function \(u(x)\), the chain rule gives

\[
\boxed{\frac{d}{dx}e^{u(x)}=e^{u(x)}u'(x).}
\]

For a positive constant base \(a\),

\[
\boxed{\frac{d}{dx}a^x=a^x\ln a},
\]

because \(a^x=e^{x\ln a}\). In particular, if \(a=1\), the derivative is zero.

**Worked example.** For \(f(x)=5e^{-0.3x^2}\),

\[
f'(x)=-3xe^{-0.3x^2}.
\]

## 2. The natural logarithm derivative

For \(x>0\),

\[
\boxed{\frac{d}{dx}\ln x=\frac1x}.
\]

One derivation uses the inverse relationship \(y=\ln x\), \(x=e^y\). Implicit differentiation yields \(1=e^y y'=xy'\), hence \(y'=1/x\).

For a differentiable \(u(x)>0\),

\[
\boxed{\frac{d}{dx}\ln u(x)=\frac{u'(x)}{u(x)}}.
\]

For a constant base \(a>0\), \(a\ne1\),

\[
\frac{d}{dx}\log_a x=\frac1{x\ln a},\qquad x>0.
\]

**Worked example.** If \(g(x)=\ln(1+2x)\), then

\[
g'(x)=\frac2{1+2x},\qquad x>-\tfrac12.
\]

A real-valued logarithm of a nonpositive argument is undefined; always state the domain.

## 3. Continuous compounding and marginal growth

With principal \(P\), continuously compounded annual rate \(r\), and horizon \(t\),

\[
A(t)=Pe^{rt}.
\]

Holding \(P\) and \(r\) constant,

\[
\boxed{\frac{dA}{dt}=rA(t)}.
\]

Holding \(P\) and \(t\) constant instead,

\[
\boxed{\frac{\partial A}{\partial r}=tA(t)}.
\]

These derivatives describe different changes: time sensitivity and rate sensitivity. For \(P=1000\), \(r=0.04\), and \(t=5\),

\[
A(5)=1000e^{0.2}\approx1221.402758,
\quad \frac{dA}{dt}=0.04A(5)\approx48.856110,
\quad \frac{\partial A}{\partial r}=5A(5)\approx6107.013790.
\]

The last derivative is per unit change in the decimal rate. A one-basis-point increase corresponds to a rate increment of \(0.0001\), not \(0.01\).

## 4. Discount-factor sensitivities

For a single cash flow \(C\) payable at \(t\ge0\),

\[
PV(r)=Ce^{-rt}.
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{\frac{\partial PV}{\partial r}=-tPV(r)},\qquad
\boxed{\frac{\partial PV}{\partial t}=-rPV(r)}
\]

when the other parameters are held constant. For \(C>0\) and \(t>0\), PV decreases as the discount rate increases. When \(r<0\), however, PV increases with time.

**Worked example.** Let \(C=5000\), \(r=0.03\), \(t=4\). Then

\[
PV=5000e^{-0.12}\approx4434.602184,\qquad
\frac{\partial PV}{\partial r}\approx-17738.408737.
\]

For a one-basis-point rate increase, the first-order change is approximately \(-1.773841\). The exact change is

\[
PV(r+h)-PV(r)=PV(r)\operatorname{expm1}(-th).
\]

The \`math.expm1\` function avoids subtracting nearly equal exponentials.

## 5. Logarithmic returns and growth rates

For positive differentiable asset price \(S(t)\),

\[
\boxed{\frac{d}{dt}\ln S(t)=\frac{S'(t)}{S(t)}}.
\]

This is the **instantaneous logarithmic growth rate**. If \(S(t)=S_0e^{\mu t}\), it equals the constant \(\mu\).

Between two positive prices \(S_0\) and \(S_1\), the log return is

\[
\ell=\ln(S_1/S_0).
\]

For a small simple return \(R=(S_1-S_0)/S_0\), use \(\ell=\operatorname{log1p}(R)\) for numerical stability. For \(S_0=100\) and \(S_1=103\), the log return is \(\ln(1.03)\approx0.0295588\), versus a 3% simple return.

## 6. Elasticity as a logarithmic derivative

For positive differentiable \(Y(x)\) and \(x>0\), the point elasticity is

\[
\boxed{\mathcal E(x)=\frac{d\ln Y}{d\ln x}
=\frac{xY'(x)}{Y(x)}}.
\]

For \(Y(x)=kx^\alpha\), \(k>0\), elasticity is \(\alpha\). This is a dimensionless local percentage sensitivity; Chapter 09 develops it in depth.

## 7. Numerical stability and domain checks

The forward difference for an exponential may lose accuracy when \(h\) is tiny:

\[
\frac{e^{r+h}-e^r}{h}
=e^r\frac{\operatorname{expm1}(h)}h.
\]

Using \`math.expm1(h)\` is more stable than directly evaluating \`math.exp(h)-1\`. Similarly, \`math.log1p(h)\` is preferable to \`math.log(1+h)\` near zero.

For a logarithm, numerical perturbations must preserve a positive argument. A centered difference around \(x\) is invalid if \(x-h\le0\).

It now combines the exact represented coefficient scale·growth and exponent growth·x before output rounding. A nonfinite final derivative raises ValueError; finite results are preserved when separate coefficients or exponentials would overflow or underflow.

## Accumulation range and sensitivity contract

For finite built-in numeric inputs, principal and horizon remain nonnegative and signed zero remains accepted. The value and two sensitivities now evaluate the complete represented-input expressions P·exp(r·t), P·r·exp(r·t) and P·t·exp(r·t). Coefficients and exponents are combined before final conversion; derivatives do not multiply an already rounded accumulated value. Consequently, a sensitivity can be finite even when the value overflows or rounds to zero.

After input validation, zero factors use signed algebraic identities. A nonfinite final output raises ValueError; finite underflow is permitted. This explicitly replaces earlier intermediate-overflow exceptions and returned infinities/NaNs. Ordinary finite roundings can also change under complete-expression evaluation. Value inputs retain principal/rate/years validation order; the time sensitivity validates rate first and the rate sensitivity validates years first.

[Independent log-amplitude references and actual checks](../validation/chapter-08-accumulation-range-followup.md) cover selected extreme inputs, signed zeros, final overflow and validation precedence. ## PV and rate-sensitivity numerical contract

The value and rate sensitivity evaluate the complete represented-input expressions C·exp(-r·t) and -C·t·exp(-r·t) before final output conversion. The derivative does not multiply an already rounded PV. Finite sensitivity can therefore survive a zero or unrepresentable PV. Zero payment/horizon identities retain signed zeros after all input/domain checks. A nonfinite final output raises ValueError; finite underflow remains permitted. This explicitly replaces inconsistent intermediate exceptions and returned infinities/NaNs, and may alter ordinary finite rounding.

Payment and horizon remain nonnegative finite built-in numbers; rates remain finite and signed. The value validates payment/rate/years in order, while the derivative validates years first. ## Complete rate-shock numerical contract

The exact model price change is C·[exp(-(r+h)·t)-exp(-r·t)] for the complete represented inputs. The shock helper now factors out the larger price and combines a signed coefficient, exponent and nonnegative exponent gap before output conversion. Neither a separately representable base PV nor a separately representable shock factor/product is required. The sum r+h and exponent products are formed exactly from represented inputs, rather than rounding an intermediate shifted rate; the mathematical shifted rate may exceed finite binary64 range when the complete change is finite.

Years and change are validated first, followed by payment/rate/year validation and the nonnegative payment/horizon domain. After those checks, zero payment, zero horizon or zero change returns the signed algebraic zero of -C·t·h without forming large products. Finite underflow is permitted; a nonfinite final change raises ValueError. Ordinary finite rounding may differ under the complete-expression convention.

[Independent complete-price-difference references and retained replays](../validation/chapter-08-rate-shock-range-followup.md) cover selected signed shocks, cancellation, tiny products, extreme inputs and validation precedence. These are numerical model checks, not an exhaustive rounding proof or reviewer/platform/release approval. Log-return, stable quotient and centered-copy obligations remain open.

## Log-return range and near-unity contract

For finite positive prices, the model log return is ln(end/start). The helper retains the existing log1p((end-start)/start) path for ratios near unity (the end price between half and twice the start), including adjacent and equal prices. For distant prices, it normalizes both inputs with frexp and evaluates a bounded mantissa-ratio logarithm plus the exponent difference times ln(2). It does not form a possibly overflowing full ratio or a relative change rounded to -1.

All accepted binary64 prices now produce finite model log returns. The normalized mantissas are positive and bounded; their ratio lies between one half and two, and the integer exponent difference is bounded by the binary64 range. Positivity, built-in numeric/finite guards, start-before-end validation and equal-price positive-zero behavior remain. [Independent exact-ratio logarithm references and actual replay evidence](../validation/chapter-08-log-return-range-followup.md) cover selected extremes, branch boundaries and near-unity controls. This is numerical evidence, not an exhaustive rounding proof or independent reviewer/platform/release acceptance. Stable quotient and centered-copy obligations remain open.

## Stable exponential quotient numerical contract

The helper evaluates the model [exp(x+h)-exp(x)]/h from complete represented inputs with an exact represented-input sum x+h. It does not form separate rounded exponentials/products before division. It factors out the larger exponential, combines the exact coefficient 1/|h| with that exponent and gap |h|, and converts only the final result.

Finite nonzero steps of either sign remain accepted after x-before-step type/finite validation; signed zero steps still reject. The exact model quotient is positive; finite underflow returns positive zero, while a nonfinite final quotient raises ValueError. A rounded float x+h need not be distinct or finite: this analytic helper uses the exact sum of represented inputs, unlike a callback stencil over rounded perturbation points.

[Independent complete exponential-difference references and retained runtime evidence](../validation/chapter-08-stable-exponential-quotient-followup.md) cover selected tiny steps, extreme exponents, cancellation, finite-result recovery and true final overflow. Ordinary finite rounding can change under complete-expression evaluation. This is numerical evidence, not an exhaustive rounding proof or independent reviewer/platform/release acceptance. The centered-difference copy and resource/review obligations remain open.
