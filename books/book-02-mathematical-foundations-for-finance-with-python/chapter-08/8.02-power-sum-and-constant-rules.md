## Learning objectives

Derive and apply the power, constant, constant-multiple, sum, and difference rules; differentiate polynomials; interpret marginal revenue, marginal cost, and marginal profit; distinguish derivatives from finite differences; and validate analytic results numerically.

## 1. The derivative rules

For differentiable functions \(f,g\) and a constant \(c\),

\[
\frac{d}{dx}c=0,\qquad
\frac{d}{dx}[cf(x)]=cf'(x),
\]

\[
\frac{d}{dx}[f(x)+g(x)]=f'(x)+g'(x),
\qquad
\frac{d}{dx}[f(x)-g(x)]=f'(x)-g'(x).
\]

These follow directly by distributing the difference quotient and taking limits. The hypotheses matter: the component functions must be differentiable at the point under consideration.

## 2. The power rule

For a nonnegative integer \(n\),

\[
\boxed{\frac{d}{dx}x^n=nx^{n-1}}.
\]

For \(n=0\), \(x^0=1\) is constant and has derivative zero. For positive integers, use the binomial expansion:

\[
(x+h)^n=x^n+nx^{n-1}h+\binom n2 x^{n-2}h^2+\cdots+h^n.
\]

Subtract \(x^n\), divide by \(h\ne0\), and let \(h\to0\). Every term except \(nx^{n-1}\) vanishes.

For negative integers, the rule holds wherever \(x\ne0\); for general real exponents, it holds on \(x>0\), with extensions on domains where the real-valued function is differentiable. The full generalization can be established using logarithmic and chain-rule differentiation in later sections.

## 3. Differentiating polynomials

For

\[
p(x)=a_0+a_1x+\cdots+a_nx^n,
\]

the rules give

\[
\boxed{p'(x)=a_1+2a_2x+\cdots+na_nx^{n-1}}.
\]

**Worked example:**

\[
p(x)=7x^4-3x^3+5x^2-9x+12.
\]

Then

\[
p'(x)=28x^3-9x^2+10x-9.
\]

At \(x=2\), \(p'(2)=224-36+20-9=199\).

## 4. Financial application: marginal revenue

Suppose unit price depends on quantity according to \(P(q)=100-0.2q\), for a range of quantities where the price is nonnegative. Revenue is

\[
R(q)=qP(q)=100q-0.2q^2.
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{R'(q)=100-0.4q}.
\]

At \(q=100\), marginal revenue is 60 currency units per unit of output. This is the instantaneous slope of revenue, not necessarily the exact revenue from selling one additional indivisible unit.

## 5. Financial application: marginal cost and profit

Let

\[
C(q)=1000+20q+0.05q^2.
\]

Then

\[
C'(q)=20+0.1q.
\]

Profit is revenue minus cost:

\[
\Pi(q)=R(q)-C(q)=-1000+80q-0.25q^2,
\]

and

\[
\boxed{\Pi'(q)=80-0.5q}.
\]

At \(q=100\), marginal profit is 30 currency units per unit. The first-order estimate for a small quantity change \(\Delta q\) is

\[
\Delta\Pi\approx\Pi'(q)\Delta q.
\]

For a finite unit change, the exact value is \(\Pi(q+1)-\Pi(q)\). At \(q=100\), this equals 29.75, not 30.

## 6. Financial application: quadratic penalty

A simple risk or transaction-cost approximation may include a quadratic penalty

\[
K(w)=aw^2+bw+c,\qquad a>0.
\]

Its marginal change is

\[
K'(w)=2aw+b.
\]

This expression measures local sensitivity to the position \(w\). It does not, by itself, establish that a model is suitable for production trading or risk management.

## 7. Numerical checks and common mistakes

A centered difference approximates \(f'(x)\):

\[
D_hf(x)=\frac{f(x+h)-f(x-h)}{2h}.
\]

For a quadratic polynomial, the centered difference equals its analytic derivative in exact arithmetic for any nonzero \(h\). Floating-point rounding may cause small discrepancies.

Avoid these errors:

- Differentiating a constant as itself rather than zero.
- Forgetting the exponent multiplier or failing to reduce the exponent by one.
- Applying the power rule directly to a product of arbitrary functions.
- Confusing \(R'(q)\) with \(P'(q)\) when \(R(q)=qP(q)\).
- Interpreting a derivative as an exact change over a large interval.
- Ignoring the domain of negative or fractional powers.

## 9. Exercises

**1.** Differentiate \(f(x)=12\).

**2.** Differentiate \(f(x)=7x^5\).

**3.** Differentiate \(f(x)=4x^3-6x+9\).

**4.** Find \(p'(2)\) for \(p(x)=7x^4-3x^3+5x^2-9x+12\).

**5.** Differentiate \(f(x)=x^{-2}\), stating its domain.

**6.** Differentiate \(f(x)=\sqrt{x}\) on \(x>0\).

**7.** For \(P(q)=100-0.2q\), derive revenue and marginal revenue.

**8.** For \(C(q)=1000+20q+0.05q^2\), find marginal cost at \(q=100\).

**9.** Find marginal profit at \(q=100\) when \(R(q)=100q-0.2q^2\) and \(C(q)=1000+20q+0.05q^2\).

**10.** Explain why marginal profit and the profit change from one additional unit need not agree exactly.

### Worked solutions

1. \(f'(x)=0\).
2. \(f'(x)=35x^4\).
3. \(f'(x)=12x^2-6\).
4. \(p'(2)=199\).
5. \(f'(x)=-2x^{-3}\), for \(x\ne0\).
6. \(f'(x)=1/(2\sqrt{x})\), for \(x>0\).
7. \(R(q)=100q-0.2q^2\), so \(R'(q)=100-0.4q\).
8. \(C'(100)=20+0.1(100)=30\).
9. \(\Pi'(q)=80-0.5q\); at 100, \(\Pi'(100)=30\).
10. The derivative is the limiting slope; the finite change includes curvature. Here \(\Pi(101)-\Pi(100)=29.75\).
