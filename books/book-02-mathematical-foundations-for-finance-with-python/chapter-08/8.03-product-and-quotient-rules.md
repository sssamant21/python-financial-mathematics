## Learning objectives

## 1. Product rule

For functions \(u\) and \(v\) differentiable at \(x\),

\[
\boxed{(uv)'=u'v+uv'.}
\]

**Derivation.** Add and subtract \(u(x+h)v(x)\) in the numerator:

\[
\begin{aligned}
\frac{u(x+h)v(x+h)-u(x)v(x)}h
&=u(x+h)\frac{v(x+h)-v(x)}h\\
&\quad+v(x)\frac{u(x+h)-u(x)}h.
\end{aligned}
\]

Differentiability implies continuity, so \(u(x+h)\to u(x)\). Taking limits gives the formula.

**Example.** For \(f(x)=x^2(3x+1)\),

\[
f'(x)=2x(3x+1)+3x^2=9x^2+2x.
\]

Do not differentiate a product by multiplying derivatives: \(u'v'\) is generally wrong.

## 2. Quotient rule

For differentiable \(u,v\) with \(v(x)\ne0\),

\[
\boxed{\left(\frac uv\right)'=\frac{u'v-uv'}{v^2}.}
\]

A useful derivation first differentiates the reciprocal. Since

\[
\frac{1/v(x+h)-1/v(x)}h
=-\frac{v(x+h)-v(x)}{h\,v(x+h)v(x)},
\]

continuity and \(v(x)\ne0\) give \((1/v)'=-v'/v^2\). Apply the product rule to \(u(1/v)\).

**Example.** For \(f(x)=(x^2+1)/(x+2)\), where \(x\ne-2\),

\[
f'(x)=\frac{2x(x+2)-(x^2+1)}{(x+2)^2}
=\frac{x^2+4x-1}{(x+2)^2}.
\]

## 3. Financial application: quantity-dependent price

Revenue equals quantity times price:

\[
R(q)=qP(q).
\]

By the product rule,

\[
\boxed{R'(q)=P(q)+qP'(q).}
\]

The first term captures revenue from the marginal unit at the current price; the second captures the price response on existing volume in the continuous model.

If \(P(q)=120-0.3q\), then

\[
R'(q)=(120-0.3q)-0.3q=120-0.6q.
\]

At \(q=100\), marginal revenue is 60 currency units per additional unit. This is a local derivative, not necessarily an exact one-unit revenue change.

## 4. Financial application: average cost

Let total cost be

\[
C(q)=500+10q+0.1q^2,\qquad q>0.
\]

Average cost per unit is

\[
A(q)=\frac{C(q)}q.
\]

Using the quotient rule,

\[
A'(q)=\frac{qC'(q)-C(q)}{q^2}.
\]

Here \(C'(q)=10+0.2q\), so

\[
\boxed{A'(q)=0.1-\frac{500}{q^2}.}
\]

At \(q=100\), \(A'(100)=0.05\) currency units per unit of average cost, per additional unit of output. The domain excludes \(q=0\), where average cost is undefined.

## 5. Financial application: a ratio of growing quantities

Suppose asset value \(A(t)=100+8t\) and liabilities \(L(t)=50+3t\), with \(L(t)>0\). The coverage ratio is

\[
Q(t)=\frac{A(t)}{L(t)}.
\]

Then

\[
Q'(t)=\frac{8(50+3t)-3(100+8t)}{(50+3t)^2}
=\boxed{\frac{100}{(50+3t)^2}}.
\]

At \(t=0\), \(Q'(0)=0.04\) ratio units per year. A positive derivative does not by itself establish credit quality; the ratio is only one stylized metric.

## 6. Discounted cash flows with a changing payment

For a differentiable payment amount \(C(t)\) and a constant continuously compounded rate \(r\),

\[
V(t)=C(t)e^{-rt}.
\]

Using the product rule and the exponential derivative (derived from the exponential limit),

\[
\boxed{V'(t)=C'(t)e^{-rt}-rC(t)e^{-rt}}.
\]

The first term reflects changing cash flow; the second reflects discounting. If the rate itself varies with time, additional modeling and chain-rule considerations are required.

## 7. Numerical validation and domain safeguards

For a smooth function, compare the analytic derivative with the centered difference

\[
D_hf(x)=\frac{f(x+h)-f(x-h)}{2h}.
\]

Check that the points \(x-h\) and \(x+h\) remain in the function's domain. A quotient with a denominator near zero can be ill-conditioned even when mathematically defined. A small difference between numerical and analytic values is supporting evidence, not a proof.

## Complete product and quotient evaluation

The generic product- and quotient-rule helpers evaluate their complete expressions using exact arithmetic on the finite represented callback values, then round once to float. Finite results survive intermediate product overflow or underflow; true final overflow raises `ValueError`, and final underflow may yield signed zero. Callback validation, one evaluation of each required callback, and the original evaluation order are preserved. A zero quotient denominator is rejected before the other callbacks are called.

These checks do not verify that the supplied derivative callbacks are mathematically correct. The section's separate financial examples and centered-difference copy have their own range obligations. [Selected exact-series and callback evidence](../validation/chapter-08-product-quotient-range-followup.md) retains initial failures and corrected runs.

## Financial rational-expression evaluation

The average-cost value and derivative and the coverage-ratio value and derivative now evaluate their complete rational expressions before rounding to float. Positive quantity remains required for average cost. The coefficient `0.1` is interpreted as the existing represented float coefficient; the implementation does not silently replace it with exact decimal one-tenth. Unrepresentable final results raise `ValueError`; underflow may return signed zero.

The algebraic coverage helpers use the exact denominator `50 + 3*t` at the represented finite input, and reject an exact zero. They no longer reject a nonzero denominator solely because a separate float evaluation rounds it to zero. This permits the closest representable points to the pole, where results can be large and financially ill-conditioned. The financial example assumes positive liabilities; the helpers retain their broader algebraic signed-time domain and do not establish economic suitability for negative liabilities. [Range and near-pole evidence](../validation/chapter-08-financial-rational-range-followup.md) retains the baseline failures and independent polynomial-quotient references.

## Discounted-payment callback evaluation

The discounted-value wrappers validate finite time and rate, with nonnegative time, before calling a payment function. The value wrapper evaluates the payment once and leaves its derivative argument unused. The derivative wrapper evaluates the derivative once, then the payment once. Required callback values remain finite numeric inputs excluding bool.

The discount exponent and full product-rule coefficient are formed from exact represented inputs before the final scaling and float conversion. Zero payment and cancelled-coefficient identities are retained without evaluating an overflowing exponential. True final overflow raises `ValueError`; final underflow may return signed zero. These are range controls, not verification of the supplied derivative callback or a financial model. [Callback and scaling evidence](../validation/chapter-08-discounted-payment-range-followup.md) retains baseline failures and independent discounted-term references.
