## Learning objectives

Derive present-value sensitivities to discount rates and cash flows; calculate continuous-rate duration and convexity; distinguish parallel shifts from key-rate shocks; reconcile analytic derivatives with exact revaluation; and identify assumptions that make reported sensitivities valid.

## 1. Discounting convention and units

For fixed cash flows \(C_i\) paid at times \(t_i\ge0\), with a flat continuously compounded decimal annual rate \(r\),

\[
\boxed{V(r)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}C_i e^{-rt_i}}.
\]

Cash flows are in currency units, maturities in years, and rates in decimal annual units. For example, 4% is \(r=0.04\); 1 basis point (bp) is \(0.0001\), and 100 bp is \(0.01\).

This model assumes deterministic, rate-independent cash flows and a common discount rate. Actual yield-curve and instrument conventions may differ.

## 2. First and second rate derivatives

Termwise differentiation gives

\[
\boxed{V'(r)=-\sum_i t_i C_i e^{-rt_i}},\qquad
\boxed{V''(r)=\sum_i t_i^2 C_i e^{-rt_i}}.
\]

For positive \(C_i\), \(V'(r)\le0\) and \(V''(r)\ge0\). Equality can hold when every maturity is zero. A rate rise therefore lowers the value of positive future fixed payments; the curve is convex.

For a small parallel shock \(h\),

\[
\Delta V\approx V'(r)h
\quad\text{or}\quad
\Delta V\approx V'(r)h+\frac12V''(r)h^2.
\]

The second expression is the second-order Taylor estimate, not an exact change.

## 3. Duration and convexity under continuous compounding

For \(V(r)>0\), define

\[
D_c(r)=-\frac{V'(r)}{V(r)},\qquad
K_c(r)=\frac{V''(r)}{V(r)}.
\]

For positive cash flows,

\[
\boxed{D_c=\frac{\sum_i t_i C_i e^{-rt_i}}{V}},\qquad
\boxed{K_c=\frac{\sum_i t_i^2 C_i e^{-rt_i}}{V}}.
\]

These are present-value-weighted first and second moments of payment times. \(D_c\) has units of years; \(K_c\) has units of years squared under the stated convention. With weights \(w_i=C_ie^{-rt_i}/V\), the identity \(K_c-D_c^2=\sum_i w_i(t_i-D_c)^2\ge0\) shows that convexity exceeds or equals squared duration for positive cash flows.

The normalized approximation is

\[
\boxed{\frac{\Delta V}{V}\approx-D_c h+\frac12K_c h^2}.
\]

**Important:** these are sensitivities to a continuously compounded rate. Modified duration and convexity defined using a periodically compounded yield require conversion and generally have different numerical values.

## 4. Worked single-payment example

Let \(C=10000\), \(t=5\), and \(r=0.04\). Then

\[
V=10000e^{-0.2}\approx8187.307531,\quad
V'=-5V\approx-40936.537655,\quad
V''=25V\approx204682.688274.
\]

Thus \(D_c=5\) years and \(K_c=25\) years squared. For a **10 bp** rate rise \(h=0.001\),

\[
\Delta V_{\rm linear}=-5V(0.001)\approx-40.936538,
\]

\[
\Delta V_{\rm quadratic}=-5V(0.001)+\frac12(25V)(0.001)^2
\approx-40.834196.
\]

The exact change is \(V\operatorname{expm1}(-0.005)\approx-40.834367\). The quadratic estimate is closer for this shock.

For a 1 bp rise, the approximate dollar change is \(V'(r)\times0.0001\), **not** \(V'(r)\) itself.

## 5. Worked two-payment portfolio

Consider \(C_1=100\) at \(t_1=1\) and \(C_2=1100\) at \(t_2=2\), with \(r=0.04\). Define

\[
v_1=100e^{-0.04},\qquad v_2=1100e^{-0.08}.
\]

Then

\[
V=v_1+v_2,\quad
D_c=\frac{v_1+2v_2}{v_1+v_2},\quad
K_c=\frac{v_1+4v_2}{v_1+v_2}.
\]

For a 50 bp parallel shock \(h=0.005\), calculate

\[
\Delta V_1=-VD_ch,\quad
\Delta V_2=-VD_ch+\tfrac12VK_ch^2,
\]

and compare against

\[
\Delta V_{\rm exact}
=v_1\operatorname{expm1}(-0.005)
+v_2\operatorname{expm1}(-0.01).
\]

The companion lab reports the exact and approximate changes without subtracting nearly equal present values.

## 6. Cash-flow sensitivity

Holding the discount rate and time fixed,

\[
\boxed{\frac{\partial V}{\partial C_i}=e^{-rt_i}}.
\]

An additional dollar paid at \(t_i\) changes current value by its discount factor, assuming a linear fixed-cash-flow model. The cash-flow elasticity is

\[
\frac{C_i}{V}\frac{\partial V}{\partial C_i}
=\frac{C_ie^{-rt_i}}{V}=w_i.
\]

This identifies each payment's proportional contribution to portfolio value. It is distinct from rate duration.

## 7. Parallel rate shifts versus yield-curve shifts

A flat-rate model uses one scalar \(r\). A simple term-structure model can instead assign a continuously compounded spot rate \(z_i\) to each cash-flow maturity:

\[
V(\boldsymbol z)=\sum_i C_i e^{-z_i t_i}.
\]

Holding other spot rates fixed,

\[
\boxed{\frac{\partial V}{\partial z_i}=-t_i C_i e^{-z_i t_i}}.
\]

These **cash-flow-level spot sensitivities** are not automatically market key-rate durations. Real key-rate reports require a specified curve interpolation, bump construction, and repricing method; bumping a curve node can affect discount factors at many maturities.

A parallel shift \(z_i\mapsto z_i+h\) for every \(i\) has derivative

\[
\frac{dV}{dh}\bigg|_{h=0}=-\sum_i t_i C_i e^{-z_i t_i}.
\]

A nonparallel scenario with shocks \(h_i\) has the first-order approximation

\[
\Delta V\approx-\sum_i t_i C_i e^{-z_i t_i}h_i.
\]

Second-order terms under independently bumped spot rates are diagonal in this simplified cash-flow model. More elaborate curves and instruments can introduce interactions.

## 8. Scenario comparison and sign conventions

Use **signed** changes: \(\Delta V=V_{\rm shocked}-V_{\rm base}\). For positive fixed future cash flows, an upward parallel shock gives \(\Delta V<0\). A downward shock gives \(\Delta V>0\).

When reporting:

1. Record valuation convention, cash flows, payment times, and base rates.
2. State whether shocks are parallel or maturity-specific, and convert bp to decimal rate units.
3. Compute analytic sensitivities and exact scenario revaluation independently.
4. Compare first-order and second-order estimates with exact changes.
5. Report errors, domain restrictions, and model assumptions.

## 9. Error and model-risk boundaries

Taylor error can be bounded using the derivative results in [Section 9.5](9.05-remainder-and-error-bounds.md). That bound concerns the mathematical approximation to the **specified model**, not errors from credit risk, changing cash flows, market calibration, rounding, or yield-curve construction.

For instruments with optionality or floating-rate payments, cash flows can change when rates change. Fixed-cash-flow duration and convexity are then incomplete risk measures. Likewise, discounting at a flat rate is not a substitute for a calibrated term structure when the decision requires one.

## 11. Exercises and worked solutions

**1.** Derive \(V'(r)\) for \(V=\sum_i C_ie^{-rt_i}\).

*Solution:* Differentiate each term: \(V'=-\sum_i t_iC_ie^{-rt_i}\).

**2.** Derive \(V''(r)\).

*Solution:* Differentiate again: \(V''=\sum_i t_i^2C_ie^{-rt_i}\).

**3.** State sufficient conditions for \(V'\le0\) and \(V''\ge0\).

*Solution:* All cash flows \(C_i>0\) and times \(t_i\ge0\). Strict inequalities require at least one positive-time payment.

**4.** Derive continuous-rate duration and convexity.

*Solution:* Divide \(-V'\) and \(V''\) by \(V>0\), respectively, yielding present-value-weighted \(t_i\) and \(t_i^2\).

**5.** Calculate \(D_c,K_c\) for one payment in five years.

*Solution:* \(D_c=5\) and \(K_c=25\).

**6.** Convert a 25 bp shock into decimal rate units.

*Solution:* \(h=25\times0.0001=0.0025\).

**7.** Calculate the approximate 25 bp value change for \(V=8000,D_c=4\), ignoring convexity.

*Solution:* \(\Delta V\approx-8000(4)(0.0025)=-80\).

**8.** Derive the sensitivity of value to a cash flow \(C_i\).

*Solution:* \(\partial V/\partial C_i=e^{-rt_i}\).

**9.** Derive a cash-flow-level spot-rate sensitivity in a term structure.

*Solution:* \(\partial V/\partial z_i=-t_iC_ie^{-z_it_i}\) when each \(z_i\) is an independent discount-rate input.

**10.** Explain why independently bumping a single spot rate is not necessarily a market key-rate duration.

*Solution:* Market key-rate shocks depend on curve-node definitions, interpolation, and the repricing of all cash flows affected by a node bump.

**11.** Prove \(K_c\ge D_c^2\) for positive fixed cash flows.

*Solution:* With positive weights summing to one, \(K_c-D_c^2=\sum_i w_i(t_i-D_c)^2\ge0\).

**12.** Why is exact revaluation still needed when duration and convexity are available?

*Solution:* Higher-order terms, large or nonparallel shocks, and rate-dependent cash flows can make local approximations inaccurate.
