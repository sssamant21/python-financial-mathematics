## Chapter assessment

This cumulative section tests Sections 9.1–9.9: absolute and relative sensitivity, elasticity, Taylor approximations, remainder bounds, discounted-value sensitivity, kinks, and reproducible scenario reporting. All interest rates are **decimal annual continuously compounded** unless explicitly stated otherwise. A basis point (bp) is \(10^{-4}\) in decimal rate units.

### Core formulas

For a smooth function \(f(x)\), a shock \(h\), and a suitable interval containing \(x\) and \(x+h\),

\[
\Delta f_1=f'(x)h,\qquad
\Delta f_2=f'(x)h+\frac12f''(x)h^2.
\]

If \(|f''|\le M_2\) and \(|f'''|\le M_3\) on that interval, then

\[
|R_1|\le \frac12M_2h^2,\qquad
|R_2|\le \frac16M_3|h|^3.
\]

For positive fixed cash flows \(C_i\) paid at \(t_i\ge0\),

\[
V(r)=\sum_i C_ie^{-rt_i},\quad
V'=-\sum_i t_iC_ie^{-rt_i},\quad
V''=\sum_i t_i^2C_ie^{-rt_i}.
\]

When \(V>0\), continuous-rate duration is \(D=-V'/V\) and convexity is \(K=V''/V\). Exact parallel-shock change is

\[
\Delta V_{\rm exact}=\sum_i C_ie^{-rt_i}\operatorname{expm1}(-t_i h).
\]

## Part A — Sensitivity and elasticity

**Exercise 1 — Absolute and relative sensitivity.** Let \(f(x)=3x^2+2\). Find \(f'(4)\) and the local relative sensitivity \(xf'(x)/f(x)\) at \(x=4\).

**Solution.** \(f'(x)=6x\), so \(f'(4)=24\). Since \(f(4)=50\), the elasticity is \(4(24)/50=1.92\). A 1% local change in \(x\) corresponds to approximately 1.92% change in \(f\).

**Exercise 2 — Exponential elasticity.** For \(f(x)=e^{2x}\), derive \(xf'(x)/f(x)\) and evaluate at \(x=0.5\).

**Solution.** \(f'=2e^{2x}\), so elasticity is \(2x\); at \(x=0.5\), it equals 1.

**Exercise 3 — When relative sensitivity fails.** Explain why \(f'(x)/f(x)\) is undefined when \(f(x)=0\). Give a financial reporting alternative.

**Solution.** The denominator vanishes. Report absolute sensitivity and scenario changes in currency units, or normalize by a documented nonzero reference scale rather than silently dividing by zero.

## Part B — Taylor approximation

**Exercise 4 — First-order approximation.** Approximate \(\sqrt{101}\) about \(x_0=100\).

**Solution.** \(f(100)=10\), \(f'(100)=1/20\), \(h=1\). Thus \(T_1=10+0.05=10.05\).

**Exercise 5 — Second-order approximation.** Improve Exercise 4 using \(f''(x)=-1/(4x^{3/2})\).

**Solution.** \(f''(100)=-1/4000\). The correction is \(-1/8000=-0.000125\), so \(T_2=10.049875\).

**Exercise 6 — Cubic exact remainder.** Expand \(f(x)=x^3\) about \(x_0=2\) through order two and find the exact remainder for \(h=-0.1\).

**Solution.** \((2+h)^3=8+12h+6h^2+h^3\). Therefore \(R_2=h^3=-0.001\) and \(|R_2|=0.001\).

**Exercise 7 — First-order error bound.** For \(f(x)=x^3\), \(x_0=2\), \(h=0.1\), bound the linear approximation error.

**Solution.** On \([2,2.1]\), \(|f''(x)|=6x\le12.6\). Hence \(|R_1|\le12.6(0.1)^2/2=0.063\). The actual signed error is \(6(0.1)^2+(0.1)^3=0.061\).

**Exercise 8 — Second-order error budget.** Suppose \(|f'''|\le48\) throughout \(|h|\le0.1\). What is the largest \(|h|\) that guarantees second-order error at most \(0.0008\)?

**Solution.** \(48|h|^3/6\le0.0008\), so \(8|h|^3\le0.0008\), \(|h|^3\le0.0001\), and \(|h|\le(0.0001)^{1/3}\approx0.046416\). This lies inside the stated domain.

## Part C — Discounted cash flows

**Exercise 9 — Single-payment value.** A payment of \(10000\) is due in five years. Find its value at \(r=0.04\).

**Solution.** \(V=10000e^{-0.2}\approx8187.307531\).

**Exercise 10 — Duration and convexity.** For the payment in Exercise 9, calculate \(V'\), \(V''\), \(D\), and \(K\).

**Solution.** \(V'=-5V\approx-40936.537655\), \(V''=25V\approx204682.688274\), \(D=5\), and \(K=25\).

**Exercise 11 — Ten-basis-point scenario.** For Exercise 9, find the linear and quadratic value changes for \(h=+0.001\).

**Solution.** \(\Delta V_1=-5V(0.001)\approx-40.936538\). The quadratic correction is \(25V(0.001)^2/2\approx0.102341\), so \(\Delta V_2\approx-40.834196\).

**Exercise 12 — Exact scenario and error.** For Exercise 11, write the exact change and the signed quadratic error.

**Solution.** \(\Delta V_{\rm exact}=V\operatorname{expm1}(-0.005)\). The signed error is \(E_2=V[\operatorname{expm1}(-0.005)+0.005-0.005^2/2]\), which is negative. Its magnitude is approximately \(0.0001703\).

**Exercise 13 — Rigorous second-order bound.** Bound the error in Exercise 12.

**Solution.** The interval is \([0.04,0.041]\), so \(r_{\min}=0.04\). \(M_3=5^3V=125V\), and \(|E_2|\le125V(0.001)^3/6\approx0.00017057\).

**Exercise 14 — Two-payment portfolio.** For payments \((100,1)\) and \((1100,2)\) at \(r=0.04\), express \(V,D,K\) in terms of \(v_1=100e^{-0.04}\), \(v_2=1100e^{-0.08}\).

**Solution.** \(V=v_1+v_2\), \(D=(v_1+2v_2)/(v_1+v_2)\), and \(K=(v_1+4v_2)/(v_1+v_2)\).

**Exercise 15 — Duration/convexity inequality.** Prove \(K\ge D^2\) for positive fixed cash flows.

**Solution.** Let \(w_i=C_ie^{-rt_i}/V\), so \(w_i>0\) and \(\sum_iw_i=1\). Then \(K-D^2=\sum_iw_i(t_i-D)^2\ge0\).

**Exercise 16 — Cash-flow sensitivity.** Find \(\partial V/\partial C_i\) for a fixed payment \(C_i\) at \(t_i\).

**Solution.** \(\partial V/\partial C_i=e^{-rt_i}\). The elasticity of \(V\) with respect to \(C_i\) is \(C_ie^{-rt_i}/V\).

## Part D — Kinks and directional risk

**Exercise 17 — Call payoff.** Let \(g(S)=\max(S-100,0)\). Find its left and right derivatives at \(S=100\).

**Solution.** Left slope 0; right slope 1. There is no two-sided derivative.

**Exercise 18 — Centered difference trap.** Calculate \([g(102)-g(98)]/4\) for Exercise 17. Explain why the result is misleading.

**Solution.** \((2-0)/4=0.5\), although the true one-sided slopes are 0 and 1. The centered quotient is not the derivative at the kink.

**Exercise 19 — Cap and floor.** For \(f(r)=1000\min(\max(r,0.02),0.05)\), state the one-sided derivatives at \(r=0.02\) and \(r=0.05\).

**Solution.** At the floor: left 0, right 1000. At the cap: left 1000, right 0.

**Exercise 20 — Offset of kinks.** Show \(\max(S-K,0)-\max(K-S,0)=S-K\).

**Solution.** For \(S\ge K\), the difference is \((S-K)-0\); for \(S<K\), it is \(0-(K-S)\). Thus the combined payoff is smooth despite nonsmooth components.

## Part E — Reporting and engineering

**Exercise 21 — Scenario units.** Convert \(-75\) bp to a decimal rate shock.

**Solution.** \(-75/10000=-0.0075\).

**Exercise 22 — Nonparallel shocks.** For independent spot rates \(z_i\) and shocks \(h_i\), derive a first-order value change.

**Solution.** \(\Delta V_1=-\sum_i t_iC_ie^{-z_it_i}h_i\). This is a cash-flow-level spot-rate sensitivity, not necessarily a market key-rate duration.

**Exercise 23 — Error denominator.** Why is \(|E_2|/|\Delta V_{\rm exact}|\) unreliable near a zero exact change?

**Solution.** It is undefined at zero and numerically unstable for tiny exact changes. Report absolute currency error and, if appropriate, error relative to a nonzero baseline value.

**Exercise 24 — Acceptance decision.** A report passes all mathematical remainder checks but uses an unverified cash-flow schedule. Can it be called production-certified?

**Solution.** No. A remainder bound verifies an approximation within the specified mathematical model. It does not validate source cash flows, curve calibration, financial assumptions, runtime evidence, or independent review.

## 25. Applied project assessment

Using the Chapter 9.9 portfolio \((120,0.5),(120,1),(120,2),(1120,3)\) at \(r=0.04\):

1. Compute the baseline value, \(V'\), \(V''\), duration, and convexity.
2. Revalue shocks of \(-100,-50,-25,0,25,50,100\) bp.
3. Compare exact, first-order, and second-order changes.
4. Compute interval-wide Lagrange bounds for each scenario.
5. Explain why the quadratic error is not necessarily smaller than the linear error for *every conceivable* model and shock.
6. Export a deterministic JSON report and document any validation failures.

**Worked solution method.** Let \(v_i=C_ie^{-0.04t_i}\). Compute \(V=\sum_i v_i\), \(V'=-\sum_i t_iv_i\), and \(V''=\sum_i t_i^2v_i\); then \(D=-V'/V\), \(K=V''/V\). For each \(h=\mathrm{bp}/10000\), compute exact change as \(\sum_i v_i\operatorname{expm1}(-t_ih)\), linear change as \(V'h\), and quadratic change as \(V'h+\frac12V''h^2\). Set \(r_{\min}=\min(0.04,0.04+h)\), calculate \(M_2=\sum_i t_i^2C_ie^{-r_{\min}t_i}\) and \(M_3=\sum_i t_i^3C_ie^{-r_{\min}t_i}\), and verify \(|E_1|\le M_2h^2/2\) and \(|E_2|\le M_3|h|^3/6\) within a documented numerical allowance. The [project implementation](code/section_9_09.py) and [assessment checks](code/check_section_9_10.py) provide reproducible calculations. The second-order term improves accuracy locally for this positive fixed-cash-flow model, but no universal improvement claim holds across arbitrary functions and shocks.
