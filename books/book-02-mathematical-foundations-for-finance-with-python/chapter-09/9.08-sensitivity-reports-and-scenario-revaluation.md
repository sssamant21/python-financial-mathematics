## Learning objectives

Build a reproducible sensitivity report; define a baseline and shocks unambiguously; compare first-order, second-order, and exact revaluations; measure approximation errors; distinguish parallel from nonparallel shocks; and specify review and acceptance controls.

## 1. What a sensitivity report must answer

A reliable report identifies (1) the valued instrument and its cash flows, (2) the discounting convention and baseline market inputs, (3) the exact shocks, (4) the analytical sensitivities, (5) the revalued outcomes, and (6) the limitations of the result. A reported duration without the associated rate convention and shock unit is incomplete.

Use a fixed-cash-flow portfolio with positive payments \(C_i\) at times \(t_i\ge0\). For a flat continuously compounded decimal rate \(r\),

\[
V(r)=\sum_i C_i e^{-rt_i}.
\]

The model intentionally excludes stochastic cash flows, default, optionality, and market-curve interpolation. The report must disclose these assumptions.

## 2. Baseline valuation and sensitivities

The rate derivatives are

\[
V'(r)=-\sum_i t_iC_ie^{-rt_i},\qquad
V''(r)=\sum_i t_i^2C_ie^{-rt_i}.
\]

For \(V>0\), define continuous-rate duration \(D=-V'/V\) and convexity \(K=V''/V\). The approximations to a parallel shock \(h\) are

\[
\Delta V_1=V'h=-VDh,
\]

\[
\Delta V_2=V'h+\frac12V''h^2
=V\left(-Dh+\frac12Kh^2\right).
\]

The exact change is

\[
\boxed{\Delta V_{\rm exact}=\sum_i C_ie^{-rt_i}\operatorname{expm1}(-t_i h).}
\]

Using \`expm1\` avoids subtracting two nearly equal portfolio values for small shocks.

## 3. Units and scenario definitions

Rates are decimal annual continuously compounded rates. Convert basis points using

\[
h=\frac{\text{shock in bp}}{10000}.
\]

For example, +25 bp is \(h=0.0025\), and −100 bp is \(h=-0.01\). These are **absolute rate shocks**, not percentage changes in the rate itself.

Use a two-sided scenario grid such as −100, −50, −25, −10, 0, +10, +25, +50, +100 bp. Always include a zero-shock control. Negative shocked rates are mathematically permitted in this continuous-discount model; actual financial instruments may have different contractual or economic constraints.

## 4. Worked portfolio

Take two fixed positive cash flows: \(100\) at year 1 and \(1100\) at year 2, with \(r=0.04\).

\[
v_1=100e^{-0.04},\qquad v_2=1100e^{-0.08},\qquad V_0=v_1+v_2.
\]

The derivative and curvature are

\[
V'=-v_1-2v_2,\qquad V''=v_1+4v_2.
\]

For +50 bp (\(h=0.005\)):

\[
\Delta V_1=(-v_1-2v_2)(0.005),
\]

\[
\Delta V_2=\Delta V_1+\frac12(v_1+4v_2)(0.005)^2,
\]

\[
\Delta V_{\rm exact}=v_1\operatorname{expm1}(-0.005)+v_2\operatorname{expm1}(-0.01).
\]

Report all three as **signed currency changes**. The corresponding shocked values equal \(V_0+\Delta V_j\). For positive fixed future payments, a positive parallel rate shock lowers value.

## 5. Approximation errors

Define signed errors using exact minus approximation:

\[
E_1=\Delta V_{\rm exact}-\Delta V_1,\qquad
E_2=\Delta V_{\rm exact}-\Delta V_2.
\]

Report \(|E_1|\) and \(|E_2|\), too. If the exact change is nonzero, a relative change-error ratio can be computed as \(|E_j|/|\Delta V_{\rm exact}|\). It is undefined for a zero exact change and unstable when the denominator is tiny.

If a report uses a tolerance \(\tau\), specify whether \(\tau\) is a currency error, fraction of baseline value, or fraction of the exact change. These are not interchangeable. Observed scenario errors do not establish a uniform bound over an interval; Section 9.5 derives rigorous remainder bounds under explicit assumptions.

## 6. Nonparallel spot-rate scenarios

For a simplified term structure with independent continuously compounded spot rates \(z_i\),

\[
V(\boldsymbol z)=\sum_i C_ie^{-z_i t_i}.
\]

For maturity-specific shocks \(h_i\), exact change is

\[
\Delta V_{\rm exact}=\sum_i C_ie^{-z_i t_i}\operatorname{expm1}(-t_i h_i).
\]

The first- and second-order estimates are

\[
\Delta V_1=-\sum_i t_i C_ie^{-z_i t_i}h_i,
\]

\[
\Delta V_2=\Delta V_1+\frac12\sum_i t_i^2 C_ie^{-z_i t_i}h_i^2.
\]

These independent cash-flow-level spot shocks are **not automatically market key-rate durations**. A market key-rate report must document curve nodes, interpolation, bump construction, and instrument repricing.

## 7. Recommended report schema

| Field | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Model | Fixed positive cash flows, continuous discounting |
| Baseline rate(s) | Decimal annual rates and convention |
| Scenario | Signed shock, with bp-to-decimal conversion |
| Baseline value | \(V_0\) in currency |
| Rate sensitivity | \(V'\) in currency per unit decimal rate |
| Curvature | \(V''\) in currency per squared rate unit |
| Duration / convexity | Normalized \(D,K\), when defined |
| Exact change | \(\Delta V_{\rm exact}\) |
| Linear / quadratic change | \(\Delta V_1,\Delta V_2\) |
| Absolute errors | \(|E_1|,|E_2|\) |
| Validation | Input guards, zero-shock test, independent reference checks |
| Caveats | Fixed cash flows, no credit/optionality/curve calibration |

For production use, add an as-of timestamp, data provenance, model version, reviewer, sign-off, and evidence of runtime execution. The educational lab supplies a deterministic demonstration, not a production pricing engine.

## 8. Acceptance gates and escalation

A scenario report should be flagged when any of the following occurs:

- Nonfinite values or inconsistent cash-flow/time inputs.
- Missing or ambiguous rate convention or shock units.
- Zero-shock exact or approximate change materially different from zero.
- A positive parallel rate shock raises the value of a portfolio of positive fixed future payments.
- An approximation exceeds the documented error tolerance.
- The scenario crosses a kink, changes cash-flow behavior, or violates a model assumption.

**Important:** A small observed error is not proof of model correctness. If a shock is large or the cash flows depend on rates, use an appropriate full revaluation model.

## 10. Exercises and worked solutions

**1.** State the minimum information needed to interpret a duration-based scenario.

*Solution:* Baseline valuation, cash-flow/model assumptions, rate convention, shock units and direction, and duration definition.

**2.** Convert +25 bp and −50 bp to decimal rate shocks.

*Solution:* \(+0.0025\) and \(-0.005\).

**3.** Derive the linear and quadratic changes for a flat continuously compounded rate shock.

*Solution:* \(\Delta V_1=V'h\); \(\Delta V_2=V'h+\tfrac12V''h^2\).

**4.** Derive the exact change without subtracting two complete portfolio values.

*Solution:* \(\sum_i C_ie^{-rt_i}\operatorname{expm1}(-t_i h)\).

**5.** Why should a scenario grid contain \(h=0\)?

*Solution:* It provides a control: exact and approximate changes should all equal zero.

**6.** For \(V=8000,D=4,h=0.0025\), calculate the first-order change.

*Solution:* \(-8000(4)(0.0025)=-80\).

**7.** Add convexity \(K=18\) to Exercise 6.

*Solution:* The correction is \(\tfrac12(8000)(18)(0.0025)^2=0.45\); quadratic change is \(-79.55\).

**8.** Define the signed quadratic approximation error.

*Solution:* \(E_2=\Delta V_{\rm exact}-\Delta V_2\).

**9.** Why can relative error based on the exact change be misleading?

*Solution:* It is undefined at zero and unstable when the exact change is extremely small.

**10.** Derive the first-order response to independent spot shocks \(h_i\).

*Solution:* \(-\sum_i t_iC_ie^{-z_it_i}h_i\).

**11.** Why are independently bumped spot rates not necessarily market key-rate durations?

*Solution:* Market key-rate shocks depend on the interpolation and bump methodology for the entire curve.

**12.** What should a reviewer do if a scenario crosses a contractual cap or floor?

*Solution:* Use a model that reprices the piecewise cash flow, report one-sided behavior where relevant, and avoid relying solely on smooth Taylor sensitivities.
