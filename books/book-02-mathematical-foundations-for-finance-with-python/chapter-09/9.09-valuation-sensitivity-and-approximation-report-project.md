## Project mission

Build a transparent, reproducible **fixed-cash-flow valuation sensitivity engine** using only the Python standard library. The deliverable must calculate baseline present value, continuous-rate duration and convexity, exact parallel-shock revaluations, first- and second-order approximations, rigorous Taylor remainder bounds, and a compact machine-readable report.

This is an educational risk-engineering exercise, **not** a production pricing or risk-management system.

## 1. Acceptance requirements

The engine must:

1. Validate positive finite cash flows, nonnegative payment times, and finite rates and shocks.
2. Use a single documented convention: decimal annual continuously compounded discount rates.
3. Convert basis-point shocks to decimal rate units explicitly.
4. Compute \(V,V',V''\), normalized duration \(D\), and convexity \(K\).
5. Calculate exact changes stably with \`math.expm1\`.
6. Produce linear and quadratic approximations, signed errors, and absolute errors.
7. Construct mathematically valid first- and second-order Taylor error bounds over each entire shock interval.
8. Check each computed error against its respective bound with an explicit numerical allowance.
9. Include upward, downward, and zero-shock scenarios.
10. Produce a JSON-compatible report with clear metadata and deterministic output.
11. Run independent reference checks for analytic identities, negative shocks, zero shocks, and invalid inputs.
12. Distinguish code execution, mathematical checks, and independent editorial approval.

## 2. Valuation model

For \(n\) fixed positive payments \(C_i\) occurring at times \(t_i\ge0\),

\[
V(r)=\sum_{i=1}^n C_i e^{-rt_i}.
\]

The first three rate derivatives are

\[
V'(r)=-\sum_i t_iC_ie^{-rt_i},
\]

\[
V''(r)=\sum_i t_i^2C_ie^{-rt_i},
\]

\[
V'''(r)=-\sum_i t_i^3C_ie^{-rt_i}.
\]

The normalized risk measures are

\[
D(r)=-\frac{V'(r)}{V(r)},\qquad K(r)=\frac{V''(r)}{V(r)}.
\]

For positive cash flows, \(D\ge0\), \(K\ge0\), and \(K\ge D^2\), because \(K-D^2\) is the present-value-weighted variance of payment time.

## 3. Shock scenarios and exact revaluation

For a parallel decimal rate shock \(h\), define

\[
\Delta V_{\mathrm{exact}}
=\sum_i C_i e^{-rt_i}\operatorname{expm1}(-t_i h).
\]

First- and second-order changes are

\[
\Delta V_1=V'h,\qquad
\Delta V_2=V'h+\frac12V''h^2.
\]

Define signed approximation errors consistently:

\[
E_1=\Delta V_{\mathrm{exact}}-\Delta V_1,\qquad
E_2=\Delta V_{\mathrm{exact}}-\Delta V_2.
\]

For positive future fixed cash flows, an upward rate shock cannot increase exact present value. A zero shock must produce zero exact and approximate changes.

## 4. Rigorous remainder bounds

Let \(r_{\min}=\min(r,r+h)\). Since all \(C_i>0\) and \(t_i\ge0\), the derivative magnitudes are bounded on the entire interval by

\[
M_2=\sum_i t_i^2C_i e^{-r_{\min}t_i},\qquad
M_3=\sum_i t_i^3C_i e^{-r_{\min}t_i}.
\]

Taylor's theorem then gives

\[
\boxed{|E_1|\le B_1=\frac12M_2h^2},\qquad
\boxed{|E_2|\le B_2=\frac16M_3|h|^3}.
\]

These bounds apply to the specified fixed-cash-flow function, not to uncertain cash flows, credit losses, curve calibration, or floating-point rounding error. A numerical bound check should therefore include a small documented rounding allowance.

## 6. Implementation architecture

The [project implementation](code/section_9_09.py) is deliberately small and auditable:

- **Input validation:** reject empty portfolios, nonpositive cash flows, negative payment times, booleans masquerading as numbers, nonfinite rates, and nonfinite shocks.
- **Baseline valuation:** compute discounted cash-flow terms and their first, second, and third derivative contributions.
- **Scenario engine:** convert basis points, calculate exact changes with \`expm1\`, and calculate Taylor approximations.
- **Bound engine:** use the lowest rate on each shock interval to bound derivative magnitudes.
- **Report builder:** return a deterministic dictionary suitable for JSON serialization, with assumptions and scenario details.
- **Acceptance tests:** compare formulas against independently constructed reference values.

| Test | Expected result |
|---|---|

| Multi-payment portfolio | \(K\ge D^2\) |

## 10. Exercises and worked solutions

**1.** Derive the first and second derivatives of \(V(r)=\sum_i C_ie^{-rt_i}\).

*Solution:* \(V'=-\sum_i t_iC_ie^{-rt_i}\); \(V''=\sum_i t_i^2C_ie^{-rt_i}\).

**2.** Derive the third derivative and explain its role.

*Solution:* \(V'''=-\sum_i t_i^3C_ie^{-rt_i}\). Its magnitude provides a second-order Taylor remainder bound.

**3.** Convert \(-75\) bp to a decimal rate shock.

*Solution:* \(-75/10000=-0.0075\).

**4.** For one cash flow at \(t=3\), calculate duration and convexity.

*Solution:* \(D=3\), \(K=9\).

**5.** Explain why \(K\ge D^2\) for positive fixed cash flows.

*Solution:* With present-value weights \(w_i>0\), \(K-D^2=\sum_i w_i(t_i-D)^2\ge0\).

**6.** Write the exact change formula for a 25 bp parallel rise.

*Solution:* \(\sum_i C_ie^{-rt_i}\operatorname{expm1}(-0.0025t_i)\).

**7.** Write the quadratic approximation for a \(-50\) bp shock.

*Solution:* \(\Delta V_2=V'(-0.005)+\tfrac12V''(0.005)^2\).

**8.** Derive the maximum second derivative magnitude over a shock interval.

*Solution:* \(M_2=\sum_i t_i^2C_ie^{-r_{\min}t_i}\), with \(r_{\min}=\min(r,r+h)\).

**9.** Why must a remainder bound cover the entire interval?

*Solution:* The unknown Taylor remainder evaluation point lies somewhere between the baseline and shocked rate, not necessarily at either endpoint.

**10.** Why is a zero-shock scenario mandatory?

*Solution:* It checks rate conversion, scenario arithmetic, and the expected identity that no shock produces no change.

**11.** Why is an observed small approximation error not proof of model accuracy?

*Solution:* It only compares an approximation with the chosen mathematical model; the model's cash-flow and discount assumptions can still be wrong.

**12.** What evidence is required before declaring the project independently certified?

*Solution:* Recorded runtime test evidence, mathematical/financial review, and an explicit approval decision under a defined acceptance process.
