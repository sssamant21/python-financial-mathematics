## Learning objectives

## 1. Compounding conventions

Let \(P\ge0\) be an initial amount, \(j\) a nominal annual rate, \(m\) the number of compounding periods per year, and \(t\ge0\) the number of years. For **integer** \(m\ge1\) and an **integer number of completed compounding periods** \(n=mt\ge0\),

\[
\boxed{FV=P\left(1+\frac jm\right)^n.}
\]

The periodic rate is \(i_p=j/m\). Require \(1+j/m>0\) for a strictly positive periodic factor. The corresponding present value is

\[
\boxed{PV=\frac{FV}{(1+j/m)^n}.}
\]

For fractional \(mt\), a fractional-power extension is mathematically possible, but it is not identical to a contract that posts interest only on completed dates. This chapter's periodic implementation requires an integer period count and documents that convention.

## 2. Effective annual rate (EAR)

An investment of one currency unit over one year becomes

\[
\left(1+\frac jm\right)^m.
\]

Therefore the effective annual rate is

\[
\boxed{EAR=\left(1+\frac jm\right)^m-1.}
\]

For a nominal annual rate of 12%, compounded monthly, \(m=12\) and \(i_p=0.01\):

\[
EAR=(1.01)^{12}-1\approx0.12682503.
\]

The effective annual rate is approximately 12.6825%, not 12%. The nominal rate specifies the quoted rate and compounding frequency; EAR describes the one-year accumulation factor.

## 3. Continuous compounding

Let the compounding frequency grow without bound while the nominal rate stays fixed:

\[
\lim_{m\to\infty}\left(1+\frac rm\right)^{mt}=e^{rt}.
\]

The continuously compounded future and present values are

\[
\boxed{FV=Pe^{rt}},\qquad
\boxed{PV=FVe^{-rt}}.
\]

For \(P=1000\), \(r=0.12\), \(t=1\),

\[
FV=1000e^{0.12}\approx1127.49685.
\]

The continuous EAR is

\[
\boxed{EAR_c=e^r-1.}
\]

A continuously compounded rate is a convention; it does not imply actual instantaneous bank postings.

## 4. Rate conversions

To convert an effective annual rate \(i>-1\) into an equivalent nominal annual rate compounded \(m\) times per year:

\[
\boxed{j=m\left((1+i)^{1/m}-1\right).}
\]

The equivalent continuous annual rate is

\[
\boxed{r_c=\ln(1+i).}
\]

Conversely,

\[
\boxed{i=e^{r_c}-1.}
\]

For a 10% EAR, \(r_c=\ln(1.1)\approx0.09531018\), or approximately 9.531% continuously compounded. These rates are **equivalent over one year**, not numerically identical.

For a nominal annual rate \(j\) compounded \(m\) times, the equivalent continuous rate is

\[
r_c=m\ln(1+j/m).
\]

These conversions require matching time units and positive accumulation factors.

## 5. Comparing frequencies

For a positive nominal annual rate of 12%, the following one-year results start from \(P=1000\):

| Compounding convention | Accumulation factor | Approximate future value |
|---|---|---:|
| Annual | \(1.12\) | 1120.0000 |
| Semiannual | \((1.06)^2\) | 1123.6000 |
| Quarterly | \((1.03)^4\) | 1125.5088 |
| Monthly | \((1.01)^{12}\) | 1126.8250 |
| Continuous | \(e^{0.12}\) | 1127.4969 |

For fixed positive nominal rate, higher frequency increases the accumulation factor toward the continuous limit. For negative rates the interpretation needs care; do not apply positive-rate comparisons indiscriminately.

## 6. Worked present-value comparison

A payment of 1,210 currency units is due after two years. At 10% **effective annual** interest,

\[
PV=\frac{1210}{(1.1)^2}=1000.
\]

At a **continuously compounded** annual rate \(r=\ln(1.1)\),

\[
PV=1210e^{-2\ln(1.1)}=1000.
\]

This confirms equivalent rates produce the same value when conventions and time horizons match.

## 7. Financial model and precision limits

The formulas assume fixed rates, no intermediate cash flows, no fees or taxes, and a consistent year-count convention. Real financial products may use day-count conventions, payment calendars, variable rates, rounding at each posting, and fees. These must be modeled explicitly.

The Python module uses finite floating-point inputs and \`math.log1p\`/\`math.expm1\` to reduce avoidable cancellation for small rates. It is a teaching implementation, **not** a settlement engine or an arbitrary-precision decimal calculator. Inputs such as NaN, infinity, booleans, invalid period counts, and nonpositive compounding factors are rejected.

## 9. Exercises

**1.** Derive EAR for a nominal annual rate compounded quarterly.

**2.** Compute the accumulation factor for 8% nominal annual interest compounded semiannually over three years.

**3.** Compare one-year future values for 1000 at 10% nominal compounded annually and monthly.

**4.** Convert a 5% effective annual rate to an equivalent continuously compounded annual rate.

**5.** Show that 10% EAR and continuous rate \(\ln1.1\) produce the same two-year accumulation factor.

**6.** Why is \(mt\) required to be an integer in the completed-period model?

**7.** Identify two real-world features omitted by the constant-rate compounding model.

**8.** Explain why the nominal annual rate alone is insufficient to determine an accumulation factor without compounding frequency.

### Worked answers

1. \(EAR=(1+j/4)^4-1\).
2. \((1+0.08/2)^{2\cdot3}=(1.04)^6\).
3. Annual: 1100. Monthly: \(1000(1+0.10/12)^{12}\), approximately 1104.7131.
4. \(r_c=\ln(1.05)\approx0.04879016\).
5. \((1.1)^2=e^{2\ln(1.1)}\).
6. The discrete formula counts actual completed interest-crediting periods. A fractional number of postings requires a separate convention.
7. Examples: variable interest rates and intermediate withdrawals; also day-count conventions, fees, taxes, or rounding.
8. The periodic rate is \(j/m\) and the number of compounding events depends on \(m\); changing \(m\) changes EAR.
