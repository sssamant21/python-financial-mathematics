## Project brief

Build a standard-library Python command-line explorer for periodic and continuous compounding. The program should compute effective annual rates, equivalent rate conventions, future and present values, and rate sensitivity. It should validate inputs, report units and conventions, and avoid unnecessary floating-point cancellation.

This project integrates Sections 4.1–4.8. It is an educational calculator, not a production settlement, tax, or investment-advice system.

## 1. Requirements and acceptance criteria

The application must:

1. Accept a principal, a nominal annual rate, an integer frequency, and a nonnegative integer number of completed periods.
2. Calculate periodic future value, present value, effective annual rate, and equivalent continuously compounded rate.
3. Accept a continuous annual rate and a nonnegative real-valued horizon in years.
4. Calculate continuous future value, present value, and equivalent effective annual rate.
5. Convert an EAR into an equivalent nominal annual rate at a specified frequency.
6. Calculate an approximate future-value sensitivity to a rate change.
7. Reject nonfinite numbers, invalid frequencies, negative horizons, negative principal amounts, and nonpositive periodic factors.
8. Provide reproducible reference checks with positive, zero, and negative rates, round-trip conversions, and invalid-input cases.

## 2. Mathematical model

For periodic compounding, with nominal annual rate \(j\), frequency \(m\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), and completed periods \(n\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}\):

\[
A_p=\left(1+\frac jm\right)^n,\qquad
FV_p=P A_p,\qquad PV_p=\frac{FV_p}{A_p}.
\]

Require \(1+j/m>0\). A completed-period model does not silently interpolate between interest-crediting dates.

The effective annual rate is

\[
EAR_p=\left(1+\frac jm\right)^m-1.
\]

The equivalent continuously compounded annual rate is

\[
r_c=m\ln(1+j/m).
\]

For continuous compounding at annual rate \(r\) over \(t\ge0\) years:

\[
A_c=e^{rt},\qquad FV_c=P A_c,\qquad PV_c=FV_c e^{-rt}.
\]

The corresponding EAR is \(e^r-1\). Given EAR \(i>-1\), the equivalent nominal annual rate at frequency \(m\) is

\[
j=m\left((1+i)^{1/m}-1\right).
\]

Use \`log1p\` and \`expm1\` to evaluate small-rate expressions.

## 3. Worked scenario

Assume a starting balance of 10,000 currency units and a nominal annual rate of 6%, compounded monthly, for 36 completed months.

\[
FV=10000(1+0.06/12)^{36}
=10000(1.005)^{36}.
\]

The corresponding EAR is

\[
(1.005)^{12}-1,
\]

and the equivalent continuous annual rate is

\[
12\ln(1.005).
\]

For a fair comparison, use the same three-year horizon in the continuous model. A continuously compounded rate converted from the monthly nominal rate should reproduce the same three-year future value, up to floating-point rounding.

## 4. Command-line interface

The [Python project](code/section_4_09.py) exposes three subcommands.

### Periodic accumulation

\`\`\`bash
python section_4_09.py periodic --principal 10000 --nominal-rate 0.06 --frequency 12 --periods 36
\`\`\`

The program reports the future value, recovered present value, EAR, and equivalent continuous annual rate.

### Continuous accumulation

\`\`\`bash
python section_4_09.py continuous --principal 10000 --continuous-rate 0.06 --years 3
\`\`\`

The program reports the future value, recovered present value, EAR, and local sensitivity to a 10-basis-point rate change.

### Rate conversion

\`\`\`bash
python section_4_09.py convert --ear 0.05 --frequency 12
\`\`\`

The program reports the equivalent nominal annual rate and continuous annual rate.

Rates are decimal fractions: 0.06 means 6%, not 0.06%. All amounts are in the same unspecified currency unit. No cash flows, fees, taxes, or period-by-period monetary rounding are modeled.

## 5. Implementation design

The program separates validated mathematical functions from CLI argument parsing and JSON output. It computes periodic accumulation through

\[
\ln A_p=n\operatorname{log1p}(j/m),
\]

then exponentiates when the result is representable. It uses \`math.expm1\` for EAR and conversion calculations, and catches domain and overflow errors with user-readable messages. JSON output is machine-readable for later integration into automated lab checks.

The implementation deliberately does not claim that an approximate floating-point answer is a contractual settlement amount.

## 6. Sensitivity experiment

For \(FV=Pe^{rt}\),

\[
\frac{\partial FV}{\partial r}=tFV.
\]

The relative first-order change for a small annual rate perturbation \(\Delta r\) is approximately \(t\Delta r\).

For the worked three-year horizon and \(\Delta r=0.001\), the predicted relative future-value change is approximately 0.003, or 0.3%. Compare this approximation with the exact ratio \(e^{t\Delta r}-1\). The distinction illustrates how first-order sensitivity is useful but approximate.

## 9. Review questions and answers

**Q1.** Why must the periodic factor be positive?

**A1.** The real logarithm used in stable accumulation requires a positive argument, and the positive-valued compounding model assumes a positive growth factor.

**Q2.** Are a nominal annual rate and an effective annual rate interchangeable?

**A2.** No. A nominal rate requires a compounding frequency. The EAR measures the actual one-year accumulation factor.

**Q3.** Why is the number of completed periods an integer?

**A3.** The project models discrete interest postings. Fractional-period accrual requires a separately stated convention.

**Q4.** What does a round-trip test demonstrate?

**A4.** It verifies that two implemented conversions are mutually consistent within a chosen numerical tolerance; it does not independently prove that the underlying financial model is suitable.

**Q5.** Can higher numerical precision fix missing fees or cash flows?

**A5.** No. Those are model omissions, not rounding errors.

**Q6.** What is the expected relative sensitivity of a 30-year continuous accumulation to a 10-basis-point annual rate change?

**A6.** Approximately \(30\times0.001=0.03\), or 3%, to first order.
