## Project mission

Build a transparent, label-safe financial exposure matching solver. Given instrument sensitivities \(A\), target factor exposures \(b\), and factor-specific tolerances, calculate instrument quantities \(x\), verify \(Ax\), assess numerical conditioning, and produce an auditable accept/reject decision. This is an **educational** project, not an executable trading recommendation or production risk engine.

## Learning outcomes

Integrate Chapters 12.1–12.8: matrix dimensions, axis labels, multiplication, elimination, rank, inverse/determinant, condition numbers, and residual-based financial acceptance.

## 1. Business scenario and data contract

A treasury risk desk wants to match RATE and CREDIT standardized factor exposures using two hypothetical instruments.

| Factor | BOND_A | BOND_B | Target | Tolerance |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| RATE | 2 | 1 | 5 | 0.15 |
| CREDIT | 1 | -1 | 1 | 0.10 |

\[
A=\begin{bmatrix}2&1\\1&-1\end{bmatrix},\qquad
b=\begin{bmatrix}5\\1\end{bmatrix}.
\]

Rows identify factor equations, columns identify instruments, and all values use explicitly declared standardized teaching units. The solver must **never infer alignment from positions alone**: factor and instrument names are part of the contract.

## 2. Functional requirements

1. Validate finite real numeric values (reject booleans), rectangular matrix shapes, unique labels, compatible units, and nonnegative factor tolerances.
2. Align targets and tolerances by factor label, preserving the coefficient matrix's factor order.
3. Solve a square, full-rank system using Gaussian elimination with partial pivoting; fail closed for singular or numerically unresolved pivots.
4. Estimate the infinity-norm condition number using a teaching-scale inverse-column solve, and expose warnings for fragile systems.
5. Calculate achieved exposures, signed residuals \(r=b-Ax\), infinity-norm residual, and normwise backward-error indicator.
6. Evaluate factor-specific tolerance limits and an optional condition-number guardrail.
7. Produce structured JSON with decision, metrics, factor results, and explicit draft/certification status.

**Non-goals:** optimization under trading constraints, least squares for inconsistent systems, production calibration, currency conversion, SVD-based rank certification, and live execution.

## 3. Mathematical method

For \(n\) factors and \(n\) instruments,

\[
Ax=b,\qquad r=b-Ax.
\]

Gaussian elimination transforms \(A\) into an upper-triangular matrix; back-substitution obtains \(x\). The implementation uses partial pivoting. It rejects pivots below a documented tolerance relative to the largest coefficient magnitude.

For nonsingular \(A\), compute a teaching-scale condition estimate

\[
\kappa_\infty(A)=\|A\|_\infty\|A^{-1}\|_\infty.
\]

The inverse norm is estimated by solving \(Ay_j=e_j\) for each canonical basis vector \(e_j\), **without explicitly using the inverse to solve the financial target**. This is intentionally costly and suitable only for small lab systems; production code should use established factorization and condition-estimation routines.

Define the normwise backward-error indicator

\[
\eta_\infty=
\frac{\|b-Ax\|_\infty}
{\|A\|_\infty\|x\|_\infty+\|b\|_\infty}.
\]

A small residual does not guarantee a stable or economically acceptable allocation.

## 4. Reference solution

The determinant is \(-3\), so the reference matrix is invertible. The unique solution is

\[
\boxed{x=(2,1)^\mathsf T}.
\]

Achieved exposures are RATE \(=5\), CREDIT \(=1\). Both residuals are zero; both factor tolerances pass. The infinity-norm condition number is \(3\). With a condition guardrail of 1,000, the reference case is accepted.

## 5. Required negative cases

**Redundant instruments:** \(A=\begin{bmatrix}1&2\\2&4\end{bmatrix}\). The matrix is singular and must be rejected before any allocation is issued.

**Near-duplicate instruments:** \(A=\begin{bmatrix}1&1\\1&1.0001\end{bmatrix}\). This is invertible but has \(\kappa_\infty\approx40004.0001\), exceeding the illustrative 1,000 guardrail. The system should report a **condition_guardrail_exceeded** rejection, not claim that the system is singular.

**Label permutation:** A target vector arriving CREDIT/RATE must produce the same result as RATE/CREDIT.

**Input integrity:** Duplicate labels, missing factor targets, ragged rows, nonfinite values, negative tolerances, and invalid unit contracts must fail closed.

## 6. Architecture

\`\`\`text
Labeled sensitivities + targets + tolerances
                 |
                 v
         Validate and align
                 |
                 v
      Pivoted linear-system solve
                 |
                 +----> Condition estimate
                 |
                 v
      Achieved exposure + residual
                 |
                 v
    Factor limits + condition guardrail
                 |
                 v
          JSON audit report
\`\`\`

The solver separates **input errors** from **numerical failure** and from **business-rule rejection**. A business rejection is a valid evaluation result, not an exception.

## 8. Operational runbook

**Before execution:** Verify source data timestamp, factor definitions, sensitivity units, instrument universe, and risk policy version. Capture a stable snapshot; never silently mix inputs from different valuation dates.

**During execution:** Validate all labels and values. Record pivot tolerance, estimated condition number, residuals, gross and net positions, and factor-specific pass/fail decisions.

**On rejection:** Do not emit an approved allocation. Preserve the reason: invalid data, unresolved singularity, condition guardrail, or factor tolerance failure. Escalate near-dependent hedges to risk-model owners rather than automatically relaxing controls.

**After execution:** Store an immutable, appropriately access-controlled audit artifact and independently reconcile achieved exposures. Avoid logging secrets or sensitive account data.

## 10. Twenty applied questions and answers

**1.** What is the shape of the reference coefficient matrix?  
*Answer:* \(2\times2\).

**2.** What do its rows represent?  
*Answer:* RATE and CREDIT factor equations.

**3.** What do its columns represent?  
*Answer:* BOND_A and BOND_B quantities.

**4.** What is the reference determinant?  
*Answer:* \(-3\).

**5.** Is the reference matrix invertible?  
*Answer:* Yes.

**6.** What is the reference allocation?  
*Answer:* \((2,1)\).

**7.** What RATE exposure does it achieve?  
*Answer:* 5.

**8.** What CREDIT exposure does it achieve?  
*Answer:* 1.

**9.** What is the signed residual convention?  
*Answer:* Target minus achieved exposure.

**10.** What is the reference residual?  
*Answer:* \((0,0)\).

**11.** What is \(\kappa_\infty(A)\)?  
*Answer:* 3.

**12.** Why align factors by label?  
*Answer:* To prevent silent target-to-equation misassignment.

**13.** Why use partial pivoting?  
*Answer:* To reduce failure and roundoff amplification from poor pivots.

**14.** Why reject a singular matrix?  
*Answer:* A unique exact allocation cannot be determined from the equations.

**15.** What is the near-duplicate matrix's approximate condition number?  
*Answer:* 40004.0001.

**16.** Does a near-duplicate matrix necessarily have determinant zero?  
*Answer:* No.

**17.** What is the purpose of a condition guardrail?  
*Answer:* To flag fragile solutions whose allocations are sensitive to input changes.

**18.** Is a small residual sufficient for trading approval?  
*Answer:* No; economic constraints and input quality also matter.

**19.** What does \`runtime_certified=false\` mean?  
*Answer:* The lab has not been accepted as runtime-certified.

**20.** What is the next step after publishing the draft?  
*Answer:* Complete manuscript coverage, then run independent numerical, integration, and editorial validation.
