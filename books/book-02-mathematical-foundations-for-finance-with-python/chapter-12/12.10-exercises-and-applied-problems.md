## Reference financial model

Throughout, use labeled factor sensitivities

\[
A=\begin{bmatrix}2&1\\1&-1\end{bmatrix},\qquad
b=\begin{bmatrix}5\\1\end{bmatrix},
\]

with rows RATE, CREDIT and columns BOND_A, BOND_B. Units are illustrative standardized teaching coordinates, not dollars or a trading recommendation.

## Part A — Matrix fundamentals

**1. Dimensions.** State the dimensions of \(A\), \(b\), and \(x\).  
*Answer:* \(A\) is \(2\times2\); \(b\) and \(x\) are \(2\times1\).

**2. Matrix-vector multiplication.** Evaluate \(A(2,1)^\mathsf T\).  
*Answer:* \((5,1)^\mathsf T\).

**3. Transpose.** Calculate \(A^\mathsf T\).  
*Answer:* \(A^\mathsf T=A\), because the matrix is symmetric.

**4. Identity.** Verify \(AI_2=A\).  
*Answer:* Multiplication by \(I_2\) preserves each column.

**5. Diagonal scaling.** Compute \(\operatorname{diag}(2,3)(4,5)^\mathsf T\).  
*Answer:* \((8,15)^\mathsf T\).

**6. Noncommutativity.** For \(B=\begin{bmatrix}1&2\\0&1\end{bmatrix}\), compare \(AB\) and \(BA\).  
*Answer:* \(AB=\begin{bmatrix}2&5\\1&1\end{bmatrix}\), whereas \(BA=\begin{bmatrix}4&-1\\1&-1\end{bmatrix}\). They differ.

## Part B — Linear systems and rank

**7. Augmented matrix.** Write \([A\mid b]\).  
*Answer:* \(\begin{bmatrix}2&1&|&5\\1&-1&|&1\end{bmatrix}\).

**8. Elimination.** Apply \(R_2\leftarrow R_2-\frac12R_1\).  
*Answer:* Row 2 becomes \((0,-1.5\mid-1.5)\).

**9. Back-substitution.** Solve the transformed system.  
*Answer:* BOND_B \(=1\), then BOND_A \(=2\).

**10. Zero pivot.** Solve \(y=1,\ 2x+3y=5\).  
*Answer:* Swap rows before elimination; \(x=1,y=1\).

**11. Redundant equations.** Classify \(x+2y=3,\ 2x+4y=6\).  
*Answer:* Infinitely many solutions: \((x,y)=(3-2t,t)\).

**12. Inconsistent equations.** Classify \(x+2y=3,\ 2x+4y=7\).  
*Answer:* No exact solution.

**13. Rank criterion.** State the consistency criterion for \(Ax=b\).  
*Answer:* \(\operatorname{rank}(A)=\operatorname{rank}([A\mid b])\).

**14. Rectangular system.** Classify \(x+y=4\).  
*Answer:* One equation in two unknowns: infinitely many solutions.

## Part C — Inverse, determinant, and conditioning

**15. Determinant.** Calculate \(\det(A)\).  
*Answer:* \(2(-1)-1(1)=-3\).

**16. Inverse.** Calculate \(A^{-1}\).  
*Answer:* \(\begin{bmatrix}1/3&1/3\\1/3&-2/3\end{bmatrix}\).

**17. Invertibility.** Explain why the reference system has a unique solution.  
*Answer:* \(\det(A)\ne0\), equivalently \(\operatorname{rank}(A)=2\).

**18. Matrix infinity norm.** Find \(\|A\|_\infty\).  
*Answer:* 3.

**19. Inverse infinity norm.** Find \(\|A^{-1}\|_\infty\).  
*Answer:* 1.

**20. Condition number.** Find \(\kappa_\infty(A)\).  
*Answer:* 3.

**21. Near-duplicate hedges.** For \(C=\begin{bmatrix}1&1\\1&1.0001\end{bmatrix}\), find \(\det(C)\).  
*Answer:* \(0.0001\).

**22. Fragile allocation.** For the same \(C\), calculate \(\kappa_\infty(C)\).  
*Answer:* \(40004.0001\), approximately.

**23. Target perturbation.** For \(C(1,1)^\mathsf T=(2,2.0001)^\mathsf T\), increase the second target by 0.001.  
*Answer:* The allocation changes by \((-10,10)\), becoming \((-9,11)\).

**24. Conditioning versus stability.** Explain the distinction.  
*Answer:* Conditioning describes sensitivity of the mathematical problem; stability describes error behavior of the numerical algorithm.

## Part D — Financial residuals and governance

**25. Imperfect allocation.** Evaluate \(A(1.9,1.1)^\mathsf T\).  
*Answer:* \((4.9,0.8)^\mathsf T\).

**26. Signed residual.** Calculate \(b-A(1.9,1.1)^\mathsf T\).  
*Answer:* \((0.1,0.2)^\mathsf T\), meaning both factor exposures are under target.

**27. Absolute residual norm.** Find \(\|r\|_\infty\).  
*Answer:* 0.2.

**28. Target-relative residual.** Find \(\|r\|_\infty/\|b\|_\infty\).  
*Answer:* \(0.2/5=0.04\).

**29. Backward-error indicator.** Find \(\|r\|_\infty/(\|A\|_\infty\|\hat x\|_\infty+\|b\|_\infty)\).  
*Answer:* \(0.2/(3\cdot1.9+5)=0.2/10.7\approx0.0186916\).

**30. Factor-specific acceptance.** RATE tolerance is 0.15; CREDIT tolerance is 0.10. Accept the imperfect allocation?  
*Answer:* No. RATE passes but CREDIT fails.

**31. Zero targets.** Why can factor-specific percentage residuals be misleading?  
*Answer:* Division by zero is undefined, and near-zero targets make ratios unstable. Use meaningful absolute tolerances or scales.

**32. Units.** Can standardized RATE and CREDIT residuals be summed and called dollar risk?  
*Answer:* No. A validated conversion and consistent units are required.

**33. Economic acceptability.** Can a zero-residual solution violate business constraints?  
*Answer:* Yes; liquidity, gross exposure, leverage, concentration, or trading limits can still fail.

**34. Auditability.** Name four required audit fields.  
*Answer:* For example, factor labels, instrument labels, target and achieved exposures, tolerance decisions, model version, and market-data timestamp.

## Part E — Applied challenges

**35. Permutation invariance.** If the target arrives as CREDIT=1, RATE=5, what should the solver return?  
*Answer:* BOND_A=2, BOND_B=1 after label alignment.

**36. Singular sensitivity matrix.** What should the solver do for \(S=\begin{bmatrix}1&2\\2&4\end{bmatrix}\)?  
*Answer:* Reject a unique-allocation request; do not fabricate an inverse.

**37. Condition guardrail.** Should a system with condition number approximately 40004 pass an illustrative maximum of 1000?  
*Answer:* No; report a condition-guardrail rejection even if residuals are tiny.

**38. Production solver choice.** Should a large financial system explicitly compute \(A^{-1}\) to solve \(Ax=b\)?  
*Answer:* Generally no; use a validated direct solver and condition diagnostics.

**39. Scenario change.** If \(b\) changes by \(\delta b=(0,0.03)\) in the reference system, find \(\delta x\).  
*Answer:* \((0.01,-0.02)\), giving new positions \((2.01,0.98)\).

**40. Reliability acceptance.** List three reasons to withhold enterprise certification.  
*Answer:* Missing executed tests, missing independent model/security review, or unvalidated business units and risk limits.
