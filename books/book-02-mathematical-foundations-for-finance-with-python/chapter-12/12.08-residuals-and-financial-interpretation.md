## Learning objectives

Calculate signed, absolute, relative, and normalized residuals; distinguish backward error, forward error, and economic tracking error; define unit-aware hedge acceptance criteria; and document the operational limits of an apparently accurate linear-system solution.

## 1. Residuals in a linear system

For a coefficient matrix \(A\in\mathbb R^{m\times n}\), positions \(x\in\mathbb R^n\), and target factor exposures \(b\in\mathbb R^m\), define the **target-minus-actual residual**

\[
\boxed{r=b-Ax}.
\]

The sign convention must be explicit: a positive residual means the portfolio is **underexposed** relative to its target on that factor; a negative residual means it is **overexposed**. Some software uses \(Ax-b\) instead, so never compare residual signs without checking conventions.

For

\[
A=\begin{bmatrix}2&1\\1&-1\end{bmatrix},\quad
b=\begin{bmatrix}5\\1\end{bmatrix},\quad
x=\begin{bmatrix}2\\1\end{bmatrix},
\]

we obtain \(Ax=(5,1)^\mathsf T\) and \(r=(0,0)^\mathsf T\).

## 2. Imperfect positions and unmatched exposures

Suppose execution constraints produce positions \(\hat x=(1.9,1.1)^\mathsf T\). Then

\[
A\hat x=\begin{bmatrix}4.9\\0.8\end{bmatrix},
\qquad
r=\begin{bmatrix}0.1\\0.2\end{bmatrix}.
\]

RATE is under target by 0.1 standardized units and CREDIT by 0.2 standardized units. The residual is not necessarily a monetary loss; translating it into dollar risk requires factor units, stress magnitudes, and a validated valuation mapping.

## 3. Absolute and relative residuals

The infinity norm reports the largest absolute mismatch:

\[
\|r\|_\infty=\max_i|r_i|=0.2.
\]

A target-relative metric is

\[
\frac{\|r\|_\infty}{\|b\|_\infty}=\frac{0.2}{5}=0.04.
\]

This is 4% of the **largest target component**, not 4% of each factor's target. A factor-specific ratio \(|r_i|/|b_i|\) is undefined when \(b_i=0\) and unstable for targets near zero. Use explicit absolute tolerances or meaningful factor scales for such cases.

A scale-aware normwise backward-error indicator is

\[
\boxed{\eta_\infty=
\frac{\|b-A\hat x\|_\infty}
{\|A\|_\infty\|\hat x\|_\infty+\|b\|_\infty}}.
\]

For this example, \(\|A\|_\infty=3\), \(\|\hat x\|_\infty=1.9\), and \(\|b\|_\infty=5\), giving \(\eta_\infty=0.2/10.7\approx0.0186916\). If the denominator is zero, define the indicator as zero only when the residual is also zero; otherwise treat it as undefined.

## 4. Forward error versus backward error

A residual measures how closely a candidate allocation satisfies the stated equations. **Forward error** measures how far it is from the exact solution. For the example, the exact solution is \((2,1)\), so \(\hat x-x=(-0.1,0.1)\).

In a poorly conditioned system, a tiny residual may coexist with a large position error. Section 12.7 showed that condition numbers connect residual-based error estimates to possible forward errors. Do not certify a hedge solely because its algebraic residual is small.

## 5. Financial interpretation and units

A production factor-exposure table should record: factor identifier, target, achieved exposure, signed residual, absolute residual, unit, permitted tolerance, market-data timestamp, and model version. RATE and CREDIT may have different units (for example, DV01 versus spread sensitivity); summing raw residual magnitudes across incompatible units is meaningless.

For the worked example, all exposures are deliberately **standardized dimensionless teaching coordinates**. A real portfolio requires separate limits per factor or a justified common normalization.

## 6. Operational acceptance criteria

A useful acceptance policy checks **all** of the following:

1. Each factor's absolute residual is below its approved unit-specific tolerance.
2. Gross and net positions, liquidity, concentration, and funding requirements are within limits.
3. The solver reports no nonfinite inputs, missing labels, or numerical instability warnings.
4. Market data and instrument sensitivities are fresh and versioned.
5. Material condition-number warnings and stress-test outcomes are reviewed.

A mathematically exact hedge may still violate trading limits, liquidity constraints, or risk appetite. Likewise, a constrained hedge may intentionally retain nonzero residuals; those residuals should be reported, not hidden.

## 7. Example acceptance decision

Set tolerances RATE \(=0.15\) and CREDIT \(=0.10\) standardized units. For \(\hat x=(1.9,1.1)\), RATE residual \(0.1\) passes, but CREDIT residual \(0.2\) fails. The overall result is **REJECT**, even though the normwise relative residual is only about 1.87%.

This illustrates why aggregate numerical metrics cannot replace per-factor business limits.

## 8. Observability and audit evidence

Capture an immutable evaluation record containing the ordered factor and instrument labels, target and achieved exposures, residuals, tolerance decisions, units, solver version, model version, market-data timestamp, and approval state. Retain enough detail to reproduce the decision without logging secrets or sensitive client data.

For SRE/DBRE operations, monitor rejected calculations, missing factors, nonfinite values, condition warnings, stale snapshots, and sudden changes in gross exposure. Distinguish a **solver incident** from a legitimate **business-constraint rejection**.

## 10. Exercises with worked answers

**1.** Define the target-minus-actual residual.  
*Answer:* \(r=b-Ax\).

**2.** What does a positive residual mean under this convention?  
*Answer:* Achieved exposure is below target.

**3.** Find \(Ax\) for \(x=(2,1)\) in the reference system.  
*Answer:* \((5,1)\).

**4.** Find its residual.  
*Answer:* \((0,0)\).

**5.** Find \(A(1.9,1.1)^\mathsf T\).  
*Answer:* \((4.9,0.8)\).

**6.** Find the imperfect allocation's residual.  
*Answer:* \((0.1,0.2)\).

**7.** Find the infinity norm of that residual.  
*Answer:* 0.2.

**8.** Find \(\|b\|_\infty\).  
*Answer:* 5.

**9.** Find the target-relative infinity residual.  
*Answer:* 0.04.

**10.** Find \(\|A\|_\infty\).  
*Answer:* 3.

**11.** Find \(\|\hat x\|_\infty\).  
*Answer:* 1.9.

**12.** Calculate the normwise backward-error indicator.  
*Answer:* \(0.2/10.7\approx0.0186916\).

**13.** What is the forward position error?  
*Answer:* \((-0.1,0.1)\).

**14.** Why is a small residual insufficient to guarantee a small forward error?  
*Answer:* The coefficient matrix may be ill-conditioned.

**15.** Does a zero target permit a factor-specific percentage residual?  
*Answer:* No; the denominator is zero.

**16.** With RATE tolerance 0.15, does RATE pass?  
*Answer:* Yes; \(0.1\le0.15\).

**17.** With CREDIT tolerance 0.10, does CREDIT pass?  
*Answer:* No; \(0.2>0.10\).

**18.** What is the overall acceptance decision?  
*Answer:* Reject.

**19.** Can standardized RATE and CREDIT residuals be assumed to equal dollars?  
*Answer:* No; monetary conversion requires validated economic units and mappings.

**20.** Name two operational checks beyond residual magnitude.  
*Answer:* For example, gross exposure limits and market-data freshness.
