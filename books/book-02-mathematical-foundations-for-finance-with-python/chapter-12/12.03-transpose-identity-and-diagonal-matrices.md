## Learning objectives

Define and compute matrix transposes; distinguish identity and diagonal matrices; derive basic transpose identities; and use labeled matrices to scale asset exposures and aggregate portfolio factor sensitivities without losing axis meaning.

## 1. Transpose

For \(A\in\mathbb R^{m\times n}\), the transpose \(A^\mathsf T\in\mathbb R^{n\times m}\) is defined by \((A^\mathsf T)_{ij}=A_{ji}\). Rows become columns and columns become rows.

For

\[
A=\begin{bmatrix}1&2&3\\4&5&6\end{bmatrix},
\qquad
A^\mathsf T=\begin{bmatrix}1&4\\2&5\\3&6\end{bmatrix}.
\]

The shape changes from \((2,3)\) to \((3,2)\). A transpose does **not** reorder values arbitrarily: it swaps the roles of two axes.

## 2. Identity matrices

The \(n\times n\) identity matrix \(I_n\) has ones on its main diagonal and zeros elsewhere. For compatible matrices \(A\),

\[
I_mA=A,\qquad AI_n=A
\]

when \(A\) has shape \((m,n)\). In a labeled financial system, an identity transformation must map each input coordinate to the **same named** output coordinate. A numeric identity applied to a shuffled asset axis is not economically an identity until labels are aligned.

## 3. Diagonal matrices

A diagonal matrix \(D=\operatorname{diag}(d_1,\ldots,d_n)\) has zero off-diagonal entries. Left multiplication \(DA\) scales the rows of \(A\); right multiplication \(AD\) scales the columns of \(A\), provided the corresponding axes are compatible.

For

\[
D=\begin{bmatrix}2&0\\0&3\end{bmatrix},
\quad
B=\begin{bmatrix}1&4\\2&5\end{bmatrix},
\]

\[
DB=\begin{bmatrix}2&8\\6&15\end{bmatrix},
\qquad
BD=\begin{bmatrix}2&12\\4&15\end{bmatrix}.
\]

The results differ because scaling rows and scaling columns are distinct transformations.

## 4. Transpose identities

For compatible matrices and scalar \(c\),

\[
(A^\mathsf T)^\mathsf T=A,\qquad
(A+B)^\mathsf T=A^\mathsf T+B^\mathsf T,\qquad
(cA)^\mathsf T=cA^\mathsf T.
\]

Crucially, product order reverses:

\[
\boxed{(AB)^\mathsf T=B^\mathsf T A^\mathsf T}.
\]

For a real matrix \(A\), \(A^\mathsf T A\) is symmetric and positive semidefinite because \(x^\mathsf T A^\mathsf T A x=\|Ax\|_2^2\ge0\). This fact becomes important in least-squares and portfolio risk calculations.

## 5. Financial application: position scaling

Let asset-by-factor standardized sensitivities be

\[
S=\begin{bmatrix}
2&1\\
-1&3\\
0&4
\end{bmatrix}
\]

with rows ALPHA/BETA/GAMMA and columns RATE/CREDIT. Let

\[
D=\operatorname{diag}(0.5,0.3,0.2).
\]

Then

\[
DS=\begin{bmatrix}
1&0.5\\
-0.3&0.9\\
0&0.8
\end{bmatrix}.
\]

Each row is scaled by its asset weight. Summing rows yields the aggregate factor vector \((0.7,2.2)\). This is equivalent to \(w^\mathsf T S\), but the diagonal form preserves the asset-level contributions for auditing.

The example uses dimensionless weights and standardized sensitivity coordinates. Real-world DV01, delta, and spread sensitivities require their own unit conventions.

## 6. Financial application: factor scaling

For \(F=\operatorname{diag}(10,100)\), the product \(SF\) multiplies RATE coordinates by 10 and CREDIT coordinates by 100. It **does not** mean the factors have become economically comparable; the transformation must specify why each scale factor is appropriate.

## 8. Exercises with worked answers

**1.** Transpose \(\begin{bmatrix}1&2&3\\4&5&6\end{bmatrix}\).  
*Answer:* \(\begin{bmatrix}1&4\\2&5\\3&6\end{bmatrix}\).

**2.** What is the transpose shape of a \(4\times2\) matrix?  
*Answer:* \(2\times4\).

**3.** What is \((A^\mathsf T)^\mathsf T\)?  
*Answer:* \(A\).

**4.** What is \(I_3\)?  
*Answer:* A \(3\times3\) matrix with diagonal entries 1 and off-diagonal entries 0.

**5.** What is \(I_2B\) for a \(2\times2\) matrix \(B\)?  
*Answer:* \(B\).

**6.** Calculate \(\operatorname{diag}(2,3)\begin{bmatrix}1&4\\2&5\end{bmatrix}\).  
*Answer:* \(\begin{bmatrix}2&8\\6&15\end{bmatrix}\).

**7.** Calculate \(\begin{bmatrix}1&4\\2&5\end{bmatrix}\operatorname{diag}(2,3)\).  
*Answer:* \(\begin{bmatrix}2&12\\4&15\end{bmatrix}\).

**8.** Does left diagonal multiplication scale rows or columns?  
*Answer:* Rows.

**9.** Does right diagonal multiplication scale rows or columns?  
*Answer:* Columns.

**10.** State the transpose-of-product rule.  
*Answer:* \((AB)^\mathsf T=B^\mathsf TA^\mathsf T\).

**11.** Find the ALPHA row of \(DS\) in the financial example.  
*Answer:* \((1,0.5)\).

**12.** Find the BETA row of \(DS\).  
*Answer:* \((-0.3,0.9)\).

**13.** Find the total RATE exposure by summing rows of \(DS\).  
*Answer:* 0.7.

**14.** Find the total CREDIT exposure by summing rows of \(DS\).  
*Answer:* 2.2.

**15.** Is \(A^\mathsf TA\) positive semidefinite for every real \(A\)?  
*Answer:* Yes, because \(x^\mathsf TA^\mathsf TAx=\|Ax\|_2^2\ge0\).

**16.** Why must an identity matrix retain asset labels?  
*Answer:* A numerical identity is not a valid economic identity if its input and output coordinates refer to different assets.
