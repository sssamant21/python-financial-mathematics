## Learning objectives

Recognize symmetric matrices; explain the real spectral theorem; construct an orthonormal eigenbasis; reconstruct a matrix as \(Q\Lambda Q^\mathsf T\); derive its action on vectors; and interpret the decomposition of a hypothetical two-factor financial risk matrix with appropriate unit and governance safeguards.

## 1. Symmetric matrices

A real square matrix is **symmetric** if

\[
A=A^\mathsf T.
\]

Every real symmetric matrix has real eigenvalues and an orthonormal basis of eigenvectors. This remains true for repeated eigenvalues, although the basis within a repeated-eigenvalue eigenspace is not unique.

## 2. The spectral theorem

Let \(v_1,\ldots,v_n\) be orthonormal eigenvectors of a real symmetric matrix \(A\), with eigenvalues \(\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_n\). Form

\[
Q=[v_1\ \cdots\ v_n],\qquad
\Lambda=\operatorname{diag}(\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_n).
\]

Because \(Q^\mathsf TQ=QQ^\mathsf T=I\),

\[
AQ=Q\Lambda,\qquad
\boxed{A=Q\Lambda Q^\mathsf T}.
\]

Equivalently,

\[
\boxed{A=\sum_{i=1}^n\lambda_iv_iv_i^\mathsf T}.
\]

Each \(v_iv_i^\mathsf T\) is an orthogonal projector onto the direction \(v_i\).

## 3. Worked financial example

Consider a hypothetical symmetric two-factor risk matrix

\[
A=\begin{bmatrix}2&1\\1&2\end{bmatrix}.
\]

From Section 14.1, its eigenvalues are \(3\) and \(1\). Choose

\[
v_1=\frac1{\sqrt2}(1,1)^\mathsf T,\quad
v_2=\frac1{\sqrt2}(1,-1)^\mathsf T.
\]

Then

\[
Q=\frac1{\sqrt2}\begin{bmatrix}1&1\\1&-1\end{bmatrix},
\quad
\Lambda=\begin{bmatrix}3&0\\0&1\end{bmatrix}.
\]

The rank-one terms are

\[
3v_1v_1^\mathsf T=\frac32
\begin{bmatrix}1&1\\1&1\end{bmatrix},\qquad
v_2v_2^\mathsf T=\frac12
\begin{bmatrix}1&-1\\-1&1\end{bmatrix}.
\]

Adding them reconstructs \(A\) exactly. The first mathematical direction moves both factors together; the second moves them in opposite directions. These are *model directions*, not automatic trading recommendations.

## 4. Coordinates in the eigenbasis

Any vector \(x\) has spectral coordinates \(y=Q^\mathsf Tx\). Therefore

\[
Ax=Q\Lambda y
=\sum_i\lambda_i(v_i^\mathsf Tx)v_i.
\]

For \(x=(2,0)^\mathsf T\),

\[
y=(\sqrt2,\sqrt2)^\mathsf T,\qquad
Ax=(4,2)^\mathsf T.
\]

The decomposition separates the matrix action into independent orthogonal directions.

## 5. Quadratic form connection

For symmetric \(A\),

\[
x^\mathsf TAx
=(Q^\mathsf Tx)^\mathsf T\Lambda(Q^\mathsf Tx)
=\sum_i\lambda_i y_i^2.
\]

For the example \(x=(2,0)\), \(x^\mathsf TAx=8\). The eigenbasis gives \(3(\sqrt2)^2+1(\sqrt2)^2=8\). This identity leads directly to Section 14.3 on quadratic forms.

## 6. Numerical validation

A credible decomposition report verifies all of the following:

1. Symmetry: \(A\approx A^\mathsf T\).
2. Orthogonality: \(Q^\mathsf TQ\approx I\).
3. Eigenpair equations: \(Av_i\approx\lambda_iv_i\).
4. Reconstruction: \(Q\Lambda Q^\mathsf T\approx A\).
5. Trace: \(\sum_i\lambda_i\approx\operatorname{tr}(A)\).
6. Determinant: \(\prod_i\lambda_i\approx\det(A)\).

Use tolerances appropriate to input scale and conditioning. An eigenvector sign flip does not change \(v_iv_i^\mathsf T\). For repeated eigenvalues, an orthogonal rotation within the eigenspace leaves the reconstructed matrix unchanged.

## 7. Financial interpretation and limits

When \(A\) is a covariance matrix estimated from consistently defined and scaled financial factors, spectral decomposition can expose principal variance directions. But this section does not estimate covariance, establish statistical significance, or forecast returns. A generic symmetric matrix need not be a covariance matrix: negative eigenvalues are allowed for symmetric matrices but not for a mathematically valid covariance matrix.

Factor units and scaling matter. Changing a factor's units changes the matrix representation and potentially its eigenvalues and eigenvectors. Document the model's data source, observation window, definitions, normalization, and any matrix repairs before interpreting the result.

## 9. Twenty worked exercises

**1.** Define symmetry.  
*Answer:* \(A=A^\mathsf T\).

**2.** State the real spectral theorem.  
*Answer:* A real symmetric matrix has an orthonormal eigenbasis and \(A=Q\Lambda Q^\mathsf T\).

**3.** What is \(Q^\mathsf TQ\)?  
*Answer:* The identity.

**4.** What is \(Q^{-1}\) for orthogonal \(Q\)?  
*Answer:* \(Q^\mathsf T\).

**5.** What are the reference eigenvalues?  
*Answer:* \(3,1\).

**6.** What is the reference matrix?  
*Answer:* \(\begin{bmatrix}2&1\\1&2\end{bmatrix}\).

**7.** Give the leading unit eigenvector.  
*Answer:* \((1,1)/\sqrt2\).

**8.** Give the second unit eigenvector.  
*Answer:* \((1,-1)/\sqrt2\).

**9.** Write the leading rank-one term.  
*Answer:* \(\frac32\begin{bmatrix}1&1\\1&1\end{bmatrix}\).

**10.** Write the second rank-one term.  
*Answer:* \(\frac12\begin{bmatrix}1&-1\\-1&1\end{bmatrix}\).

**11.** What does their sum equal?  
*Answer:* \(A\).

**12.** Compute \(Ax\) for \(x=(2,0)\).  
*Answer:* \((4,2)\).

**13.** Compute \(Q^\mathsf Tx\).  
*Answer:* \((\sqrt2,\sqrt2)\).

**14.** Compute \(x^\mathsf TAx\).  
*Answer:* \(8\).

**15.** Write the spectral quadratic form.  
*Answer:* \(\sum_i\lambda_i y_i^2\).

**16.** Can a symmetric matrix have a negative eigenvalue?  
*Answer:* Yes.

**17.** Can a valid covariance matrix have a negative eigenvalue?  
*Answer:* No, excluding numerical roundoff.

**18.** Does flipping an eigenvector's sign change its rank-one projector?  
*Answer:* No.

**19.** Are eigenvectors uniquely determined for repeated eigenvalues?  
*Answer:* No; an eigenspace may admit many orthonormal bases.

**20.** What is the next topic?  
*Answer:* Quadratic Forms.

## 10. Applied assessment

For the reference matrix, construct \(Q\) and \(\Lambda\); reconstruct \(A\); compute \(Ax\) and \(x^\mathsf TAx\) for \(x=(2,0)\); verify trace and determinant; and explain why the decomposition's economic meaning depends on the matrix's definition and units.

**Acceptance:** eigenvalues \(3,1\), reconstruction \(A\), \(Ax=(4,2)\), quadratic value \(8\), and near-zero numerical diagnostic residuals.
