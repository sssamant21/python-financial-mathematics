## Reference datasets

**Matrix A:** \(A=\begin{bmatrix}2&1\\1&2\end{bmatrix}\).

**Matrix C (annualized covariance):** \(C=\begin{bmatrix}0.04&0.01\\0.01&0.09\end{bmatrix}\), with weights \(w=(0.6,0.4)^\mathsf T\).

**Exposure matrix E:** \(E=\begin{bmatrix}4&0\\0&2\\0&0\end{bmatrix}\), with target \(t=(8,4,5)^\mathsf T\).

These are hypothetical teaching examples, not market estimates.

## Part A — Twenty exercises with worked answers

### 1. Characteristic polynomial

Find \(\det(A-\lambda I)\).

**Answer:** \((2-\lambda)^2-1=\lambda^2-4\lambda+3\).

### 2. Eigenvalues

Find the eigenvalues of \(A\).

**Answer:** \(\lambda_1=3\), \(\lambda_2=1\).

### 3. Eigenvectors

Give normalized eigenvectors for \(A\).

**Answer:** \(v_1=(1,1)/\sqrt2\) and \(v_2=(1,-1)/\sqrt2\), respectively.

### 4. Spectral reconstruction

Reconstruct \(A\) from its eigenpairs.

**Answer:** \(A=3v_1v_1^\mathsf T+v_2v_2^\mathsf T=\begin{bmatrix}2&1\\1&2\end{bmatrix}\).

### 5. Quadratic form

Evaluate \(x^\mathsf TAx\) at \(x=(1,2)^\mathsf T\).

**Answer:** \(2(1)^2+2(1)(2)+2(2)^2=14\).

### 6. Quadratic gradient

Find the gradient of \(q(x)=x^\mathsf TAx\).

**Answer:** \(\nabla q(x)=2Ax\). At \(x=(1,2)\), this is \((8,10)^\mathsf T\).

### 7. Quadratic Hessian

Find the Hessian of \(q\).

**Answer:** \(\nabla^2q=2A=\begin{bmatrix}4&2\\2&4\end{bmatrix}\).

### 8. Positive definiteness

Prove \(A\) is SPD using Sylvester's criterion.

**Answer:** \(a_{11}=2>0\) and \(\det A=3>0\).

### 9. Covariance determinant

Compute \(\det C\).

**Answer:** \(0.04(0.09)-0.01^2=0.0035\).

### 10. Covariance definiteness

Show \(C\) is SPD.

**Answer:** \(C_{11}=0.04>0\), \(\det C=0.0035>0\), and \(C\) is symmetric.

### 11. Portfolio variance

Compute \(w^\mathsf TCw\).

**Answer:** \(0.6^2(0.04)+2(0.6)(0.4)(0.01)+0.4^2(0.09)=0.0336\).

### 12. Portfolio volatility

Compute annualized volatility.

**Answer:** \(\sqrt{0.0336}\approx0.183303\), or 18.33%, assuming annualized covariance units.

### 13. Cholesky factor

Find a lower-triangular \(L\) with \(C=LL^\mathsf T\).

**Answer:** \(L=\begin{bmatrix}0.2&0\\0.05&\sqrt{0.0875}\end{bmatrix}\).

### 14. Cholesky reconstruction

Verify the lower-right reconstructed covariance entry.

**Answer:** \(0.05^2+0.0875=0.09\).

### 15. Exposure singular values

Find the singular values of \(E\).

**Answer:** Since \(E^\mathsf TE=\operatorname{diag}(16,4)\), the singular values are \(4,2\).

### 16. Exposure rank and conditioning

Find the rank and full-column-rank spectral condition number of \(E\).

**Answer:** Rank \(2\); condition number \(4/2=2\).

### 17. Least-squares replication

Find \(\arg\min_x\|Ex-t\|_2^2\).

**Answer:** \(x=(2,2)\). The achieved exposure is \((8,4,0)\).

### 18. Residual and objective

Compute the signed target residual and half-squared residual objective.

**Answer:** \(t-Ex=(0,0,5)\); \(\tfrac12\|t-Ex\|_2^2=12.5\).

### 19. Rank-one compression

Find the best rank-one approximation of \(E\), its Frobenius error, and retained squared singular-value energy.

**Answer:** \(E_1=\begin{bmatrix}4&0\\0&0\\0&0\end{bmatrix}\); error \(2\); retained fraction \(16/(16+4)=0.8\).

### 20. Model risk interpretation

Does retaining 80% of squared singular-value energy imply preserving 80% of economic risk?

**Answer:** No. The discarded direction could represent a mandatory stress factor or materially affect a specific portfolio. Numerical reconstruction quality is not a substitute for economic model validation.

## Part B — Additional applied problems

### A. Compare covariance risk and exposure replication

Explain why \(w^\mathsf TCw\) and \(\|Ex-t\|_2^2\) cannot be directly compared.

**Solution:** They measure different quantities and may have different units. The former is a return-variance quantity under a covariance model; the latter is a squared factor-exposure mismatch. Their numerical values do not define a shared optimization objective without documented scaling and economic weights.

### B. Analyze a singular covariance matrix

Consider \(S=\begin{bmatrix}1&1\\1&1\end{bmatrix}\).

**Solution:** Its eigenvalues are \(2\) and \(0\), so it is PSD but not PD. Standard positive-pivot Cholesky cannot complete with strictly positive diagonal pivots. A suitable eigen-based factorization or documented regularization may be used when justified.

### C. Diagnose an indefinite matrix

Consider \(H=\begin{bmatrix}1&2\\2&1\end{bmatrix}\).

**Solution:** Its eigenvalues are \(3\) and \(-1\); \(H\) is indefinite. The quadratic form takes both positive and negative values. Do not attempt a standard SPD Cholesky factorization.

### D. Bound compression exposure error

For \(E\) and \(E_1\), let \(x=(2,2)\). Compute the compression exposure error and compare it with the spectral-norm bound.

**Solution:** \((E-E_1)x=(0,4,0)\), so the error norm is \(4\). The bound is \(\|E-E_1\|_2\|x\|_2=2\sqrt8\approx5.657\); the observed error respects the bound.

### E. Interpret a nearly singular exposure model

Suppose singular values are \(4\) and \(10^{-8}\).

**Solution:** The spectral condition number is \(4\times10^8\). Least-squares positions may be extremely sensitive to data perturbations. Investigate scaling, rank thresholds, uncertainty, and downstream risk limits; do not silently discard the smaller direction.
