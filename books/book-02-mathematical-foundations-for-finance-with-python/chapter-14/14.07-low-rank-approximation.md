## Learning objectives

Explain low-rank approximation through truncated SVD, state the Eckart–Young–Mirsky theorem, compute Frobenius and spectral-norm errors, interpret retained energy, and assess financial factor compression without confusing matrix reconstruction quality with economic validity.

## 1. Why approximate a matrix?

A financial exposure matrix may contain many instruments and risk factors. If most of its singular-value magnitude is concentrated in a few directions, a low-rank matrix can represent much of its numerical structure using fewer components.

For \(A\in\mathbb R^{m\times n}\), a rank-\(k\) approximation \(A_k\) has at most \(k\) independent columns or rows. Compression is useful for storage, visualization, diagnostics, and some downstream computations, but discarded directions can still be important for stress testing and regulatory risk.

## 2. Truncated singular value decomposition

Let

\[
A=U\Sigma V^\mathsf T=\sum_{i=1}^{r}\sigma_i u_iv_i^\mathsf T,
\]

where \(r=\operatorname{rank}(A)\) and \(\sigma_1\ge\cdots\ge\sigma_r>0\). Retain the first \(k\) terms:

\[
\boxed{A_k=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\sigma_i u_iv_i^\mathsf T},\qquad 0\le k\le r.
\]

The Eckart–Young–Mirsky theorem says that \(A_k\) minimizes reconstruction error among all matrices of rank at most \(k\), in both Frobenius norm and spectral norm:

\[
\boxed{\|A-A_k\|_F=\sqrt{\sum_{i>k}\sigma_i^2}},
\qquad
\boxed{\|A-A_k\|_2=\sigma_{k+1}}
\]

for \(k<r\); the error is zero for \(k=r\).

If singular values tie at the cutoff, a best rank-\(k\) approximation need not be unique.

## 3. Worked rectangular financial exposure example

Consider the hypothetical factor-by-instrument matrix

\[
A=\begin{bmatrix}4&0\\0&2\\0&0\end{bmatrix}.
\]

Its singular values are \(4\) and \(2\), with squared values \(16\) and \(4\). The full Frobenius norm is

\[
\|A\|_F=\sqrt{4^2+2^2}=\sqrt{20}.
\]

A rank-one truncation retains the dominant factor:

\[
A_1=\begin{bmatrix}4&0\\0&0\\0&0\end{bmatrix}.
\]

The residual matrix is

\[
A-A_1=\begin{bmatrix}0&0\\0&2\\0&0\end{bmatrix}.
\]

Therefore,

\[
\|A-A_1\|_F=2,\qquad \|A-A_1\|_2=2.
\]

The retained squared singular-value fraction is

\[
\frac{4^2}{4^2+2^2}=\frac{16}{20}=\boxed{80\%}.
\]

This is **80% of the matrix's squared Frobenius norm**, not automatically 80% of economic risk, predictive accuracy, or portfolio return variance.

## 4. Choosing rank

Common diagnostics include:

- Cumulative squared singular-value fraction: \(\sum_{i=1}^k\sigma_i^2/\sum_i\sigma_i^2\).
- Absolute or relative reconstruction error.
- Downstream task error, such as factor-replication residuals.
- Stability under data perturbations and rolling windows.
- Minimum rank required to preserve specific critical factors.

For a zero matrix, the squared-energy denominator is zero; define and disclose a convention rather than dividing by zero. The companion lab returns a null retained-energy fraction in that case.

An energy threshold alone is not a sufficient acceptance criterion for a financial model. A small discarded direction might correspond to a rare but severe stress scenario.

## 5. Connection to principal component analysis

For a centered data matrix \(X\), the right singular vectors of \(X\) identify principal directions in the feature space. Under the usual sample covariance convention,

\[
S=\frac{X^\mathsf TX}{n-1},
\]

the covariance eigenvalues are \(\sigma_i^2/(n-1)\) when \(n>1\). This relationship assumes consistent centering, sample definitions, and units. PCA does not imply causality or stable economic factor identities.

## 6. Portfolio exposure implications

Suppose rows represent risk factors and columns represent instruments. A low-rank approximation may simplify sensitivity reporting, but a portfolio \(w\) produces exposures \(Aw\) while the compressed model gives \(A_kw\). The exposure error is

\[
(A-A_k)w.
\]

A norm bound is

\[
\boxed{\|(A-A_k)w\|_2\le\|A-A_k\|_2\|w\|_2}.
\]

Thus even a small matrix approximation error can lead to a meaningful absolute exposure error for large positions. Position limits and stress factors must be reviewed separately.

## 7. Numerical and governance safeguards

Check matrix shape and finite entries, preserve factor units, record rank and singular-value cutoff, and validate the reconstruction against the original matrix. For production-sized matrices use a vetted SVD implementation; forming normal equations for general SVD can amplify conditioning problems. Do not drop small singular directions solely because they look statistically unimportant without testing tail-risk and business impact.

*Answer:* 2.
