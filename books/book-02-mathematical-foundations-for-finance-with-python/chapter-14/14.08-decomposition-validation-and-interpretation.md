## Learning objectives

Validate eigenvalue decompositions, Cholesky factorizations, singular value decompositions, and low-rank approximations; compute absolute and relative reconstruction residuals; check orthogonality, positive definiteness, and solve residuals; interpret conditioning; and separate numerical correctness from economic validity.

## 1. Validation is more than obtaining a factor

A decomposition is an intermediate mathematical representation. A robust workflow checks input assumptions, factor dimensions, reconstruction, orthogonality, residual scale, and the financial interpretation of the resulting directions. Passing a numerical check does **not** establish that a covariance estimate, exposure model, or optimization objective is economically appropriate.

For a matrix \(A\) and reconstructed \(\widehat A\), define

\[
r_F=\|A-\widehat A\|_F,\qquad
r_{\mathrm{rel}}=\frac{\|A-\widehat A\|_F}{\max(\|A\|_F,\epsilon_{\mathrm{scale}})}.
\]

The scale floor \(\epsilon_{\mathrm{scale}}>0\) is a documented validation convention; it must not silently replace an application's error budget. For the zero matrix, report absolute error explicitly.

## 2. Eigenvalue decomposition checks

For real symmetric \(A\), the spectral theorem gives \(A=Q\Lambda Q^\mathsf T\). Check:

\[
\|A-Q\Lambda Q^\mathsf T\|_F,\qquad
\|Q^\mathsf TQ-I\|_F,\qquad
\|Av_i-\lambda_iv_i\|_2.
\]

The first checks reconstruction, the second orthonormality, and the third each eigenpair. Eigenvector signs are arbitrary; within repeated-eigenvalue subspaces, the basis is not unique. Compare reconstructed matrices or invariant subspaces rather than expecting identical eigenvector entries.

**Worked example:** \(A=\begin{bmatrix}2&1\\1&2\end{bmatrix}\) has eigenvalues \(3,1\) and eigenvectors \(v_1=(1,1)/\sqrt2\), \(v_2=(1,-1)/\sqrt2\). The exact reconstruction and orthogonality residuals are zero; floating-point implementations should be judged using stated tolerances.

## 3. Cholesky checks

For SPD \(A=LL^\mathsf T\), verify:

- \(A\) is symmetric within the explicitly chosen input tolerance.
- \(L\) is lower triangular with strictly positive diagonal.
- \(\|A-LL^\mathsf T\|_F\) is acceptably small.
- For \(Ax=b\), the independently computed residual \(\|Ax-b\|_2\) is acceptably small.

For \(A=\begin{bmatrix}4&2\\2&3\end{bmatrix}\),

\[
L=\begin{bmatrix}2&0\\1&\sqrt2\end{bmatrix},
\quad
LL^\mathsf T=A.
\]

With \(b=(6,5)\), the solution \(x=(1,1)\) gives \(Ax-b=0\). A tiny residual is useful evidence but does not guarantee small forward error for an ill-conditioned matrix.

## 4. SVD checks

For a reduced SVD \(A=U_r\Sigma_rV_r^\mathsf T\), verify compatible dimensions, nonnegative ordered singular values, column orthonormality of \(U_r\) and \(V_r\), and reconstruction error. When checking singular triplets, verify

\[
Av_i\approx\sigma_i u_i,\qquad A^\mathsf Tu_i\approx\sigma_i v_i.
\]

For \(A=\begin{bmatrix}3&0\\0&2\\0&0\end{bmatrix}\), the reduced factors in Section 14.6 reconstruct \(A\) exactly and the singular values are \(3,2\). This 3×2 example is full-column-rank, with condition number \(3/2\).

## 5. Low-rank approximation checks

For a truncated SVD \(A_k\), report:

\[
\|A-A_k\|_F,\quad
\|A-A_k\|_2,\quad
\frac{\sum_{i\le k}\sigma_i^2}{\sum_i\sigma_i^2}.
\]

For \(A=\begin{bmatrix}4&0\\0&2\\0&0\end{bmatrix}\) and rank \(k=1\), the best approximation retains the 4-valued direction. The Frobenius and spectral errors are both \(2\), and retained squared singular-value energy is \(16/20=80\%\). This is a numerical statement, not a claim that 80% of financial risk is preserved.

## 6. Conditioning and tolerance policy

Conditioning describes sensitivity of a mathematical problem to perturbations; a small backward residual does not by itself guarantee a small error in the inferred solution. For full-column-rank \(A\),

\[
\kappa_2(A)=\frac{\sigma_{\max}}{\sigma_{\min}}.
\]

The condition number is infinite for rank-deficient matrices when measured across all input directions. For matrices with entries of different physical units, scaling can strongly affect the singular values and condition number; document units before comparing results.

Choose tolerances using matrix scale, floating-point precision, data uncertainty, and the decision's risk. Record the exact tolerance, norm, software environment, and validation evidence. Never label a matrix PSD solely because negative eigenvalues are small without disclosing the threshold.

## 7. Financial interpretation checklist

Before using a decomposition to support a financial decision, answer:

1. What do rows, columns, and entries represent, and in what units?
2. Is the matrix a covariance estimate, exposure mapping, Hessian, or something else?
3. Were missing values, stale observations, and alignment problems handled?
4. Are singular or eigen directions stable across samples and market regimes?
5. Does compression preserve mandated factors and stress scenarios?
6. Are residual thresholds connected to a downstream risk or accuracy requirement?
7. Are any numerical repairs, truncations, or regularizations documented?
8. Has the model received independent review before production use?

These are review questions, not proof of model validity.

## 9. Twenty worked exercises

**1.** Define the Frobenius reconstruction residual.  
*Answer:* \(\|A-\widehat A\|_F\).

**2.** What does \(\|Q^\mathsf TQ-I\|_F\) test?  
*Answer:* Orthogonality of eigenvectors.

**3.** State the eigenpair residual.  
*Answer:* \(\|Av_i-\lambda_iv_i\|_2\).

**4.** What does Cholesky reconstruct?  
*Answer:* \(LL^\mathsf T\).

**5.** What diagonal sign is required for standard Cholesky?  
*Answer:* Strictly positive.

**6.** What does a solve residual measure?  
*Answer:* \(\|Ax-b\|_2\).

**7.** Does a small solve residual always mean a small forward error?  
*Answer:* No, not for ill-conditioned problems.

**8.** What does SVD reconstruct?  
*Answer:* \(U\Sigma V^\mathsf T\).

**9.** Are SVD singular values negative?  
*Answer:* No.

**10.** How is SVD orthogonality checked?  
*Answer:* \(U_r^\mathsf TU_r\approx I\), \(V_r^\mathsf TV_r\approx I\).

**11.** What is the reference eigenvalue matrix?  
*Answer:* \(\begin{bmatrix}2&1\\1&2\end{bmatrix}\).

**12.** What are its eigenvalues?  
*Answer:* \(3,1\).

**13.** What is the reference Cholesky matrix?  
*Answer:* \(\begin{bmatrix}4&2\\2&3\end{bmatrix}\).

**14.** What is its factor?  
*Answer:* \(\begin{bmatrix}2&0\\1&\sqrt2\end{bmatrix}\).

**15.** What are the reference SVD singular values?  
*Answer:* \(3,2\).

**16.** What is the reference SVD condition number?  
*Answer:* \(3/2\).

**17.** What is the rank-one approximation error for singular values \(4,2\)?  
*Answer:* Frobenius error \(2\).

**18.** What is its retained squared energy?  
*Answer:* \(80\%\).

**19.** Does successful reconstruction prove financial model validity?  
*Answer:* No.
