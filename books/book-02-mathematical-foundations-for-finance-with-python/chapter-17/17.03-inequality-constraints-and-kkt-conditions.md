## Learning objectives

Formulate inequalities in a consistent sign convention, derive the Karush–Kuhn–Tucker (KKT) system, distinguish necessary from sufficient optimality conditions, interpret complementary slackness and dual multipliers, and independently verify a constrained financial allocation.

## 1. Canonical constrained problem

Consider

\[
\min_x f(x)\quad\text{subject to}\quad h_j(x)=0,\quad g_i(x)\le0.
\]

Define the Lagrangian with **plus signs**:

\[
\mathcal L(x,\lambda,\mu)=f(x)+\sum_j\lambda_j h_j(x)+\sum_i\mu_i g_i(x).
\]

Equality multipliers \(\lambda_j\) are unrestricted in sign. For minimization with inequalities written \(g_i\le0\), inequality multipliers must satisfy \(\mu_i\ge0\). Changing the sign convention changes multiplier signs.

## 2. Four KKT conditions

At a candidate \((x^*,\lambda^*,\mu^*)\), check:

1. **Primal feasibility:** \(h_j(x^*)=0\) and \(g_i(x^*)\le0\).
2. **Dual feasibility:** \(\mu_i^*\ge0\).
3. **Stationarity:** \(\nabla f(x^*)+J_h(x^*)^\mathsf T\lambda^*+J_g(x^*)^\mathsf T\mu^*=0\).
4. **Complementary slackness:** \(\mu_i^*g_i(x^*)=0\) for each inequality.

Complementary slackness means a strictly inactive constraint has zero multiplier. An active constraint may have either a zero or positive multiplier; activity alone does not imply a strictly positive shadow value.

## 3. When are KKT conditions necessary?

For a differentiable local optimum, KKT multipliers generally require a **constraint qualification**, such as LICQ (linear independence of active constraint gradients), MFCQ, or another appropriate regularity condition. Without a qualification, even an optimum may have no KKT multipliers.

For example, minimize \(f(x)=x\) subject to \(g(x)=x^2\le0\). The only feasible point is \(x=0\), hence it is optimal, but stationarity would require \(1+2\mu x=1=0\), impossible. The active inequality gradient vanishes, so a standard qualification fails.

## 4. When are KKT conditions sufficient?

For a differentiable convex objective \(f\), affine equalities, and convex inequality functions \(g_i\), any feasible point with multipliers satisfying all KKT conditions is a **global minimum**. A qualification such as Slater's condition is useful for guaranteeing the existence of multipliers and strong duality under standard convex-program assumptions; it is not required to turn an already existing KKT certificate into a global-optimality proof.

Strict convexity of the objective on the feasible set yields uniqueness when a minimizer exists.

## 5. A scalar financial allocation example

Minimize

\[
f(x)=(x-0.8)^2\quad\text{subject to}\quad 0\le x\le0.6.
\]

The unconstrained minimizer \(x=0.8\) violates the upper cap. Express constraints as \(g_1(x)=-x\le0\) and \(g_2(x)=x-0.6\le0\).

The Lagrangian is

\[
\mathcal L=(x-0.8)^2-\mu_1x+\mu_2(x-0.6).
\]

At \(x^*=0.6\), the lower bound is inactive and \(\mu_1=0\). Stationarity gives \(2(0.6-0.8)+\mu_2=0\), so \(\mu_2=0.4\). Both multipliers are nonnegative and complementary slackness holds. Convexity certifies the unique global minimum.

## 6. Two-asset minimum-variance portfolio

Let

\[
\Sigma=\begin{bmatrix}0.04&0.01\\0.01&0.09\end{bmatrix},\qquad
V(w)=w^\mathsf T\Sigma w,
\]

with constraints \(w_1+w_2=1\), \(w_1,w_2\ge0\), and \(w_1\le0.6\). The equality-only optimum is \((8/11,3/11)\), which violates the cap. The constrained optimum is

\[
\boxed{w^*=(0.6,0.4),\qquad V(w^*)=0.0336.}
\]

Use \(h=w_1+w_2-1\), \(g_1=-w_1\), \(g_2=-w_2\), and \(g_3=w_1-0.6\). Then

\[
\mathcal L=V+\lambda h+\mu_1g_1+\mu_2g_2+\mu_3g_3.
\]

At \(w^*\), the lower bounds are inactive, so \(\mu_1=\mu_2=0\). The objective gradient is

\[
\nabla V(w^*)=2\Sigma w^*=(0.056,0.084).
\]

Stationarity gives \(0.084+\lambda=0\) and \(0.056+\lambda+\mu_3=0\). Therefore

\[
\boxed{\lambda=-0.084,\quad \mu_3=0.028.}
\]

The cap is active, its multiplier is positive, and all four KKT conditions hold. Since the covariance matrix is positive definite and the constraints are affine, this is the unique global minimum.

## 7. Shadow-price interpretation

Write the upper cap as \(g_3(w,u)=w_1-u\le0\). Under differentiability and regularity, the optimal value satisfies

\[
\frac{dV^*(u)}{du}=-\mu_3^*.
\]

At \(u=0.6\), the derivative is \(-0.028\): relaxing the cap slightly lowers the minimum variance. This is a **local** marginal effect with units of variance per unit of portfolio weight, not a monetary trading price. For \(u\ge8/11\), the cap becomes inactive and the multiplier becomes zero.

## 8. Complementarity and numerical tolerances

A numerical KKT report should retain the signed equality residuals, inequality residuals, multiplier signs, stationarity vector, and products \(\mu_i g_i\). Do not classify an inequality as active based solely on rounded display values. Use explicit tolerances and preserve raw floating-point values.

A solver status of “success” is not an independent KKT certificate. Recalculate the objective gradient and constraints from the original problem specification.

## 10. Twenty worked exercises

**1.** State the standard inequality sign convention.  
*Solution:* \(g_i(x)\le0\).

**2.** What sign must inequality multipliers have?  
*Solution:* \(\mu_i\ge0\) for minimization with the plus Lagrangian convention.

**3.** Are equality multipliers sign-restricted?  
*Solution:* No.

**4.** State primal feasibility.  
*Solution:* All equalities vanish and all inequalities are nonpositive.

**5.** State dual feasibility.  
*Solution:* Every inequality multiplier is nonnegative.

**6.** State stationarity.  
*Solution:* \(\nabla f+J_h^\mathsf T\lambda+J_g^\mathsf T\mu=0\).

**7.** State complementary slackness.  
*Solution:* \(\mu_i g_i=0\) for every inequality.

**8.** Can an inactive constraint have a positive multiplier?  
*Solution:* No, complementary slackness forces its multiplier to zero.

**9.** Must an active constraint have a positive multiplier?  
*Solution:* No; zero is possible.

**10.** Why can KKT necessity fail?  
*Solution:* A constraint qualification may fail.

**11.** Find the scalar capped optimum for \((x-0.8)^2\) on \([0,0.6]\).  
*Solution:* \(x=0.6\).

**12.** Find its upper-bound multiplier.  
*Solution:* \(0.4\).

**13.** Find its lower-bound multiplier.  
*Solution:* \(0\).

**14.** Find the equality-only first portfolio weight.  
*Solution:* \(8/11\).

**15.** Is that weight feasible under a \(0.6\) cap?  
*Solution:* No.

**16.** Find the capped portfolio.  
*Solution:* \((0.6,0.4)\).

**17.** Find its variance.  
*Solution:* \(0.0336\).

**18.** Find its equality multiplier.  
*Solution:* \(-0.084\).

**19.** Find its cap multiplier and sensitivity.  
*Solution:* \(\mu_3=0.028\), so \(dV^*/du=-0.028\) locally.
