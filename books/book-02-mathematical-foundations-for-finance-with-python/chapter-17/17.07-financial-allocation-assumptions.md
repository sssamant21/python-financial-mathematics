## Learning objectives

Translate financial assumptions into objective functions and constraints, distinguish estimated parameters from known quantities, audit return and covariance units, test budget/position/liquidity feasibility, and explain why an optimizer cannot repair an invalid financial model.

## 1. A model is a set of assumptions

Financial optimization requires a decision vector \(w\), a declared objective, inputs, restrictions, and a decision horizon. A mathematical optimum is conditional on all of these. It does not establish that expected returns, covariance estimates, liquidity assumptions, or future market conditions are correct.

A typical long-only, fully invested portfolio has

\[
\boldsymbol1^\mathsf Tw=1,\qquad 0\le w_i\le u_i.
\]

The weights are fractions of portfolio value. A cash position, leverage, borrowing, and short selling require explicit additional variables or changed constraints; they must not be inferred from an unconstrained solution.

## 2. Expected return and estimation risk

Given a vector of expected simple returns \(\mu\), modeled portfolio return is \(\mu^\mathsf Tw\). The units and horizon must match: monthly estimates must not be combined silently with annual covariance. Expected returns are forecasts, not realized guarantees.

Estimation error can dominate a portfolio's apparent improvement. Scenario analysis, holdout evaluation, and stability under input perturbations are essential before operational use.

## 3. Covariance, variance, and convexity

For covariance matrix \(\Sigma\), portfolio variance is

\[
V(w)=w^\mathsf T\Sigma w.
\]

A valid covariance matrix is symmetric positive semidefinite. An empirical estimate can be singular, especially with more assets than observations, or numerically nearly singular. Adding a ridge term \(\epsilon I\) may improve conditioning but changes the risk model and must be disclosed.

The matrix

\[
\Sigma=\begin{bmatrix}0.04&0.01\\0.01&0.09\end{bmatrix}
\]

is positive definite: its leading principal minors are \(0.04\) and \(0.0035\). The first and second asset volatilities are \(20\%\) and \(30\%\), respectively, if the covariance is annualized simple-return covariance.

## 4. Mean–variance objectives

A common continuous allocation model is

\[
\min_w\;\frac{\gamma}{2}w^\mathsf T\Sigma w-\mu^\mathsf Tw,
\]

with \(\gamma>0\) a declared risk-aversion coefficient. If the return vector and covariance are measured over a consistent period, the objective is a model-dependent tradeoff, not a monetary profit calculation.

For a pure minimum-variance problem, minimize \(w^\mathsf T\Sigma w\) subject to the chosen portfolio constraints.

## 5. Budget, caps, and feasibility

A full-investment constraint requires weights to sum to one. Under long-only caps \(0\le w_i\le u_i\), a necessary and sufficient feasibility condition for a portfolio with only these constraints is

\[
\sum_i u_i\ge1
\]

assuming every cap is nonnegative. For two assets capped at \(0.4\) each, full investment is impossible. A solver's failure on this model is a data/model feasibility problem, not an invitation to loosen tolerances.

Additional sector limits, factor targets, minimum holdings, and eligibility rules can make a model infeasible even if the simple cap-sum condition holds.

## 6. Liquidity and concentration

A position cap is not a liquidity guarantee. Trading a target weight requires a capital amount, a price, and an execution model. For a portfolio worth \(C\), a target position \(w_i\) represents notional \(Cw_i\). If a trading policy limits orders to a fraction \(q_i\) of estimated daily traded value \(D_i\), a simple liquidity constraint is

\[
C|w_i-w_i^{(0)}|\le q_iD_i.
\]

This is an illustrative notional limit; market impact, spreads, execution windows, and settlement constraints need separate treatment.

## 7. Transaction costs and turnover

Given current weights \(w^{(0)}\), a common turnover proxy is

\[
T(w)=\sum_i|w_i-w_i^{(0)}|.
\]

One-way traded notional is often defined as \(\tfrac C2T(w)\) for a self-financing portfolio, while gross buys plus sells equal \(CT(w)\). State the convention explicitly.

A proportional transaction-cost approximation \(k_iC|w_i-w_i^{(0)}|\) is convex when \(k_i\ge0\), but real transaction costs may include fixed fees, minimum trade sizes, nonlinear market impact, and taxes. These can change the optimization class.

## 8. Two-asset worked example

Let \(w_1+w_2=1\), \(w_i\ge0\), and \(w_1\le0.6\), using the covariance matrix above. Minimizing variance gives

\[
V(w_1)=0.11w_1^2-0.16w_1+0.09.
\]

The equality-only optimum is \(w_1=8/11\), above the cap. Therefore

\[
\boxed{w^*=(0.6,0.4),\quad V(w^*)=0.0336.}
\]

If the expected annual simple returns are \(\mu=(0.08,0.05)\), the modeled portfolio return is \(0.6(0.08)+0.4(0.05)=0.068\), or \(6.8\%\). This is an assumption-driven forecast, not a promised outcome.

Suppose the current weights are \((0.5,0.5)\), portfolio capital is \(\$1{,}000{,}000\), and proportional trading cost is 10 basis points on gross traded notional. Gross traded notional is \(C(0.1+0.1)=\$200{,}000\), so the modeled cost is \(\$200\). The trade buys \(\$100{,}000\) of asset 1 and sells \(\$100{,}000\) of asset 2. Taxes and slippage are excluded.

## 9. Sensitivity, governance, and auditability

A useful allocation report records input timestamps, estimation windows, return conventions, missing-data rules, covariance diagnostics, cap definitions, starting holdings, transaction-cost assumptions, tolerance settings, and the optimizer version.

Separate (a) mathematical feasibility, (b) numerical optimality, (c) economic plausibility, and (d) operational executability. A model can pass one category and fail another.

## 10. Twelve-step hands-on lab

1. Declare the investment horizon and return convention.
2. Specify the two-asset covariance and expected-return inputs.
3. Verify covariance symmetry and positive definiteness.
4. Record the budget and long-only constraints.
5. Apply the first-asset position cap.
6. Calculate the minimum-variance allocation.
7. Independently recompute portfolio variance.
8. Calculate modeled expected return.
9. Specify current holdings and portfolio capital.
10. Calculate gross traded notional and proportional costs.
11. Test contradictory caps, invalid covariance, and sensitivity to assumptions.
12. Run independent reference checks and retain execution evidence for the deferred book-wide QA phase.

## 11. Twenty worked exercises

**1.** What does a portfolio weight represent?  
*Solution:* A fraction of portfolio value allocated to an asset.

**2.** Write the fully invested condition.  
*Solution:* \(\sum_iw_i=1\).

**3.** State long-only bounds.  
*Solution:* \(w_i\ge0\).

**4.** What is a position cap?  
*Solution:* An upper bound \(w_i\le u_i\).

**5.** When are long-only full-investment caps feasible if there are no other restrictions?  
*Solution:* All caps are nonnegative and their sum is at least one.

**6.** Are caps \(0.4,0.4\) feasible?  
*Solution:* No; they sum to \(0.8\).

**7.** Define modeled portfolio expected return.  
*Solution:* \(\mu^\mathsf Tw\).

**8.** Define portfolio variance.  
*Solution:* \(w^\mathsf T\Sigma w\).

**9.** What matrix property ensures variance is convex?  
*Solution:* \(\Sigma\succeq0\).

**10.** What are the example individual volatilities?  
*Solution:* \(20\%\) and \(30\%\).

**11.** Find the unconstrained-on-budget minimum-variance first weight.  
*Solution:* \(8/11\).

**12.** Find the capped optimum.  
*Solution:* \((0.6,0.4)\).

**13.** Find the capped variance.  
*Solution:* \(0.0336\).

**14.** Find the modeled expected return for \(\mu=(0.08,0.05)\).  
*Solution:* \(0.068\).

**15.** What is the first-asset target notional on \(\$1\) million?  
*Solution:* \(\$600{,}000\).

**16.** Find gross weight turnover from \((0.5,0.5)\) to \((0.6,0.4)\).  
*Solution:* \(0.2\).

**17.** Find gross traded notional.  
*Solution:* \(\$200{,}000\).

**18.** Find cost at 10 basis points of gross traded notional.  
*Solution:* \(\$200\).

**19.** Does a feasible allocation guarantee practical execution?  
*Solution:* No; liquidity, taxes, settlement, and other constraints may intervene.
