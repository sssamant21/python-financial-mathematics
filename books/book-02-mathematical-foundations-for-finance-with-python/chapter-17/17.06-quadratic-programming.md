## Learning objectives

Recognize quadratic programs (QPs), distinguish positive-semidefinite and indefinite Hessians, formulate portfolio variance minimization, derive KKT conditions, compute a constrained optimum, and audit primal and dual residuals.

## 1. Canonical quadratic program

A quadratic program minimizes

\[
\boxed{\tfrac12x^\mathsf TQx+c^\mathsf Tx}
\]

subject to affine equalities \(Ax=b\) and inequalities \(Gx\le h\). Assume \(Q\) symmetric without loss of generality because only its symmetric part contributes to \(x^\mathsf TQx\). The gradient is \(Qx+c\), and the Hessian is \(Q\).

If \(Q\succeq0\), the objective is convex; with affine constraints, the QP is convex. If \(Q\succ0\), the objective is strictly convex, so an attained optimum is unique. If \(Q\) is indefinite, local stationarity alone does not certify global optimality.

## 2. QP versus LP

An LP has a linear objective and linear constraints. A QP allows quadratic curvature while retaining affine constraints. LPs are special QPs with \(Q=0\). Quadratic constraints, integer decisions, or nonlinear equalities generally place a model outside the ordinary QP class.

The convexity of a quadratic objective depends on eigenvalues of its symmetric Hessian, not merely on positive diagonal entries.

## 3. Portfolio variance as a QP

Let \(w\) be portfolio weights and \(\Sigma\) a covariance matrix. Portfolio variance is \(w^\mathsf T\Sigma w\), so the canonical QP uses \(Q=2\Sigma\) and \(c=0\).

Consider

\[
\Sigma=\begin{bmatrix}0.04&0.01\\0.01&0.09\end{bmatrix},
\quad w_1+w_2=1,\quad w_1,w_2\ge0,\quad w_1\le0.6.
\]

Because \(\Sigma\succ0\), the objective is strictly convex. The equality-only optimum is \((8/11,3/11)\); it violates the position cap. The capped optimum is

\[
\boxed{w^*=(0.6,0.4),\quad V^*=0.0336.}
\]

The portfolio weights are dimensionless, and variance is in squared return units.

## 4. Derivation by eliminating the equality

Set \(w_2=1-w_1\). The objective becomes

\[
V(w_1)=0.11w_1^2-0.16w_1+0.09.
\]

Its derivative is \(0.22w_1-0.16\), and its second derivative is \(0.22>0\). The feasible interval under the cap is \(0\le w_1\le0.6\), while the unrestricted minimizer \(8/11\) lies above the interval. Hence the minimum occurs at \(w_1=0.6\).

This reduction is useful for transparent two-asset examples but does not replace robust numerical QP methods for larger portfolios.

## 5. KKT system and certificate

Using inequalities \(g_1=-w_1\), \(g_2=-w_2\), \(g_3=w_1-0.6\), and equality \(h=w_1+w_2-1\), define

\[
\mathcal L=V+\lambda h+\mu_1g_1+\mu_2g_2+\mu_3g_3.
\]

At \(w=(0.6,0.4)\), the lower bounds are inactive, so \(\mu_1=\mu_2=0\). Since \(2\Sigma w=(0.056,0.084)\), stationarity gives

\[
\boxed{\lambda=-0.084,\quad \mu_3=0.028.}
\]

The primal constraints hold, all inequality multipliers are nonnegative, the stationarity vector is zero, and every complementarity product \(\mu_i g_i\) is zero. Convexity makes this KKT certificate sufficient for global optimality.

## 6. Sensitivity and shadow prices

For a general first-asset cap \(u\), the optimal first weight is \(\min(u,8/11)\) for \(0\le u\le1\). When \(0<u<8/11\),

\[
V^*(u)=0.11u^2-0.16u+0.09,\qquad
\mu_{\rm cap}(u)=0.16-0.22u.
\]

Consequently \(dV^*/du=-\mu_{\rm cap}\). At \(u=0.6\), relaxing the cap by a small amount lowers minimum variance at a local rate of \(0.028\) variance units per unit of weight. This derivative is local; it changes once the cap becomes inactive.

## 7. Numerical issues in QP

Nearly singular covariance matrices can cause large weight changes and unstable linear solves. Regularization such as \(\Sigma+\epsilon I\) changes the objective and must be documented, not silently applied. Estimated covariance matrices may be noisy or fail positive-semidefinite checks because of estimation or data issues.

Check symmetry, eigenvalue or positive-semidefinite evidence, feasibility tolerances, KKT residuals, scaling, and reproducibility. Avoid assuming a solver's reported success guarantees a mathematically correct solution.

## 8. Common model variations

A mean-variance objective can be written \(\tfrac12\gamma w^\mathsf T\Sigma w-\mu^\mathsf Tw\) with risk-aversion parameter \(\gamma>0\). Linear exposure targets, factor-neutrality requirements, sector caps, and turnover constraints can often be represented as affine restrictions when modeled appropriately. Cardinality and discrete-lot constraints generally require mixed-integer optimization, not an ordinary continuous QP.
