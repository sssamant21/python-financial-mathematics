## Chapter synthesis

This section consolidates feasible-set geometry, equality and inequality constraints, Lagrange multipliers, complementary slackness, linear programming, quadratic programming, portfolio assumptions, and independent optimality certificates. The exercises are intended to be solved first without looking at the answers. All numerical examples are hypothetical, educational models rather than investment advice.

## Core reference model

Throughout the quadratic portfolio exercises, use

\[
\Sigma=\begin{bmatrix}0.04&0.01\\0.01&0.09\end{bmatrix},\qquad
V(w)=w^\mathsf T\Sigma w,
\]

with \(w_1+w_2=1,\ w_i\ge0,\ w_1\le u\). Eliminating \(w_2\) yields

\[
V(w_1)=0.11w_1^2-0.16w_1+0.09,\qquad
w_1^*(u)=\min(u,8/11)
\]

for \(0\le u\le1\). For \(0<u<8/11\), the first-asset cap multiplier is \(0.16-0.22u\). At \(u=8/11\), the cap is active with zero multiplier; at \(u=0\), dependent active bounds may make the multipliers nonunique.

Use the minimization sign convention \(g_i(x)\le0\), with Lagrangian \(f+\lambda^\mathsf Th+\mu^\mathsf Tg\) and \(\mu\ge0\).

## Part A — Twenty worked exercises with solutions

**1. Feasible-set definition.** Define feasibility for \(h(x)=0\) and \(g(x)\le0\).  
*Solution:* All equality residuals are zero and every inequality residual is nonpositive.

**2. Convexity of linear constraints.** Is \(\{x:Ax\le b,\ Ex=d\}\) convex?  
*Solution:* Yes. Each half-space and affine set is convex, and intersections preserve convexity.

**3. Budget constraint.** Translate full investment for two asset weights.  
*Solution:* \(w_1+w_2=1\).

**4. Cap feasibility.** Are two long-only caps \(u_1=0.45\) and \(u_2=0.50\) feasible under full investment?  
*Solution:* No. Their sum is \(0.95<1\).

**5. Equality-only optimum.** Find the minimizer of \(0.11x^2-0.16x+0.09\).  
*Solution:* \(0.22x-0.16=0\), so \(x=8/11\).

**6. Second-order check.** Prove the reduced objective is strictly convex.  
*Solution:* Its second derivative is \(0.22>0\).

**7. Capped portfolio.** Find the optimum with \(u=0.6\).  
*Solution:* \((w_1,w_2)=(0.6,0.4)\).

**8. Capped variance.** Evaluate variance at \((0.6,0.4)\).  
*Solution:* \(0.04(0.6)^2+0.02(0.6)(0.4)+0.09(0.4)^2=0.0336\).

**9. Portfolio gradient.** Find \(\nabla V\) at \((0.6,0.4)\).  
*Solution:* \(2\Sigma w=(0.056,0.084)\).

**10. Equality multiplier.** Find \(\lambda\) at the capped optimum.  
*Solution:* \(\lambda=-0.084\).

**11. Cap multiplier.** Find the active upper-bound multiplier at \(u=0.6\).  
*Solution:* \(\mu_{\mathrm{cap}}=0.084-0.056=0.028\).

**12. Complementarity.** What is the product of cap multiplier and signed cap residual?  
*Solution:* \(0.028(0.6-0.6)=0\).

**13. Inactive cap.** What is the solution at \(u=0.9\)?  
*Solution:* \((8/11,3/11)\); the cap multiplier is zero.

**14. Weak activity.** What happens at \(u=8/11\)?  
*Solution:* The cap is active, but its multiplier is zero.

**15. Cap sensitivity.** Find \(dV^*/du\) at \(u=0.6\).  
*Solution:* \(0.22(0.6)-0.16=-0.028=-\mu_{\mathrm{cap}}\).

**16. Linear program.** Maximize \(5x+4y\) subject to \(x+y\le8,\ 2x+y\le10,\ x,y\ge0\).  
*Solution:* The feasible vertices are \((0,0),(5,0),(2,6),(0,8)\), with objective values \(0,25,34,32\). The optimum is \((2,6)\), value \(34\).

**17. LP dual certificate.** Find a dual feasible pair with objective 34.  
*Solution:* \((u_1,u_2)=(3,1)\) satisfies \(u_1+2u_2\ge5,\ u_1+u_2\ge4\), and \(8u_1+10u_2=34\). Equal primal and dual values certify optimality.

**18. Infeasible candidate.** Evaluate the first-asset cap violation for \((0.7,0.3)\) with \(u=0.6\).  
*Solution:* \(0.7-0.6=0.1>0\), so the point is infeasible.

**19. Financial reporting.** With expected returns \((0.08,0.05)\), find modeled return at \((0.6,0.4)\).  
*Solution:* \(0.6(0.08)+0.4(0.05)=0.068=6.8\%\).

**20. Turnover and costs.** Rebalance from \((0.5,0.5)\) to \((0.6,0.4)\) with capital \(\$1{,}000{,}000\) and a cost of 10 basis points on gross traded notional.  
*Solution:* Gross weight turnover is \(0.2\); traded notional is \(\$200{,}000\); modeled cost is \(\$200\). Taxes and slippage are excluded.

## Part B — Extended applied problems

### Problem 1 — Active-set transition report

Evaluate the portfolio for caps \(u=0.4,0.6,8/11,0.9\). Report weights, variance, activity of the upper cap, and cap multiplier.

*Solution:* At \(u=0.4\), weights \((0.4,0.6)\), variance \(0.0436\), cap active, multiplier \(0.072\). At \(u=0.6\), weights \((0.6,0.4)\), variance \(0.0336\), cap active, multiplier \(0.028\). At \(u=8/11\), weights \((8/11,3/11)\), variance \(7/220\), cap active with multiplier zero. At \(u=0.9\), weights \((8/11,3/11)\), variance \(7/220\), cap inactive with multiplier zero.

### Problem 2 — Two-sided caps

Let \(w_1\le0.9\) and \(w_2\le0.2\), with full investment and long-only weights. Find the feasible interval and optimum.

*Solution:* Since \(w_2=1-w_1\), the second cap requires \(w_1\ge0.8\). Therefore \(w_1\in[0.8,0.9]\). The unconstrained minimizer \(8/11\) is below the interval, so \(w^*=(0.8,0.2)\), variance \(0.032\)? **Recalculate carefully:** \(0.11(0.8)^2-0.16(0.8)+0.09=0.0704-0.128+0.09=0.0324\). The second-asset cap is active with positive multiplier \(0.016\).

### Problem 3 — False success flag

A program reports success at \(w=(0.7,0.3)\) with \(u=0.6\). Explain why the result must be rejected.

*Solution:* The first-asset cap has signed residual \(+0.1\). Primal infeasibility invalidates the claimed constrained solution regardless of objective value or reported solver status.

### Problem 4 — Model and execution risk

A portfolio passes the mathematical KKT check. Can it be automatically traded?

*Solution:* No. Independent checks of data freshness, transaction costs, liquidity, order sizes, permissions, taxes, settlement, compliance, and risk controls remain necessary.
