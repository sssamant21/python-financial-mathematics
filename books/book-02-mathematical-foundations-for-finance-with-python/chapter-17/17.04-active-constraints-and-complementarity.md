## 1. Active inequalities and the KKT convention

Consider minimizing \(f(x)\) subject to \(h(x)=0\) and \(g_i(x)\le0\). With

\[
\mathcal L(x,\lambda,\mu)=f(x)+\lambda^\mathsf T h(x)+\mu^\mathsf T g(x),
\]

the KKT conditions are primal feasibility, \(\mu\ge0\), stationarity \(\nabla f+J_h^\mathsf T\lambda+J_g^\mathsf T\mu=0\), and complementarity

\[
\boxed{\mu_i g_i(x)=0.}
\]

At a feasible point, \(g_i(x)=0\) means **active**; \(g_i(x)<0\) means **inactive**. Complementarity forces \(\mu_i=0\) for inactive inequalities, but an active constraint can have \(\mu_i=0\). This is called a **weakly active** or degenerate constraint. **Strict complementarity** means each inequality has either strict slack or a strictly positive multiplier. Strict complementarity is useful but not universally guaranteed.

## 2. Why active sets matter

When the active set is known, an inequality-constrained problem can often be solved by temporarily treating active inequalities as equalities. A candidate is valid only if the resulting point satisfies every inequality, the active multipliers have the correct signs, and the full stationarity and complementarity equations hold.

An active-set algorithm therefore alternates between solving equality-constrained subproblems and adding or dropping constraints. Blindly fixing an active set can produce negative multipliers, infeasible points, or nonoptimal candidates.

## 3. A one-dimensional example with three regimes

Consider

\[
\min_x (x-0.8)^2\quad\text{subject to}\quad 0\le x\le u.
\]

For \(u\ge0\), the solution is \(x^*(u)=\min(0.8,u)\). With \(g_1=-x\) and \(g_2=x-u\), the Lagrangian is

\[
\mathcal L=(x-0.8)^2-\mu_1x+\mu_2(x-u).
\]

When \(0<u<0.8\), the cap is active, \(\mu_1=0\), and \(\mu_2=2(0.8-u)>0\). When \(u>0.8\), the cap is inactive and \(\mu_2=0\). At \(u=0.8\), the cap is active but \(\mu_2=0\): a weakly active constraint. At \(u=0\), both lower and upper bounds are active, and the multiplier representation is nonunique because the active gradients are dependent.

## 4. Two-asset financial allocation

Let

\[
\Sigma=\begin{bmatrix}0.04&0.01\\0.01&0.09\end{bmatrix},\quad
V(w)=w^\mathsf T\Sigma w,\quad
w_1+w_2=1,\quad w_1,w_2\ge0,\quad w_1\le u.
\]

For \(0\le u\le1\), eliminate \(w_2=1-w_1\) and obtain

\[
V(w_1)=0.11w_1^2-0.16w_1+0.09.
\]

The equality-only minimum occurs at \(w_1=8/11\). Thus

\[
\boxed{w_1^*(u)=\min(u,8/11),\qquad w_2^*(u)=1-w_1^*(u).}
\]

For \(0<u<8/11\), the upper cap is active with a positive multiplier. For \(8/11<u\le1\), it is inactive. At \(u=8/11\), it is active but its multiplier is zero. At \(u=0\), the first-asset lower bound and upper cap are both active, so the simple multiplier representation need not be unique.

## 5. Deriving the cap multiplier

With \(h=w_1+w_2-1\), \(g_1=-w_1\), \(g_2=-w_2\), and \(g_3=w_1-u\), stationarity is

\[
2\Sigma w+\lambda(1,1)^\mathsf T-\mu_1(1,0)^\mathsf T-\mu_2(0,1)^\mathsf T+\mu_3(1,0)^\mathsf T=0.
\]

For \(0<u<8/11\), both lower bounds are inactive. Therefore

\[
\lambda=-2(0.01u+0.09(1-u))=-0.18+0.16u
\]

and

\[
\boxed{\mu_3=0.16-0.22u>0.}
\]

At \(u=0.6\), \(\lambda=-0.084\) and \(\mu_3=0.028\). The value function on the binding-cap regime is

\[
V^*(u)=0.11u^2-0.16u+0.09,
\]

so \(dV^*/du=0.22u-0.16=-\mu_3\). Above the threshold \(8/11\), the optimal value is constant and the cap shadow price is zero. At the threshold, the value function is differentiable with zero derivative, although its second derivative changes.

## 6. Binding versus valuable constraints

A constraint can be active without having positive marginal value. The cap \(u=8/11\) touches the unconstrained optimum but does not change its objective; its multiplier is zero. A positive multiplier at a regular optimum indicates a local benefit from relaxing the corresponding upper limit, under the stated sign convention.

Do not confuse **binding**, **positive multiplier**, **strict complementarity**, and **sensitivity**. These are related but distinct concepts.

## 7. Degeneracy and numerical classification

Active-set decisions in floating-point arithmetic require tolerances. If a signed inequality residual is \(-10^{-11}\) and the declared activity tolerance is \(10^{-9}\), the constraint may be classified numerically active, but that does not prove mathematical equality.

A robust report retains the raw signed inequality residuals, primal violation, dual violation, stationarity vector, complementary products, chosen tolerance, and regime label. At a degenerate boundary, the numerical active set and multiplier representation may be sensitive to perturbations.

## 8. Second-order and global-optimality checks

For the portfolio example, \(\Sigma\) is positive definite and the constraints are affine. Thus any feasible KKT point is globally minimizing, and strict convexity makes it unique. In nonconvex problems, satisfying KKT conditions does not guarantee global optimality.

An active-set calculation is a candidate-generation procedure, not a substitute for independent feasibility and optimality verification.

## Cap-zero multiplier family — validation follow-up

At the exact endpoint \(u=0\), feasibility fixes \(w=(0,1)\), with gradient \((1/50,9/50)\) and inequalities \((0,-1,0)\). Complementarity forces \(\mu_2=0\). The second stationarity equation gives \(\lambda=-9/50\); the first gives \(\mu_3-\mu_1=4/25\). Thus every

\[
\boxed{\lambda=-9/50,\qquad (\mu_1,\mu_2,\mu_3)=(a,0,a+4/25),\quad a\ge0}
\]

is a valid multiplier representation of the same unique portfolio. The helper returns the canonical choice \(a=0\). Positive \(a\) makes both active inequality multipliers positive; strict complementarity therefore depends on the multiplier choice here. The opposite active gradients are linearly dependent, so unique primal weights do not imply unique dual multipliers.

[Runnable family checks](code/check_degenerate_multipliers.py) test six selected parameters, independently derived stationarity/complementarity, and isolated sign, stationarity and complementarity failures. Run `python check_degenerate_multipliers.py` from `chapter-17/code`. Floating residuals use a declared numerical tolerance: a sufficiently small negative multiplier may pass that tolerance while remaining mathematically dual-infeasible. Zero-tolerance diagnostics can reject valid exact-model families after float rounding. These are distinct from exact KKT statements; no general tolerance-scaling or platform guarantee follows.
