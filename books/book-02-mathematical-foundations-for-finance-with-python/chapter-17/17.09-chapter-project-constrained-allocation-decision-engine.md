## Project purpose

Design an auditable **two-asset minimum-variance allocation decision engine** that separates financial assumptions, mathematical optimization, independent verification, and operational interpretation. This project consolidates Sections 17.1–17.8. It is an educational reference implementation, not investment advice or a production trading system.

## Learning outcomes

By completing the project, readers will be able to:

- Express budgets, long-only bounds, and asset caps as explicit mathematical constraints.
- Derive a unique optimum using convex quadratic programming.
- Detect infeasible allocation requirements before optimization.
- Reconstruct an independent KKT certificate.
- Evaluate expected return, turnover, and modeled transaction costs without confusing these with the optimization objective.
- Produce a structured, reproducible decision report with assumptions and limitations.

## 1. Financial problem statement

Allocate a fully invested portfolio across two assets. Let \(w_1,w_2\) be fractions of total capital, with

\[
w_1+w_2=1,\qquad 0\le w_1\le u_1,\qquad 0\le w_2\le u_2.
\]

Assume annualized covariance

\[
\Sigma=\begin{bmatrix}0.04&0.01\\0.01&0.09\end{bmatrix}.
\]

Minimize

\[
\boxed{V(w)=w^\mathsf T\Sigma w.}
\]

For illustrative reporting, use annual expected simple returns \(\mu=(0.08,0.05)\), initial weights \((0.5,0.5)\), capital \(\$1{,}000{,}000\), and a proportional transaction-cost assumption of 10 basis points of gross traded notional. These reporting assumptions do **not** enter the minimum-variance objective.

## 2. Input contract

The engine accepts asset caps \(u_1,u_2\), current weights, portfolio capital, and cost basis points. Reject nonfinite values, invalid weight sums, negative capital, negative costs, caps outside \([0,1]\), and infeasible cap combinations. Do not silently normalize incorrect current weights.

For this two-asset long-only full-investment model, feasibility is equivalent to

\[
0\le u_1,u_2\le1,\qquad u_1+u_2\ge1.
\]

The feasible interval for the first asset is

\[
L=\max(0,1-u_2),\qquad U=\min(1,u_1).
\]

## 3. Analytical optimization engine

Using \(w_2=1-w_1\),

\[
V(w_1)=0.11w_1^2-0.16w_1+0.09.
\]

The unconstrained-on-budget minimizer is \(w_1=8/11\). Strict convexity implies the constrained optimum is its projection onto the feasible interval:

\[
\boxed{w_1^*=\min(U,\max(L,8/11)),\quad w_2^*=1-w_1^*.}
\]

This analytical solution avoids introducing a general solver dependency into the pedagogical implementation. The engine is **not** a general-purpose QP solver.

## 4. Dual certificate and sign convention

Write inequalities as

\[
g_1=-w_1,\quad g_2=-w_2,\quad
g_3=w_1-u_1,\quad g_4=w_2-u_2,
\]

and equality \(h=w_1+w_2-1\). The Lagrangian is

\[
\mathcal L=V+\lambda h+\sum_{i=1}^4\mu_i g_i,\qquad\mu_i\ge0.
\]

Stationarity requires

\[
2\Sigma w+\lambda(1,1)^\mathsf T
-\mu_1(1,0)^\mathsf T-\mu_2(0,1)^\mathsf T
+\mu_3(1,0)^\mathsf T+\mu_4(0,1)^\mathsf T=0.
\]

For the default case \(u_1=0.6,u_2=1\), both lower bounds and the second cap are inactive. The unique optimal weights are \((0.6,0.4)\), with \(\lambda=-0.084\) and \((\mu_1,\mu_2,\mu_3,\mu_4)=(0,0,0.028,0)\).

For other cap combinations, dependent active constraints may make multipliers nonunique. The implementation constructs **one** valid nonnegative multiplier certificate rather than asserting uniqueness.

## 5. Independent verification

The checker must accept the proposed weights and multipliers as inputs, and independently recompute:

1. Full-investment equality residual.
2. Four signed inequality residuals and maximum primal violation.
3. Objective value and gradient.
4. Stationarity vector.
5. Dual feasibility violations.
6. Complementarity products.
7. Covariance positive definiteness.

A feasible KKT point is globally optimal because the covariance is positive definite and the constraints are affine. The checker must not call the optimizer or trust a solver-provided residual.

## 6. Financial decision report

For the default allocation, the modeled expected return is

\[
0.6(0.08)+0.4(0.05)=0.068.
\]

Gross turnover from \((0.5,0.5)\) is \(0.2\), representing \(\$200{,}000\) of total buys and sells. At 10 basis points of gross traded notional, modeled cost is \(\$200\). Neither return nor transaction costs are part of the minimum-variance objective in this project. Taxes, market impact, slippage, lot sizes, and settlement are excluded.

## 7. Failure modes and governance

**Infeasible caps:** \(u_1=u_2=0.4\) cannot support full investment. Reject before optimization.

**Invalid data:** NaN, infinity, Boolean-as-number, or negative cost assumptions must be rejected.

**Bad certificate:** A candidate may satisfy weights and caps but fail stationarity; another may be stationary for an unconstrained objective but violate a cap.

**Degenerate active sets:** At exactly binding endpoint configurations, active gradients may be linearly dependent. Report a valid certificate without claiming unique multipliers.

**Model risk:** A mathematically certified portfolio is not proof that expected returns, covariance forecasts, or execution assumptions will hold.

## 8. Twelve-step hands-on lab

1. Define the financial assumption register and unit conventions.
2. Validate the covariance matrix's symmetry and positive definiteness.
3. Parse and validate asset caps, current holdings, capital, and costs.
4. Calculate the feasible interval \([L,U]\).
5. Reject an infeasible cap configuration.
6. Compute the equality-only minimum-variance benchmark.
7. Project that benchmark onto the feasible interval.
8. Construct one KKT multiplier certificate.
9. Run the independent primal-dual verifier.
10. Compute modeled return, turnover, traded notional, and costs.
11. Exercise a binding-cap case, inactive-cap case, and degenerate endpoint.
12. Run the independent reference tests and preserve output for deferred book-wide validation.

## 9. Twenty worked exercises

**1.** State the decision variables.  
*Solution:* \(w_1,w_2\), fractions of total portfolio capital.

**2.** State full investment.  
*Solution:* \(w_1+w_2=1\).

**3.** State the long-only bounds.  
*Solution:* \(w_1,w_2\ge0\).

**4.** State the two cap constraints.  
*Solution:* \(w_1\le u_1,\ w_2\le u_2\).

**5.** Give the cap feasibility condition.  
*Solution:* \(u_1+u_2\ge1\), with each cap in \([0,1]\).

**6.** Find \(L\).  
*Solution:* \(\max(0,1-u_2)\).

**7.** Find \(U\).  
*Solution:* \(\min(1,u_1)\).

**8.** Give the reduced objective.  
*Solution:* \(0.11w_1^2-0.16w_1+0.09\).

**9.** Find the equality-only optimum.  
*Solution:* \(w_1=8/11\).

**10.** State the projection rule.  
*Solution:* \(w_1^*=\min(U,\max(L,8/11))\).

**11.** Find the optimum when \(u_1=0.6,u_2=1\).  
*Solution:* \((0.6,0.4)\).

**12.** Find the optimal variance.  
*Solution:* \(0.0336\).

**13.** Find the gradient at the optimum.  
*Solution:* \((0.056,0.084)\).

**15.** Find the first-asset cap multiplier.  
*Solution:* \(0.028\).
