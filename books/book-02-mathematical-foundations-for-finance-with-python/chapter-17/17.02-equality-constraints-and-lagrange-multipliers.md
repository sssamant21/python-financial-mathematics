## Learning objectives

Construct a Lagrangian for equality-constrained optimization, derive first-order stationarity equations, explain constraint qualifications and second-order tests, interpret multipliers as marginal values, and verify a fully invested minimum-variance portfolio analytically and numerically.

## 1. Equality-constrained optimization

Consider

\[
\min_{x\in\mathbb R^n}f(x)\quad\text{subject to}\quad h(x)=0,
\]

where \(h:\mathbb R^n\to\mathbb R^p\). Equality constraints restrict feasible directions. An unconstrained optimum may violate them; the constrained optimum must be sought on the feasible surface.

For a fully invested portfolio, \(h(w)=\boldsymbol1^\mathsf Tw-1=0\).

## 2. Lagrangian and stationarity

Using the sign convention

\[
\mathcal L(x,\lambda)=f(x)+\lambda^\mathsf T h(x),
\]

the first-order Lagrange equations are

\[
\boxed{\nabla_x f(x^*)+J_h(x^*)^\mathsf T\lambda^*=0,\qquad h(x^*)=0.}
\]

Here \(J_h\) is the Jacobian of the equality constraints, with one row per constraint. The multiplier vector \(\lambda\) balances the objective gradient against constraint normals.

These conditions are **necessary** at a differentiable local optimum under an appropriate constraint qualification, such as linearly independent equality-constraint gradients. They are not automatically sufficient for a minimum.

## 3. Geometric interpretation

At a regular feasible point, a first-order feasible direction \(d\) satisfies \(J_h(x)d=0\). The stationarity equation implies

\[
\nabla f(x^*)^\mathsf Td=0
\]

for every tangent direction. Thus the objective has no first-order improvement along the feasible surface. Its gradient need not vanish in the ambient space.

For the line \(x+y=1\), the tangent directions satisfy \(d_x+d_y=0\), while the constraint normal is \((1,1)\).

## 4. Constraint qualification and degeneracy

If equality-constraint gradients are linearly dependent, the usual Lagrange multiplier theorem may not apply in its simple form and multipliers may be nonunique. For example, \(h_1(x)=x-1\) and \(h_2(x)=2x-2\) describe the same feasible point, but their gradients are dependent.

An even more delicate example is \(h(x)=x^2=0\), whose only feasible point is \(x=0\), yet \(h'(0)=0\). For the objective \(f(x)=x\), \(x=0\) is trivially the constrained minimum and maximum, but the stationarity equation \(1+\lambda(0)=0\) has no solution. Constraint qualifications are substantive assumptions, not technical decoration.

## 5. Second-order conditions

For a regular equality-constrained local minimum, the Lagrangian Hessian must be nonnegative on feasible tangent directions:

\[
d^\mathsf T\nabla_{xx}^2\mathcal L(x^*,\lambda^*)d\ge0
\quad\text{for all }d\ne0\text{ with }J_h(x^*)d=0.
\]

Strict positivity on all nonzero tangent directions is a common sufficient condition for a strict local minimum when combined with the first-order conditions and suitable smoothness and regularity assumptions.

Do not test only whether the ambient objective Hessian is positive definite; the relevant geometry is the feasible tangent space.

## 6. Financial example: minimum-variance portfolio

Let

\[
\Sigma=\begin{bmatrix}0.04&0.01\\0.01&0.09\end{bmatrix},
\qquad f(w)=w^\mathsf T\Sigma w,
\qquad w_1+w_2=1.
\]

Use \(\mathcal L(w,\lambda)=w^\mathsf T\Sigma w+\lambda(w_1+w_2-1)\). The stationarity conditions are

\[
2\Sigma w+\lambda\boldsymbol1=0,\qquad \boldsymbol1^\mathsf Tw=1.
\]

Because \(\Sigma\) is positive definite, it is invertible. Eliminating the multiplier gives

\[
\boxed{w^*=\frac{\Sigma^{-1}\boldsymbol1}{\boldsymbol1^\mathsf T\Sigma^{-1}\boldsymbol1}.}
\]

For the stated covariance matrix,

\[
\boxed{w_1^*=\frac8{11},\quad w_2^*=\frac3{11},\quad
f(w^*)=\frac7{220}\approx0.03181818.}
\]

The multiplier is \(\lambda^*=-2f(w^*)=-7/110\). The equality is satisfied exactly in rational arithmetic. The portfolio is also long-only, although long-only constraints were not imposed in this particular equality-only derivation.

## 7. Shadow-price interpretation

For a parameterized equality \(h(x,b)=a^\mathsf Tx-b=0\), with Lagrangian \(f(x)+\lambda(a^\mathsf Tx-b)\), the envelope theorem gives

\[
\frac{dV(b)}{db}=-\lambda^*
\]

when the optimal value function \(V\) is differentiable and regularity conditions hold. The sign changes if the constraint or Lagrangian convention changes.

For the portfolio example with total allocation \(b\) instead of \(1\), the optimal weights scale as \(b(8/11,3/11)\), so \(V(b)=(7/220)b^2\). At \(b=1\), \(V'(1)=7/110=-\lambda^*\). A multiplier is a local marginal sensitivity, not necessarily a market-traded price.

## 8. Analytical versus numerical verification

Independent checks should recompute the constraint residual \(w_1+w_2-1\), stationarity residual components \(2\Sigma w+\lambda\boldsymbol1\), objective value, and tangent curvature. For the tangent vector \(d=(1,-1)\),

\[
d^\mathsf T(2\Sigma)d=0.22>0.
\]

This confirms positive curvature along the feasible line and, together with the quadratic structure, the unique equality-constrained global minimum.

Numerical tolerance should be documented; the analytical reference and floating-point output must be distinguished.

## 10. Twenty worked exercises

**1.** State an equality-constrained optimization problem.  
*Solution:* Minimize \(f(x)\) subject to \(h(x)=0\).

**2.** Define the Lagrangian using the plus convention.  
*Solution:* \(\mathcal L=f+\lambda^\mathsf Th\).

**3.** State primal feasibility.  
*Solution:* \(h(x^*)=0\).

**4.** State first-order stationarity.  
*Solution:* \(\nabla f+J_h^\mathsf T\lambda=0\).

**5.** What is a feasible tangent direction?  
*Solution:* A vector \(d\) satisfying \(J_h d=0\).

**6.** What does stationarity imply along feasible tangent directions?  
*Solution:* \(\nabla f^\mathsf Td=0\).

**7.** Give the portfolio budget equality.  
*Solution:* \(w_1+w_2-1=0\).

**8.** Differentiate \(w^\mathsf T\Sigma w\) for symmetric \(\Sigma\).  
*Solution:* \(2\Sigma w\).

**9.** State the portfolio Lagrangian.  
*Solution:* \(w^\mathsf T\Sigma w+\lambda(w_1+w_2-1)\).

**10.** Write the portfolio stationarity equation.  
*Solution:* \(2\Sigma w+\lambda\boldsymbol1=0\).

**11.** Give the general equality-constrained minimum-variance formula.  
*Solution:* \(\Sigma^{-1}\boldsymbol1/(\boldsymbol1^\mathsf T\Sigma^{-1}\boldsymbol1)\), assuming \(\Sigma\succ0\).

**12.** Find the first optimal weight.  
*Solution:* \(8/11\).

**13.** Find the second optimal weight.  
*Solution:* \(3/11\).

**14.** Find the minimum variance.  
*Solution:* \(7/220\).

**15.** Find the Lagrange multiplier.  
*Solution:* \(-7/110\).

**16.** Is the portfolio fully invested?  
*Solution:* Yes, \((8+3)/11=1\).

**17.** Find a tangent direction for the budget constraint.  
*Solution:* \(d=(1,-1)\).

**18.** Find its Lagrangian curvature.  
*Solution:* \(0.22\).

**19.** What is the marginal value \(V'(1)\) under the stated sign convention?  
*Solution:* \(7/110=-\lambda^*\).
