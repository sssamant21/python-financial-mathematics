## Learning objectives

Distinguish feasibility from optimality, independently reconstruct primal and dual residuals, use KKT and duality certificates with their correct assumptions, select transparent numerical tolerances, and report failures without overstating solver reliability.

## 1. Verification must be independent

A solver's success flag is not a proof that a financial allocation is feasible or optimal. Reconstruct the original objective and constraints from the model specification rather than trusting cached solver residuals. Separate **model correctness**, **primal feasibility**, **stationarity and dual feasibility**, **global-optimality assumptions**, and **implementation readiness**.

For a minimization problem

\[
\min f(x)\quad\text{subject to }h(x)=0,\quad g(x)\le0,
\]

define a Lagrangian \(\mathcal L=f+\lambda^\mathsf Th+\mu^\mathsf Tg\), with \(\mu\ge0\).

## 2. Primal feasibility residuals

For equality constraints, retain signed residuals \(r_h=h(x)\). For inequalities, retain signed residuals \(r_g=g(x)\), and compute positive violations \(\max(0,g_i(x))\). The nonnegative scalar summary

\[
r_{\mathrm{primal}}=\max\left(\|h(x)\|_\infty,\ \|\max(g(x),0)\|_\infty\right)
\]

is convenient, but the full vector is necessary for diagnosis. If there are no constraints of one kind, take that part's maximum as zero.

Use scaled residuals where model coefficients or units differ substantially. A tolerance of \(10^{-8}\) has different economic meaning for a dimensionless weight and a dollar-denominated cash constraint.

## 3. Stationarity, dual feasibility, and complementarity

The stationarity vector is

\[
r_s=\nabla f(x)+J_h(x)^\mathsf T\lambda+J_g(x)^\mathsf T\mu.
\]

Dual violation is \(\max_i\max(0,-\mu_i)\). Complementarity residuals are \(r_{c,i}=\mu_i g_i(x)\). A complete numerical KKT report contains the signed raw vectors and their norms, not just a Boolean pass/fail.

KKT necessity requires appropriate constraint qualifications. KKT sufficiency for global optimality holds for a convex differentiable objective with convex inequality functions and affine equalities when a feasible KKT certificate exists. In nonconvex problems, KKT conditions can identify saddles or local maxima.

## 4. Duality gap and independent bounds

For a primal maximization LP and its dual minimization LP, weak duality gives

\[
\text{primal objective}\le\text{dual objective}.
\]

If a primal-feasible and dual-feasible pair has equal objectives, it certifies global optimality. For a convex QP, a valid dual lower bound for minimization can similarly support an optimality certificate; simply subtracting two arbitrary objective values is not a meaningful duality gap.

Always confirm the direction of optimization and sign conventions before reporting a gap.

## 5. Main financial example

Minimize portfolio variance

\[
V(w)=w^\mathsf T
\begin{bmatrix}0.04&0.01\\0.01&0.09\end{bmatrix}w
\]

subject to \(w_1+w_2=1\), \(w_1,w_2\ge0\), and \(w_1\le0.6\).

The proposed solution and multipliers are

\[
w^*=(0.6,0.4),\quad\lambda^*=-0.084,\quad
(\mu_1,\mu_2,\mu_3)=(0,0,0.028).
\]

Recompute the objective independently:

\[
V(w^*)=0.04(0.6)^2+2(0.01)(0.6)(0.4)+0.09(0.4)^2=0.0336.
\]

The equality residual is zero. The signed inequality residuals, in the order \((-w_1,-w_2,w_1-0.6)\), are \((-0.6,-0.4,0)\). The gradient is \((0.056,0.084)\). The stationarity vector is

\[
(0.056,0.084)+(-0.084,-0.084)+(0.028,0)=(0,0).
\]

All multipliers are nonnegative and complementary products vanish. Since the covariance is positive definite and constraints are affine, this feasible KKT point is the unique global minimizer.

## 6. Detecting an incorrect candidate

Consider \(w=(0.7,0.3)\). It satisfies full investment and long-only bounds but violates the first-asset cap by \(0.1\). A low variance or a small gradient cannot make this candidate acceptable.

Consider \(w=(0.5,0.5)\) with all inequality multipliers zero and an arbitrary equality multiplier. This candidate is feasible but not generally stationary for the stated variance objective. Feasibility alone is not optimality.

## 7. Feasibility before optimization

For a long-only fully invested portfolio with only individual upper caps \(u_i\ge0\), feasibility requires \(\sum_i u_i\ge1\). If two assets have caps \(0.4\) and \(0.4\), no feasible allocation exists. Additional sector, exposure, or turnover constraints require a more comprehensive feasibility test.

An infeasible model must not be “fixed” by silently changing constraints or increasing tolerance. Document proposed relaxations and obtain an explicit modeling decision.

## 8. Tolerances and conditioning

Use separate tolerances for feasibility, stationarity, complementarity, and dual violations. Where practical, compare both absolute and appropriately scaled residuals. Record the floating-point precision, matrix conditioning, solver configuration, and original input values.

An apparently small residual may conceal poor conditioning; a high-precision result may still reflect incorrect assumptions. Do not round before checking constraints.

## 9. Independent implementation design

An independent checker should accept a candidate vector and multipliers rather than call the same optimizer. It should recompute constraints, gradients, complementarity, and the objective from explicit formulas. Its test suite should include correct solutions, deliberately infeasible points, feasible but nonstationary points, incorrect multiplier signs, invalid numerical inputs, and sensitivity checks.

The companion code is a pedagogical two-asset certificate, not a production optimization engine.

## 10. Twelve-step hands-on lab

1. Record the exact objective and all constraints.
2. Declare units, signs, and numerical tolerances.
3. Evaluate the proposed portfolio's equality residual.
4. Evaluate signed inequality residuals and positive violations.
5. Independently calculate variance.
6. Independently calculate the objective gradient.
7. Reconstruct stationarity from candidate multipliers.
8. Check dual feasibility and complementary slackness.
9. Verify positive definiteness of the covariance matrix.
10. Reject an intentionally cap-violating portfolio.
11. Reject a feasible but nonstationary portfolio.
12. Run the reference checks and preserve evidence for deferred book-wide QA.
