## Learning objectives

Define constrained optimization, distinguish equality and inequality constraints, classify feasible, infeasible, interior, boundary, and active points, connect convex feasible sets with portfolio allocation, and validate candidate allocations without confusing feasibility with optimality.

## 1. The constrained optimization problem

An unconstrained optimizer searches the entire parameter domain. A constrained optimizer searches only an admissible **feasible set**:

\[
\boxed{\min_{x\in\mathbb R^n}f(x)\quad\text{subject to}\quad h_j(x)=0,\ j=1,\ldots,p;\quad g_i(x)\le0,\ i=1,\ldots,m.}
\]

The feasible set is

\[
\mathcal F=\{x:h_j(x)=0\ \forall j,\ g_i(x)\le0\ \forall i\}.
\]

The sign convention matters: an upper bound \(x\le u\) is \(g(x)=x-u\le0\), while a lower bound \(x\ge l\) is \(g(x)=l-x\le0\). An infeasible point is not an admissible solution even if its objective value is attractive.

## 2. Equality constraints

Equality constraints require exact relationships in the mathematical model. A fully invested portfolio has

\[
\sum_{i=1}^n w_i=1.
\]

For two assets, \(w_1+w_2=1\) defines a line in \(\mathbb R^2\). A solver's finite-precision result is checked against a declared **feasibility tolerance** rather than by exact floating-point equality.

Equality constraints can reduce the degrees of freedom: substituting \(w_2=1-w_1\) reduces a two-variable problem to one variable. Such elimination must preserve all remaining bounds.

## 3. Inequality constraints

Common portfolio restrictions include

\[
w_i\ge0\quad\text{(long-only)},\qquad w_i\le u_i\quad\text{(position caps)}.
\]

A two-asset, fully invested, long-only portfolio satisfies \(w_1+w_2=1\) and \(w_1,w_2\ge0\). Its feasible set is the closed line segment from \((1,0)\) to \((0,1)\).

A position cap \(w_1\le0.6\) restricts the segment further. If both weights are capped at \(0.4\), the fully invested portfolio is infeasible because \(w_1+w_2\le0.8<1\).

## 4. Active and inactive constraints

For \(g_i(x)\le0\), the inequality is **active** at a feasible point when \(g_i(x)=0\); it is **inactive** when \(g_i(x)<0\). All equality constraints hold at every feasible point.

At \(w=(0.6,0.4)\), the cap \(w_1\le0.6\) is active, whereas the long-only lower bounds are inactive. At \(w=(1,0)\), the constraint \(w_2\ge0\) is active.

Activity alone does not prove optimality or a nonzero Lagrange multiplier. Later sections derive the necessary conditions under appropriate regularity assumptions.

## 5. Interior, boundary, and relative interior

A point is interior to \(\mathcal F\) if a small open ball around it remains feasible. Equality constraints commonly make the feasible set lower-dimensional, so its interior in the ambient space is empty.

For the fully invested two-asset segment, \((0.5,0.5)\) is **not** an interior point of \(\mathcal F\subset\mathbb R^2\), but it is in the **relative interior** of the segment. Its endpoints are relative-boundary points. This distinction matters when discussing feasible directions and constraint qualifications.

## 6. Convex and nonconvex feasible sets

A set \(\mathcal F\) is convex when \(\lambda x+(1-\lambda)y\in\mathcal F\) for every \(x,y\in\mathcal F\) and \(0\le\lambda\le1\).

Affine equality constraints, affine inequalities, and box bounds define convex sets; their intersection is convex. Thus a long-only fully invested portfolio with linear caps is convex, assuming the constraints are jointly feasible.

A cardinality rule such as “hold at most one of two assets” makes the set nonconvex: both \((1,0)\) and \((0,1)\) are permitted, but their midpoint \((0.5,0.5)\) is not.

A convex objective on a convex feasible set is a convex optimization problem. Convexity of the objective alone is insufficient when the feasible set is nonconvex.

## 7. Financial risk example

Let portfolio variance be

\[
V(w)=w^\mathsf T\Sigma w,\qquad
\Sigma=\begin{bmatrix}0.04&0.01\\0.01&0.09\end{bmatrix}.
\]

The matrix is positive definite because its first leading principal minor is \(0.04>0\) and determinant is \(0.04(0.09)-0.01^2=0.0035>0\). Hence variance is strictly convex.

The unconstrained minimizer is \(w=(0,0)\), which violates full investment. A constrained solution must minimize \(V\) over the feasible portfolio set. For a two-asset fully invested portfolio, substituting \(w_2=1-w_1\) gives

\[
V(w_1)=0.11w_1^2-0.16w_1+0.09.
\]

Its stationary point is \(w_1=0.16/0.22=8/11\approx0.72727\), \(w_2=3/11\approx0.27273\). Both are long-only, so this is the unique minimum on the full-investment simplex. If the first asset is capped at \(0.6\), the constrained optimum moves to \((0.6,0.4)\); the cap is active.

## 8. Feasibility is not optimality

A candidate portfolio can be feasible but suboptimal. For example, \((0.5,0.5)\) is feasible under long-only full investment, but it has higher variance than \((8/11,3/11)\) for the covariance matrix above.

Conversely, an unconstrained optimum can be infeasible. Check feasibility first, then objective comparisons or mathematical optimality conditions. Solver success messages are not substitutes for independent checks.

## 9. Numerical feasibility diagnostics

For equality residuals \(h_j(x)\), report \(\max_j|h_j(x)|\). For inequalities in \(g_i(x)\le0\) form, report the maximum positive violation \(\max_i\max(0,g_i(x))\), with zero when there are no inequalities.

These residuals can have different physical units. Use per-constraint tolerances or justified normalization rather than indiscriminately applying one tolerance to dollars, rates, and weights.

A tolerance-aware numerical check is not the same as mathematical exact feasibility. Preserve the raw residuals in the audit record.

## 10. Twelve-step hands-on lab

1. Write the objective and identify all decision variables.
2. Express full investment as an equality.
3. Express long-only bounds using the \(g_i\le0\) convention.
4. Add a first-asset position cap.
5. Test a feasible interior-of-segment candidate.
6. Test a feasible candidate with an active cap.
7. Test a candidate that violates full investment.
8. Test a candidate that violates long-only bounds.
9. Construct contradictory caps and explain infeasibility.
10. Evaluate portfolio variance and derive the unrestricted simplex minimizer.
11. Compare that minimizer with the capped solution and record residuals.
12. Run independent reference checks and preserve evidence for deferred QA.

## 11. Twenty worked exercises

**1.** Define a feasible set.  
*Solution:* All decision vectors satisfying every equality and inequality constraint.

**2.** Write a full-investment equality for three assets.  
*Solution:* \(w_1+w_2+w_3=1\).

**3.** Convert \(w_1\ge0\) to \(g\le0\) form.  
*Solution:* \(-w_1\le0\).

**4.** Convert \(w_1\le0.6\) to \(g\le0\) form.  
*Solution:* \(w_1-0.6\le0\).

**5.** Is \((0.5,0.5)\) long-only and fully invested?  
*Solution:* Yes.

**6.** Is \((0.8,0.2)\) feasible with \(w_1\le0.6\)?  
*Solution:* No.

**7.** Is \((0.6,0.4)\) feasible under that cap?  
*Solution:* Yes.

**8.** Which cap is active at \((0.6,0.4)\)?  
*Solution:* \(w_1\le0.6\).

**9.** Is \(w_2\ge0\) active at \((0.6,0.4)\)?  
*Solution:* No.

**10.** Is \(w_2\ge0\) active at \((1,0)\)?  
*Solution:* Yes.

**11.** Why are caps of \(0.4\) on both weights infeasible under full investment?  
*Solution:* Their maximum sum is \(0.8<1\).

**12.** Is the long-only fully invested two-asset set convex?  
*Solution:* Yes, it is a line segment.

**13.** Is the set containing only \((1,0)\) and \((0,1)\) convex?  
*Solution:* No, its midpoint is absent.

**14.** Is \((0.5,0.5)\) an ambient interior point of the simplex segment?  
*Solution:* No, but it is in the relative interior.

**15.** Find the determinant of the example covariance matrix.  
*Solution:* \(0.0035\).

**16.** Is the covariance matrix positive definite?  
*Solution:* Yes.

**17.** Find the fully invested minimum-variance first weight.  
*Solution:* \(8/11\).

**18.** Find the second weight.  
*Solution:* \(3/11\).

**19.** What is the first weight under an additional cap of \(0.6\)?  
*Solution:* \(0.6\).

**20.** What is the next section?  
*Solution:* 17.2 — Equality Constraints and Lagrange Multipliers.
