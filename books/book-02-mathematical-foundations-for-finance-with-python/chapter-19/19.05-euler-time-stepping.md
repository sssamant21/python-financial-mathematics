## Learning objectives

Derive forward Euler from the definition of a derivative; implement an initial-value solver; distinguish local truncation, global discretization, and floating-point error; analyze linear stability; compare simulated account balances against exact benchmarks; and report step sizes, units, and numerical limitations.

## 1. From derivatives to a discrete update

For an initial-value problem

\[
y'(t)=f(t,y(t)),\qquad y(t_0)=y_0,
\]

a first-order Taylor expansion gives

\[
y(t+h)=y(t)+h f(t,y(t))+O(h^2).
\]

Discarding the higher-order term produces **forward Euler**:

\[
\boxed{t_{n+1}=t_n+h,\qquad y_{n+1}=y_n+h f(t_n,y_n)}.
\]

The method uses the slope at the beginning of each step. For a fixed horizon \(T\), select a positive integer \(N\) and use \(h=(T-t_0)/N\). The update must preserve consistent units: \(h f\) has the units of the state.

## 2. Local and global error

For a sufficiently smooth exact solution, the **one-step local truncation error** is \(O(h^2)\). Over a fixed finite interval, under suitable regularity and stability assumptions, the accumulated **global discretization error** is \(O(h)\).

If halving \(h\) approximately halves the global error, the numerical evidence is consistent with first-order convergence. This observation is not a universal guarantee: rounding, nonsmooth events, instability, and model misspecification can dominate.

## 3. Financial account model

Consider a balance \(A(t)\) in currency units with continuously compounded rate \(r\) in inverse years and net continuous cash-flow rate \(u\) in currency per year:

\[
A'=rA+u,\qquad A(0)=A_0.
\]

Euler gives

\[
\boxed{A_{n+1}=A_n+h(rA_n+u)}.
\]

For constant \(r,u\), the exact solution is

\[
A(t)=A_0e^{rt}+u\frac{\operatorname{expm1}(rt)}{r}
\]

when \(r\ne0\), and \(A(t)=A_0+ut\) when \(r=0\). This exact solution is an independent benchmark for the Euler implementation.

## 4. Pure growth example

With \(A_0=1000\), \(r=0.05\) per year, and \(T=2\) years, Euler gives

\[
A_N=1000\left(1+\frac{0.1}{N}\right)^N,
\]

while the exact value is

\[
A(2)=1000e^{0.1}\approx1105.170918.
\]

For positive \(r\) and finite \(N\), Euler underestimates exponential growth because \((1+x/N)^N<e^x\) for \(x>0\). The approximation improves as \(N\) increases.

## 5. Linear stability

Consider the test equation

\[
y'=\lambda y,\qquad \lambda<0.
\]

Its exact solution decays to zero. Euler produces

\[
y_{n+1}=(1+h\lambda)y_n.
\]

Numerical decay requires

\[
\boxed{|1+h\lambda|<1},
\]

equivalently \(0<h<-2/\lambda\). For \(1+h\lambda<0\), numerical iterates alternate signs even though the exact positive solution does not.

**Example:** For \(\lambda=-2\) per year, Euler is asymptotically stable for \(0<h<1\) year. At \(h=1\), its amplification factor is \(-1\), so the numerical sequence oscillates without decaying.

The stability condition is about the numerical method, not the economic stability of a portfolio.

## 6. Positivity and financial constraints

For pure decay \(A'=-kA\) with \(A_0>0\), Euler updates \(A_{n+1}=(1-kh)A_n\). Nonnegative balances require \(kh\le1\), which is stricter than the stability condition \(kh<2\).

With fixed withdrawals, Euler may cross below zero between steps. If negative balances are prohibited, the model requires event detection or a documented boundary rule; silently clamping every negative iterate can hide numerical and modeling errors. Section 19.7 develops these issues.

## 7. Step-size studies

A useful convergence study compares \(N\), \(2N\), and \(4N\) steps on the same horizon. Report the step size, final balance, signed error, absolute error, and error ratio. When both errors are well above rounding noise, a ratio near two is consistent with first-order convergence.

A step-size study is not a substitute for independent reference solutions or sensitivity analysis of model parameters.

## 8. Time-dependent rates and flows

For

\[
A'=r(t)A+u(t),
\]

Euler evaluates the rate and cash-flow functions at the **left endpoint** of each interval:

\[
A_{n+1}=A_n+h[r(t_n)A_n+u(t_n)].
\]

Abrupt changes in cash-flow rates must be aligned with step boundaries or handled explicitly. Lump-sum cash flows are jump events, not ordinary smooth contributions.

## 9. Floating-point and diagnostic reliability

Record the step count, actual step size, final state, whether all values are finite, and any breached financial constraints. Reject negative horizons, nonpositive or noninteger step counts, and nonfinite inputs. Large step counts can accumulate floating-point error and computational cost.

Euler is an educational baseline, not a general-purpose production integrator. Higher-order and adaptive methods appear in Section 19.6.

## 11. Twenty worked exercises

**1.** State the forward Euler formula.  
*Solution:* \(y_{n+1}=y_n+h f(t_n,y_n)\).

**2.** What slope does Euler use?  
*Solution:* The slope at the beginning of the step.

**3.** How is \(h\) chosen for \(N\) equal steps over \([0,T]\)?  
*Solution:* \(h=T/N\).

**4.** What is Euler's local truncation error order?  
*Solution:* \(O(h^2)\) under sufficient smoothness.

**5.** What is its global discretization error order?  
*Solution:* \(O(h)\) under standard regularity and stability assumptions.

**6.** Write Euler for \(A'=rA+u\).  
*Solution:* \(A_{n+1}=A_n+h(rA_n+u)\).

**7.** What units does \(h u\) have?  
*Solution:* Currency units.

**8.** State the exact pure-growth solution.  
*Solution:* \(A=A_0e^{rt}\).

**9.** State the \(N\)-step pure-growth Euler result.  
*Solution:* \(A_N=A_0(1+rT/N)^N\).

**10.** Does Euler overestimate positive exponential growth?  
*Solution:* No; for positive \(r\), it underestimates at finite positive \(N\).

**11.** Give Euler's amplification factor for \(y'=\lambda y\).  
*Solution:* \(1+h\lambda\).

**12.** State the asymptotic numerical stability condition.  
*Solution:* \(|1+h\lambda|<1\).

**13.** For \(\lambda=-2\), what stable step-size range applies?  
*Solution:* \(0<h<1\).

**14.** What happens at \(h=1\) for \(\lambda=-2\)?  
*Solution:* The amplification factor is \(-1\), causing undamped alternating iterates.

**15.** What positivity condition applies to \(A'=-kA\)?  
*Solution:* \(kh\le1\) for nonnegative Euler iterates from nonnegative states.

**16.** What does a global error ratio near two indicate when halving \(h\)?  
*Solution:* Behavior consistent with first-order convergence.

**17.** How are time-varying inputs sampled by forward Euler?  
*Solution:* At the left endpoint \(t_n\).

**18.** Can Euler handle an instantaneous lump sum as a smooth rate without an event rule?  
*Solution:* Not faithfully; an explicit jump is needed.

**19.** What status should a nonfinite trajectory produce?  
*Solution:* An explicit numerical failure, not a valid balance.

**20.** What comes next?

## 12. Applied problems

**A. Growth convergence:** Simulate \(A'=0.05A\), \(A_0=1000\), \(T=2\) with \(N=10,20,40\). Compare against \(1000e^{0.1}\).

**B. Contributions:** Simulate \(A'=0.04A+200\) over five years and compare with the exact integrating-factor solution.

**C. Decay stability:** For \(y'=-2y\), compare \(h=0.25\), \(0.75\), and \(1.25\); explain monotone decay, alternating decay, and growth in magnitude.

**D. Positivity:** For \(A'=-3A\), \(A_0=100\), determine step sizes that preserve nonnegative Euler balances and those that create negative iterates.

**E. Model audit:** Explain why smaller step sizes do not correct an incorrect rate convention, omitted deposits, or an unmodeled withdrawal event.
