## 1. Why go beyond Euler?

For the initial-value problem

\[
y'(t)=f(t,y(t)),\qquad y(t_0)=y_0,
\]

forward Euler evaluates one slope per step and has global error of order \(O(h)\) under standard smoothness assumptions. Higher-order Runge–Kutta methods combine several slope evaluations to improve accuracy at practical step sizes.

A numerical solver approximates a *specified mathematical model*. Greater solver precision cannot repair incorrect cash-flow timing, an inconsistent interest-rate convention, or an invalid financial assumption.

## 2. Classical fourth-order Runge–Kutta

For step size \(h>0\), define

\[
\begin{aligned}
k_1&=f(t_n,y_n),\\
k_2&=f(t_n+h/2,y_n+hk_1/2),\\
k_3&=f(t_n+h/2,y_n+hk_2/2),\\
k_4&=f(t_n+h,y_n+hk_3).
\end{aligned}
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{y_{n+1}=y_n+\frac{h}{6}(k_1+2k_2+2k_3+k_4)}.
\]

Classical RK4 has local truncation error \(O(h^5)\) and global error \(O(h^4)\) for sufficiently smooth solutions on a fixed interval under appropriate stability assumptions. It normally uses four right-hand-side evaluations per step.

## 3. Financial balance model

Consider

\[
A'=r(t)A+u(t),\qquad A(0)=A_0,
\]

where \(A\) is currency, \(t\) is years, \(r(t)\) is inverse years, and \(u(t)\) is currency per year. RK4 samples the right-hand side at four intermediate states and times; it is not simply four Euler steps.

For constant \(r,u\), the exact analytical benchmark is

\[
A(T)=A_0e^{rT}+u\frac{\operatorname{expm1}(rT)}{r}
\]

for \(r\ne0\), and \(A(T)=A_0+uT\) for \(r=0\). Use this to compare Euler, RK4, and adaptive approximations.

## 4. Fixed-step convergence

For a fixed horizon, run RK4 with \(N\), \(2N\), and \(4N\) steps. When discretization error dominates roundoff and the asymptotic regime has been reached, halving the step size should reduce global error by approximately \(2^4=16\).

This expected ratio is conditional, not a certification rule. Near machine precision, observed ratios become unreliable; discontinuities or insufficiently resolved dynamics may also prevent fourth-order behavior.

## 5. Adaptive time stepping

A fixed step size may waste work in smooth regions or miss rapidly changing dynamics. Adaptive methods estimate local error and choose a new step size.

An **embedded Runge–Kutta pair** computes two approximations of different orders using related stage evaluations. Their difference estimates the local error. A commonly used approach is an embedded 5(4) pair such as Dormand–Prince, as available through SciPy's \`solve_ivp(method="RK45")\`.

A simple educational alternative uses **step doubling** with RK4: compare one full step of length \(h\) with two half steps. If the local RK4 error is approximately \(C h^5\), then

\[
y_{\mathrm{half,half}}-y_{\mathrm{full}}\approx -\frac{15}{16}C h^5.
\]

Thus \(|y_{\mathrm{half,half}}-y_{\mathrm{full}}|/15\) estimates the error in the two-half-step approximation. The sign and coefficient are meaningful only in the smooth asymptotic regime.

## 6. Absolute and relative tolerances

An error-control scale typically combines absolute and relative tolerances:

\[
\mathrm{scale}=\mathrm{atol}+\mathrm{rtol}\max(|y_n|,|y_{n+1}|).
\]

A trial is accepted when the estimated local error is no larger than the scale. The absolute tolerance has units of the state; relative tolerance is dimensionless.

For a financial balance, a numerical tolerance of \(10^{-6}\) currency units does not mean the underlying economic forecast is accurate to that amount. Tolerances control numerical approximation, not data quality or model uncertainty.

## 7. Step-size adaptation

For an error estimate behaving approximately like \(h^5\), a controller can use

\[
h_{\mathrm{new}}=h\,s\left(\frac{\mathrm{scale}}{\mathrm{error}}\right)^{1/5},
\]

with safety factor \(0<s<1\) and lower/upper growth clamps. A zero estimated error permits a bounded increase rather than division by zero.

Rejected steps do not advance the simulated time. Enforce maximum accepted and rejected step budgets, a minimum representable step, and a finite-value policy. A solver must report failure explicitly when it cannot satisfy its requested tolerance.

## 8. Diagnostics and reliability

A useful solver result records method, status, final time, final state, accepted and rejected steps, right-hand-side evaluations, absolute and relative tolerances, and any failure reason.

For step-doubling RK4, one attempted trial evaluates RK4 three times (one full and two half steps), typically twelve right-hand-side calls. This educational implementation does not reuse stages or provide the same efficiency as a production embedded RK pair.

## 9. Stiffness and limitations

Explicit RK4 and RK45 can require very small steps for stiff systems, even when the desired solution varies slowly. In SciPy, implicit methods such as \`Radau\` or \`BDF\` may be more appropriate for stiff problems, after validating the model and solver settings.

Abrupt contribution changes, lump-sum events, and depletion boundaries require event-aware treatment. Adaptive error control alone does not guarantee that a discontinuity or event is located accurately. Section 19.7 develops event and boundary handling.

## 11. Twenty worked exercises

**1.** What is RK4's global order under standard smoothness and stability assumptions?  
*Solution:* Fourth order, \(O(h^4)\).

**2.** How many slope evaluations does classical RK4 use per step?  
*Solution:* Four.

**3.** State \(k_1\).  
*Solution:* \(f(t_n,y_n)\).

**4.** State \(k_2\).  
*Solution:* \(f(t_n+h/2,y_n+hk_1/2)\).

**5.** State \(k_3\).  
*Solution:* \(f(t_n+h/2,y_n+hk_2/2)\).

**6.** State \(k_4\).  
*Solution:* \(f(t_n+h,y_n+hk_3)\).

**7.** Give the RK4 weighted update.  
*Solution:* \(y_{n+1}=y_n+h(k_1+2k_2+2k_3+k_4)/6\).

**8.** What is the local RK4 error order?  
*Solution:* \(O(h^5)\).

**9.** What is the expected error reduction on halving \(h\) in the asymptotic regime?  
*Solution:* Approximately sixteenfold.

**10.** Why might this ratio fail near machine precision?  
*Solution:* Floating-point rounding can dominate discretization error.

**11.** What is an embedded RK pair?  
*Solution:* Two approximations of differing orders sharing stage evaluations for error estimation.

**12.** What is RK4 step doubling?  
*Solution:* Compare one full step with two half steps over the same interval.

**13.** What does the difference divided by 15 approximate?  
*Solution:* The leading local error of the two-half-step RK4 result.

**14.** State a common error scale.  
*Solution:* \(\mathrm{atol}+\mathrm{rtol}\max(|y_n|,|y_{n+1}|)\).

**15.** What units does \(\mathrm{atol}\) have?  
*Solution:* The units of the state variable.

**16.** What happens to time after a rejected step?  
*Solution:* It does not advance.

**17.** What is a typical controller exponent for RK4 step doubling?  
*Solution:* \(1/5\).

**18.** Name one stiff ODE solver available in SciPy.  
*Solution:* \`Radau\` or \`BDF\`.

**19.** Does a small numerical error imply a correct financial model?  
*Solution:* No.

*Solution:* 19.7 — Depletion, Events, and Boundary Rules.
