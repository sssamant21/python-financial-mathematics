## Chapter synthesis

This final chapter section consolidates initial-value problems, separable and linear first-order equations, equilibria, local stability, Euler time stepping, Runge–Kutta methods, adaptive error control, depletion events, scheduled transactions, and discrete-versus-continuous financial comparisons.

**Core model:**

\[
A'(t)=r(t)A(t)+u(t),\qquad A(0)=A_0.
\]

The state \(A\) has currency units; \(r\) has inverse-time units; and \(u\) has currency-per-time units. A mathematical solution is not automatically an economically valid prediction.

## 1. Reference formulas

For constant \(r,u\),

\[
A(t)=
\begin{cases}
A_0e^{rt}+u\,\dfrac{\operatorname{expm1}(rt)}{r},&r\ne0,\\
A_0+ut,&r=0.
\end{cases}
\]

For fixed withdrawals \(u=-w\), \(w>0\), the depletion time for \(A_0>0\) is

\[
t_*=
\begin{cases}
-\dfrac{\ln(1-rA_0/w)}{r},&r\ne0,\ 1-rA_0/w>0,\\
A_0/w,&r=0.
\end{cases}
\]

If \(r>0\) and \(w\le rA_0\), there is no finite-time depletion. A lump-sum transaction at time \(t_j\) changes the state according to \(A(t_j^+)=A(t_j^-)+J_j\).

For an autonomous scalar ODE \(x'=f(x)\), an equilibrium \(x_*\) satisfies \(f(x_*)=0\). A negative derivative \(f'(x_*)\) implies local asymptotic stability, a positive derivative implies instability, and a zero derivative requires additional analysis.

Forward Euler:

\[
y_{n+1}=y_n+h f(t_n,y_n).
\]

Classical RK4:

\[
\begin{aligned}
k_1&=f(t_n,y_n),\\
k_2&=f(t_n+h/2,y_n+hk_1/2),\\
k_3&=f(t_n+h/2,y_n+hk_2/2),\\
k_4&=f(t_n+h,y_n+hk_3),\\
y_{n+1}&=y_n+\frac h6(k_1+2k_2+2k_3+k_4).
\end{aligned}
\]

Under appropriate smoothness and stability assumptions, Euler has global error \(O(h)\) and RK4 has global error \(O(h^4)\).

## 2. Twenty worked exercises with solutions

**Exercise 1 — Initial condition.** Solve \(A'=0.05A\), \(A(0)=1000\).  
**Solution:** \(A(t)=1000e^{0.05t}\).

**Exercise 2 — Separable equation.** Solve \(x'=-3x\), \(x(0)=2\).  
**Solution:** \(x(t)=2e^{-3t}\).

**Exercise 3 — Constant contributions.** Solve \(A'=0.04A+200\), \(A(0)=1000\).  
**Solution:** \(A(t)=1000e^{0.04t}+200\operatorname{expm1}(0.04t)/0.04\).

**Exercise 4 — Zero-rate limit.** Find \(A(5)\) when \(A'=200\), \(A(0)=1000\).  
**Solution:** \(A(5)=2000\).

**Exercise 5 — Mean reversion.** Solve \(A'=0.5(1000-A)\), \(A(0)=1200\).  
**Solution:** \(A(t)=1000+200e^{-0.5t}\).

**Exercise 6 — Half-life.** Calculate the deviation half-life for \(\kappa=0.5\) per year.  
**Solution:** \(t_{1/2}=\ln2/0.5\approx1.38629\) years.

**Exercise 7 — Equilibrium.** Find the equilibrium of \(A'=0.04A-100\).  
**Solution:** \(A_*=100/0.04=2500\).

**Exercise 8 — Stability.** Classify that equilibrium.  
**Solution:** Since \(f'(A)=0.04>0\), it is unstable.

**Exercise 9 — Logistic equilibria.** For \(A'=0.3A(1-A/1000)\), find equilibria.  
**Solution:** \(A_*=0\) and \(A_*=1000\).

**Exercise 10 — Logistic stability.** Classify the two equilibria.  
**Solution:** \(f'(0)=0.3>0\), so zero is unstable; \(f'(1000)=-0.3<0\), so 1000 is asymptotically stable.

**Exercise 11 — Nonhyperbolic example.** Classify \(x'= -x^3\) at zero.  
**Solution:** The derivative test is inconclusive, but phase-line arrows point toward zero on both sides, so zero is asymptotically stable.

**Exercise 12 — Euler step.** With \(A'=0.05A\), \(A_0=1000\), \(h=1\), compute one step.  
**Solution:** \(A_1=1000+1(0.05)(1000)=1050\).

**Exercise 13 — Euler stability.** For \(y'=-2y\), find stable positive step sizes.  
**Solution:** \(|1-2h|<1\), hence \(0<h<1\).

**Exercise 14 — RK4 accuracy.** State the expected global order for classical RK4.  
**Solution:** Fourth order under standard smoothness and stability conditions.

**Exercise 15 — Error refinement.** What RK4 error ratio is expected when \(h\) is halved?  
**Solution:** Approximately 16 in the asymptotic regime before roundoff dominates.

**Exercise 16 — Adaptive tolerances.** State a typical scalar error scale.  
**Solution:** \(\mathrm{atol}+\mathrm{rtol}\max(|y_n|,|y_{n+1}|)\).

**Exercise 17 — Depletion.** Find depletion time for \(A_0=1000\), \(r=0.04\), \(w=100\).  
**Solution:** \(t_*=-\ln(0.6)/0.04\approx12.77064\) years.

**Exercise 18 — No depletion.** Determine whether \(A_0=1000\), \(r=0.04\), \(w=40\) depletes.  
**Solution:** No; \(w=rA_0\), so the account remains at 1000.

**Exercise 19 — Scheduled deposit.** An account begins at 1000, grows continuously at 5%, and receives 200 at year 2. Find its year-5 balance.  
**Solution:** \(A(5)=1000e^{0.25}+200e^{0.15}\).

**Exercise 20 — Continuous/discrete equivalence.** Convert nominal annual rate \(j=0.06\), compounded monthly, to the equivalent continuously compounded annual rate.  
**Solution:** \(r_c=12\log(1+0.06/12)\). The resulting full-year balances match when the same initial balance is used.

## 4. Applied assessment problems

**Problem A — Growth and withdrawals.** Model \(A'=0.04A-100\), \(A(0)=1000\). Derive the exact balance, identify the unstable equilibrium, compute depletion time, and explain why allowing negative balances would require a separate policy.

**Problem B — Numerical comparison.** Simulate \(A'=0.05A\), \(A_0=1000\), over two years using Euler and RK4 at 10, 20, and 40 steps. Compare against \(1000e^{0.1}\) and discuss first- and fourth-order convergence.

**Problem C — Target adjustment.** For \(A'=0.6(5000-A)\), \(A_0=3000\), derive the solution, determine equilibrium stability, and compute the deviation half-life.

**Problem D — Jump timing.** Compare a deposit of 500 at year 1 with the same deposit at year 3 for a continuously growing account over five years. Keep all other assumptions fixed.

**Problem E — Verification versus validation.** Construct a checklist distinguishing analytical correctness, numerical accuracy, financial assumptions, input conventions, and independent review.

## 5. Failure modes and audit questions

A complete solution must explain why a model can fail even when a numerical solver returns successfully. Review the following risks: nonfinite values; negative horizons; inconsistent time units; confusing nominal and continuous rates; incorrect deposit timing; missed depletion events; applying a future deposit after terminal depletion; RK4 steps that are too large; unstable Euler steps; and misleading relative errors near zero.

**Review questions:** Does the model use continuous flows or discrete jumps? Is the state domain explicit? Are terminal events and simultaneous events ordered? Are analytical benchmarks independent of the implementation? Were tests actually executed? Has an independent reviewer approved the model?
