## Learning objectives

Recognize first-order linear ODEs, derive integrating-factor solutions, solve financial balance equations with continuous contributions or withdrawals, handle time-varying rates, preserve units and initial conditions, evaluate zero-rate limits stably, and compare analytical solutions with independent numerical approximations.

## 1. Standard form

A first-order linear ODE has the form

\[
y'(t)+p(t)y(t)=q(t),\qquad y(t_0)=y_0.
\]

The unknown \(y\) and its derivative appear to the first power; coefficients depend on time but not on \(y\). The equation is *inhomogeneous* when \(q\ne0\), and *homogeneous* when \(q=0\).

Unlike a separable equation, the general linear equation can contain both a state-dependent growth term and an additive external input.

## 2. Integrating-factor derivation

Define

\[
\mu(t)=\exp\left(\int_{t_0}^t p(s)\,ds\right).
\]

Because \(\mu'(t)=p(t)\mu(t)\), multiplying the ODE by \(\mu\) gives

\[
\frac{d}{dt}\bigl[\mu(t)y(t)\bigr]=\mu(t)q(t).
\]

Integrate from \(t_0\) to \(t\), using \(\mu(t_0)=1\):

\[
\boxed{y(t)=\mu(t)^{-1}\left[y_0+\int_{t_0}^t\mu(s)q(s)\,ds\right]}.
\]

This expression directly satisfies the initial condition and is the general integrating-factor solution when the coefficients are sufficiently regular.

## 3. Financial account with net cash flows

Let \(A(t)\) be a balance in currency units, \(r(t)\) a continuously compounded rate per year, and \(u(t)\) a net contribution rate in currency per year:

\[
A'(t)=r(t)A(t)+u(t),\qquad A(0)=A_0.
\]

Write \(A'-r(t)A=u(t)\), so \(p(t)=-r(t)\). With

\[
R(t)=\int_0^t r(s)\,ds,
\]

the integrating factor is \(e^{-R(t)}\), and

\[
\boxed{A(t)=e^{R(t)}\left[A_0+\int_0^t e^{-R(s)}u(s)\,ds\right]}.
\]

A positive \(u\) represents deposits; a negative \(u\) represents withdrawals. These are *continuous rates*, not lump sums.

## 4. Constant rate and constant cash flow

For constant \(r\) and \(u\),

\[
A(t)=A_0e^{rt}+u\frac{e^{rt}-1}{r},\quad r\ne0.
\]

At \(r=0\),

\[
\boxed{A(t)=A_0+ut}.
\]

For numerical stability near \(rt=0\), compute \(e^{rt}-1\) with \`math.expm1(rt)\`. The zero-rate formula is the continuous limit, not an arbitrary special case.

**Worked example:** Starting with \(A_0=1000\), continuously compounded \(r=0.04\) per year, and \(u=200\) currency units per year over \(T=5\) years,

\[
A(5)=1000e^{0.2}+200\frac{e^{0.2}-1}{0.04}.
\]

The interest term and contribution term both have currency units.

## 5. Constant withdrawals and depletion

With \(u=-w\), where \(w>0\) is a fixed withdrawal rate in currency per year,

\[
A(t)=A_0e^{rt}-w\frac{e^{rt}-1}{r}.
\]

For \(r>0\) and \(w>rA_0\), the account reaches zero at

\[
\boxed{t_*=\frac{1}{r}\ln\left(\frac{w}{w-rA_0}\right)}.
\]

If \(w=rA_0\), the balance remains constant. If \(0<w<rA_0\), it grows and never depletes under this idealized model. For \(r=0\), depletion occurs at \(t_*=A_0/w\). A financial simulator should stop or apply a documented boundary rule at zero if negative balances are disallowed.

## 6. Time-varying rate example

For \(r(t)=a+bt\) and no external flows,

\[
A(t)=A_0e^{at+bt^2/2}.
\]

For constant \(u\ne0\), the solution becomes

\[
A(t)=e^{at+bt^2/2}\left[A_0+u\int_0^t e^{-as-bs^2/2}\,ds\right].
\]

The remaining integral may require numerical quadrature. An exact integrating-factor representation does not always mean an elementary closed-form solution.

## 7. Piecewise cash flows

A contribution rate may change at a known date. Solve the equation on each interval, carrying the final balance of one interval forward as the next interval's initial condition. With no lump-sum event, the balance remains continuous at the change point even if its derivative jumps.

A lump-sum transfer instead requires an explicit jump condition, such as \(A(t^+)=A(t^-)+J\); it is not represented by an ordinary finite continuous contribution rate at a single instant.

## 8. Exact transition and Euler comparison

For constant \(r,u\), the exact transition over a step \(h\) is

\[
A(t+h)=e^{rh}A(t)+u\frac{\operatorname{expm1}(rh)}{r}
\]

for \(r\ne0\), with \(A(t+h)=A(t)+uh\) for \(r=0\).

Forward Euler approximates

\[
A_{n+1}=A_n+h(rA_n+u).
\]

The exact transition is a strong analytical benchmark for this constant-coefficient model; Euler generally incurs time-discretization error. The rate convention and step units must match.

## 9. Mathematical verification

For constant \(r,u\), differentiate

\[
A(t)=A_0e^{rt}+u\frac{e^{rt}-1}{r}
\]

to obtain

\[
A'(t)=rA_0e^{rt}+ue^{rt}=rA(t)+u.
\]

Also verify \(A(0)=A_0\). For time-varying rates, differentiate the integrating-factor expression using the product rule and Fundamental Theorem of Calculus.

## 11. Twenty worked exercises

**1.** State the standard linear first-order form.  
*Solution:* \(y'+p(t)y=q(t)\).

**2.** What makes this equation linear?  
*Solution:* \(y\) and \(y'\) appear only to the first power with coefficients independent of \(y\).

**3.** Give its integrating factor.  
*Solution:* \(\mu=e^{\int p(t)\,dt}\).

**4.** State the product derivative identity.  
*Solution:* \((\mu y)'=\mu q\).

**5.** State the IVP solution with \(\mu(t_0)=1\).  
*Solution:* \(y(t)=\mu(t)^{-1}[y_0+\int_{t_0}^t\mu(s)q(s)\,ds]\).

**6.** Write the financial balance ODE.  
*Solution:* \(A'=r(t)A+u(t)\).

**7.** What are the units of \(u\)?  
*Solution:* Currency per year.

**8.** What is the integrating factor for \(A'-r(t)A=u(t)\)?  
*Solution:* \(e^{-\int_0^t r(s)\,ds}\).

**9.** State the general financial solution.  
*Solution:* \(A=e^{R(t)}[A_0+\int_0^t e^{-R(s)}u(s)\,ds]\).

**10.** Give the constant-rate, constant-flow solution.  
*Solution:* \(A=A_0e^{rt}+u(e^{rt}-1)/r\) for \(r\ne0\).

**11.** Give the zero-rate solution.  
*Solution:* \(A=A_0+ut\).

**12.** Why use \`expm1\`?  
*Solution:* It avoids cancellation when evaluating \(e^{rt}-1\) near zero.

**13.** State the constant-withdrawal ODE.  
*Solution:* \(A'=rA-w\).

**14.** When does positive-rate depletion occur?  
*Solution:* When \(w>rA_0\).

**15.** Give its depletion time.  
*Solution:* \(t_*=\ln[w/(w-rA_0)]/r\).

**16.** Give the zero-rate depletion time.  
*Solution:* \(A_0/w\) for \(A_0,w>0\).

**17.** Solve the no-flow model with \(r(t)=a+bt\).  
*Solution:* \(A=A_0e^{at+bt^2/2}\).

**18.** What happens to balance at a change in contribution rate?  
*Solution:* It remains continuous absent a lump-sum jump.

**19.** Give the forward Euler step.  
*Solution:* \(A_{n+1}=A_n+h(rA_n+u)\).
