## Learning objectives

Explain ordinary differential equations (ODEs), state variables, initial conditions, units, and solution trajectories; derive simple financial balance equations; distinguish continuous rates from annual effective rates; solve constant-coefficient growth and cash-flow models; and compare analytical solutions against independently derived discrete approximations.

## 1. From a balance to a rate of change

Let \(A(t)\) be an account balance in currency units, with time \(t\) measured in years. The derivative \(A'(t)=dA/dt\) has units of currency per year. A differential equation relates this derivative to the state, time, and external inputs:

\[
\frac{dA}{dt}=f(t,A(t)).
\]

An **ordinary differential equation** has derivatives with respect to one independent variable. Its **order** is the highest derivative present. The function \(A(t)\) is an unknown trajectory; the equation describes its evolution rather than giving one value immediately.

## 2. Initial-value problems

An initial-value problem (IVP) consists of the differential equation and an initial condition:

\[
A'(t)=f(t,A(t)),\qquad A(0)=A_0.
\]

The initial condition selects a trajectory from a family of possible solutions. For \(A'=rA\), the general solution is \(A(t)=Ce^{rt}\); specifying \(A(0)=A_0\) fixes \(C=A_0\).

A solution need not exist or be unique for arbitrary \(f\). Continuity and suitable local Lipschitz conditions in the state variable are common sufficient conditions for local existence and uniqueness. A financial simulation must also enforce its domain and business constraints.

## 3. Continuous account growth

If the instantaneous growth rate is constant at \(r\) per year,

\[
A'(t)=rA(t),\qquad A(0)=A_0.
\]

Separating variables for nonzero \(A\) gives \(dA/A=r\,dt\). Integrating yields \(\ln|A|=rt+C\), and therefore

\[
\boxed{A(t)=A_0e^{rt}}.
\]

The zero initial balance produces the identically zero solution. Positive \(r\) produces exponential growth; negative \(r\) produces exponential decay. The rate \(r\) is **continuously compounded**.

**Example:** With \(A_0=1000\), \(r=0.05\) per year, and \(t=2\), the balance is \(1000e^{0.1}\approx1105.170918\).

## 4. Continuous versus annual effective compounding

A continuously compounded rate \(r_c\) and an annual effective rate \(r_e\) are equivalent over one year when

\[
e^{r_c}=1+r_e,\qquad r_c=\ln(1+r_e),\quad r_e=e^{r_c}-1.
\]

Do not substitute a quoted annual effective rate directly into \(A'=rA\) without converting its convention. In the example, 5% continuously compounded is **not** the same as 5% annually effective.

## 5. External cash flows

Let \(u(t)\) be the net cash-flow rate into the account in currency per year. Then

\[
A'(t)=rA(t)+u(t),\qquad A(0)=A_0.
\]

A positive \(u\) is a deposit rate; a negative \(u\) is a withdrawal rate. The equation assumes continuous flows, not isolated lump-sum transfers.

For constant \(r\) and \(u\),

\[
\boxed{A(t)=A_0e^{rt}+u\frac{e^{rt}-1}{r}},\qquad r\ne0.
\]

For \(r=0\), \(A(t)=A_0+ut\). Numerically, use \(\operatorname{expm1}(rt)\) for small \(|rt|\), avoiding cancellation in \(e^{rt}-1\).

For general integrable \(u(t)\), an integrating factor gives

\[
A(t)=e^{rt}\left[A_0+\int_0^t e^{-rs}u(s)\,ds\right].
\]

## 6. Depletion and financial constraints

For \(A_0=1000\), \(r=0\), and withdrawals of 100 currency units per year, \(u=-100\):

\[
A(t)=1000-100t.
\]

The mathematical trajectory reaches zero at \(t=10\). If negative balances are prohibited, the physical model must stop, clamp, or change dynamics at depletion; continuing the unconstrained ODE into negative balances may be inappropriate. Events and boundary rules receive dedicated treatment in Section 19.7.

## 7. Discrete-time comparison

A forward Euler approximation over step \(h\) is

\[
A_{n+1}=A_n+h(rA_n+u).
\]

For pure growth, \(A_n=A_0(1+rh)^n\), which approaches \(A_0e^{rt}\) as \(h=t/n\to0\). This is a convergence statement for fixed \(t\) under the model assumptions, not an exact equality for finite \(h\).

The exact one-step transition for constant \(r,u\) is

\[
A(t+h)=e^{rh}A(t)+u\frac{e^{rh}-1}{r}.
\]

It provides an independent benchmark for numerical time stepping.

## 8. Assumptions, units, and limitations

- \(t\): years; \(A(t)\): currency; \(r\): inverse years; \(u\): currency per year.
- The account model is deterministic; stochastic investment returns are outside this section.
- Continuous deposits and withdrawals are not equivalent to end-of-period lump sums.
- A model may be mathematically well posed while economically unrealistic.
- Floating-point outputs are approximations; analytical formulas serve as reference benchmarks, not evidence of production readiness.

## 10. Twenty worked exercises

**1.** What is an ODE?  
*Solution:* An equation involving derivatives of an unknown function with respect to one independent variable.

**2.** What is a state variable?  
*Solution:* A quantity whose evolution the model describes, such as account balance.

**3.** What are the units of \(A'(t)\)?  
*Solution:* Currency per year.

**4.** What are the units of \(r\) in \(A'=rA\)?  
*Solution:* Inverse years.

**5.** State the initial condition for an account starting at 1000.  
*Solution:* \(A(0)=1000\).

**6.** Solve \(A'=rA\) with \(A(0)=A_0\).  
*Solution:* \(A=A_0e^{rt}\).

**7.** What if \(A_0=0\) and there are no cash flows?  
*Solution:* \(A(t)=0\) for all \(t\).

**8.** What if \(r=0\) and \(u=0\)?  
*Solution:* \(A(t)=A_0\).

**9.** What is the one-year effective rate equivalent to continuous rate \(r\)?  
*Solution:* \(e^r-1\).

**10.** What is the continuous rate equivalent to effective rate \(i>-1\)?  
*Solution:* \(\ln(1+i)\).

**11.** Give the ODE with constant net cash-flow rate \(u\).  
*Solution:* \(A'=rA+u\).

**12.** State its solution for \(r\ne0\).  
*Solution:* \(A=A_0e^{rt}+u(e^{rt}-1)/r\).

**13.** State its solution for \(r=0\).  
*Solution:* \(A=A_0+ut\).

**14.** Why use \`expm1\`?  
*Solution:* It accurately evaluates \(e^x-1\) near zero.

**15.** Give the integrating-factor solution for time-dependent \(u(t)\).  
*Solution:* \(A(t)=e^{rt}[A_0+\int_0^t e^{-rs}u(s)\,ds]\).

**16.** Write the Euler step for \(A'=rA+u\).  
*Solution:* \(A_{n+1}=A_n+h(rA_n+u)\).

**17.** Write Euler's pure-growth result after \(n\) steps.  
*Solution:* \(A_n=A_0(1+rh)^n\).

**18.** When does Euler approach the exponential solution?  
*Solution:* As \(h=t/n\to0\) for fixed \(t\) under the standard assumptions.

**19.** Find depletion time for \(A'= -100\), \(A(0)=1000\).  
*Solution:* \(t=10\) years.

**20.** What comes next?  
*Solution:* 19.2 — Separable Equations.

## 11. Applied problems

**A. Growth:** Derive and calculate the two-year value of 1000 currency units growing at a 5% continuously compounded annual rate. Compare with 5% annual effective compounding.

**B. Contributions:** For \(A_0=1000\), \(r=0.04\), \(u=200\) currency units per year, derive the five-year balance and verify its zero-rate limit.

**C. Withdrawals:** Model \(A_0=1000\), \(r=0\), \(u=-100\). Identify depletion time and explain the required business boundary rule.

**D. Numerical comparison:** Compare 10, 20, and 40 Euler steps against the analytic growth solution over two years; report absolute errors and the assumptions behind expected convergence.

**E. Model audit:** Identify which inputs require unit and compounding-convention validation, and distinguish analytical correctness from financial suitability.
