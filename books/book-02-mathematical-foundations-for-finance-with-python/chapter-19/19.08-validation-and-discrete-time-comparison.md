## Learning objectives

Distinguish verification, validation, and model-risk review; compare continuous and discrete compounding under consistent rate conventions; compare continuous cash-flow models with end-of-period deposits; quantify numerical convergence; verify depletion-event timing; and construct reproducible acceptance evidence.

## 1. Three different questions

**Verification:** Does the code implement the stated mathematical equations correctly?

**Numerical validation:** Do numerical approximations converge to independently derived solutions as step size decreases?

**Model validation:** Do the assumptions, parameter estimates, cash-flow conventions, and boundary policies represent the intended financial situation?

Passing a unit test cannot establish the third claim. A mathematically exact solution can still be economically inappropriate.

## 2. Continuous versus discrete compounding

For an initial balance \(A_0\), constant continuously compounded annual rate \(r_c\), and time \(T\) in years,

\[
A_c(T)=A_0e^{r_cT}.
\]

For a nominal annual rate \(j\) compounded \(m\) times per year,

\[
A_d(T)=A_0\left(1+\frac jm\right)^{mT}
\]

when \(mT\) is an integer and the periodic rate is admissible. The formulas cannot be compared fairly using an identical numeric rate without identifying the rate convention.

To obtain equivalent growth over a full year, set

\[
r_c=m\ln(1+j/m),
\qquad
j=m\bigl(e^{r_c/m}-1\bigr).
\]

Stable implementations use \`log1p(j/m)\` and \`expm1(r_c/m)\`. For noninteger periods, a discrete model must specify the treatment of partial periods rather than silently assuming a fractional number of compounding transactions.

## 3. Continuous contributions

For constant continuous contribution rate \(u\) (currency per year) and continuous rate \(r\),

\[
A'=rA+u,\qquad A(0)=A_0.
\]

The exact result is

\[
\boxed{A(T)=A_0e^{rT}+u\frac{\operatorname{expm1}(rT)}{r}}
\]

for \(r\ne0\), and \(A(T)=A_0+uT\) for \(r=0\).

The term \(u\,dt\) represents cash deposited continuously. It is not a deposit of \(u\) currency units at every numerical time step.

## 4. Discrete end-of-period contributions

For \(N\) periods of duration \(h=T/N\), with effective periodic growth \(q=e^{rh}\), and an end-of-period contribution \(c=uh\),

\[
A_{n+1}=qA_n+c.
\]

Therefore,

\[
\boxed{A_N=q^NA_0+c\frac{q^N-1}{q-1}}
\]

for \(q\ne1\), with \(A_N=A_0+Nc\) when \(q=1\).

The discrete formula differs from the continuous-flow model because of deposit timing. For positive rates and positive contributions, end-period deposits earn less interest than contributions distributed continuously; the ordering can differ for negative rates or withdrawals. As \(h\to0\) with \(c=uh\), the discrete balance approaches the continuous-flow solution.

For small arguments, evaluate the factor using \(T\,\operatorname{exprel}(rT)/\operatorname{exprel}(rh)\), where \(\operatorname{exprel}(x)=\operatorname{expm1}(x)/x\) for nonzero \(x\) and \(\operatorname{exprel}(0)=1\). This handles arguments that round to zero; extreme values may still overflow or lose accuracy and need separate numerical analysis.

## 5. Rate and timing conventions

A fair comparison must specify:

- Whether the quoted rate is nominal, effective annual, or continuously compounded.
- Whether deposits occur continuously, at period beginnings, or at period ends.
- Whether withdrawals are fixed currency amounts or proportional rates.
- Whether time is measured in years, months, or days.
- Whether fees, taxes, overdrafts, and depletion boundaries are included.

Changing a convention changes the financial model, not merely the solver.

## 6. Euler and RK4 convergence

For \(A'=rA+u\), compare numerical approximations against the exact continuous-flow benchmark.

Forward Euler:

\[
A_{n+1}=A_n+h(rA_n+u)
\]

typically has global error \(O(h)\).

Classical RK4 has global error \(O(h^4)\) under suitable smoothness and stability conditions. A step-refinement study using \(N,2N,4N\) should show approximate error ratios of \(2\) and \(16\), respectively, **only** when discretization error dominates roundoff and the method is in its asymptotic regime.

Report absolute and relative error separately. Near a zero reference balance, relative error may be undefined or misleading; use a documented absolute tolerance.

## 7. Event-time validation

For fixed withdrawals \(w>0\),

\[
A'=rA-w,\qquad A_0>0.
\]

When \(r>0\) and \(w>rA_0\), the exact depletion time is

\[
t_*=-\frac{\ln(1-rA_0/w)}{r}.
\]

For \(r=0\), \(t_*=A_0/w\). A numerical event detector should be checked against these analytical benchmarks. State the event direction, terminal policy, interpolation method, and tolerance. A small balance error at the final horizon does not imply an accurate depletion timestamp.

## 8. Residual and invariant checks

For a differentiable candidate solution \(\widehat A(t)\), define its ODE residual

\[
R(t)=\widehat A'(t)-[r(t)\widehat A(t)+u(t)].
\]

A small residual is useful supporting evidence but does not replace checking initial conditions and independent reference values. For numerical solutions, derivative approximations can introduce additional error.

Useful invariants and boundary checks include initial balance, zero-flow exponential growth, zero-rate linear accumulation, nonnegative balance policies, and no double application of scheduled deposits.

## Selected contribution-frequency evidence

For n end-period payments of u*T/n, each payment compounds for T−k*T/n years. Summing those separately gives the discrete terminal balance. With h=T/n and a fixed continuous rate r, the contribution portion equals the continuous contribution value multiplied by (r*h)/(exp(r*h)−1). Thus its leading continuous-minus-discrete gap is u*T*(exp(r*T)−1)/(2*n). At zero rate the two balances coincide.

[Selected frequency checks](../validation/chapter-19-contribution-frequency-followup.md) compare finite cash-flow sums, the integral limit, signed errors and observed first-order ratios. These checks use declared rounding allowances and do not establish arbitrary-flow or all-range accuracy.

## Rate-conversion range and saturation

The companion preserves tiny rate-to-frequency quotients through the complete conversion and accepts positive integer frequencies without first converting them to a float. Selected small quotients use bounded series; other finite-range cases use wider arithmetic. Nonfinite final rates or nonzero rates rounding to zero raise `ValueError`.

For sufficiently negative continuous rates, the nominal result can round to minus the frequency. Its mathematical periodic gross factor remains positive, but the rounded nominal value lies on the inverse conversion's domain boundary. Do not interpret round-trip failure there as a new economic model property. See the [conversion range checks](../validation/chapter-19-rate-conversion-range-followup.md) for independent references, original failures and the rerun.

## Growth balance range in the companion

The continuous and discrete growth helpers evaluate the final scaled amount through the shared Section19.2 routine. The discrete helper retains the nominal-rate quotient and the complete period count, including selected very large integer frequencies. Valid zero-amount or zero-period shortcuts follow domain validation. A nonzero final value that rounds to zero, or a nonfinite final value, raises `ValueError`. The [growth-balance checks](../validation/chapter-19-growth-balance-range-followup.md) preserve independent finite-power references, initial failures and the corrected run.

## Contribution-balance range and evaluation domain

The contribution helpers retain complete accumulation and signed-flow cancellation before converting the final amount to float. Exact zero-rate/time and equilibrium identities are preserved; nonfinite results and nonzero results rounding to zero raise `ValueError`.

For the educational end-period helper, nontrivial cases require `abs(rate*years) <= 4000`. Cases outside this evaluation domain raise `ValueError`; an excluded case may still have a finite mathematical balance. The [contribution range checks](../validation/chapter-19-contribution-balance-range-followup.md) preserve this limit, initial failures, an intermediate false-finite probe and the expanded final run. Broader numerical-range and independent mathematical review remain open.

## Depletion classification and representable times

The companion classifies the complete represented-input ratio r*A/w before evaluating a crossing time. A rounded intermediate value of one must not hide a ratio below the nondepletion threshold. Zero-rate quotients use the same final-range guard: a nonzero time that rounds to zero, or an unrepresentable time, raises `ValueError`. A zero initial balance still returns zero, and no withdrawal still means no finite depletion event. See the [depletion range checks](../validation/chapter-19-depletion-classification-range-followup.md).

## Affine Euler/RK4 method range

The companion's constant-rate, constant-flow account methods retain the complete step and affine method increment before rounding each state once. Euler uses A+h*(r*A+u); the combined RK4 increment multiplies h*(r*A+u) by 1+z/2+z²/6+z³/24, where z=r*h. This identity is specific to the autonomous affine account equation. The complete method state may be finite even when a separate derivative or RK4 weighted sum overflows. Valid zero-time inputs return A after validation. A nonzero method state rounding to zero, or a nonfinite state, raises ValueError; exact zero states are allowed.

[Independent geometric method references](../validation/chapter-19-account-step-range-followup.md) retain the original failures and corrected scoped run. The change alters floating-point operation order and does not certify generic callback solvers, stability, global ODE accuracy or real financial assumptions.
