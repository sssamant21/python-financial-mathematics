## Project mission

Build an auditable deterministic financial simulator that integrates continuously compounded account growth, constant continuous contributions or withdrawals, scheduled lump-sum transfers, and zero-balance depletion events. The project consolidates Sections 19.1–19.8 into a coherent, reproducible workflow.

**Scope:** a scalar continuous-time account balance under piecewise constant rates and continuous net cash-flow rates. It is not a stochastic investment forecast, retirement recommendation, tax engine, or production ledger.

## 1. Requirements and units

The account state \(A(t)\) is measured in currency units. Time \(t\) is measured in years. A continuously compounded annual growth rate \(r\) has units of inverse years. The net continuous cash-flow rate \(u\) has units of currency per year. A positive \(u\) represents contributions; a negative \(u\) represents withdrawals. A scheduled lump-sum transfer \(J\) has currency units.

Between events,

\[
\boxed{A'(t)=rA(t)+u,\qquad A(0)=A_0.}
\]

At a scheduled transfer time \(t_j\),

\[
\boxed{A(t_j^+)=A(t_j^-)+J_j.}
\]

A nonnegative-account policy terminates the scenario when the account reaches zero, unless an explicit alternative policy is selected.

## 2. Exact transition engine

For a constant-rate interval of duration \(\Delta\),

\[
A(t+\Delta)=A(t)e^{r\Delta}
+u\frac{\operatorname{expm1}(r\Delta)}{r}
\]

when \(r\ne0\). At \(r=0\),

\[
A(t+\Delta)=A(t)+u\Delta.
\]

The exact transition avoids numerical integration error within each constant-coefficient interval. The implementation must still validate floating-point finiteness, input units, event ordering, and the economic domain.

## 3. Depletion before a scheduled event

A scheduled deposit at a future time cannot retroactively prevent earlier depletion. Before advancing to each scheduled event, determine whether the continuous trajectory reaches zero in the intervening interval.

For \(A_0>0\) and \(u<0\), write \(w=-u>0\). For \(r\ne0\), the candidate depletion time is

\[
t_*=-\frac{\ln(1-rA_0/w)}{r},
\]

provided \(1-rA_0/w>0\); for \(r=0\), \(t_*=A_0/w\). With \(r>0\), depletion occurs only if \(w>rA_0\). If \(u\ge0\), a positive initial balance does not reach zero in finite time under this constant-rate model, even if \(r<0\).

The event check must be performed **before** applying a later scheduled jump. If depletion and a jump coincide, this project adopts the explicit policy **depletion takes precedence over the scheduled jump**. A different policy is possible, but must be documented and tested.

## 4. Jump processing and ordering

Each scheduled jump has a time and signed amount. Times must be unique, strictly increasing, and within the scenario horizon. A jump at \(t=0\) is applied before the initial depletion test. A jump at \(t=T\) is applied at the final horizon if the account has not already depleted.

A jump that makes the balance nonpositive causes an immediate terminal depletion event at that timestamp. The jump log records the raw post-transfer balance, which may be negative; the terminal result reports a zero balance under this model's stopping policy. No later transfers are applied after a terminal event.

## 5. Simulator outputs

The simulator returns a machine-readable report containing:

- Model metadata and units.
- Validated initial balance, rate, continuous flow, horizon and the complete scheduled jump list under the `inputs` key, including transfers not applied after depletion.
- Scheduled jump count and applied jump count.
- Final status: \`completed\` or \`depleted\`.
- Final simulation time and final balance.
- Depletion time when applicable.
- Event log showing transfers and terminal depletion.
- Validation metadata explicitly indicating runtime certification and independent review are pending.

The `inputs` snapshot is captured before time-zero transfers or other state updates. Jump times and amounts are retained as normalized numeric pairs; JSON represents these pairs as arrays. The report also names the constant-rate model and its time-zero and coincident-event policies. Mutating the caller's jump list after simulation does not change the captured schedule.

Event comparisons use the computed floating-point depletion time directly, with no additional event-time tolerance. A representable time just before that crossing can permit a deposit, while the crossing itself and a later time terminate first. This is a numerical policy, not a guarantee of exact event ordering for every near-coincident calculation.

The event log is a model trace, not an accounting ledger. Production systems would additionally require durable transaction identifiers, timezone/calendar rules, currency rounding, idempotency, and audit controls.

## 6. Scenario A — Continuous growth

Let \(A_0=1000\), \(r=0.04\), \(u=0\), \(T=5\), and no scheduled transfers.

\[
A(5)=1000e^{0.2}.
\]

This is the baseline exponential-growth benchmark.

## 7. Scenario B — Continuous contributions

Let \(A_0=1000\), \(r=0.04\), \(u=200\), \(T=5\).

\[
A(5)=1000e^{0.2}+200\frac{\operatorname{expm1}(0.2)}{0.04}.
\]

The contribution rate is currency per year, not a discrete deposit of 200 at each time step.

## 8. Scenario C — Fixed withdrawals and depletion

Let \(A_0=1000\), \(r=0.04\), \(u=-100\), \(T=20\).

Since \(100>0.04(1000)=40\), the account depletes at

\[
t_*=-\frac{\ln(0.6)}{0.04}\approx12.77064\text{ years}.
\]

The simulator must stop at \(t_*\), report a final balance of zero, and avoid producing negative balances as though they were ordinary savings balances.

## 9. Scenario D — Scheduled deposit

Let \(A_0=1000\), \(r=0.05\), \(u=0\), \(T=5\), and deposit \(J=200\) at \(t=2\).

\[
A(5)=1000e^{0.25}+200e^{0.15}.
\]

The initial balance grows for five years; the scheduled deposit grows for three.

## 10. Scenario E — Deposit after depletion

Let \(A_0=100\), \(r=0\), \(u=-50\), \(T=5\), and schedule a deposit of 1000 at \(t=3\).

The account depletes at \(t=2\). Under the terminal policy, the deposit at \(t=3\) is **not applied**. This case is essential: checking only the final balance after all transfers would incorrectly conceal depletion.

## 11. Comparison and sensitivity

Compare scenarios by final balance, terminal status, depletion time, and applied transfers. Vary \(r\), \(u\), and transfer timing while keeping units and rate conventions fixed. Discuss how a contribution immediately before depletion may change the result, while a contribution after depletion cannot under the terminal policy.

Sensitivity is not uncertainty quantification. The model has no volatility, changing interest rates, fees, taxes, credit limits, or behavioral responses unless explicitly extended.

## 13. Twenty worked exercises

**1.** Write the continuous account ODE.  
*Solution:* \(A'=rA+u\).

**2.** What units does \(A\) have?  
*Solution:* Currency.

**3.** What units does \(r\) have?  
*Solution:* Inverse years.

**4.** What units does \(u\) have?  
*Solution:* Currency per year.

**5.** State the exact constant-rate transition for \(r\ne0\).  
*Solution:* \(A_{\rm next}=Ae^{r\Delta}+u\operatorname{expm1}(r\Delta)/r\).

**6.** State the zero-rate transition.  
*Solution:* \(A_{\rm next}=A+u\Delta\).

**7.** What does a positive \(u\) represent?  
*Solution:* Continuous contributions.

**8.** What does a negative \(u\) represent?  
*Solution:* Continuous withdrawals.

**9.** Define a lump-sum jump.  
*Solution:* \(A(t_j^+)=A(t_j^-)+J_j\).

**10.** What happens if a jump reduces the balance below zero?  
*Solution:* The terminal policy records depletion at the jump time and stops.

**11.** Why test depletion before a future deposit?  
*Solution:* The deposit cannot prevent earlier depletion.

**12.** State the zero-rate depletion time.  
*Solution:* \(A_0/w\) for \(A_0,w>0\).

**13.** State the positive-rate depletion condition.  
*Solution:* \(w>rA_0\).

**14.** What happens if \(w=rA_0\) with \(r>0\)?  
*Solution:* The balance stays constant.

**15.** What is the final balance in Scenario A?  
*Solution:* \(1000e^{0.2}\).

**16.** What is the final balance in Scenario B?  
*Solution:* \(1000e^{0.2}+200\operatorname{expm1}(0.2)/0.04\).

**17.** What is the depletion time in Scenario C?  
*Solution:* \(-\ln(0.6)/0.04\) years.

**18.** What is the final balance in Scenario D?  
*Solution:* \(1000e^{0.25}+200e^{0.15}\).

**19.** Is the scheduled deposit applied in Scenario E?  
*Solution:* No; depletion at year 2 terminates the simulation before year 3.

## 14. Applied project extensions

**A. Monthly transfers:** Add deposits at month-end while preserving continuous growth between deposits. Compare against a continuous-flow approximation.

**B. Withdrawal sensitivity:** Vary the withdrawal rate around \(rA_0\) and explain the transition from nondepletion to finite-time depletion.

**C. Jump timing:** Compare an identical deposit at years 1, 2, and 3, assuming no earlier depletion.

**D. Boundary policies:** Design, but do not silently implement, an alternative policy allowing overdrafts with a separate borrowing rate.

**E. Auditability:** Propose transaction identifiers, reproducible parameter files, currency precision rules, and independent validation evidence needed before production use.
