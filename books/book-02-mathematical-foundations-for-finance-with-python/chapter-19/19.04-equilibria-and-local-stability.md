## Learning objectives

Find equilibrium solutions of autonomous ODEs; classify local stability using derivatives and phase lines; distinguish stable, asymptotically stable, and unstable behavior; recognize limitations of linearization at nonhyperbolic equilibria; and interpret financial examples with appropriate economic caveats.

## 1. Autonomous differential models

An autonomous scalar ODE is

\[
\frac{dx}{dt}=f(x).
\]

An **equilibrium** \(x_*\) satisfies \(f(x_*)=0\). If \(x(0)=x_*\), the constant function \(x(t)=x_*\) solves the ODE. An equilibrium is a property of the model's dynamics, not a guarantee that real-world financial conditions remain unchanged.

For \(x'=f(t,x)\), a constant equilibrium \(x_*\) must satisfy \(f(t,x_*)=0\) for every relevant time \(t\).

## 2. Stability definitions

An equilibrium is **Lyapunov stable** if trajectories starting sufficiently close remain close for all future times in the model's domain. It is **asymptotically stable** if it is stable and sufficiently nearby trajectories also converge to it. It is **unstable** if the stability condition fails.

These are mathematical properties under a specified model and admissible state domain; they are not the same as low market risk or guaranteed portfolio safety.

## 3. Linearization and derivative test

Let \(x=x_*+\eta\). For differentiable \(f\),

\[
\eta'=f(x_*+\eta)=f'(x_*)\eta+O(\eta^2).
\]

The linearized perturbation obeys

\[
\eta'= \lambda\eta,\qquad \lambda=f'(x_*),
\]

so \(\eta(t)\approx\eta(0)e^{\lambda t}\).

For a continuously differentiable scalar autonomous system:

- \(f'(x_*)<0\): locally asymptotically stable.
- \(f'(x_*)>0\): unstable.
- \(f'(x_*)=0\): the linearization test is **inconclusive**; inspect higher-order terms or the phase line.

The eigenvalue-like quantity \(\lambda\) has inverse-time units.

## 4. Phase-line analysis

Mark equilibria on the state axis. Where \(f(x)>0\), the state moves right as time increases; where \(f(x)<0\), it moves left. Arrows pointing toward an equilibrium from both sides indicate attraction; arrows pointing away indicate repulsion.

Phase lines also reveal **semistability**: attraction from one side and repulsion from the other. This distinction matters when \(f'(x_*)=0\).

## 5. Financial model A: mean reversion

Consider a stylized target-balance adjustment model:

\[
A'=\kappa(A_*-A),\qquad \kappa>0.
\]

Here \(A_*\) is a target balance in currency units and \(\kappa\) has units of inverse years. The equilibrium is \(A=A_*\). Since \(f'(A)=-\kappa<0\), it is globally asymptotically stable on the real line.

The exact solution is

\[
\boxed{A(t)=A_*+(A_0-A_*)e^{-\kappa t}}.
\]

For \(A_*=1000\), \(A_0=1200\), and \(\kappa=0.5\) per year, \(A(2)=1000+200e^{-1}\). This is a *behavioral adjustment* model, not a guaranteed bank-account process.

## 6. Financial model B: constant contributions and withdrawals

For

\[
A'=rA+u,\qquad r\ne0,
\]

the equilibrium is

\[
A_*=-\frac{u}{r}.
\]

The derivative \(f'(A)=r\). If \(r<0\), the equilibrium is asymptotically stable. If \(r>0\), it is unstable. For a positive return \(r>0\) and fixed withdrawals \(u=-w\), the threshold \(A_*=w/r\) is unstable: a balance above it grows, while one below it declines and may deplete.

If \(r=0\) and \(u\ne0\), there is no equilibrium. If both vanish, every balance is an equilibrium, and none is asymptotically stable in the usual whole-line sense.

## 7. Financial model C: logistic saturation

Consider

\[
A'=rA\left(1-\frac{A}{K}\right),\qquad r>0,\ K>0.
\]

The equilibria are \(A_*=0\) and \(A_*=K\). Since

\[
f'(A)=r\left(1-\frac{2A}{K}\right),
\]

we have \(f'(0)=r>0\), so zero is unstable, and \(f'(K)=-r<0\), so \(K\) is locally asymptotically stable. On the nonnegative state domain, \(K\) attracts all positive initial states under this idealized logistic model.

The parameter \(K\) is a saturation level, not a financial regulatory capital requirement unless the model explicitly justifies that interpretation.

## 8. Nonhyperbolic equilibria

For \(x'=-x^3\), the equilibrium \(x_*=0\) has \(f'(0)=0\), but \(f(x)>0\) for \(x<0\) and \(f(x)<0\) for \(x>0\). Both sides move toward zero: the equilibrium is asymptotically stable.

For \(x'=x^2\), \(f'(0)=0\), but \(f(x)>0\) on both sides. Trajectories from below approach zero while those above move away; zero is not Lyapunov stable on the whole real line. A zero derivative alone cannot classify either example.

## 9. Perturbation decay and half-life

For the mean-reverting model, the deviation \(D(t)=A(t)-A_*\) satisfies \(D(t)=D(0)e^{-\kappa t}\). Its half-life is

\[
t_{1/2}=\frac{\ln 2}{\kappa}.
\]

This measures the decay of deviations within the model. It should not be confused with an observed market recovery time.

## 10. Modeling boundaries

A mathematical equilibrium may lie outside the economically admissible domain, such as a negative account balance where overdrafts are forbidden. Always state the domain, intervention or boundary rules, and whether a target is feasible.

Local stability does not imply global stability; deterministic stability does not establish robustness to stochastic shocks; and parameter estimation errors can reverse a stability classification.

## 12. Twenty worked exercises

**1.** Define an equilibrium of \(x'=f(x)\).  
*Solution:* A state \(x_*\) with \(f(x_*)=0\).

**2.** What does Lyapunov stability require?  
*Solution:* Nearby trajectories remain nearby for all future time in the model domain.

**3.** What additional property defines asymptotic stability?  
*Solution:* Nearby trajectories converge to the equilibrium.

**4.** State the linearization coefficient.  
*Solution:* \(\lambda=f'(x_*)\).

**5.** What does \(f'(x_*)<0\) imply for a smooth scalar autonomous system?  
*Solution:* Local asymptotic stability.

**6.** What does \(f'(x_*)>0\) imply?  
*Solution:* Instability.

**7.** What does \(f'(x_*)=0\) imply?  
*Solution:* The derivative test is inconclusive.

**8.** Interpret \(f(x)>0\) on a phase line.  
*Solution:* The state increases.

**9.** Interpret \(f(x)<0\).  
*Solution:* The state decreases.

**10.** Find the equilibrium of \(A'=\kappa(A_*-A)\).  
*Solution:* \(A=A_*\).

**11.** Classify it for \(\kappa>0\).  
*Solution:* Asymptotically stable.

**12.** Give its exact solution.  
*Solution:* \(A=A_*+(A_0-A_*)e^{-\kappa t}\).

**13.** Give the deviation half-life.  
*Solution:* \(\ln 2/\kappa\).

**14.** Find the equilibrium of \(A'=rA+u\), \(r\ne0\).  
*Solution:* \(A_*=-u/r\).

**15.** Classify it for \(r>0\).  
*Solution:* Unstable.

**16.** Classify it for \(r<0\).  
*Solution:* Asymptotically stable.

**17.** State logistic equilibria for \(r,K>0\).  
*Solution:* \(0\) and \(K\).

**18.** Classify the logistic zero equilibrium.  
*Solution:* Unstable.

**19.** Classify the logistic capacity equilibrium.  
*Solution:* Asymptotically stable.
