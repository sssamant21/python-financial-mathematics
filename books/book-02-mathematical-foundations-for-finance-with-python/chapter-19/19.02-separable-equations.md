## Learning objectives

Identify separable first-order ODEs; derive implicit and explicit solutions with initial conditions; preserve equilibrium solutions that can be lost by division; analyze growth, decay, and saturation; recognize finite-time singularities and domain restrictions; and check analytical financial trajectories independently.

## 1. What is a separable differential equation?

A first-order ODE is **separable** when it can be expressed as

\[
\frac{dy}{dt}=g(t)h(y).
\]

Where \(h(y)\ne0\), rearrange formally:

\[
\frac{dy}{h(y)}=g(t)\,dt.
\]

Integrating both sides gives

\[
\int\frac{dy}{h(y)}=\int g(t)\,dt+C.
\]

This produces an implicit relationship; solving explicitly for \(y(t)\) may require algebra and domain restrictions. A constant of integration must be determined using the initial condition.

**Critical caution:** Dividing by \(h(y)\) excludes any constant solution \(y(t)\equiv y_*\) with \(h(y_*)=0\). Check equilibria before dividing.

## 2. Growth and decay

For

\[
A'(t)=rA(t),\qquad A(0)=A_0,
\]

and \(A\ne0\),

\[
\frac{dA}{A}=r\,dt
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\ln|A|=rt+C.
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{A(t)=A_0e^{rt}}.
\]

The equilibrium \(A(t)\equiv0\), excluded by division, is also a valid solution. If \(r<0\), the balance decays exponentially; if \(r>0\), it grows exponentially. The rate \(r\) has units of inverse time.

**Financial example:** \(A_0=1000\) currency units and continuously compounded annual rate \(r=0.04\) yield \(A(3)=1000e^{0.12}\). The rate convention must be stated explicitly.

## 3. Time-varying proportional growth

For \(A'=r(t)A\), separation yields

\[
\boxed{A(t)=A_0\exp\left(\int_0^t r(s)\,ds\right)}.
\]

For \(r(t)=a+bt\), the result is

\[
A(t)=A_0e^{at+\frac12bt^2}.
\]

Units: \(a\) is inverse years and \(b\) is inverse years squared when \(t\) is in years. The integral is dimensionless.

## 4. Nonlinear growth: logistic model

Consider

\[
A'=rA\left(1-\frac{A}{K}\right),\qquad r>0,\ K>0.
\]

Here \(K\) is a saturation level in currency units. The equilibrium solutions are \(A\equiv0\) and \(A\equiv K\).

For \(A_0>0\), separation and partial fractions give

\[
\frac{1}{A(1-A/K)}=\frac1A+\frac{1}{K-A},
\]

and

\[
\ln\left|\frac{A}{K-A}\right|=rt+C.
\]

Solving with \(A(0)=A_0\) yields

\[
\boxed{A(t)=\frac{K}{1+(K/A_0-1)e^{-rt}}}.
\]

For \(0<A_0<K\), the trajectory increases toward \(K\); for \(A_0>K\), it decreases toward \(K\). This is a **stylized capacity-constrained model**, not a standard bank-account interest model. Its economic justification must be assessed separately.

For \(A_0=0\), use the equilibrium solution \(A(t)=0\), rather than the formula involving \(K/A_0\).

## 5. Finite-time blow-up

For

\[
A'=kA^2,\qquad A(0)=A_0>0,\ k>0,
\]

separation gives

\[
-\frac1A=kt+C,
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\boxed{A(t)=\frac{A_0}{1-kA_0t}}.
\]

The solution exists only while \(t<1/(kA_0)\). The finite-time singularity shows that a mathematically valid ODE can be inappropriate as a long-horizon financial model. Do not evaluate through the singularity and present the result as a meaningful continuation.

## 6. Withdrawals proportional to balance

If a portfolio has continuously compounded gross return \(r\) and proportional continuous withdrawal rate \(w\), with both in inverse years,

\[
A'=(r-w)A.
\]

Thus

\[
A(t)=A_0e^{(r-w)t}.
\]

When \(w>r\), the account decays asymptotically toward zero but does not reach zero at a finite time under this model. Fixed withdrawals instead lead to the generally **nonseparable** form \(A'=rA-u\) when \(r\ne0\) and \(u\ne0\), though it is solvable as a linear first-order equation (Section 19.3).

## 7. Initial conditions and solution domains

A general antiderivative is not yet a complete IVP solution. Always:

1. Identify excluded values before division.
2. Check constant equilibrium solutions.
3. Apply the initial condition to determine constants.
4. Restrict the solution to a connected interval where the formula and model are valid.
5. Check the solution by differentiating it and substituting into the original ODE.

## 8. Analytical and numerical verification

For a proposed solution \(A(t)\), compute \(A'(t)\) analytically and compare it with the ODE right-hand side. Separately check \(A(0)=A_0\).

Finite-difference derivative checks are useful secondary evidence, but they do not replace an analytical derivation. Numerical tests must respect boundaries and singularities.

## 10. Twenty worked exercises

**1.** Define a separable ODE.  
*Solution:* \(y'=g(t)h(y)\).

**2.** State the formal separated equation.  
*Solution:* \(dy/h(y)=g(t)\,dt\), wherever \(h(y)\ne0\).

**3.** What must be checked before dividing by \(h(y)\)?  
*Solution:* Constant solutions satisfying \(h(y)=0\).

**4.** Separate \(A'=rA\).  
*Solution:* \(dA/A=r\,dt\) for nonzero \(A\).

**5.** Integrate the preceding equation.  
*Solution:* \(\ln|A|=rt+C\).

**6.** Solve with \(A(0)=A_0\).  
*Solution:* \(A=A_0e^{rt}\).

**7.** What if \(A_0=0\)?  
*Solution:* \(A(t)=0\).

**8.** What units does \(r\) have?  
*Solution:* Inverse years if \(t\) is in years.

**9.** Solve \(A'=r(t)A\).  
*Solution:* \(A=A_0\exp(\int_0^t r(s)\,ds)\).

**10.** Solve for \(r(t)=a+bt\).  
*Solution:* \(A=A_0e^{at+bt^2/2}\).

**11.** Give the logistic ODE.  
*Solution:* \(A'=rA(1-A/K)\).

**12.** State its equilibria.  
*Solution:* \(A=0\) and \(A=K\).

**13.** State its solution for \(A_0>0\).  
*Solution:* \(A=K/[1+(K/A_0-1)e^{-rt}]\) on its valid interval.

**14.** What happens when \(0<A_0<K\) and \(r>0\)?  
*Solution:* \(A\) increases toward \(K\).

**15.** What happens when \(A_0>K\) and \(r>0\)?  
*Solution:* \(A\) decreases toward \(K\).

**16.** Solve \(A'=kA^2\) for \(A_0>0\).  
*Solution:* \(A=A_0/(1-kA_0t)\).

**17.** Give its blow-up time for \(k>0\).  
*Solution:* \(1/(kA_0)\).

**18.** Solve proportional withdrawals \(A'=(r-w)A\).  
*Solution:* \(A=A_0e^{(r-w)t}\).

**19.** Does exponential decay reach zero in finite time?  
*Solution:* Not for positive \(A_0\) under a finite constant decay rate.

## 11. Applied problems

**A. Continuous growth:** Derive and compute the three-year balance for \(A_0=1000\) and \(r=0.04\). Compare the analytical derivative with \(rA(t)\).

**B. Changing rates:** Solve \(A'=(0.03+0.002t)A\) with \(A(0)=1000\); explain the units of both coefficients.

**C. Saturation:** For \(A_0=200\), \(K=1000\), and \(r=0.4\), calculate the logistic balance at \(t=5\), identify equilibria, and discuss whether the model is economically appropriate.

**D. Singular growth:** For \(A_0=100\), \(k=0.001\), find the blow-up time and explain why values at or after it are invalid.

**E. Withdrawal policy:** Compare \(r=0.04\), \(w=0.06\) proportional withdrawal dynamics with a fixed currency withdrawal rate. Identify which model is separable.
