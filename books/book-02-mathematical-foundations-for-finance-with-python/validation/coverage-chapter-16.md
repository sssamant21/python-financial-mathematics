## Applied requirements, stopping and oracles

16.10 Problem A method comparison has six successful runs with rates/status and one-step Newton assertions. Final losses and every per-run gradient tolerance are not independently asserted in this suite;16.9 does assert gradient magnitude. Problem B double-well is checked in16.7, not this lab. Problem C variance0.0336 is checked; principal-minor proof and fully-invested feasibility reasoning are manual. Problem D recomputes metrics at the analytic benchmark rather than optimizer final rates, so the report's residual evidence is not a per-run validation of each returned allocation.

Likewise16.9 provisional acceptance combines solver status, distance to benchmark, gradient, benchmark training metrics and one holdout condition. It is not financial approval. The holdout remains outside fitting, which source inspection confirms. Deliberate acceptance failures, modified data, invalid observation shapes/times, NaN/Inf path coverage and all report/configuration fields are not demonstrated by its numbered check.16.8 positive metric checks do not demonstrate invalid-input rejection.

The objectives have intentionally different scales:16.1–16.3 squared raw price residual curvature8million,16.5–16.6 normalized quadratic curvature2,16.9–16.10 normalized three-observation half-squared curvature14, and16.10's first written exercise curvature4. Keep gradient thresholds and expected histories tied to the exact objective; do not reuse them as if equivalent.

16.5 includes substantive stagnation and strict-decrease regressions from the [focused stopping review](chapter-16-stopping-review.md).16.7 computes the polynomial objective change locally to avoid subtracting nearly equal levels. Its checks verify successful reference runs but do not directly force no_descent, max_iterations, no-successful-start or boundary status paths. Other optimizer copies lack all the same guard/report details.

The scalar finite-difference helpers in16.2–16.4 check positive steps, without full representable-perturbation and h-squared range guards. Selected numerical agreement does not establish tiny/huge-step reliability. Reported hardcoded Hessians and condition fields are examples, not automatically computed numerical diagnoses.

Twelve-step labs include proofs, derivations, input units and evidence retention alongside executable operations. These require manual review; no count of successful scripts measures completion of all twelve tasks. No actual grading or independent reviewer sign-off has occurred.
