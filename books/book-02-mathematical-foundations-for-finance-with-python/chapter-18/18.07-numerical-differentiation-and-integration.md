## Learning objectives

Derive forward, central, and second-order finite differences; explain truncation versus rounding error; apply composite trapezoidal and Simpson quadrature; estimate errors using step refinement; and compute financial sensitivities and discounted continuous cash-flow values with independent analytic benchmarks.

## 1. Numerical differentiation

For a smooth scalar function \(f\), the forward difference is

\[
D_h^+f(x)=\frac{f(x+h)-f(x)}{h}
=f'(x)+\frac h2f''(x)+O(h^2).
\]

The central difference is

\[
D_h^0f(x)=\frac{f(x+h)-f(x-h)}{2h}
=f'(x)+\frac{h^2}{6}f'''(x)+O(h^4).
\]

The second central difference is

\[
D_h^{(2)}f(x)=\frac{f(x+h)-2f(x)+f(x-h)}{h^2}
=f''(x)+O(h^2).
\]

These expansions assume sufficient smoothness on the sampled interval. The central first derivative generally has smaller truncation error than the forward derivative for the same step, but requires evaluations on both sides of the target.

## 2. Choosing the step

A smaller \(h\) reduces truncation error only until floating-point cancellation becomes significant. For the forward difference, a simplified total-error model is

\[
E(h)\approx C_1h+C_2u/h,
\]

suggesting a step proportional to \(\sqrt{u}\) times a problem-dependent scale. For central differences, the model \(C_1h^2+C_2u/h\) suggests a step proportional to \(u^{1/3}\). These are heuristics, not universal optimal choices. Near rate-domain boundaries, a symmetric step may be invalid.

A useful practical diagnostic is to compare results for \(h\), \(h/2\), and \(2h\), and report disagreement rather than blindly choosing the smallest representable step.

## 3. Financial example: bond price sensitivity

For a single zero-coupon cash flow \(F\) at maturity \(T\) under annual effective compounding,

\[
P(r)=F(1+r)^{-T},\qquad r>-1.
\]

The analytic first and second derivatives are

\[
P'(r)=-TF(1+r)^{-T-1},
\quad
P''(r)=T(T+1)F(1+r)^{-T-2}.
\]

The modified duration under this rate convention is

\[
D_{\mathrm{mod}}=-\frac{P'(r)}{P(r)}=\frac{T}{1+r},
\]

and convexity is \(C=P''(r)/P(r)=T(T+1)/(1+r)^2\). These definitions depend on the yield convention and units. Numerical finite differences can independently approximate both.

For \(F=1000,T=2,r=0.05\), the exact modified duration is \(2/1.05\approx1.9047619048\).

## 4. Numerical integration

For a continuous cash-flow rate \(c(t)\) in currency per year, discounted continuously at a constant continuously compounded rate \(\rho\), the present value is

\[
PV=\int_0^T c(t)e^{-\rho t}\,dt.
\]

The units of the integrand are currency per year; integrating over years yields currency. The discount-rate convention here differs from the annual effective convention used in the bond derivative example.

## 5. Composite trapezoidal rule

For \(n\) equally spaced subintervals of width \(h=(b-a)/n\),

\[
T_n=h\left[\frac{f(a)+f(b)}2+\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}f(a+ih)\right].
\]

If \(f\in C^2[a,b]\) and \(|f''|\le M_2\), then

\[
\left|\int_a^b f(x)\,dx-T_n\right|
\le \frac{(b-a)h^2M_2}{12}.
\]

The composite trapezoidal rule is generally second-order accurate for smooth integrands.

## 6. Composite Simpson rule

For an **even** number \(n\) of subintervals,

\[
S_n=\frac h3\left[f(a)+f(b)
+4\sum_{\substack{i=1\\i\text{ odd}}}^{n-1}f(a+ih)
+2\sum_{\substack{i=2\\i\text{ even}}}^{n-2}f(a+ih)\right].
\]

If \(f\in C^4[a,b]\) and \(|f^{(4)}|\le M_4\),

\[
\left|\int_a^b f(x)\,dx-S_n\right|
\le\frac{(b-a)h^4M_4}{180}.
\]

Simpson's rule is fourth-order for sufficiently smooth integrands; it is not automatically superior for discontinuities, singularities, or noisy data.

## 7. Analytic integration benchmark

For constant cash-flow rate \(c(t)=C\) and constant \(\rho\),

\[
PV=C\int_0^T e^{-\rho t}\,dt
=
\begin{cases}
C(1-e^{-\rho T})/\rho,&\rho\ne0,\\
CT,&\rho=0.
\end{cases}
\]

For small \(|\rho T|\), evaluate the nonzero-rate formula as

\[
\boxed{PV=C\frac{-\operatorname{expm1}(-\rho T)}{\rho}}
\]

to avoid cancellation. This supplies an independent benchmark for both quadrature methods.

## 8. Error estimation by refinement

When a quadrature method has leading error proportional to \(h^p\), comparing solutions at \(n\) and \(2n\) panels can estimate the finer-grid error:

\[
E_{2n}\approx \frac{|Q_{2n}-Q_n|}{2^p-1}.
\]

Use \(p=2\) for trapezoidal and \(p=4\) for Simpson under the required smoothness and asymptotic conditions. The estimate is **not a certified bound**. Convergence may be irregular for nonsmooth, oscillatory, or singular integrands.

## 9. Numerical reliability controls

Reject nonfinite integrand values, invalid bounds, nonpositive panel counts, odd Simpson panel counts, and invalid rate domains. Avoid overflow from very large exponents. Report panel counts, method, step size, and independent reference discrepancies.

Derivative and integration accuracy cannot repair incorrect financial assumptions, cash-flow timing, or compounding conventions.

## 11. Twenty worked exercises

**1.** Define the forward difference.  
*Solution:* \([f(x+h)-f(x)]/h\).

**2.** Give its leading truncation-error order.  
*Solution:* \(O(h)\).

**3.** Define the central first difference.  
*Solution:* \([f(x+h)-f(x-h)]/(2h)\).

**4.** Give its leading truncation-error order.  
*Solution:* \(O(h^2)\).

**5.** Define the central second difference.  
*Solution:* \([f(x+h)-2f(x)+f(x-h)]/h^2\).

**6.** Why can extremely small \(h\) be harmful?  
*Solution:* Subtractive cancellation and rounding error can dominate.

**7.** Give the approximate forward-difference step scaling.  
*Solution:* \(h\propto u^{1/2}\) after accounting for problem scale.

**8.** Give the approximate central-difference step scaling.  
*Solution:* \(h\propto u^{1/3}\) after accounting for problem scale.

**9.** State the zero-coupon price formula.  
*Solution:* \(P=F(1+r)^{-T}\).

**10.** Differentiate it once.  
*Solution:* \(P'=-TF(1+r)^{-T-1}\).

**11.** Differentiate it twice.  
*Solution:* \(P''=T(T+1)F(1+r)^{-T-2}\).

**12.** State modified duration for this price model.  
*Solution:* \(T/(1+r)\).

**13.** Compute duration for \(T=2,r=0.05\).  
*Solution:* \(2/1.05\approx1.9047619048\).

**14.** State the trapezoidal endpoint weights.  
*Solution:* One half each.

**15.** What panel-count condition does composite Simpson require?  
*Solution:* A positive even number.

**16.** State the interior Simpson weights.  
*Solution:* Alternating 4 for odd interior indices and 2 for even interior indices.

**17.** State the typical trapezoidal convergence order.  
*Solution:* Second order for smooth integrands.

**18.** State the typical Simpson convergence order.  
*Solution:* Fourth order under sufficient smoothness.

**19.** Give the stable PV of a constant continuously discounted cash-flow rate.  
*Solution:* \(-C\,\operatorname{expm1}(-\rho T)/\rho\) for nonzero \(\rho\), and \(CT\) at zero rate.

**20.** What comes next?  
*Solution:* 18.8 — Stopping Rules and Diagnostics.
