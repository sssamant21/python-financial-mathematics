## Learning objectives

Distinguish residual, step size, bracket width, and true error; select scale-aware absolute and relative tolerances; detect stagnation and divergence; construct auditable solver diagnostics; and interpret stopping rules in financial yield calculations.

## 1. Why stopping rules matter

A numerical algorithm must decide whether to return an approximation, continue iterating, or report failure. Stopping because a value looks stable is not the same as proving that it is accurate. A robust solver reports both the result and the reason it stopped.

For a root equation \(f(x)=0\), the **residual** is \(|f(x_k)|\); the **step** is \(|x_k-x_{k-1}|\); and the **true error** is \(|x_k-x^*|\), where \(x^*\) is an exact root. Usually \(x^*\) is unknown, so true error is unavailable at runtime.

## 2. Absolute and relative tolerances

A common step criterion is

\[
|x_k-x_{k-1}|\le a_x+r_x\max(|x_k|,|x_{k-1}|),
\]

where \(a_x>0\) is an absolute tolerance in the units of \(x\) and \(r_x\ge0\) is dimensionless. For rates, \(a_x=10^{-8}\) corresponds to \(10^{-6}\) percentage points, or \(0.0001\) basis points.

For a bracket \([a,b]\), use

\[
b-a\le a_x+r_x\max(|a|,|b|).
\]

A small step can indicate convergence, but also stagnation. A bracket-width criterion has a stronger meaning when continuity and a sign-change invariant are maintained.

## 3. Residual versus root error

A small residual does not always imply a small root error. For \(f(x)=\epsilon(x-1)\) with very small \(\epsilon\), the residual may be tiny even when \(x\) is far from the root.

Near a simple root, a first-order estimate is

\[
|x-x^*|\approx \frac{|f(x)|}{|f'(x^*)|}.
\]

This approximation depends on the derivative and local behavior; it is not a universal certified error bound. Residual tolerances must be chosen in the units of the objective, such as currency for NPV.

## 4. Bracket-based certification

If \(f\) is continuous on \([a,b]\) and endpoint values have opposite signs, at least one root lies inside. The midpoint \(m\) is within \((b-a)/2\) of some bracketed root.

This certificate is conditional on continuity and correct evaluations. It does not establish uniqueness or correctness of the financial model.

## 5. Stagnation

Floating-point arithmetic can make a proposed new iterate identical to the old iterate. In bisection, if the computed midpoint equals an endpoint, the bracket cannot be reduced using that arithmetic. Report \`stagnation\` unless an exact root has already been found or a previously satisfied stopping criterion justifies success.

## 6. Divergence and iteration budgets

Monitor finite evaluations, bracket validity, and maximum iteration count. For unrestricted iterative methods, growing steps or residuals may indicate divergence, but a single increase does not prove failure.

An iteration limit is a resource control, not a convergence guarantee. Return a distinct \`max_iter\` status with the best available bracket and diagnostics.

## 7. Financial example: zero-coupon yield

For \(F=1000\), \(P=900\), \(T=2\) years, solve

\[
f(r)=1000(1+r)^{-2}-900=0,\qquad r>-1.
\]

The analytic reference is

\[
r^*=\sqrt{1000/900}-1\approx0.054092553389.
\]

The residual is measured in currency, while the bracket width is measured in decimal yield. The two tolerances cannot be compared numerically without considering their units.

## 8. Diagnostic report design

An auditable result should include:

- The candidate root, final bracket, width, and residual.
- Iteration count, function-evaluation count, and stopping status.
- Absolute and relative tolerances and the iteration budget.
- Whether a sign-change bracket was preserved.
- A trace of bracket widths and midpoint residuals, when feasible.
- The model and rate conventions and whether independent validation has occurred.

Statuses should distinguish \`converged\`, \`exact_root\`, \`endpoint_root\`, \`stagnation\`, and \`max_iter\`. Invalid inputs should raise explicit errors rather than return misleading numerical results.

## 9. Error budgets

Separate numerical solver error from input error, model error, and market-data uncertainty. A highly precise yield calculation cannot make an incorrect bond price or cash-flow schedule reliable.

For example, a \(10^{-10}\) yield tolerance may be unnecessarily strict if the instrument price is quoted only to cents and its cash flows are uncertain.

## 10. Independent checks

Verify that the final bracket contains the analytic reference when one exists, that successful width-based termination satisfies its declared tolerance, and that iteration counts and evaluation counts are consistent. Deliberately test invalid brackets, nonfinite functions, exact endpoint roots, low iteration budgets, and floating-point stagnation.

## 12. Twenty worked exercises

**1.** Define residual.  
*Solution:* \(|f(x_k)|\).

**2.** Define step size.  
*Solution:* \(|x_k-x_{k-1}|\).

**3.** Define true error.  
*Solution:* \(|x_k-x^*|\).

**4.** Why is true error usually unavailable?  
*Solution:* The exact root is unknown.

**5.** Give a mixed absolute-relative step rule.  
*Solution:* \(|\Delta x|\le a_x+r_x\max(|x_k|,|x_{k-1}|)\).

**6.** What units does absolute rate tolerance have?  
*Solution:* Decimal rate units.

**7.** What units does NPV residual tolerance have?  
*Solution:* Currency.

**8.** Can a tiny residual imply a large root error?  
*Solution:* Yes, when the objective is flat.

**9.** State the local residual-to-error estimate.  
*Solution:* \(|x-x^*|\approx |f(x)|/|f'(x^*)|\) near a simple root.

**10.** State the bracket-width rule.  
*Solution:* \(b-a\le a_x+r_x\max(|a|,|b|)\).

**11.** What theorem supports a sign-change bracket?  
*Solution:* The Intermediate Value Theorem under continuity.

**12.** Bound midpoint error for a valid bracket.  
*Solution:* At most half its width relative to some bracketed root.

**13.** What is floating-point stagnation?  
*Solution:* A computed update fails to change the representable iterate or bracket.

**14.** Does reaching \`max_iter\` prove convergence?  
*Solution:* No.

**15.** What does an exact endpoint root mean?  
*Solution:* One initial endpoint evaluates to zero.

**16.** What is the zero-coupon yield equation?  
*Solution:* \(1000(1+r)^{-2}-900=0\).

**17.** Give its analytic yield.  
*Solution:* \(\sqrt{1000/900}-1\).

**18.** Why record function evaluations?  
*Solution:* To audit computational cost and solver behavior.

**19.** Does numerical precision eliminate model error?  
*Solution:* No.

## Diagnostic evidence follow-up

The audited bisection report retains `bracket_endpoint_residuals`, and each trace row records `lower_residual` and `upper_residual`. Its `bracket_invariant` flag is computed from cached endpoint signs and nested trace intervals, rather than from the termination-status name. A collapsed endpoint/exact-root bracket requires a zero residual. Report construction does not call the function again, preserving evaluation accounting.

The reference checks independently reevaluate deterministic example functions at reported points, verify sign preservation and the actual half-interval updates, and count observed callbacks across endpoint roots, an exact midpoint root, convergence, budget exhaustion and floating-point stagnation. A preserved sign bracket does not prove continuity, uniqueness or successful convergence; stagnation and exhausted budgets retain their separate failure statuses.
