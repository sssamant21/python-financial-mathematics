## 1. Formula reference

For continuous \(f\) with opposite-sign endpoint values, a bracket \([a,b]\) contains at least one root. Bisection uses \(m=a+(b-a)/2\) and retains the sign-changing half. After \(n\) successful halvings, bracket width is \((b_0-a_0)/2^n\); midpoint error is at most half that width.

Newton's method proposes

\[
x_{k+1}=x_k-\frac{f(x_k)}{f'(x_k)}.
\]

Its quadratic convergence requires a sufficiently smooth function, a simple root, and a sufficiently close initial guess. A safeguarded method falls back to bisection when a Newton proposal is unusable.

For annual effective rates, the present value of a zero-coupon payment is

\[
P(r)=F(1+r)^{-T},\qquad r>-1.
\]

For periodic cash flows \(C_t\),

\[
NPV(r)=\sum_{t=0}^n C_t(1+r)^{-t}.
\]

For continuous cash flow rate \(c(t)\) and continuously compounded discount rate \(\rho\),

\[
PV=\int_0^T c(t)e^{-\rho t}\,dt.
\]

Do not mix these two compounding conventions without explicit conversion.

## 2. Twenty worked exercises with solutions

### Exercise 1 — Absolute and relative error

An approximation is \(1.001\) and the exact reference is \(1\). Find absolute and relative error.

**Solution:** Absolute error \(=0.001\); relative error \(=0.001/1=0.1\%\).

### Exercise 2 — Cancellation

Why is \(\sqrt{x^2+1}-x\) inaccurate for very large positive \(x\), and how can it be stabilized?

**Solution:** It subtracts nearly equal numbers. Rationalize:

\[
\sqrt{x^2+1}-x=\frac{1}{\sqrt{x^2+1}+x}.
\]

### Exercise 3 — Stable exponential discounting

Express \((1+r)^{-T}\) in a form suitable for small \(r\).

**Solution:** \(\exp[-T\operatorname{log1p}(r)]\), valid for \(r>-1\).

### Exercise 4 — Initial sign-change bracket

For \(f(x)=x^2-2\), does \([1,2]\) bracket a root?

**Solution:** \(f(1)=-1\), \(f(2)=2\). Yes, by continuity.

### Exercise 5 — Bisection count

How many bisections reduce a width of \(1\) to at most \(10^{-6}\)?

**Solution:** \(n\ge\lceil\log_2(10^6)\rceil=20\).

### Exercise 6 — First Newton step

Apply Newton's method to \(f(x)=x^2-2\) from \(x_0=1.5\).

**Solution:** \(x_1=1.5-(2.25-2)/3=17/12\approx1.4166666667\).

### Exercise 7 — Small derivative

Why might \(f'(x)\approx0\) invalidate a Newton proposal?

**Solution:** Division by a tiny derivative can produce an enormous step, domain violation, or numerical instability. Fall back to a bracketed method.

### Exercise 8 — Zero-coupon yield

Solve \(900=1000/(1+r)^2\).

**Solution:** \(r=\sqrt{1000/900}-1\approx0.054092553389\).

### Exercise 9 — Yield derivative

Differentiate \(f(r)=1000(1+r)^{-2}-900\).

**Solution:** \(f'(r)=-2000(1+r)^{-3}\).

### Exercise 10 — One-period IRR

Find the IRR for \((-1000,1100)\).

**Solution:** \(-1000+1100/(1+r)=0\) implies \(r=0.10\).

### Exercise 11 — Multiple IRRs

Find the IRRs for \((-100,230,-132)\).

**Solution:** Multiply NPV by \((1+r)^2\) to obtain \(100r^2-30r+2=0\), whose roots are \(0.10\) and \(0.20\).

### Exercise 12 — Failed wide bracket

Why does \([0,0.3]\) not bracket the two-IRR example by a sign-change test?

**Solution:** NPV is negative at both endpoints. Two roots can occur within an interval without opposite endpoint signs.

### Exercise 13 — Linear interpolation

Interpolate annual zero rates of 4% at one year and 5% at three years to two years.

**Solution:** \(z(2)=0.04+\frac{2-1}{3-1}(0.05-0.04)=0.045\).

### Exercise 14 — Discount-factor convention

Is interpolating zero rates equivalent to interpolating discount factors?

**Solution:** No. For annual effective compounding \(D(T)=(1+z(T))^{-T}\), a nonlinear transformation, so the two methods generally differ.

### Exercise 15 — Forward difference

Approximate \(f'(2)\) for \(f(x)=x^2\) with \(h=0.01\).

**Solution:** \(((2.01)^2-4)/0.01=4.01\), compared with exact \(4\).

### Exercise 16 — Central difference

Use the same \(f\), \(x\), and \(h\) with a central difference.

**Solution:** \(((2.01)^2-(1.99)^2)/0.02=4\), exactly in real arithmetic.

### Exercise 17 — Modified duration

Find the modified duration of a two-year zero-coupon bond at a 5% annual effective yield.

**Solution:** \(D_{\mathrm{mod}}=T/(1+r)=2/1.05\approx1.9047619048\).

### Exercise 18 — Trapezoidal integration

Apply a single trapezoid to \(f(t)=t\) over \([0,2]\).

**Solution:** \(2[f(0)+f(2)]/2=2\), equal to the exact integral.

### Exercise 19 — Simpson integration

Apply Simpson's rule with two panels to \(f(t)=t^3\) over \([0,2]\).

**Solution:** \((1/3)[f(0)+4f(1)+f(2)]=(0+4+8)/3=4\), the exact integral.

### Exercise 20 — Stopping diagnostics

What should be reported when a solver reaches its iteration budget without satisfying the tolerance?

**Solution:** Report `max_iter`, retain the last valid bracket and residual, and do not claim convergence.

## 3. Applied problems

**A. Yield solver comparison.** Derive the zero-coupon yield analytically, then solve it by bisection and safeguarded Newton. Compare iteration counts, bracket widths, and residuals. Explain why the comparison does not certify the implementation.

**B. Multiple-IRR investigation.** Use the cash flows \((-100,230,-132)\). Find both IRRs algebraically, identify separate sign-change brackets, and evaluate NPV at required returns of 5%, 15%, and 25%. Explain why an IRR-only decision rule is ambiguous.

**C. Yield-curve convention sensitivity.** From one- and three-year annual effective zero rates of 4% and 5%, estimate the two-year discount factor by interpolating rates, discount factors, and log discount factors. Label each result as model-derived.

**D. Numerical Greek/sensitivity study.** Approximate first and second derivatives of a zero-coupon price at a 5% yield using three step sizes. Compare with analytic duration and convexity, and discuss truncation versus cancellation.

**E. Continuous cash-flow valuation.** For a cash-flow rate of 100 currency units per year over five years at a 3% continuously compounded rate, compare trapezoidal and Simpson quadrature against

\[
PV=100\frac{-\operatorname{expm1}(-0.03\cdot5)}{0.03}.
\]

State the integration units and convergence limitations.

**F. Failure-case audit.** Test invalid rate domains, nonfinite objective values, no-sign-change brackets, zero derivatives, odd Simpson panel counts, and exhausted iteration budgets. Distinguish expected input errors from numerical stopping statuses.
