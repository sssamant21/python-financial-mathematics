## Learning objectives

Distinguish interpolation from extrapolation, derive linear and polynomial interpolation, estimate interpolation error under smoothness assumptions, recognize instability from high-degree polynomial fits, and apply interpolation to discount factors and financial yield curves without treating estimated market values as observations.

## 1. Interpolation versus extrapolation

Given distinct observations \((x_i,y_i)\), **interpolation** estimates values inside the observed \(x\)-range, while **extrapolation** evaluates beyond it. Neither establishes that the chosen functional form represents the economic mechanism. Interpolated points are model-derived, not newly observed data.

In finance, interpolation may estimate discount factors at intermediate maturities, while extrapolation might estimate values beyond the last liquid market quote. Extrapolation is especially sensitive to assumptions.

## 2. Linear interpolation

For \(x_0<x_1\), define

\[
t=\frac{x-x_0}{x_1-x_0},\qquad
L(x)=(1-t)y_0+ty_1.
\]

For \(x\in[x_0,x_1]\), \(0\le t\le1\); the estimate is a convex combination of endpoint values. Outside the interval, \(t<0\) or \(t>1\), so the same formula is an extrapolation and no longer preserves endpoint bounds.

**Example:** Observed annual effective zero rates are 4% at one year and 5% at three years. Linear interpolation of the quoted rates at two years gives 4.5%. This is an **assumed interpolation convention**, not an arbitrage-free yield-curve construction.

## 3. Linear interpolation error

If \(f\) is twice continuously differentiable on \([x_0,x_1]\), then for each interior \(x\), some \(\xi\in(x_0,x_1)\) satisfies

\[
f(x)-L(x)=\frac{f''(\xi)}{2}(x-x_0)(x-x_1).
\]

If \(|f''(x)|\le M\), the absolute interpolation error is bounded by

\[
|f(x)-L(x)|\le \frac{M}{8}(x_1-x_0)^2.
\]

This bound is conditional on the smoothness and curvature bound. It does not cover quote error or economic-model misspecification.

## 4. Piecewise linear interpolation

For increasing knots \(x_0<\cdots<x_n\), locate the interval containing the query and apply linear interpolation to that segment. A piecewise-linear curve is continuous but generally has discontinuous slopes at knots.

Duplicate maturities, unsorted data, nonfinite values, and unsupported extrapolation requests must be handled explicitly. Do not silently interpolate across incompatible instruments or quote conventions.

## 5. Polynomial interpolation

For \(n+1\) distinct nodes, there is a unique polynomial of degree at most \(n\) passing through the observations. In Lagrange form,

\[
P_n(x)=\sum_{i=0}^n y_i
\prod_{\substack{j=0\\j\ne i}}^n\frac{x-x_j}{x_i-x_j}.
\]

For sufficiently smooth \(f\), the error at \(x\) has the form

\[
f(x)-P_n(x)=\frac{f^{(n+1)}(\xi)}{(n+1)!}\prod_{i=0}^n(x-x_i)
\]

for a suitable \(\xi\) in the node interval. Higher degree is not automatically better: equally spaced high-degree interpolation can oscillate near interval boundaries (Runge phenomenon).

For production numerical work, barycentric formulations or established interpolation libraries are often preferable to direct Lagrange products.

## 6. Stable implementation considerations

When interpolating between nearby \(x\)-values, use the local segment to limit unnecessary arithmetic. Reject duplicate nodes to avoid division by zero. For high-degree polynomial interpolation, the simple reference implementation is educational and not a general numerical-stability guarantee.

Avoid comparing results with arbitrary exact equality. Use independent analytic benchmarks, carefully selected absolute/relative tolerances, and scale-aware checks.

## 7. Interpolating rates versus discount factors

A zero rate \(z(T)\) under an annual effective compounding convention corresponds to

\[
D(T)=(1+z(T))^{-T},\qquad z(T)>-1.
\]

**Interpolating zero rates**, **interpolating discount factors**, and **interpolating log discount factors** generally produce different intermediate results.

For example, given one-year rate \(0.04\) and three-year rate \(0.05\), let

\[
D_1=(1.04)^{-1},\qquad D_3=(1.05)^{-3}.
\]

At \(T=2\):

- Linear zero-rate interpolation: \(z_2=0.045\), \(D_2=(1.045)^{-2}\).
- Linear discount-factor interpolation: \(D_2=(D_1+D_3)/2\).
- Linear log-discount interpolation: \(D_2=\sqrt{D_1D_3}\).

These are distinct modeling conventions. No-arbitrage properties and curve construction require additional assumptions and constraints.

## 8. Extrapolation risk

A linear extrapolation can become negative, violate monotonicity, or imply implausible long-term rates. For discount factors, a negative value is invalid, and some curve shapes may imply economically inappropriate forward rates.

Always label extrapolated outputs, disclose the extrapolation policy, and perform scenario and sensitivity analysis. In the companion lab, extrapolation is **disabled by default**.

## 9. Independent validation

An independent checker should verify:

1. Exact recovery of supplied knots.
2. The linear midpoint identity.
3. A known quadratic polynomial benchmark.
4. Differences among rate, discount-factor, and log-discount conventions.
5. Explicit rejection of duplicate nodes and unauthorized extrapolation.
6. Appropriate finite-value and domain handling.

Matching interpolation nodes is necessary but not sufficient for a financially credible curve.

## 11. Twenty worked exercises

**1.** Define interpolation.  
*Solution:* Estimation within the observed independent-variable range.

**2.** Define extrapolation.  
*Solution:* Estimation beyond that range.

**3.** State the linear interpolation weight.  
*Solution:* \(t=(x-x_0)/(x_1-x_0)\).

**4.** State the linear interpolant.  
*Solution:* \(L(x)=(1-t)y_0+ty_1\).

**5.** What is \(t\) at the midpoint?  
*Solution:* \(1/2\).

**6.** Interpolate 4% at one year and 5% at three years to two years.  
*Solution:* 4.5%.

**7.** Does linear interpolation necessarily preserve endpoint bounds?  
*Solution:* Yes inside the segment, for scalar values, because it is a convex combination.

**8.** Does linear extrapolation preserve those bounds?  
*Solution:* Not generally.

**9.** State the conditional linear error bound.  
*Solution:* \(M(x_1-x_0)^2/8\) when \(|f''|\le M\).

**10.** What happens when the segment width is halved, under the same curvature bound?  
*Solution:* The bound is reduced by a factor of four.

**11.** What is a piecewise-linear curve?  
*Solution:* A curve formed by linear interpolation on consecutive knot intervals.

**12.** Is its derivative generally continuous at knots?  
*Solution:* No.

**13.** How many distinct knots determine a polynomial of degree at most two?  
*Solution:* Three.

**14.** State the Lagrange interpolation basis.  
*Solution:* \(\prod_{j\ne i}(x-x_j)/(x_i-x_j)\).

**15.** Why reject duplicate knots?  
*Solution:* They make the interpolation formula singular and the input ambiguous.

**16.** Is a higher polynomial degree always more accurate?  
*Solution:* No; oscillation and conditioning can worsen.

**17.** Define the annual-effective zero-rate discount factor.  
*Solution:* \(D(T)=(1+z(T))^{-T}\).

**18.** Are interpolated zero rates and interpolated discount factors equivalent?  
*Solution:* Not generally.

**19.** Why label extrapolated values?  
*Solution:* They depend on assumptions outside the observed domain.

**20.** What comes next?  
*Solution:* 18.7 — Numerical Differentiation and Integration.

## Implementation range policy and validation follow-up

For represented finite inputs, the lab evaluates the complete affine expression and the Lagrange polynomial with exact rational arithmetic before one conversion to float. This prevents intermediate differences, basis products or cancelling terms from destroying a representable answer. An exact zero is valid; final overflow and a nonzero value that rounds to zero raise ValueError. This is an arithmetic range policy, not a guarantee of good conditioning or financial suitability. Rational polynomial evaluation uses O(n²) basis steps and is intended for small educational data sets.

Section18.6 piecewise interpolation rejects extrapolation by default and permits it only when requested. The Section18.10 two-point interpolate helper retains mathematical extrapolation and shares Section18.6's affine implementation.
