## Objectives

Derive Newton's method; distinguish local quadratic convergence from global guarantees; diagnose small derivatives, domain violations, and stagnation; construct a bracket-preserving Newton–bisection hybrid; and independently verify financial yield and break-even rate solutions.

## 1. Root finding as a financial problem

A root solves \(f(x)=0\). For a zero-coupon instrument with face value \(F>0\), price \(P>0\), and maturity \(T>0\), solve

\[
f(r)=F(1+r)^{-T}-P=0,\qquad r>-1.
\]

The analytical benchmark is

\[
r^*=\exp\left(\frac{\log(F/P)}{T}\right)-1.
\]

For \(F=1000\), \(P=900\), and \(T=2\), \(r^*\approx0.054092553389\). An annual effective yield is not interchangeable with a nominal, continuously compounded, or period-specific yield.

## 2. Newton's method

Linearizing near \(x_k\),

\[
f(x)\approx f(x_k)+f'(x_k)(x-x_k).
\]

Setting the approximation to zero gives

\[
\boxed{x_{k+1}=x_k-\frac{f(x_k)}{f'(x_k)}}.
\]

The derivative must be nonzero and numerically reliable. For the zero-coupon model,

\[
f'(r)=-TF(1+r)^{-T-1}.
\]

For \(r>-1\), this derivative is strictly negative, so the model is strictly decreasing and has at most one root.

## 3. Local convergence

If \(f\) is sufficiently smooth near a simple root \(x^*\), \(f'(x^*)\ne0\), and the initial guess is sufficiently close, Newton's method is locally quadratically convergent:

\[
|e_{k+1}|\approx \left|\frac{f''(x^*)}{2f'(x^*)}\right||e_k|^2,
\qquad e_k=x_k-x^*.
\]

This is a local statement, not a guarantee from arbitrary initial guesses. Multiple roots, poor derivatives, or distant guesses can cause slow convergence, oscillation, or divergence.

## 4. Failure cases

- **Small or zero derivative:** the Newton step can become undefined or excessively large.
- **Out-of-domain step:** a proposed yield \(r\le-1\) invalidates the chosen real-valued discount model.
- **Unreliable derivative:** finite differences can suffer truncation and rounding errors.
- **Stagnation:** the new floating-point iterate equals the old iterate without meeting the acceptance criteria.
- **Misleading residual:** a small \(|f(x)|\) does not always mean a small root error for an ill-conditioned equation.
- **Multiple roots:** a local iteration may converge to a different root from the intended one.

## 5. Safeguarded Newton–bisection

Begin with a continuous function and a bracket \([a,b]\) with opposite-sign endpoint values. At each iteration:

1. Form a Newton proposal \(n=x-f(x)/f'(x)\) if the derivative is finite and nonzero. The companion rejects a nonfinite derivative with an evaluation error and falls back for an exactly zero derivative; it does not impose a separate small-derivative threshold.
2. Accept it only if it lies **strictly inside** the current bracket and is not too close to an endpoint.
3. Otherwise use the midpoint \(m=a+(b-a)/2\).
4. Evaluate the chosen candidate and retain the subinterval with opposite-sign endpoints.
5. Stop on an exact root, a sufficiently narrow bracket, iteration exhaustion, or floating-point stagnation.

The code companion uses a conservative central-region acceptance rule: Newton proposals must lie within the middle half of the bracket. This prevents repeated tiny endpoint progress and retains the robust bisection fallback. The procedure is an educational hybrid, not a replacement for a professionally maintained production solver.

## 6. Bracket invariant and error bound

Under continuity and the initial sign-change assumption, the retained interval always contains at least one root. A midpoint of the final bracket has an error no greater than half the final width relative to some bracketed root.

Unlike unrestricted Newton iterations, the safeguarded method reports a meaningful interval. The numerical certificate is still conditional on a correct function implementation and continuity.

## 7. Stopping rules

Use a width criterion such as

\[
b-a\le \mathrm{atol}+\mathrm{rtol}\max(|a|,|b|),
\]

with positive absolute tolerance and nonnegative relative tolerance. Also record the function residual and the number of iterations. Exact-zero evaluation may terminate early. Report a distinct status when a floating-point midpoint cannot divide the bracket or the iteration budget is exhausted.

A residual-only stopping rule is not a substitute for the bracket guarantee.

## 8. Stable discount evaluation

For \(r>-1\), evaluate the discount factor as

\[
(1+r)^{-T}=\exp(-T\log(1+r)),
\]

using \`math.log1p(r)\`. Its derivative can be computed as

\[
f'(r)=-\frac{T}{1+r}F\exp(-T\operatorname{log1p}(r)).
\]

The implementation checks that intermediate evaluations are finite. Overflow, underflow, and domain errors require explicit handling.

## 9. Second financial example: one-period NPV

For an initial investment of 1000 and one payment of 1100 in one year,

\[
NPV(r)=-1000+\frac{1100}{1+r},\qquad r>-1.
\]

Its derivative is \(-1100/(1+r)^2\), and the unique root is \(r=0.10\). A bracket \([0,0.2]\) allows independent comparison against the exact answer.

## 10. Independent mathematical checks

For each reported solution, independently:

- Recompute the original financial equation from its inputs.
- Check the domain and sign-change bracket.
- Check that the analytical benchmark lies within the reported bracket when one exists.
- Check the final bracket width and status.
- Compare the root with an analytical solution without invoking the solver's internal derivative or update routine.
- Test invalid brackets, zero derivatives, nonfinite function values, and iteration exhaustion.

The financial input assumptions, pricing conventions, and cash-flow correctness still require separate review.

## 12. Twenty worked exercises

**1.** What is a root?  
*Solution:* A point \(x^*\) for which \(f(x^*)=0\).

**2.** State Newton's update.  
*Solution:* \(x_{k+1}=x_k-f(x_k)/f'(x_k)\).

**3.** How is the update derived?  
*Solution:* Set the tangent-line approximation at \(x_k\) equal to zero.

**4.** What is a simple root?  
*Solution:* A root with \(f'(x^*)\ne0\).

**5.** What is Newton's typical local convergence order at a smooth simple root?  
*Solution:* Quadratic, given a sufficiently close initial guess.

**6.** Is Newton globally convergent from every initial point?  
*Solution:* No.

**7.** Why is a near-zero derivative dangerous?  
*Solution:* It can create an enormous or unstable update.

**8.** What is the valid yield domain?  
*Solution:* \(r>-1\).

**9.** State the zero-coupon pricing equation.  
*Solution:* \(P=F(1+r)^{-T}\).

**10.** Give the yield residual.  
*Solution:* \(f(r)=F(1+r)^{-T}-P\).

**11.** Differentiate the yield residual.  
*Solution:* \(f'(r)=-TF(1+r)^{-T-1}\).

**12.** Give the closed-form yield.  
*Solution:* \(r=(F/P)^{1/T}-1\).

**13.** Evaluate the yield for \(F=1000,P=900,T=2\).  
*Solution:* Approximately \(0.054092553389\).

**14.** Why is this yield root unique?  
*Solution:* Its residual is strictly decreasing on \(r>-1\).

**15.** What condition starts a safeguarded solve?  
*Solution:* A valid continuous function and a sign-changing bracket, or an exact endpoint root.

**16.** When should a Newton proposal be rejected?  
*Solution:* If its derivative is unusable or the proposal fails the bracket-interior acceptance rule.

**17.** What is the fallback step?  
*Solution:* The bracket midpoint.

**18.** What does the final bracket certify?  
*Solution:* Existence of a root inside it, conditional on continuity and the maintained sign change.

**19.** Solve \(-1000+1100/(1+r)=0\).  
*Solution:* \(r=0.1\).

**20.** What comes next?  
*Solution:* 18.5 — Multiple Roots and Cash-Flow Equations.

## Observed local order and hybrid scope

The local quadratic statement concerns Newton updates that are actually taken near a smooth simple root. The companion's strict middle-half acceptance rule can reject the next Newton proposal after an accurate step puts the current iterate near a bracket endpoint. Therefore this hybrid need not retain quadratic convergence, even close to a simple root.

[Observed proposal/order checks](../validation/chapter-18-newton-order-followup.md) compare24 isolated accepted steps against independent quadratic identities and100-digit root references, then audit three complete hybrid runs with actual callbacks. The isolated steps approach order2; these full runs each accept one Newton step followed by midpoint fallbacks. This is selected numerical evidence, not a universal convergence theorem or reviewer approval.
