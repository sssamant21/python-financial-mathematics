## Learning objectives

Define column space and null space, connect both to rank and dimension, identify attainable factor targets, construct all allocations that share a modeled exposure, and distinguish modeled risk neutrality from economic neutrality.

## 1. Column space: which targets are attainable?

For an \(m\times n\) matrix \(A=[a_1\ \cdots\ a_n]\), the **column space** is

\[
\operatorname{col}(A)=\{Ax:x\in\mathbb R^n\}
=\operatorname{span}\{a_1,\ldots,a_n\}\subseteq\mathbb R^m.
\]

In finance, each column can represent an instrument's exposure to \(m\) risk factors. The column space is the set of exposure vectors reachable through unrestricted signed quantities of those instruments.

A target \(b\) is attainable exactly when \(Ax=b\) has a solution, or equivalently

\[
\operatorname{rank}(A)=\operatorname{rank}([A\mid b]).
\]

## 2. Null space: what can change without changing modeled exposure?

The **null space** is

\[
\ker(A)=\{z\in\mathbb R^n:Az=0\}\subseteq\mathbb R^n.
\]

A null-space vector is an allocation change that leaves all modeled factor exposures unchanged. If \(x_0\) solves \(Ax=b\), every solution is

\[
\boxed{x=x_0+z,\qquad z\in\ker(A)}.
\]

This follows because \(A(x_0+z)=b+0=b\). Conversely, if \(Ax_1=Ax_0=b\), then \(A(x_1-x_0)=0\).

**Important:** Null-space neutrality applies only to the chosen linear factor model. It does not guarantee zero P&L, transaction costs, basis risk, nonlinear Greeks, liquidity risk, or stress losses.

## 3. Worked example: three instruments, two factors

Let rows represent RATE and CREDIT and columns represent BOND_A, BOND_B, BOND_C:

\[
A=\begin{bmatrix}2&1&4\\1&-1&2\end{bmatrix}.
\]

The first two columns are independent; the third equals twice the first. Therefore

\[
\operatorname{rank}(A)=2,\qquad
\operatorname{col}(A)=\mathbb R^2.
\]

For target \(b=(5,1)^\mathsf T\), one solution is

\[
x_0=(2,1,0)^\mathsf T.
\]

Since \(A(-2,0,1)^\mathsf T=0\), a null-space basis is

\[
z=(-2,0,1)^\mathsf T.
\]

Every solution is

\[
\boxed{x(t)=(2-2t,\ 1,\ t)^\mathsf T,\quad t\in\mathbb R}.
\]

For example, \(t=0\) gives \((2,1,0)\), and \(t=1\) gives \((0,1,1)\). Both produce exactly the same modeled RATE/CREDIT exposure.

## 4. Rank-nullity theorem

For \(A:\mathbb R^n\to\mathbb R^m\),

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{rank}(A)+\dim\ker(A)=n}.
\]

The example has \(n=3\), rank 2, and nullity 1. The number of instruments minus the number of independent exposure directions gives the number of independent null-space degrees of freedom.

This is a statement about vector-space dimensions, not about how many economically safe trades exist.

## 5. An unattainable target

Now consider

\[
B=\begin{bmatrix}1&2\\2&4\end{bmatrix}.
\]

Its column space is the line \(\{(s,2s):s\in\mathbb R\}\). The target \((3,6)\) is attainable, while \((3,7)\) is not. For the latter, a least-squares approximation can be considered in later sections, but no exact solution exists.

Its null space is generated by \((-2,1)^\mathsf T\), because \(B(-2,1)^\mathsf T=0\).

## 6. Financial interpretation

Null-space directions can be used to study **portfolio alternatives** without changing modeled first-order factor exposure. Different allocations may have different gross notional, instrument concentrations, bid-ask costs, funding needs, liquidity profiles, and unmodeled risks.

For the three-instrument example:

- \(x(0)=(2,1,0)\) has gross quantity 3.
- \(x(1)=(0,1,1)\) has gross quantity 2.
- \(x(-1)=(4,1,-1)\) has gross quantity 6 and includes a short.

The mathematically equivalent exposures do not imply economically equivalent portfolios. Optimizing among feasible allocations requires additional objectives and constraints.

## 7. Geometry and numerical caution

Column space lives in **factor space** \(\mathbb R^m\); null space lives in **instrument-position space** \(\mathbb R^n\). They are not generally subspaces of the same ambient space.

For nearly dependent columns, the estimated rank and nullity can vary with numerical tolerance. The teaching lab uses a small, explicitly scaled pivoted elimination routine. Production applications should use rank-revealing QR or SVD and consistent factor units.

## 9. Twenty worked exercises

**1.** Define column space.  
*Answer:* The set of all vectors \(Ax\).

**2.** Where does column space live for an \(m\times n\) matrix?  
*Answer:* In \(\mathbb R^m\).

**3.** Define null space.  
*Answer:* The set of all \(z\) satisfying \(Az=0\).

**4.** Where does null space live?  
*Answer:* In \(\mathbb R^n\).

**5.** What is the rank of the three-instrument reference matrix?  
*Answer:* 2.

**6.** What is its nullity?  
*Answer:* \(3-2=1\).

**7.** Give a null-space vector.  
*Answer:* \((-2,0,1)\).

**8.** Verify it lies in the null space.  
*Answer:* RATE: \(-4+4=0\); CREDIT: \(-2+2=0\).

**9.** Give one solution to \(Ax=(5,1)\).  
*Answer:* \((2,1,0)\).

**10.** Give another solution.  
*Answer:* \((0,1,1)\).

**11.** Describe every solution.  
*Answer:* \((2-2t,1,t)\), \(t\in\mathbb R\).

**12.** What is the column space of the reference matrix?  
*Answer:* All of \(\mathbb R^2\).

**13.** State rank-nullity.  
*Answer:* \(\operatorname{rank}(A)+\operatorname{nullity}(A)=n\).

**14.** Find the rank of \(\begin{bmatrix}1&2\\2&4\end{bmatrix}\).  
*Answer:* 1.

**15.** Find its nullity.  
*Answer:* 1.

**16.** Give a null-space basis for that matrix.  
*Answer:* \(\{(-2,1)\}\).

**17.** Is \((3,6)\) in its column space?  
*Answer:* Yes.

**18.** Is \((3,7)\) in its column space?  
*Answer:* No.

**19.** Does a zero modeled factor residual imply zero economic risk?  
*Answer:* No; unmodeled, nonlinear, liquidity, and transaction risks may remain.

**20.** Why should numerical null-space results disclose tolerance?  
*Answer:* Near-dependence can change estimated rank and nullity under different tolerances.

## 10. Applied assessment

Construct a three-instrument, two-factor exposure matrix. Identify its rank and nullity, verify \(Az=0\), produce two allocations with the same target exposure, and compare gross positions. Then analyze a rank-one matrix with an unattainable target. Reject missing labels, duplicate labels, nonfinite inputs, and inconsistent shapes.

**Acceptance:** reference rank 2, nullity 1, null vector \((-2,0,1)\), and equal achieved exposures for \((2,1,0)\) and \((0,1,1)\).
