## Learning objectives

Explain why raw exposure residuals cannot always be combined directly; formulate weighted least squares; derive its gradient and normal equations; distinguish importance weights from measurement precision; implement unit-aware standardized residuals; and separate optimization from risk-policy acceptance.

## 1. The unit problem

Suppose a hedge leaves residual RATE sensitivity of \(0.2\) basis-point-value units and CREDIT sensitivity of \(100\) currency units. Minimizing

\[
r_{\mathrm{RATE}}^2+r_{\mathrm{CREDIT}}^2
\]

without a unit convention is not economically meaningful: adding squares of unlike units does not produce an interpretable objective. Even when all factors are numerically represented as floats, the result depends on how the units were encoded.

For example, expressing a rate exposure in dollars rather than thousands of dollars changes its numerical magnitude by \(1000\). A model should not change its risk preference merely because of a reporting-unit conversion.

## 2. Standardized residuals

For factor \(i\), define a positive reference scale \(s_i\) with the same unit as the exposure residual \(r_i=b_i-(Ax)_i\). The standardized residual is

\[
z_i=\frac{r_i}{s_i}.
\]

Each \(z_i\) is dimensionless. Scales might represent approved risk tolerances, economic conversion factors, or estimated measurement uncertainty. Their interpretation must be documented; they are not interchangeable.

The standardized least-squares objective is

\[
\boxed{J(x)=\tfrac12\sum_{i=1}^{m}\left(\frac{b_i-(Ax)_i}{s_i}\right)^2}.
\]

A residual of one unit of approved scale contributes \(1/2\) to the objective, regardless of its original physical unit.

## 3. Weighted least squares

Let \(W=\operatorname{diag}(w_1,\ldots,w_m)\) with \(w_i>0\). Then

\[
\boxed{J_W(x)=\tfrac12(b-Ax)^\mathsf TW(b-Ax)}.
\]

Choosing \(w_i=1/s_i^2\) gives the standardized-residual objective. Define transformed data

\[
\widetilde A=W^{1/2}A,\qquad \widetilde b=W^{1/2}b.
\]

Then \(J_W(x)=\tfrac12\|\widetilde b-\widetilde Ax\|_2^2\), so a QR least-squares solver can operate on the transformed system.

## 4. Gradient, Hessian, and normal equations

For symmetric positive-definite \(W\),

\[
\nabla J_W(x)=A^\mathsf TW(Ax-b),\qquad
\nabla^2J_W(x)=A^\mathsf TWA.
\]

The weighted normal equations are

\[
\boxed{A^\mathsf TWAx=A^\mathsf TWb}.
\]

The residual satisfies \(A^\mathsf TWr=0\). This is **weighted orthogonality**, not generally ordinary Euclidean orthogonality. In production, solve the transformed system using QR/SVD instead of explicitly inverting \(A^\mathsf TWA\).

## 5. Worked financial example

One hypothetical instrument has RATE and CREDIT exposures \(a=(1,2)^\mathsf T\). The target is \(b=(3,7)^\mathsf T\).

Unweighted least squares gives

\[
q_{\mathrm{OLS}}=\frac{1(3)+2(7)}{1^2+2^2}=\frac{17}{5}=3.4.
\]

Now choose approved factor scales \(s_{\mathrm{RATE}}=1\) and \(s_{\mathrm{CREDIT}}=2\), giving \(W=\operatorname{diag}(1,1/4)\). The weighted optimum is

\[
q_W=\frac{1(3)/1^2+2(7)/2^2}{1^2/1^2+2^2/2^2}
=\frac{3+3.5}{1+1}
=\boxed{3.25}.
\]

The achieved exposures are \((3.25,6.5)\) and residuals \(r=(-0.25,0.5)\). The standardized residuals are \((-0.25,0.25)\), so

\[
J_W(q_W)=\tfrac12(0.25^2+0.25^2)=\boxed{0.0625}.
\]

The weighted first-order condition holds:

\[
1(-0.25)/1^2+2(0.5)/2^2=-0.25+0.25=0.
\]

The unweighted optimum is **not** optimal for this weighted objective: its standardized residuals are \((-0.4,0.1)\) and \(J_W(3.4)=0.085\).

## 6. Measurement precision versus policy priorities

If \(s_i\) is an estimated standard deviation of independent measurement error, \(w_i=1/s_i^2\) can represent inverse-variance weighting under a specified statistical model. If \(s_i\) is a risk appetite or limit, the same algebra instead represents a business preference. The interpretation changes even when the formula does not.

Correlated errors or factor relationships require a non-diagonal weight matrix; this introductory lab implements only diagonal positive weights. Covariance matrices, statistical estimation, and generalized least squares require additional assumptions beyond this section.

## 7. Units and conversion invariance

Suppose one factor is converted to a new unit by a positive conversion factor \(c_i\). If \(A_{i,:}\), \(b_i\), and \(s_i\) are all multiplied by \(c_i\), then

\[
\frac{c_i(b_i-(Ax)_i)}{c_i s_i}
=\frac{b_i-(Ax)_i}{s_i}.
\]

The standardized objective and optimal positions are unchanged. This is a crucial unit-consistency test. Converting the target but not the instrument sensitivities or scale is an invalid comparison.

## 8. Financial controls remain separate

A weighted minimum does not guarantee that each individual factor is within its hard tolerance. In the example, suppose RATE has a hard limit \(0.20\) and CREDIT has a hard limit \(0.60\). The weighted optimum has RATE residual magnitude \(0.25\), so the policy decision is **REJECT** even though the weighted objective is minimal.

Weight selection, factor units, and policy limits should be independently reviewed and versioned. Never use a low weighted objective to bypass hard risk constraints.

## 10. Worked exercises

**1.** Why can raw squared residuals be misleading?  
*Answer:* Factor residuals can have different units and scales.

**2.** Define a standardized residual.  
*Answer:* \(z_i=r_i/s_i\), with \(s_i>0\).

**3.** What is the unit of \(z_i\)?  
*Answer:* Dimensionless.

**4.** Write the diagonal weighted objective.  
*Answer:* \(\frac12\sum_iw_ir_i^2\).

**5.** How are weights related to scales?  
*Answer:* \(w_i=1/s_i^2\).

**6.** State the weighted gradient.  
*Answer:* \(A^\mathsf TW(Ax-b)\).

**7.** State the weighted Hessian.  
*Answer:* \(A^\mathsf TWA\).

**8.** State the weighted normal equations.  
*Answer:* \(A^\mathsf TWAx=A^\mathsf TWb\).

**9.** State weighted residual orthogonality.  
*Answer:* \(A^\mathsf TWr=0\).

**10.** What are the reference factor scales?  
*Answer:* RATE 1 and CREDIT 2.

**11.** What are the corresponding weights?  
*Answer:* 1 and \(1/4\).

**12.** Compute the weighted position.  
*Answer:* \(q=3.25\).

**13.** Compute achieved exposures.  
*Answer:* \((3.25,6.5)\).

**14.** Compute residuals \(b-Aq\).  
*Answer:* \((-0.25,0.5)\).

**15.** Compute standardized residuals.  
*Answer:* \((-0.25,0.25)\).

**16.** Compute the weighted objective.  
*Answer:* \(0.0625\).

**17.** Compute the weighted objective at \(q=3.4\).  
*Answer:* \(0.085\).

**18.** Does changing all three quantities \(A_i,b_i,s_i\) by the same unit factor change the optimum?  
*Answer:* No.

**19.** Does an inverse-variance interpretation automatically apply to business limits?  
*Answer:* No.

**20.** Does the weighted optimum pass RATE hard limit \(0.20\)?  
*Answer:* No; \(|r_{\mathrm{RATE}}|=0.25\).

## 11. Applied assessment

Build the reference weighted model; calculate the optimum and objective; verify the weighted gradient is zero; compare with the unweighted optimum; convert RATE exposure, target, scale, and hard limit from one reporting unit to another; verify position and objective invariance; and reject inconsistent factor labels, missing units, nonpositive scales, negative limits, and nonfinite inputs.

**Acceptance:** \(q=3.25\), \(J_W=0.0625\), standardized residuals \((-0.25,0.25)\), and policy REJECT under RATE limit \(0.20\), CREDIT limit \(0.60\).
