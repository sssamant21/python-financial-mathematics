## Learning objectives

Distinguish exact rank deficiency from near-collinearity; explain nonunique and unstable hedge positions; derive ridge/Tikhonov regularization; understand its bias–stability trade-off; implement a reproducible two-instrument lab; and recognize the limits of regularization as a financial control.

## 1. Rank deficiency

For \(A\in\mathbb R^{m\times n}\), rank deficiency means \(\operatorname{rank}(A)<n\). Its columns are linearly dependent, so some nonzero \(z\) satisfies \(Az=0\). If \(x_0\) is a least-squares minimizer, so is every \(x_0+z\) with \(z\in\ker(A)\). The achieved exposure \(Ax\) is unique, but the positions may not be.

Example: two instruments with identical exposure \(a_1=a_2=(1,2)^\mathsf T\) give

\[
A=\begin{bmatrix}1&1\\2&2\end{bmatrix},\qquad b=(3,6)^\mathsf T.
\]

Every portfolio satisfying \(x_1+x_2=3\) replicates the target. The null-space direction is \((1,-1)\). Allocations \((3,0)\), \((0,3)\), and \((1.5,1.5)\) match the same modeled exposure.

## 2. Near-collinearity

If columns are almost, but not exactly, dependent, the least-squares solution can be unique yet highly sensitive to small perturbations in exposures or target values. A small residual does not establish stable position estimates.

For example,

\[
A_\epsilon=\begin{bmatrix}1&1\\1&1+\epsilon\end{bmatrix}
\]

is invertible for every nonzero \(\epsilon\), but its condition number grows as \(\epsilon\to0\). Tiny measurement changes can cause large offsetting long/short positions. Risk reviews should examine coefficient magnitude, leverage, sensitivity to perturbations, and rank diagnostics.

## 3. Ridge/Tikhonov regularization

A common remedy adds a position penalty:

\[
\boxed{J_\lambda(x)=\tfrac12\|Ax-b\|_2^2+
\tfrac{\lambda}{2}\|x\|_2^2},\qquad\lambda>0.
\]

The gradient is

\[
\nabla J_\lambda(x)=A^\mathsf T(Ax-b)+\lambda x.
\]

The stationary condition becomes

\[
\boxed{(A^\mathsf TA+\lambda I)x=A^\mathsf Tb}.
\]

For \(\lambda>0\), the Hessian \(A^\mathsf TA+\lambda I\) is positive definite, so the penalized problem has a unique minimizer even if \(A\) is rank deficient.

**Important:** The penalty changes the objective; it does not recover an unknown uniquely correct unregularized allocation.

## 4. Worked example: two redundant hedges

Return to

\[
A=\begin{bmatrix}1&1\\2&2\end{bmatrix},\qquad b=(3,6)^\mathsf T.
\]

By symmetry, ridge assigns equal positions \(x_1=x_2=t\). The objective becomes

\[
J_\lambda(t,t)=\tfrac12\bigl[(3-2t)^2+(6-4t)^2\bigr]+\lambda t^2.
\]

Differentiating gives

\[
-10(3-2t)+2\lambda t=0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\boxed{t=\frac{15}{10+\lambda}}.
\]

For \(\lambda=2\), each position is \(1.25\), so the achieved exposures are \((2.5,5)\), residuals \(r=(0.5,1)\), and

\[
J_{\mathrm{data}}=\tfrac12(0.5^2+1^2)=0.625,
\]

\[
J_{\mathrm{penalty}}=\tfrac{2}{2}(1.25^2+1.25^2)=3.125,
\qquad J_{\mathrm{total}}=3.75.
\]

The regularized portfolio deliberately accepts some factor mismatch to reduce position magnitude. As \(\lambda\to0^+\), \(t\to1.5\), the minimum-Euclidean-norm exact allocation. As \(\lambda\to\infty\), \(t\to0\).

## 5. Generalized penalties and scaling

The penalty \(\|x\|_2^2\) assumes positions have comparable units and economic meaning. A generalized formulation is

\[
J(x)=\tfrac12\|W^{1/2}(Ax-b)\|_2^2+
\tfrac{\lambda}{2}\|L(x-x_{\mathrm{ref}})\|_2^2.
\]

Here \(W\) weights exposure residuals, \(L\) scales or couples position penalties, and \(x_{\mathrm{ref}}\) represents a reference portfolio. The first-order condition is

\[
(A^\mathsf TWA+\lambda L^\mathsf TL)x
=A^\mathsf TWb+\lambda L^\mathsf TLx_{\mathrm{ref}}.
\]

A positive \(\lambda\) alone does **not** ensure uniqueness for arbitrary singular \(L\); uniqueness requires the stacked system \(\begin{bmatrix}W^{1/2}A\\\sqrt{\lambda}L\end{bmatrix}\) to have full column rank.

## 6. Choosing a regularization strength

There is no universally correct \(\lambda\). Its interpretation depends on factor scaling, position units, and the penalty definition. Evaluate candidates using out-of-sample or scenario-based exposure errors, coefficient stability, position magnitude, stress tests, and trading constraints. Document the decision and avoid tuning only to make a historical fit look good.

## 7. Numerical implementation

A robust general implementation solves an augmented least-squares system:

\[
\begin{bmatrix}A\\\sqrt{\lambda}I\end{bmatrix}x
\approx
\begin{bmatrix}b\\0\end{bmatrix}
\]

using QR or SVD. This avoids explicitly forming \(A^\mathsf TA\). The companion standard-library lab instead implements the **closed-form symmetric two-instrument example** to keep its assumptions auditable. It does not claim to be a general ridge solver.

## 8. Risk controls remain distinct

Ridge is a soft penalty, not a hard cap on positions, leverage, concentration, funding, or liquidity. The optimized portfolio may still violate individual factor tolerances. In the example, hard residual limits RATE \(0.4\) and CREDIT \(1.1\) reject the solution because RATE residual \(0.5\) breaches its limit.

## 10. Twenty worked exercises

**1.** Define rank deficiency.  
*Answer:* Matrix rank is smaller than its number of columns.

**2.** What is a null-space direction?  
*Answer:* A nonzero position change \(z\) satisfying \(Az=0\).

**3.** Are least-squares coordinates unique under rank deficiency?  
*Answer:* Not necessarily.

**4.** Is the projected exposure unique?  
*Answer:* Yes.

**5.** Give a null-space vector for two identical columns.  
*Answer:* \((1,-1)\).

**6.** List two exact allocations when \(x_1+x_2=3\).  
*Answer:* \((3,0)\) and \((0,3)\).

**7.** Why is near-collinearity dangerous?  
*Answer:* Small input changes can cause large position changes.

**8.** Write the ridge objective.  
*Answer:* \(\frac12\|Ax-b\|^2+\frac{\lambda}{2}\|x\|^2\).

**9.** State its gradient.  
*Answer:* \(A^\mathsf T(Ax-b)+\lambda x\).

**10.** State its normal equations.  
*Answer:* \((A^\mathsf TA+\lambda I)x=A^\mathsf Tb\).

**11.** Why does ordinary ridge have a unique solution for \(\lambda>0\)?  
*Answer:* Its Hessian is positive definite.

**12.** For the example, give \(t(\lambda)\).  
*Answer:* \(15/(10+\lambda)\).

**13.** Compute positions for \(\lambda=2\).  
*Answer:* \((1.25,1.25)\).

**14.** Compute achieved exposures.  
*Answer:* \((2.5,5)\).

**15.** Compute signed residuals.  
*Answer:* \((0.5,1)\).

**16.** Compute the data-fit objective.  
*Answer:* \(0.625\).

**17.** Compute the penalty objective.  
*Answer:* \(3.125\).

**18.** Compute the total objective.  
*Answer:* \(3.75\).

**19.** What happens as \(\lambda\to0^+\)?  
*Answer:* Positions approach \((1.5,1.5)\).

**20.** Does ridge enforce hard factor limits?  
*Answer:* No.
