## Chapter assessment goals

This closing section assesses the full Chapter 13 learning sequence: spans and independence; rank, column space, and null space; orthogonal projection; least-squares objectives; QR factorization; weighted least squares; and ridge regularization. The exercises use small, independently verifiable financial factor-exposure models.

**Convention:** \(A\) contains instrument exposure columns, \(x\) contains positions, \(b\) is the target, and \(r=b-Ax\) is the signed residual. All numerical examples are hypothetical, not trading advice.

## Part A — Vector spaces and geometry

### Exercise 1 — Span

Given \(u=(1,2)\), is \(b=(3,6)\) in \(\operatorname{span}\{u\}\)?

**Solution:** Yes. \(b=3u\).

### Exercise 2 — Independence

Are \(u=(1,2)\) and \(v=(2,4)\) linearly independent?

**Solution:** No. \(v=2u\).

### Exercise 3 — Dimension

What is the dimension of \(\operatorname{span}\{(1,0),(0,1)\}\)?

**Solution:** Two.

### Exercise 4 — Column space

For \(A=\begin{bmatrix}1&1\\2&2\end{bmatrix}\), describe \(\operatorname{col}(A)\).

**Solution:** The line \(\operatorname{span}\{(1,2)\}\).

### Exercise 5 — Null space

Find a nonzero null-space vector for the matrix in Exercise 4.

**Solution:** \(z=(1,-1)\) since \(Az=0\).

### Exercise 6 — Rank-nullity

Find rank and nullity of that \(2\times2\) matrix.

**Solution:** Rank one; nullity one. Their sum is two.

### Exercise 7 — Orthogonal projection

Project \(b=(3,7)\) onto the line spanned by \(u=(1,2)\).

**Solution:** \(u^\mathsf Tb=17\), \(u^\mathsf Tu=5\), so \(p=(17/5,34/5)=(3.4,6.8)\).

### Exercise 8 — Residual orthogonality

Find \(r=b-p\) from Exercise 7 and verify orthogonality.

**Solution:** \(r=(-0.4,0.2)\); \(u^\mathsf Tr=-0.4+2(0.2)=0\).

### Exercise 9 — Pythagorean identity

Verify \(\|b\|_2^2=\|p\|_2^2+\|r\|_2^2\).

**Solution:** \(58=57.8+0.2\).

### Exercise 10 — Closest attainable exposure

Explain why \(p\) is the closest vector in the span to \(b\).

**Solution:** For every \(t\), \(\|b-tu\|^2=\|r\|^2+\|p-tu\|^2\ge\|r\|^2\).

## Part B — Least squares and stable numerical solving

### Exercise 11 — Objective

Write the ordinary least-squares objective for \(A=(1,2)^\mathsf T\), \(b=(3,7)^\mathsf T\), and scalar position \(q\).

**Solution:** \(J(q)=\frac12[(3-q)^2+(7-2q)^2]\).

### Exercise 12 — Gradient

Differentiate Exercise 11.

**Solution:** \(J'(q)=5q-17\).

### Exercise 13 — Minimizer

Find the optimal position.

**Solution:** \(q^*=17/5=3.4\).

### Exercise 14 — Objective value

Calculate the minimum objective.

**Solution:** \(J(q^*)=\frac12(0.16+0.04)=0.1\).

### Exercise 15 — Normal equations

State the normal equations for general \(A\) and \(b\).

**Solution:** \(A^\mathsf TAx=A^\mathsf Tb\).

### Exercise 16 — Convexity

Why is ordinary least squares convex?

**Solution:** Its Hessian \(A^\mathsf TA\) is positive semidefinite.

### Exercise 17 — QR

For full-column-rank \(A=QR\), what system solves least squares?

**Solution:** \(Rx=Q^\mathsf Tb\).

### Exercise 18 — Conditioning

Why are normal equations often less numerically stable than QR?

**Solution:** Their condition number is approximately the square of the original matrix's condition number.

### Exercise 19 — Financial two-hedge model

Let \(A=\begin{bmatrix}1&0\\0&1\\1&1\end{bmatrix}\) and \(b=(2,3,6)\). Find the least-squares positions.

**Solution:** \(x=(7/3,10/3)\).

### Exercise 20 — Residual and objective

For Exercise 19, find \(r\) and \(J\).

**Solution:** \(r=(-1/3,-1/3,1/3)\) and \(J=1/6\).

## Part C — Weighted least squares and regularization

### Exercise 21 — Standardized residuals

Define the standardized residual for factor \(i\) with positive scale \(s_i\).

**Solution:** \(z_i=r_i/s_i\).

### Exercise 22 — Weighted objective

Write the objective with diagonal weights \(w_i=1/s_i^2\).

**Solution:** \(J_W=\frac12\sum_i(r_i/s_i)^2\).

### Exercise 23 — Weighted optimum

For \(a=(1,2)\), \(b=(3,7)\), and scales \(s=(1,2)\), find the optimal scalar position.

**Solution:** \(q=(3+14/4)/(1+4/4)=3.25\).

### Exercise 24 — Weighted residual

Find the signed and standardized residuals for Exercise 23.

**Solution:** \(r=(-0.25,0.5)\); \(z=(-0.25,0.25)\).

### Exercise 25 — Weighted objective value

Compute the weighted objective at \(q=3.25\).

**Solution:** \(J_W=\frac12(0.25^2+0.25^2)=0.0625\).

### Exercise 26 — Unit conversion

If RATE exposure, target, and scale are all multiplied by 1000, does the weighted optimum change?

**Solution:** No; the standardized RATE residual remains unchanged.

### Exercise 27 — Rank-deficient replication

Two instruments have identical exposure \((1,2)\), and target \(b=(3,6)\). Describe all exact solutions.

**Solution:** \(x_1+x_2=3\).

### Exercise 28 — Ridge objective

Write the ordinary ridge objective and normal equations.

**Solution:** \(J_\lambda(x)=\frac12\|Ax-b\|^2+\frac{\lambda}{2}\|x\|^2\); \((A^\mathsf TA+\lambda I)x=A^\mathsf Tb\).

### Exercise 29 — Symmetric ridge allocation

For the redundant hedges in Exercise 27 and \(\lambda=2\), find the ridge positions.

**Solution:** Both positions equal \(15/(10+2)=1.25\).

### Exercise 30 — Ridge decomposition

Calculate achieved exposure, residual, data-fit objective, penalty, and total objective for Exercise 29.

**Solution:** Achieved \((2.5,5)\), residual \((0.5,1)\), data objective \(0.625\), penalty \(3.125\), total \(3.75\).

## Part D — Applied financial and governance cases

### Exercise 31 — Hard factor limits

For the two-hedge least-squares model in Exercise 19, suppose hard limits are RATE 0.40, CREDIT 0.40, and LIQUIDITY 0.25. What is the policy result?

**Solution:** REJECT. The LIQUIDITY residual magnitude \(1/3\) exceeds 0.25.

### Exercise 32 — Regularization is not a limit

Does ridge regularization guarantee a maximum gross notional or maximum position?

**Solution:** No. It is a soft penalty; hard limits require separate checks or constrained optimization.

### Exercise 33 — Model risk

Why can a mathematically zero residual fail to imply an economically perfect hedge?

**Solution:** The modeled factors may omit relevant risks, use uncertain sensitivities, or ignore costs, liquidity, basis risk, and future exposure changes.

### Exercise 34 — Solver selection

A matrix is nearly rank deficient. Which methods help diagnose numerical rank, and what should an educational solver do if it cannot resolve rank reliably?

**Solution:** Rank-revealing QR or SVD; the educational solver should reject rather than silently return an unstable allocation.

### Exercise 35 — Evidence checklist

Name five fields required in a reproducible hedge-analysis report.

**Solution:** Example: factor/instrument labels, exposure units, solver and tolerance, positions and residuals, objective and risk-policy decision.
