## Learning objectives

Compute L1, Euclidean L2, and infinity norms; understand norm axioms and induced distances; evaluate portfolio exposure deviations; distinguish Euclidean distance from statistical tracking error; and implement label-safe calculations.

## 1. Vector norms

For \(\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb R^n\),

\[
\boxed{\|\mathbf{x}\|_1=\sum_{i=1}^n|x_i|},\qquad
\boxed{\|\mathbf{x}\|_2=\sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^nx_i^2}},\qquad
\boxed{\|\mathbf{x}\|_\infty=\max_i|x_i|}.
\]

For \(\mathbf{x}=(3,-4,12)\), the three norms are \(19\), \(13\), and \(12\), respectively. L1 measures total absolute magnitude; L2 measures Euclidean magnitude; infinity norm measures the largest component magnitude.

A norm obeys nonnegativity and definiteness, absolute homogeneity \(\|a\mathbf{x}\|=|a|\|\mathbf{x}\|\), and the triangle inequality \(\|\mathbf{x}+\mathbf{y}\|\le\|\mathbf{x}\|+\|\mathbf{y}\|\). The L2 norm equals \(\sqrt{\mathbf{x}^{\mathsf T}\mathbf{x}}\); its triangle inequality follows from Cauchy–Schwarz.

## 2. Distances and alignment

The norm-induced distance is

\[
\boxed{d_p(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})=\|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y}\|_p.}
\]

Vectors must share component labels, axis, and units. An asset exposure feed ordered ALPHA/BETA/GAMMA must be aligned to another feed ordered GAMMA/ALPHA/BETA before subtracting. Equal length does not imply compatibility. For two-dimensional vectors \((1,2)\) and \((4,6)\), distances are \(d_1=7\), \(d_2=5\), and \(d_\infty=4\).

## 3. Financial exposure comparison

Let actual and benchmark USD exposures, ordered ALPHA/BETA/GAMMA, be

\[
\mathbf e=(100000,-20000,40000),\qquad
\mathbf b=(90000,-10000,50000).
\]

Then \(\Delta=\mathbf e-\mathbf b=(10000,-10000,-10000)\) USD. Therefore

\[
\|\Delta\|_1=30000\text{ USD},\quad
\|\Delta\|_2=10000\sqrt3\text{ USD},\quad
\|\Delta\|_\infty=10000\text{ USD}.
\]

The signed difference sums to \(-10000\) USD, concealing offsetting deviations. L1 may measure aggregate absolute mismatch, L2 geometric deviation, and infinity norm the worst single-asset mismatch. These are not interchangeable risk measures.

A dimensionless relative L2 deviation is \(\|\mathbf e-\mathbf b\|_2/\|\mathbf b\|_2\), defined only for nonzero benchmark norm. A zero benchmark requires a separately specified absolute threshold.

## 4. Financial interpretation and limitations

A vector of USD exposures has USD-valued norms. Do not combine USD, shares, DV01, and percentages in an unscaled Euclidean vector. The L1 difference of positions is not necessarily realized turnover; execution prices, trading rules, and cash flows matter. A Euclidean distance of active returns is not statistical **tracking error**, which is ordinarily the standard deviation of active returns with explicit sampling and annualization assumptions. Covariance-aware portfolio volatility involves \(\sqrt{\mathbf w^\mathsf T\Sigma\mathbf w}\), not generally \(\|\mathbf w\|_2\).

## 6. Exercises with worked solutions

**1.** Find \(\|(3,-4,12)\|_1\). *Solution:* 19.

**2.** Find \(\|(3,-4,12)\|_2\). *Solution:* 13.

**3.** Find \(\|(3,-4,12)\|_\infty\). *Solution:* 12.

**4.** Find the L2 norm of the zero vector. *Solution:* 0.

**5.** Find \(\|2(3,-4)\|_2\). *Solution:* 10.

**6.** Find \(\|-(3,-4)\|_1\). *Solution:* 7.

**7.** Compute \(d_2((1,2),(4,6))\). *Solution:* 5.

**8.** Compute \(d_1((1,2),(4,6))\). *Solution:* 7.

**9.** Compute \(d_\infty((1,2),(4,6))\). *Solution:* 4.

**10.** Find the L1 magnitude of \((10000,-10000,-10000)\). *Solution:* 30000 USD.

**11.** Find its L2 magnitude. *Solution:* \(10000\sqrt3\) USD.

**12.** Interpret an infinity-norm limit of 10000 USD. *Solution:* No individual absolute component exceeds 10000 USD.

**13.** Why can signed sums understate deviations? *Solution:* Positive and negative components can cancel.

**14.** Is Euclidean distance automatically tracking-error volatility? *Solution:* No; statistical volatility requires a defined return series and sampling convention.

## Relative-distance validation follow-up

The relative distance is `norm(actual - benchmark) / norm(benchmark)` using the same selected norm in numerator and denominator; it is generally asymmetric. For actual `(1,2)` and benchmark `(4,6)`, the L1, L2 and infinity values are `0.7`, `5/sqrt(52)` and `2/3`. Reversing the inputs gives `7/3`, `5/sqrt(5)` and `2`.

The checker now asserts these values across selected common scales and label permutations, along with self-distance zero and zero-actual distance one. A zero benchmark raises `ValueError`, including zero-versus-zero. The smallest positive subnormal benchmark is not treated as zero, while an unrepresentable relative ratio is rejected by existing finite validation. These checks do not establish an all-range, scale-invariant implementation for arbitrary vectors.
