## Learning objectives

Define and compute the Euclidean inner product; interpret units and signs; apply label-safe inner products to portfolio market value, weighted returns, and factor exposures; prove core identities; and recognize when dot-product assumptions are invalid.

## 1. Definition

For \(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}\in\mathbb R^n\), their **Euclidean inner product** (dot product) is

\[
\boxed{\langle\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}\rangle=\mathbf{x}^{\mathsf T}\mathbf{y}=\sum_{i=1}^n x_i y_i.}
\]

It produces a **scalar**, not a vector. Each component pair must refer to the same label. Equal vector length alone is insufficient.

For \(\mathbf{x}=(2,3,4)^\mathsf T\) and \(\mathbf{y}=(5,-1,2)^\mathsf T\),

\[
\mathbf{x}^{\mathsf T}\mathbf{y}=2(5)+3(-1)+4(2)=\boxed{15}.
\]

## 2. Geometric interpretation

For nonzero vectors,

\[
\mathbf{x}^{\mathsf T}\mathbf{y}=\|\mathbf{x}\|_2\|\mathbf{y}\|_2\cos\theta.
\]

The sign indicates whether the angle is acute, right, or obtuse. Zero inner product means orthogonality in Euclidean geometry. Financial interpretations depend on units and meaning: zero price-holdings dot product does not automatically mean a risk-free or economically neutral portfolio.

## 3. Portfolio market value

Let \(\mathbf{p}\) be prices in USD/share and \(\mathbf{q}\) holdings in shares. Then

\[
\boxed{V=\mathbf{p}^{\mathsf T}\mathbf{q}=\sum_i p_iq_i}
\]

has units USD. For assets ALPHA/BETA/GAMMA:

| Asset | Price (USD/share) | Holdings (shares) | Position value (USD) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| ALPHA | 100 | 2 | 200 |
| BETA | 50 | 4 | 200 |
| GAMMA | 25 | 10 | 250 |

Thus \(V=200+200+250=\boxed{650\text{ USD}}\).

The holdings feed may be in a different order. It must be realigned by asset label before the dot product.

## 4. Signed holdings and net versus gross value

Short holdings are negative. With \(\mathbf q=(2,-4,10)\), the signed market value is

\[
V=100(2)+50(-4)+25(10)=\boxed{250\text{ USD}}.
\]

Gross notional is instead \(\sum_i|p_iq_i|=200+200+250=\boxed{650\text{ USD}}\). Signed and gross amounts answer different questions; neither alone captures margin, leverage, or full risk.

## 5. Weighted portfolio return

Let \(\mathbf{w}=(0.5,0.3,0.2)\) be portfolio weights and \(\mathbf{r}=(0.02,-0.01,0.04)\) be asset returns over the same period. Then

\[
R_p=\mathbf w^\mathsf T\mathbf r
=0.5(0.02)+0.3(-0.01)+0.2(0.04)
=\boxed{0.015=1.5\%}.
\]

This simple weighted-return identity assumes weights and returns refer to the same assets, horizon, currency basis, and return convention; in common applications, weights are beginning-of-period capital weights with no external flows or rebalancing inside the measurement interval. If weights sum to one, the portfolio is fully allocated on a net basis, but leverage and shorts may still be present.

## 6. Factor exposures and linear sensitivities

For positions with factor sensitivities \(\mathbf{s}\) in USD per unit factor move, and a factor-shock vector \(\Delta\mathbf f\) in factor units, the linearized portfolio P&L is

\[
\boxed{\Delta V_{\mathrm{linear}}\approx\mathbf{s}^{\mathsf T}\Delta\mathbf f.}
\]

Example: sensitivities \((10000,-5000)\) USD per unit and factor shocks \((0.01,-0.02)\) units yield \(10000(0.01)+(-5000)(-0.02)=\boxed{200\text{ USD}}\). This is a first-order approximation, not a full nonlinear repricing.

## 7. Algebraic properties

For vectors \(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y},\mathbf{z}\in\mathbb R^n\) and scalar \(a\),

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbf{x}^\mathsf T\mathbf{y}&=\mathbf{y}^\mathsf T\mathbf{x},\\
(\mathbf{x}+\mathbf{y})^\mathsf T\mathbf{z}
&=\mathbf{x}^\mathsf T\mathbf{z}+\mathbf{y}^\mathsf T\mathbf{z},\\
(a\mathbf{x})^\mathsf T\mathbf{y}&=a(\mathbf{x}^\mathsf T\mathbf{y}),\\
\mathbf{x}^\mathsf T\mathbf{x}&=\sum_i x_i^2\ge0.
\end{aligned}
\]

Furthermore, \(\mathbf{x}^\mathsf T\mathbf{x}=0\) if and only if \(\mathbf{x}=\mathbf0\). In floating-point calculations, use tolerant checks for algebraic identities, and reject nonfinite intermediate results.

## 8. Label and unit contract

The lab enforces unique asset labels, exact axis compatibility, and explicit expected units. It aligns the second vector to the first vector's label order. A generic dot product can return a numeric scalar, but its unit must be defined by the calling financial model.

Examples:

- USD/share × shares = USD.
- dimensionless weights × dimensionless simple returns = dimensionless return.
- USD/factor-unit × factor-unit = USD.
- USD exposure × EUR exposure is not a meaningful monetary sum without a documented conversion and model.

Do not infer semantic compatibility merely from matching array sizes or equal unit strings.

## 10. Exercises with worked solutions

**1.** Compute \((2,3,4)\cdot(5,-1,2)\).  
*Solution:* \(10-3+8=\boxed{15}\).

**2.** What is the output dimension of an inner product?  
*Solution:* A scalar.

**3.** Compute \((1,2)\cdot(3,4)\).  
*Solution:* \(3+8=\boxed{11}\).

**4.** Compute \((2,-3)\cdot(2,-3)\).  
*Solution:* \(4+9=\boxed{13}\).

**5.** What is the dot product of \((1,0)\) and \((0,1)\)?  
*Solution:* Zero; the vectors are orthogonal.

**6.** Compute the market value of 2 shares at \$100 and 4 shares at \$50.  
*Solution:* \(200+200=\boxed{\$400}\).

**7.** Compute the three-asset example's market value.  
*Solution:* \(\boxed{\$650}\).

**8.** What is the signed value for holdings \((2,-4,10)\) and prices \((100,50,25)\)?  
*Solution:* \(\boxed{\$250}\).

**9.** What is gross notional for Exercise 8?  
*Solution:* \(\boxed{\$650}\).

**10.** Compute the weighted return for weights \((0.5,0.3,0.2)\) and returns \((0.02,-0.01,0.04)\).  
*Solution:* \(\boxed{1.5\%}\).

**11.** Why must the weights and returns use the same period?  
*Solution:* Otherwise their componentwise products do not represent a coherent portfolio-period return.

**12.** Compute \( (10000,-5000)\cdot(0.01,-0.02)\).  
*Solution:* \(\boxed{200}\) USD of linearized P&L.

**13.** Does a zero dot product prove a portfolio is risk-free?  
*Solution:* No; it is a geometric statement, not a comprehensive risk assessment.

**14.** Why is a label-safe dot product preferable to positional multiplication?  
*Solution:* It preserves instrument identity and detects missing or unexpected assets.
