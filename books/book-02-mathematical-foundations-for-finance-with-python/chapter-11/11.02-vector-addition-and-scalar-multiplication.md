## Learning objectives

Add and subtract vectors componentwise; scale vectors by real numbers; interpret the operations financially; verify vector-space identities; and enforce financial label, axis, and unit compatibility.

## 1. Vector addition

For vectors \(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}\in\mathbb R^n\),

\[
\boxed{\mathbf{x}+\mathbf{y}=(x_1+y_1,\ldots,x_n+y_n)^\mathsf T.}
\]

Addition is defined only when both vectors have the same dimension. In finance, they must also refer to the **same labeled axis and compatible units**. A three-component holdings vector for ALPHA/BETA/GAMMA cannot be safely added by position to a holdings vector for a different asset universe.

**Example:** A portfolio holds \(\mathbf q=(10,5,8)^\mathsf T\) shares. A trade vector \(\Delta\mathbf q=(2,-1,4)^\mathsf T\) shares changes the holdings to

\[
\mathbf q_{\mathrm{new}}=\mathbf q+\Delta\mathbf q
=(12,4,12)^\mathsf T\text{ shares}.
\]

Negative trade components mean sales. A negative resulting holding can represent a short position, subject to the portfolio's mandate.

## 2. Vector subtraction

\[
\boxed{\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y}=(x_1-y_1,\ldots,x_n-y_n)^\mathsf T.}
\]

Subtraction can represent an exposure difference between actual and target holdings, or the net change between two portfolio snapshots. The snapshots must use comparable units, valuation conventions, and asset identities.

**Example:** Actual exposure \((200,300,150)\) USD and target \((180,320,150)\) USD imply a deviation \((20,-20,0)\) USD.

## 3. Scalar multiplication

For scalar \(a\in\mathbb R\),

\[
\boxed{a\mathbf{x}=(ax_1,\ldots,ax_n)^\mathsf T.}
\]

Multiplication by a dimensionless scalar preserves the vector's units. For example, scaling a position-value vector by \(1.10\) represents a hypothetical 10% increase in every position value. This is a **scenario assumption**, not a prediction.

- \(0\mathbf{x}=\mathbf0\): all components become zero.
- \(1\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{x}\): the identity.
- \((-1)\mathbf{x}=-\mathbf{x}\): direction reverses.
- \(0.5\mathbf{x}\): halves every component.

If the scalar has physical or financial units, the result's units change. The companion lab deliberately accepts **dimensionless scalars** to keep its unit contract explicit.

## 4. Linear combinations

A linear combination is an expression

\[
\boxed{a\mathbf{x}+b\mathbf{y}.}
\]

In portfolio construction, two compatible holdings vectors might represent independently managed sleeves. If \(\mathbf q_A=(4,2,0)\) and \(\mathbf q_B=(0,3,6)\), then \(2\mathbf q_A+\mathbf q_B=(8,7,6)\). Interpret coefficients as dimensionless sleeve multipliers.

Linear combinations require all component vectors to share an axis and compatible units. A return vector and a holdings vector cannot be combined by addition merely because they have equal lengths.

## 5. Algebraic laws

For vectors \(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y},\mathbf{z}\in\mathbb R^n\) and real scalars \(a,b\):

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbf{x}+\mathbf{y}&=\mathbf{y}+\mathbf{x},\\
(\mathbf{x}+\mathbf{y})+\mathbf{z}&=\mathbf{x}+(\mathbf{y}+\mathbf{z}),\\
\mathbf{x}+\mathbf0&=\mathbf{x},\\
\mathbf{x}+(-\mathbf{x})&=\mathbf0,\\
a(\mathbf{x}+\mathbf{y})&=a\mathbf{x}+a\mathbf{y},\\
(a+b)\mathbf{x}&=a\mathbf{x}+b\mathbf{x},\\
a(b\mathbf{x})&=(ab)\mathbf{x}.
\end{aligned}
\]

These are exact mathematical identities over real numbers. Floating-point implementations may differ slightly because arithmetic is rounded and addition is not exactly associative in binary floating point. Use numerical tolerances in tests, and avoid treating a tolerance as permission to misalign labels.

## 6. Financial example: aggregating two desks

Two desks report USD exposure to the same assets, but in different label orders:

| Asset | Desk A (USD) | Desk B (USD) |
|---|---:|---:|
| ALPHA | 100,000 | 50,000 |
| BETA | -20,000 | 30,000 |
| GAMMA | 40,000 | -10,000 |

The aggregate exposure is

\[
\mathbf e_A+\mathbf e_B=
(150000,10000,30000)^\mathsf T\text{ USD}.
\]

The total signed exposure is 190,000 USD. It is **not** a gross exposure measure: the latter sums absolute components, and netting may be disallowed across entities or risk buckets.

## 7. Why labels are essential

Suppose Desk B's source order is GAMMA/ALPHA/BETA. Direct positional addition would produce \((90000,30000,70000)\) USD, a wrong allocation by asset, even though the signed sum happens to remain 190,000 USD. **Aggregate totals alone cannot detect misalignment.** Reorder by label before componentwise operations.

The lab rejects duplicate labels, missing assets, mismatched axes, and mismatched units. It preserves the left-hand vector's label order in the result.

## 8. Units, currencies, and risk controls

Do not add USD exposures to EUR exposures without an explicit FX conversion and valuation timestamp. Do not add dollar-value exposures to shares, returns, or DV01. Likewise, a vector of asset exposures and a vector of time-series cash flows occupy different axes even if both are three-dimensional.

The Python reference implementation uses exact string equality for units and axis metadata. A production system would require stronger unit typing, identifier governance, FX conversion policy, and lifecycle controls.

## 10. Exercises with worked solutions

**1.** Add \((2,4,6)\) and \((1,3,5)\).  
*Solution:* \((3,7,11)\).

**2.** Subtract \((1,3,5)\) from \((2,4,6)\).  
*Solution:* \((1,1,1)\).

**3.** Multiply \((2,-3,4)\) by 3.  
*Solution:* \((6,-9,12)\).

**4.** Multiply \((2,-3,4)\) by \(-1\).  
*Solution:* \((-2,3,-4)\).

**5.** Find \(2(1,2)+(3,4)\).  
*Solution:* \((5,8)\).

**6.** Compute \(0(7,-2)\).  
*Solution:* \((0,0)\).

**7.** Show \(\mathbf x+\mathbf0=\mathbf x\) for \(\mathbf x=(5,-1)\).  
*Solution:* \((5,-1)+(0,0)=(5,-1)\).

**8.** Show \(\mathbf x+(-\mathbf x)=\mathbf0\) for \(\mathbf x=(5,-1)\).  
*Solution:* \((5,-1)+(-5,1)=(0,0)\).

**9.** Combine holdings \((10,5,8)\) and trades \((2,-1,4)\).  
*Solution:* \((12,4,12)\) shares.

**10.** Find the difference between actual \((200,300,150)\) and target \((180,320,150)\).  
*Solution:* \((20,-20,0)\) USD.

**11.** Combine Desk A and Desk B in the worked exposure example.  
*Solution:* \((150000,10000,30000)\) USD.

**12.** Why can the signed total conceal label misalignment?  
*Solution:* Permutation changes which asset owns each exposure without necessarily changing the sum.

**13.** Can USD and EUR exposures be added directly?  
*Solution:* No; convert using a specified FX rate and valuation convention first.

**14.** Why does Python floating-point arithmetic require tolerant comparisons?  
*Solution:* Rounding means mathematically equivalent evaluation orders may yield slightly different binary results.

## 11. Applied assessment

Receive Desk A exposures in ALPHA/BETA/GAMMA order and Desk B exposures in GAMMA/ALPHA/BETA order. Validate and align labels, calculate combined USD exposures, calculate the signed total, and report a deliberately incorrect positional result. Then apply a 10% stress multiplier and verify distributivity:

\[
1.1(\mathbf e_A+\mathbf e_B)\approx1.1\mathbf e_A+1.1\mathbf e_B.
\]
