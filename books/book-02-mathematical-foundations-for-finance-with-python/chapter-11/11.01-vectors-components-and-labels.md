## Learning objectives

Represent financial observations as ordered vectors; distinguish a vector's components from its labels; check units and dimensions; implement label-safe alignment in Python; and explain why a correct numerical calculation can still be financially wrong when assets are misaligned.

## 1. What is a vector?

A vector in \(\mathbb R^n\) is an ordered tuple of \(n\) real components:

\[
\mathbf{x}=(x_1,\ldots,x_n)^\mathsf T.
\]

The superscript \(\mathsf T\) denotes transpose: a column-vector representation. A vector's **dimension** is its number of components, not the number of decimal places or the physical units of the components.

In finance, vectors represent prices, holdings, returns, cash flows, factor sensitivities, and other aligned collections. For example,

\[
\mathbf p=\begin{bmatrix}100\\50\\25\end{bmatrix}
\quad\text{and}\quad
\mathbf q=\begin{bmatrix}2\\4\\10\end{bmatrix}
\]

might represent three asset prices (currency/share) and holdings (shares). Their order must refer to the same assets.

## 2. Components, coordinates, and labels

Mathematical notation uses positions \(1,\ldots,n\). Production data uses **labels** such as asset identifiers. For example,

| Asset label | Price (USD/share) | Holdings (shares) |
|---|---:|---:|
| ALPHA | 100 | 2 |
| BETA | 50 | 4 |
| GAMMA | 25 | 10 |

A position-only vector \((100,50,25)\) loses the labels unless an external contract preserves them. A labeled vector can be modeled as a mapping from a fixed, ordered set of unique asset labels to numeric values.

**Alignment invariant:** before componentwise operations, the label at every position must identify the same financial instrument, period, scenario, or factor on both sides.

## 3. Financial quantities and units

A vector of prices has components measured in currency/share. A vector of holdings has components measured in shares. Multiplying corresponding components yields position market values in currency:

\[
\mathbf v=
\begin{bmatrix}
100(2)\\50(4)\\25(10)
\end{bmatrix}
=
\begin{bmatrix}
200\\200\\250
\end{bmatrix}\text{ USD}.
\]

The total is \(200+200+250=\boxed{650\text{ USD}}\). This sum is a preview of an inner product, developed in Section 11.3.

Never add a price vector directly to a holdings vector: although both may contain three numbers, their units are incompatible.

## 4. Dimension and shape

Two vectors with three components belong to \(\mathbb R^3\), but that alone does not make them semantically compatible. Prices for ALPHA/BETA/GAMMA cannot be safely paired by position with holdings ordered GAMMA/ALPHA/BETA.

A one-dimensional Python sequence does not intrinsically distinguish a mathematical row vector from a column vector. Explicit conventions and validation are required. Later chapters introduce matrices and shape-aware numerical computing.

## 5. Why label alignment matters

Suppose the holdings arrive in this order:

| Asset | Holdings |
|---|---:|
| GAMMA | 10 |
| ALPHA | 2 |
| BETA | 4 |

Naively multiplying prices and holdings by list position gives

\[
100(10)+50(2)+25(4)=1200,
\]

which is **incorrect**. Aligning by asset label restores the correct total of 650. The numerical arithmetic is valid, but the financial interpretation is wrong.

The companion lab rejects duplicate labels, missing labels, nonfinite values, and ambiguous ordering. It aligns holdings to the price labels before valuation.

## 6. Returns and exposures as vectors

A simple-return vector may be

\[
\mathbf r=(0.02,-0.01,0.03)^\mathsf T.
\]

Returns are dimensionless ratios. A vector of portfolio weights might be

\[
\mathbf w=(0.4,0.3,0.3)^\mathsf T.
\]

Weights are also dimensionless, but returns and weights are **not the same kind of quantity**. Their eventual inner product estimates a weighted portfolio return under specified assumptions, but componentwise addition of these vectors has no usual financial interpretation.

Risk exposures can use still other units: currency per basis point, factor loading, shares, or delta-equivalent units. Always record the unit and meaning of each vector.

## 7. Time and scenario axes

Labels need not be assets. A vector \((C_1,C_2,C_3)\) could represent cash flows at years 1, 2, and 3. In that case, time labels and payment conventions matter. A vector of scenarios may instead be labeled base, stress, and recovery. Never silently mix axes.

**Data contract:** record the axis type, label order, units, valuation timestamp, currency, and missing-data policy.

## 9. Exercises and worked solutions

**1.** What is the dimension of \((3,5,7,9)\)?  
*Solution:* Four.

**2.** Write a column vector with components 2, 4, and 6.  
*Solution:* \((2,4,6)^\mathsf T\).

**3.** What does the label ALPHA identify?  
*Solution:* The asset corresponding to a component; it is not the numeric value.

**4.** What are the units of a stock-price vector?  
*Solution:* Typically currency/share.

**5.** What are the units of a holdings vector?  
*Solution:* Shares.

**6.** What are the units of price times holdings?  
*Solution:* Currency.

**7.** Compute the market value of 2 shares at \$100/share.  
*Solution:* \$200.

**8.** Compute the total value of positions 200, 200, and 250 USD.  
*Solution:* \$650.

**9.** Is \((100,50,25)\) aligned with holdings ordered GAMMA, ALPHA, BETA?  
*Solution:* Not when the prices are ordered ALPHA, BETA, GAMMA.

**10.** What is the incorrect positional result in the example?  
*Solution:* \(100(10)+50(2)+25(4)=1200\).

**11.** Why is the incorrect result dangerous?  
*Solution:* It is numerically plausible while violating asset identity.

**12.** Can price and holdings vectors be added meaningfully?  
*Solution:* No; their units are incompatible.

**13.** Are simple returns dimensionless?  
*Solution:* Yes, they are ratios.

**14.** Name two necessary vector metadata fields.  
*Solution:* Label order and units (also axis type, currency, timestamp, or missing-data policy).

## 10. Applied assessment

Receive two independent feeds: prices ordered ALPHA/BETA/GAMMA and holdings ordered GAMMA/ALPHA/BETA. Validate unique labels and finite numbers; align by label; calculate position market values and total portfolio value; demonstrate the incorrect positional result; and produce an audit record containing units and ordered labels.
