## Learning objectives

Derive scalar and vector projection onto a nonzero direction; decompose a vector into parallel and orthogonal components; prove the residual's orthogonality and least-squares optimality; and apply label-safe projection to financial exposure vectors.

## 1. The projection problem

Given \(\mathbf x,\mathbf d\in\mathbb R^n\) with \(\mathbf d\ne\mathbf0\), find the point on the line \(\{a\mathbf d:a\in\mathbb R\}\) closest to \(\mathbf x\) in Euclidean distance. We seek \(a\) minimizing

\[
f(a)=\|\mathbf x-a\mathbf d\|_2^2
=\mathbf x^\mathsf T\mathbf x-2a\mathbf x^\mathsf T\mathbf d+a^2\mathbf d^\mathsf T\mathbf d.
\]

Differentiate:

\[
f'(a)=-2\mathbf x^\mathsf T\mathbf d+2a\mathbf d^\mathsf T\mathbf d.
\]

Because \(\mathbf d^\mathsf T\mathbf d>0\), the unique minimizer is

\[
\boxed{a^\ast=\frac{\mathbf x^\mathsf T\mathbf d}{\mathbf d^\mathsf T\mathbf d}}.
\]

The **vector projection** is

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{proj}_{\mathbf d}\mathbf x
=\frac{\mathbf x^\mathsf T\mathbf d}{\|\mathbf d\|_2^2}\mathbf d}.
\]

The **signed scalar projection** onto the unit direction \(\mathbf u=\mathbf d/\|\mathbf d\|_2\) is

\[
\boxed{\mathbf x^\mathsf T\mathbf u
=\frac{\mathbf x^\mathsf T\mathbf d}{\|\mathbf d\|_2}}.
\]

The coefficient \(a^\ast\) and the scalar projection are different quantities: the former depends on the chosen scale of \(\mathbf d\), whereas the latter measures signed length in the units of \(\mathbf x\) when the direction is dimensionless.

## 2. Orthogonal decomposition

Define the residual

\[
\mathbf r=\mathbf x-\operatorname{proj}_{\mathbf d}\mathbf x.
\]

Then

\[
\mathbf r^\mathsf T\mathbf d
=\mathbf x^\mathsf T\mathbf d
-\frac{\mathbf x^\mathsf T\mathbf d}{\mathbf d^\mathsf T\mathbf d}
\mathbf d^\mathsf T\mathbf d=0.
\]

Thus \(\mathbf x=\mathbf p+\mathbf r\), with \(\mathbf p\perp\mathbf r\). Pythagoras gives

\[
\boxed{\|\mathbf x\|_2^2=\|\mathbf p\|_2^2+\|\mathbf r\|_2^2}.
\]

This identity is a useful mathematical reconciliation check.

## 3. Worked two-dimensional example

Let \(\mathbf x=(3,4)\) and \(\mathbf d=(1,0)\). The coefficient is \(a^\ast=3\), the projection is \((3,0)\), and the residual is \((0,4)\). The decomposition has squared lengths \(25=9+16\).

For \(\mathbf d=(1,1)\), \(a^\ast=7/2\), so the projection is \((3.5,3.5)\) and residual is \((-0.5,0.5)\).

## 4. Financial factor-exposure decomposition

Suppose a portfolio's exposure vector, in USD, is

\[
\mathbf x=(100,50,-20)^\mathsf T
\]

over ALPHA/BETA/GAMMA. Choose a **dimensionless** factor direction

\[
\mathbf d=(1,1,0)^\mathsf T.
\]

Then

\[
a^\ast=\frac{100+50}{1+1}=\boxed{75\text{ USD}},
\]

\[
\mathbf p=(75,75,0)^\mathsf T\text{ USD},
\qquad
\mathbf r=(25,-25,-20)^\mathsf T\text{ USD}.
\]

The residual is orthogonal to \(\mathbf d\): \(25-25=0\). This decomposition describes geometric alignment with a chosen direction. It does **not** establish factor neutrality in a covariance-weighted risk model or guarantee a hedge.

## 5. Interpretation and limitations

The projection depends on the chosen coordinate system and inner product. Rescaling one asset's exposure coordinate changes the Euclidean projection. A covariance-weighted or economically calibrated projection requires a different metric, developed later.

A zero direction has no unique projection coefficient and must be rejected. An extremely small direction may be mathematically nonzero but numerically poorly conditioned. The reference implementation uses scaled calculations to avoid unnecessary overflow; it still cannot guarantee accuracy for arbitrarily ill-conditioned inputs.

The scalar coefficient \(a^\ast\) is not automatically a tradable hedge ratio: transaction costs, instrument availability, risk factor calibration, and constraints are outside this section.

## 7. Exercises with worked solutions

**1.** Project \((3,4)\) onto \((1,0)\).  
*Answer:* \((3,0)\).

**2.** Find the residual for Exercise 1.  
*Answer:* \((0,4)\).

**3.** Find the projection coefficient for Exercise 1.  
*Answer:* 3.

**4.** Project \((3,4)\) onto \((1,1)\).  
*Answer:* \((3.5,3.5)\).

**5.** Find the residual for Exercise 4.  
*Answer:* \((-0.5,0.5)\).

**6.** Verify orthogonality for Exercise 5.  
*Answer:* \((-0.5,0.5)\cdot(1,1)=0\).

**7.** Project \((0,5)\) onto \((1,0)\).  
*Answer:* \((0,0)\).

**8.** Project \((2,0)\) onto \((-1,0)\).  
*Answer:* \((2,0)\); the line is unchanged by reversing direction.

**9.** What is the signed scalar projection of \((3,4)\) onto \((0,2)\)?  
*Answer:* 4.

**10.** Why is projection onto the zero vector undefined?  
*Answer:* The denominator \(\mathbf d^\mathsf T\mathbf d\) is zero.

**11.** Find the projection of \((100,50,-20)\) USD onto \((1,1,0)\).  
*Answer:* \((75,75,0)\) USD.

**12.** Find its residual.  
*Answer:* \((25,-25,-20)\) USD.

**13.** What is the squared residual norm in Exercise 12?  
*Answer:* \(25^2+(-25)^2+(-20)^2=\boxed{1650\text{ USD}^2}\).

**14.** Does Euclidean residual orthogonality imply statistical independence?  
*Answer:* No; covariance and distributional assumptions are distinct.
