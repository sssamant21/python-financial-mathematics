## Part A — Foundations and worked solutions

**1. Vector addition.** Given \(\mathbf a=(2,-1,3)\) and \(\mathbf b=(-4,5,1)\), calculate \(\mathbf a+\mathbf b\).

*Solution:* \((-2,4,4)\).

**2. Scaling.** Calculate \(-2\mathbf a\).

*Solution:* \((-4,2,-6)\).

**3. Dot product.** Calculate \(\mathbf a^\mathsf T\mathbf b\).

*Solution:* \(2(-4)+(-1)5+3(1)=-10\).

**4. L1 norm.** Find \(\|\mathbf a\|_1\).

*Solution:* \(2+1+3=6\).

**5. L2 norm.** Find \(\|\mathbf a\|_2\).

*Solution:* \(\sqrt{4+1+9}=\sqrt{14}\).

**6. Infinity norm.** Find \(\|\mathbf a\|_\infty\).

*Solution:* 3.

**7. L2 distance.** Find \(d_2(\mathbf a,\mathbf b)\).

*Solution:* \(\mathbf a-\mathbf b=(6,-6,2)\), so \(d_2=\sqrt{36+36+4}=\sqrt{76}\).

**8. Orthogonality.** Are \((2,1)\) and \((1,-2)\) orthogonal?

*Solution:* Yes; \(2(1)+1(-2)=0\).

**9. Angle.** Find the angle between \((1,0)\) and \((1,1)\).

*Solution:* \(\cos\theta=1/\sqrt2\), hence \(\theta=45^\circ\).

**10. Projection.** Project \((4,2)\) onto direction \((1,1)\).

*Solution:* \(a^\ast=(4+2)/(1+1)=3\), so the projected vector is \((3,3)\).

**11. Residual.** Find the residual for Exercise 10 and check orthogonality.

*Solution:* \((1,-1)\); its dot product with \((1,1)\) is zero.

**12. Pythagorean identity.** Verify the decomposition in Exercise 10.

*Solution:* \(4^2+2^2=20\), projected squared norm \(=18\), residual squared norm \(=2\); \(20=18+2\).

## Part B — Applied finance and worked solutions

**13. Signed market value.** Prices \((100,50,25)\) USD/share and aligned signed holdings \((500,-200,800)\) shares. Calculate position values.

*Solution:* \((50000,-10000,20000)\) USD.

**14. NAV weights.** Given independently verified NAV \(100000\) USD, calculate risky-position weights.

*Solution:* \((0.5,-0.1,0.2)\).

**15. Net exposure.** Calculate the sum of risky weights.

*Solution:* \(0.6\), or 60% of NAV.

**16. Gross exposure.** Calculate total absolute risky weight.

*Solution:* \(0.5+0.1+0.2=0.8\), or 80% of NAV.

**17. Long and short exposures.** Calculate each separately.

*Solution:* Long \(=0.7\), short magnitude \(=0.1\).

**18. NAV reconciliation.** Calculate NAV minus signed risky-position value.

*Solution:* \(100000-60000=40000\) USD. Do not assume the difference is cash without accounting evidence.

**19. Scenario P&L.** Simple asset returns \((0.02,-0.01,0.04)\). Find marked-position P&L.

*Solution:* \(50000(0.02)+(-10000)(-0.01)+20000(0.04)=1900\) USD.

**20. NAV-normalized contribution.** Express Exercise 19 as a fraction of NAV.

*Solution:* \(1900/100000=0.019=1.9\%\). This is a modeled contribution, not necessarily the realized total return.

**21. Concentration limit.** Does the portfolio satisfy \(|w_i|\le0.4\) for all assets?

*Solution:* No; ALPHA's absolute weight is \(0.5\).

**22. Gross cap.** Does the portfolio satisfy gross exposure \(\le0.75\)?

*Solution:* No; \(0.8>0.75\).

**23. Misaligned holdings.** Prices are labeled ALPHA/BETA/GAMMA with values \((100,50,25)\), while holdings are labeled GAMMA/ALPHA/BETA with values \((800,500,-200)\). What is the correct value?

*Solution:* Align holdings to \((500,-200,800)\) first; signed value is \(60000\) USD. Naive positional multiplication gives \(100(800)+50(500)+25(-200)=100000\) USD, an incorrect overstatement of 40000 USD.

**24. Return matrix.** A two-date, three-asset return matrix has rows \((0.01,-0.02,0.03)\) and \((0.02,0.01,-0.01)\). For fully invested weights \((0.5,0.3,0.2)\), calculate the two portfolio returns.

*Solution:* \(0.005\) and \(0.011\), or 0.5% and 1.1%.

## Part C — Reasoning and model-risk questions

**25.** Why is an ordinary L2 exposure distance not automatically portfolio volatility?

*Solution:* Volatility requires a covariance model and a time horizon; the ordinary Euclidean metric alone does not incorporate return covariances.

**26.** Does zero dot product of two asset-exposure vectors establish statistical independence?

*Solution:* No; it is coordinate-space orthogonality, not independence of asset returns.

**27.** Why is projection onto a zero direction undefined?

*Solution:* The denominator \(\mathbf d^\mathsf T\mathbf d\) is zero.

**28.** Why can NumPy broadcasting be dangerous even when no shape error occurs?

*Solution:* Broadcasting checks dimensional compatibility, not matching financial labels or axis meanings; \`(3,1)\` combined with \`(3,)\` can silently produce a \`(3,3)\` array.

**29.** Why is positive NAV an explicit input contract in the capstone?

*Solution:* NAV is the independently governed denominator; dividing by zero or silently inferring NAV from signed risky positions produces misleading weights.

**30.** Is a feasible portfolio necessarily optimal or suitable?

*Solution:* No; feasibility only establishes that the particular stated constraints are satisfied.
