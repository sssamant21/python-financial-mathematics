## Learning objectives

Implement and compare continuous and effective annual discounting, specify units and valid domains, value a stream of signed cash flows, and design independent checks for boundaries and numerical range.

## 1. Financial contract

A cash flow is an amount associated with a time. Positive amounts represent receipts and negative amounts payments. All amounts in a calculation use the same currency. Times are nonnegative years measured from the valuation date; day-count conventions and calendar dates are outside this example.

A rate convention is part of the input. A decimal value of 0.05 denotes 5% per year. Continuous and effective annual rates with the same numerical value generally produce different discount factors. Do not silently interpret one as the other.

## 2. Continuous compounding

For constant continuously compounded annual rate \(r\), the discount factor and present value are

\[
D_c(r,t)=e^{-rt},\qquad P=FD_c(r,t).
\]

Any finite real \(r\) is mathematically permitted; \(t\ge0\). For a stream of paired amounts and times,

\[
V=\sum_{i=1}^{m} C_i e^{-rt_i}.
\]

An empty sum is zero by convention. Negative rates may yield factors above one. These equations are deterministic and do not model credit risk, taxes, transaction costs, or changes in the rate curve.

## 3. Effective annual compounding

For a constant annual effective rate \(i>-1\), the positive annual accumulation factor is \(1+i\). For a nonnegative real time \(t\), the convention used here extends that factor as

\[
D_e(i,t)=(1+i)^{-t}
       =\exp[-t\log(1+i)].
\]

This fractional-year extension is an assumption; actual instruments may use different day-count and compounding rules. The equivalent continuous rate is \(r=\log(1+i)\), and the inverse conversion is \(i=e^r-1\). Use log1p and expm1 for improved accuracy near zero. Conversion may overflow or round to the boundary \(i=-1\) for extreme rates; such results cannot be represented faithfully in ordinary floating point.

## 4. Worked valuation

At zero rate, payments of 100 in year one and 200 in year two have PV 300. At a continuous rate of \(\ln2\), a payment of 100 in one year has PV 50. Under an effective rate of 25%, 125 due after one year has PV \(125/1.25=100\).

If \(i=0.25\), its equivalent continuous rate is \(\log(1.25)\). Applying either convention with these equivalent rates produces the same mathematical factor, subject to floating-point rounding. The numeric rate 0.25 under continuous compounding is a different assumption.

## 5. Input and failure behavior

Reject Boolean values, strings, non-finite numbers, unsupported conventions, and negative times. For effective annual rates, reject \(i\le-1\). A sequence contains amount-time pairs; a malformed pair is an input error. The module returns a float in currency units. It reports overflow rather than returning infinity. Underflow can produce zero for a mathematically positive discount factor; the illustrative code does not detect all underflow.

For exact cash accounting, floating-point output and rounding policy need separate assessment. This educational implementation is not a production valuation system.

## 6. Verification strategy

Use independent analytic anchors, zero-time and zero-rate cases, negative-rate cases, invalid inputs and empty streams. Check equivalent rate conversion and split/permutation properties with justified tolerances. Avoid relying on round-trip conversion alone: two defects can cancel. Record actual results and environment only after executing the checks.

## 7. Twenty worked exercises

1. Continuous discount factor at \(t=0\) is 1.
2. Continuous discount factor at \(r=0\) is 1.
3. \(P(100,\ln2,1)=50\).
4. Two zero-rate payments of 100 and 200 total 300.
5. A zero-time payment of -25 has PV -25.
6. A negative continuous rate is admissible in this contract.
7. For positive \(F,T\), a negative rate gives PV greater than \(F\).
8. An empty stream has value zero.
9. Effective annual rate must exceed -1.
10. At effective rate 0.25, 125 due in one year has PV 100.
11. The equivalent continuous rate is \(\log(1.25)\).
12. The inverse conversion is \(i=\operatorname{expm1}(r)\).
13. Equal numeric effective and continuous rates are generally not equivalent.
14. Fractional-year effective discounting uses the explicitly stated power convention.
15. Negative time is rejected.
16. NaN is rejected.
17. True is rejected even though Python treats bool as an integer subclass.
18. Splitting a payment at one date preserves its PV mathematically.
19. Reordering amount-time pairs preserves the sum mathematically.
20. A positive mathematical discount factor can underflow to zero in float arithmetic.
