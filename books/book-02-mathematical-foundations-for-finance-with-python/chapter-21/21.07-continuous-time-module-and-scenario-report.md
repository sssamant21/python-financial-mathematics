## Learning objectives

Derive a constant-coefficient account transition, specify its domain and units, distinguish a mathematical trajectory from a depletion policy, and prepare a scenario report with evidence and limitations.

## 1. Account model

Let \(A(t)\) be an account balance in currency units. For a constant continuously compounded rate \(r\) per year and constant net contribution \(u\) in currency units per year,

\[
A'(t)=rA(t)+u,\qquad A(0)=A_0,\qquad t\ge0.
\]

Positive \(u\) contributes to the account; negative \(u\) withdraws from it. Rates and flows are deterministic and unchanged over the interval. The equation itself permits a negative balance; a rule that stops or changes withdrawals at depletion must be specified separately.

## 2. Exact transition

Using an integrating factor,

\[
A(T)=A_0e^{rT}+u\frac{e^{rT}-1}{r}\quad(r\ne0).
\]

At \(r=0\), the continuous extension is

\[
A(T)=A_0+uT.
\]

The finite-horizon expression has a removable singularity at zero. Near zero, expm1 improves the computation of \(e^{rT}-1\). At \(T=0\), \(A(T)=A_0\).

For constant coefficients, the exact transition composes: applying intervals \(s\) and \(t\) in sequence gives the same mathematical result as one interval \(s+t\). This assumes no intervening jump or boundary intervention.

## 3. Worked cases

With \(A_0=1000,r=0,u=200,T=3\), the balance is \(1000+200(3)=1600\). With \(A_0=1000,r=\ln2,u=0,T=1\), the balance is 2000. With \(A_0=100,r=0,u=-60,T=2\), the unconstrained solution is -20; it crosses zero at \(T=100/60\) years. A no-overdraft rule would require a defined event and subsequent policy, not merely clipping the final value to zero.

## 4. Scenario report

A report should record the model and version, initial balance, rate convention, contribution rate, horizon, units, output, boundary policy, numerical limitations, execution evidence and review status. If it compares several scenarios, each must retain its own inputs and assumptions.

The lab produces a synthetic report for the unconstrained model. It records execution and independent review as pending: merely creating the object does not demonstrate that a validation suite passed or that a model is approved.

## 5. Numerical and financial limitations

The code accepts finite real values, rejects Boolean values and negative horizons, and reports non-finite results as numerical failure. Very large exponents may overflow; underflow and cancellation can still affect accuracy. The implementation is educational and does not model variable rates, discrete deposits, fees, taxes, default or overdraft policy.

An analytic formula is not automatically safe for every floating-point input. Compare selected cases with independently derived values and, where necessary, higher-precision methods. A report should explain the limits of its evidence.

## 6. Twenty worked exercises

1. \(A_0\) is the initial balance.
2. \(r\) is a continuous annual rate.
3. \(u\) is a net flow per year.
4. A negative \(u\) represents withdrawals.
5. At zero rate, \(A(T)=A_0+uT\).
6. For 1000, 0, 200 and 3 years, \(A(T)=1600\).
7. With zero flow, \(A(T)=A_0e^{rT}\).
8. With \(A_0=1000,r=\ln2,T=1,u=0\), the result is 2000.
9. At time zero the balance is \(A_0\).
10. A negative rate is mathematically admissible in this model.
11. The zero-rate closed-form singularity is removable.
12. expm1 helps evaluate \(e^x-1\) near zero.
13. Constant-coefficient transitions compose over adjacent intervals.
14. An intervening deposit jump changes the naive composition.
15. The unconstrained balance may become negative.
16. A depletion policy is an additional modeling choice.
17. Clipping the final result does not generally model a path-dependent event.
18. A finite input can produce an overflow.
19. A scenario report must identify its assumptions and units.
20. A generated report is not independent review or release approval.

## 7. Twelve-step lab

1. Specify balance, rate, flow and time units.
2. State the constant-coefficient assumptions.
3. Derive the integrating-factor solution.
4. Define the zero-rate branch.
5. Verify a zero-horizon case.
6. Verify zero-rate growth with contributions.
7. Verify zero-flow exponential growth.
8. Check transition composition.
9. Explore a withdrawal case crossing zero.
10. Record the chosen boundary policy.
11. Produce a synthetic report with pending evidence.
12. Record environment, actual test outcomes and independent review when executed.
