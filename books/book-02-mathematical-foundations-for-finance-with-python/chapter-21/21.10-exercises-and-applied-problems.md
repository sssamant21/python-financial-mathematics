# 21.10 — Exercises and Applied Problems

## Objectives

Apply the capstone's deterministic cash-flow, sensitivity, replication, optimization, continuous-time and validation concepts. State assumptions and units, derive results independently, and distinguish a mathematical result from an authorized production decision.

Unless stated otherwise, amounts are signed currency units, times are nonnegative years, rates are decimals per year, and all data are synthetic.

## Part A — Requirements and interfaces

1. State the capstone's intended use.
2. List three financial effects outside the basic cash-flow model.
3. Explain why a rate convention belongs in an interface contract.
4. Specify the units of a continuous annual rate and a horizon.
5. Explain why reporting should be separate from valuation mathematics.
6. Identify four fields in an interface contract.
7. Explain why a generated report is not a model approval.
8. Explain why Boolean values deserve explicit treatment in Python numeric validation.

## Part B — Cash flows and rates

Use \(P(F,r,T)=Fe^{-rT}\) for a constant continuous rate.

9. Evaluate \(P(100,0,2)\).
10. Evaluate \(P(100,\ln2,1)\).
11. Evaluate \(P(-25,0.05,0)\).
12. State whether negative continuous rates are mathematically permitted.
13. Explain the significance of a discount factor above one.
14. State the contract's behavior for an empty cash-flow stream.
15. Calculate the zero-rate value of 100 at year one and 200 at year two.
16. Find the one-year PV of 125 at a 25% annual effective rate.
17. Express the continuous rate equivalent to an effective annual rate \(i>-1\).
18. Express the effective annual rate equivalent to continuous rate \(r\).
19. Explain why numerically equal effective and continuous rates generally differ.
20. Explain the mathematical and implementation issues near the effective-rate boundary \(i=-1\).

## Part C — Sensitivity

21. Derive the first derivative of \(P\) with respect to \(r\).
22. Derive its second derivative.
23. Evaluate both derivatives for \(F=100,T=2,r=0\).
24. Estimate the change for a one-basis-point increase in exercise 23.
25. Calculate first- and second-order approximations at \(r+h\) for exercise 23 when \(h=0.01\).
26. Compare them with exact revaluation \(100e^{-0.02}\).
27. Describe central-difference truncation and rounding tradeoffs.
28. State the sign of rate sensitivity for a negative cash flow and positive horizon.

## Part D — Linear algebra and optimization

29. Solve \(x_1+x_2=3\), \(x_1+2x_2=5\).
30. Compute the exact residual for your solution.
31. Calculate the cost of those positions at prices 10 and 20.
32. Give a singular consistent two-equation system.
33. Give a singular inconsistent system using the same coefficient rows.
34. Explain why a small residual need not imply a small solution error.
35. Define the long-only, fully invested feasible set for two weights.
36. Determine the equality residual and long-only violation for \((1.2,-0.2)\).
37. For the objective \((w_1-0.7)^2+(w_2-0.2)^2\), find the constrained optimum.
38. Calculate its objective value.
39. Show that \((0.5,0.5)\) is feasible but not optimal for that objective.
40. Explain why the specific clipping solution from Section 21.6 cannot be applied blindly to other optimization problems.

## Part E — Continuous-time accounts

41. Write the solution of \(A'=rA+u\), \(A(0)=A_0\), for constant nonzero \(r\).
42. State the zero-rate solution.
43. Calculate the ending balance for \(A_0=1000,r=0,u=200,T=3\).
44. Calculate the ending balance for \(A_0=1000,r=\ln2,u=0,T=1\).
45. Find the unconstrained balance for \(A_0=100,r=0,u=-60,T=2\).
46. Find its first zero-crossing time.
47. Explain why clipping only the final balance is not a complete depletion policy.

## Part F — Validation, evidence, and applied capstone

48. Give two independent known-value checks for the toolkit.
49. Give a metamorphic relation and its assumptions.
50. Explain how an always-zero implementation can pass a property test.
51. Specify a tolerance and its units for a synthetic currency-valued check; explain why it is not universal.
52. List the evidence needed to reproduce a test run.
53. Distinguish authored, executed, reviewed and approved.
54. Explain why a fixed random seed does not prove statistical validity.
55. Design a report that includes one result from each capstone module, limitations and pending review status.
56. Write a finding for an incorrect rate convention and describe remediation and independent verification.
57. A test suite passes but mathematical review is pending. State an appropriate release status.
58. Explain one additional modeling question that arises when a future cash flow is random.

## Twelve-step final lab

1. Inventory all module contracts and assumptions.
2. Define synthetic inputs and their units.
3. Derive analytic reference values.
4. Check rate conversion and cash-flow value.
5. Compare analytic and numerical sensitivities.
6. Solve and inspect a replication residual.
7. Check allocation feasibility and objective.
8. Evaluate a continuous-account scenario.
9. Test boundary and invalid inputs.
10. Record versions, commands, tolerances and outputs when execution occurs.
11. Document findings and independent review.
12. Prepare a decision record for the intended use.
