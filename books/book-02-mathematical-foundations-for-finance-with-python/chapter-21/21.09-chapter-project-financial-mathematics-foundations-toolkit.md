## Project objective

Integrate the chapter's deterministic cash-flow, sensitivity, replication, optimization and continuous-account examples into a synthetic scenario report. Document assumptions, units, independent benchmarks, diagnostics, limitations and evidence status. This is an educational project, not a production valuation or investment system.

## 1. The synthetic scenario

Use these independent teaching examples:

| Component | Inputs | Expected result |
|---|---|---|
| Cash-flow value | 100 at year 1, continuous rate \(\ln2\) | 50 currency units |
| Rate sensitivity | 100, rate 0, horizon 2 years | First derivative -200; second derivative 400 |
| Replication | \(A=\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\1&2\end{pmatrix}, b=(3,5)^T\) | Positions \((1,2)\), zero exact residual |
| Allocation | Targets \((0.7,0.2)\), long-only, sum 1 | Weights \((0.75,0.25)\), objective 0.005 |
| Continuous account | Initial 1000, rate 0, flow 200/year, three years | Ending balance 1600 |

These components illustrate different problems; their numbers do not represent one coherent investable portfolio. Cash flows and balances are currency units, weights are dimensionless, and times are years. Derivatives are with respect to a decimal continuous annual rate.

## 2. Module contracts

1. **Cash flow:** paired amounts and nonnegative times; a stated rate convention; finite inputs and explicit numerical failure.
2. **Sensitivity:** analytic derivatives and finite-difference diagnostics with units and step size.
3. **Replication:** compatible dimensions, position units, residual and conditioning limitations.
4. **Optimization:** stated objective and constraints, candidate, objective value and feasibility diagnostics.
5. **Continuous account:** constant rate and flow, initial balance, horizon and boundary policy.
6. **Reporting:** associate results with inputs and assumptions; maintain execution, review and approval as separate states.

Each companion module has its own limitations. A composed report inherits those limitations; a successful report does not establish production suitability.

## 3. Independent validation design

Test the table's analytically derived values, zero and boundary cases, invalid inputs, and selected metamorphic relations. Use justified numerical tolerances and record the norm and units where applicable. Compare the replication residual and allocation feasibility separately from economic suitability and optimality. The allocation optimum follows from strict convexity for the specific two-weight objective, not from a generic success flag.

Include integration checks for correct rate convention, amount-time pairing and consistent units. An always-zero valuation may pass scaling and must be caught by a nonzero benchmark.

## 4. Scenario report and evidence

A report should include model identity, inputs, formulas or conventions, units, outputs, diagnostics and exclusions. A validation record should capture exact source and dirty state, command, Python and dependency versions, actual outputs, exit code, tolerance, test failures and reviewer scope. Avoid credentials and sensitive data.

The provided report labels **validation execution not run** and review and approval **pending**. It computes sample results when invoked, but computing a result is not execution of the independent validation suite. Update evidence only after actual execution and review.

## 5. Error handling and limits

The report intentionally calls the educational modules directly; errors propagate so invalid inputs or numerical failures are not converted to plausible values. Each module has bounded applicability. In particular, the replication solver is a limited two-by-two method; the allocation solution applies only to its stated quadratic; the account model allows negative balances and does not enforce depletion policy.

Floating-point underflow, overflow, ill-conditioning and cancellation remain possible. Before any real use, the owner must establish error budgets, model assumptions, data governance, operational controls and independent review.

## 6. Twenty worked exercises

1. The one-year continuous PV at \(\ln2\) of 100 is 50.
2. The rate sensitivity of 100 at zero rate and two years is -200.
3. Its second derivative is 400.
4. A one-basis-point increase predicts a first-order change of -0.02.
5. The replication positions in the table are 1 and 2.
6. The exact replication residual is zero.
7. A small numerical residual does not prove economic suitability.
8. The allocation weights are 0.75 and 0.25.
9. Their sum is 1.
10. Both weights are nonnegative.
11. The stated quadratic objective is 0.005.
12. Feasibility alone does not establish optimality.
13. Zero-rate account balance is 1600.
14. A negative account balance is permitted by the unconstrained equation.
15. A depletion rule requires a separate policy.
16. A scenario report must identify the rate convention.
17. An analytic expected value is a benchmark, not proof the code ran.
18. A passing test does not imply independent review.
19. A source commit does not capture uncommitted modifications.
20. The educational report is not an investment recommendation.
