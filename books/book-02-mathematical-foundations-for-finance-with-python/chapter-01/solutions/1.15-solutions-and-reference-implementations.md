## Problem 01 — Minor-Unit Allocation

Use nonnegative integer total cents and a strictly positive integer recipient count, excluding Boolean inputs. Division gives 101 = 4 × 25 + 1. The first recipient receives 26 cents and the other three receive 25 each. Total is 101 and the largest difference is one cent.

The exact share 101/4 = 25.25 cents lies outside the required integer allocation domain. Rounding every share independently can fail to preserve the total, so allocate the remainder explicitly. Python's Boolean/integer relationship does not make `True` a meaningful recipient-count input under this interface.

## Problem 02 — Scenario Roles

Principal 500 and horizon 2 stay fixed. Rate is the varied scenario parameter; final balance is the output. At 2%, balance is 500(1.02)^2 = 2601/5 = 520.2. At 4%, it is 500(1.04)^2 = 2704/5 = 540.8.

An input can vary across scenarios while remaining fixed within each calculation. No objective, feasible set, or selection rule has been supplied, so this is not an optimization decision. The computations also do not establish that either hypothetical rate will occur.

## Problem 03 — Domain Intersection

The intersection is [1/4, 3/4), or {w in the reals: 1/4 ≤ w < 3/4}. Membership for 0.25, 0.50, 0.75, and −0.01 is respectively true, true, false, false.

The upper endpoint is excluded even though 0.75 satisfies the separate long-only restriction. A conjunction must meet both domains. Replacing the strict upper inequality with a non-strict one would change the policy.

## Problem 04 — Indexed Schedule

Use the sum from t=0 through t=2 of C_t/(1.1)^t. It is −200 + 110/1.1 + 121/1.21 = −200 + 100 + 100 = 0. Pass sequence `(-200, 110, 121)` with decimal rate `"0.10"` to the reference function.

Position zero is an immediate payment, so three entries span two future periods. The signs encode a selected cash-flow perspective. They do not specify default risk, uncertainty, liquidity, or whether the discount rate is economically appropriate.

## Problem 05 — Equation Classification

For 2x+7=x+12, subtract x+7 to obtain x=5. Original sides both equal 17 at that value. It is the unique real solution.

The second equation expands to 3x+6=3x+6 and holds for every real x. The third reduces to 1=3 and has no real solution. The classifications are unique, all, and none under the published solver's terminology.

A separate domain x≥10 excludes the first equation's only real solution. Algebraic solvability and financial admissibility must be checked separately.

## Problem 06 — Fee Budget

The fee condition 3+0.02x≤13 gives 0.02x≤10 and x≤500. Combining nonnegativity and the amount cap gives [0,500]. Maximum is 500.

At 500 the fee is 13 and fee-budget slack is zero; it is feasible. At 505 the fee is 13.1 and fee-budget slack is −0.1; it is infeasible despite satisfying the 600 amount cap. The amount-cap slacks are 100 and 95.

The stated fee formula charges three at zero. The transfer model's exemption at zero is a different piecewise definition and cannot be imported without changing this problem.

## Problem 07 — Negative Coefficient

Subtract 10 to get −2x≤−6; dividing by −2 reverses the inequality, giving x≥3. Intersecting with x≥0 leaves [3,infinity). Values 2,3,4 give original left sides 6,4,2, so the outcomes are false, true, true.

Equality is allowed. For the strict version, the solution is x>3, and 3 is excluded. A sign-sensitive rearrangement should be checked at a boundary and on either side, not only at one passing point.

## Problem 08 — Revenue and Profit

Take revenue 100 and cost 120, both nonnegative. Revenue is positive but profit is −20, refuting the universal implication.

Write P for positive revenue and Q for positive profit. The converse is Q implies P: positive profit implies positive revenue. Under these nonnegative cost assumptions that converse happens to be true, but it is not logically equivalent to the original implication in general.

The contrapositive is not Q implies not P: nonpositive profit implies nonpositive revenue. It is logically equivalent to P implies Q and is false for the same counterexample. Contraposition preserves truth value; it cannot rescue an incorrect claim.

## Problem 09 — Quantifier Negation

The universal statement is false. Its negation says there exists an index i in {0,1,2} with C_i≤0. Both i=1 and i=2 are witnesses. The first payment is positive, so it is false that all payments are nonpositive.

A universal statement on an empty mathematical domain is true because no element contradicts it. A data application requiring payments must separately verify that the expected schedule was supplied. This is the distinction between logical truth on the supplied domain and completeness of evidence.

## Problem 10 — Effective Fee Rates

With F=0 and k=0.005=1/200, both e(100) and e(200) equal 1/200. Equality contradicts strict decrease while respecting every assumption.

For arbitrary 0<a<b, subtract the effective rates:

\[
e(a)-e(b)=F(1/a-1/b)=F(b-a)/(ab).
\]

The denominator is positive and b−a is positive. For F≥0, the difference is nonnegative, so e(b)≤e(a). For F>0 it is strictly positive, so the effective rate strictly decreases. Nonnegative k is part of the model but cancels from this comparison because the same schedule is used for both amounts.

## Problem 11 — Units and Rate Changes

Position value is (80 shares)(12.50 USD/share)=1000 USD. Shares cancel. Rate difference is 3.75%−3%=0.75 percentage point, or 75 basis points. Relative increase is (0.0375−0.03)/0.03=1/4=25%.

These rate changes are dimensionless comparisons with specified scales; the position value has currency units. A rate can enter an appropriate model through multiplication or another defined relation, but adding it directly to a currency amount has no intended interpretation here.

## Problem 12 — Leap-Day Interest

The interval includes two actual days: February 28 and February 29. ACT/360 gives 9000(0.04)(2/360)=2 USD. ACT/365F gives 9000(0.04)(2/365)=720/365=144/73 USD, approximately 1.972603 USD.

The denominator under ACT/365F remains 365 even in a leap year. Actual/Actual is a different family of conventions and is not selected merely by seeing a leap date. To state a cents result, specify the monetary rounding rule and the point at which it is applied. The exact calculations above impose no rounding policy.

## Problem 13 — Minimum Transfer

Minimize x≥0 subject to 150+x≥650 and 1200−x−g(x)≥400, with the stated piecewise fee. The destination gap is L=500. For positive transfers the upper bound is (1200−400−6)/1.02=39700/51, approximately 778.4314. Since 500 is below this bound, minimum transfer is 500.

Fee is 6+0.02(500)=16. Source ends at 684 and destination at 650. Initial combined total is 1350; final total is 1334, a reduction of 16.

With target 950, gap is 800, exceeding the same upper bound. Its fee would be 22, source would end at 378, and destination at 950. The reserve shortfall is 22, so no feasible outgoing transfer meets the new target. Larger transfers reduce source balance further because debit increases with x.

## Problem 14 — Grouping and Operators

The correct expression is `80 * (1 + Fraction("0.25")) ** 2`, which gives 125. The grouped-principal expression is `(80 * (1 + Fraction("0.25"))) ** 2`, which gives 10000.

`2 ** 3` gives 8. `2 ^ 3` gives 1 because the caret performs bitwise XOR on integers. All of these are executable expressions; only the first growth expression translates the supplied model.

## Problem 15 — Timing and Present Value

Supply `(-80, 44, "48.4")`. At 10%, the terms are −80, 40, and 40, totaling zero. At zero rate, the total is −80+44+48.4=12.4=62/5.

Schedule `(0,0,121)` has the receipt at time two and value 100. Schedule `(0,121)` moves the receipt to time one and value 110. Removing a zero changes the mapping from position to time.

A future-only list beginning with C_1 needs an explicitly different index start or an inserted time-zero entry. Passing it unchanged to this interface would incorrectly treat its first item as immediate.

## Problem 16 — Tolerance and Feasibility

With reference zero and observation 10^−10, relative allowance is 10^−6 times 10^−10 = 10^−16, smaller than the discrepancy. Absolute tolerance zero therefore fails. Absolute tolerance 10^−10 passes at the included boundary.

The separate reserve shortfall is not waived by numerical closeness. A policy must specify arithmetic and any permitted monetary rounding or boundary treatment. A test tolerance alone supplies no authority to change an inequality constraint.

## Problem 17 — Missing-Period Audit

Correct balance is 300(1.1)^2=363. Candidate 330 has signed discrepancy −33 and absolute error 33. Relative error against 363 is 33/363=1/11, approximately 9.09%.

Both nonnegativity and the lower bound of 300 pass. Repeated updates give 300→330→363 and independently expose the missing step. Discounting 330 by 1.21 gives 3000/11 rather than 300. The round trip detects inconsistency but uses the same power factor as the direct formula, so it is related evidence rather than an independent model assessment.

## Problem 20 — Evidence and Limits
