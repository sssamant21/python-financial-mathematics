## 1.7.1 Learning Objectives

By the end of this section, you should be able to:

- Translate financial limits into strict and non-strict inequalities.
- Rearrange inequalities while accounting for the sign of a multiplier or divisor.
- Combine simultaneous constraints and recognize empty feasible sets.
- Express an absolute-deviation limit as a two-sided interval.
- Interpret slack and identify a binding inequality.
- Distinguish feasibility checking, computing a bound, and optimizing an objective.
- Implement exact instructional budget checks without rounding a violation into acceptance.

An equation asks where quantities are equal. An inequality describes an allowed direction or range. A feasible decision must satisfy every applicable condition, including its underlying domain.

## 1.7.2 Translate the Words Before Rearranging

Financial specifications often use phrases whose endpoints matter:

| Wording | Mathematical condition | Boundary included? |
|---|---|---|
| At most 15 | c<=15 | Yes |
| Less than 15 | c<15 | No |
| At least 100 | x>=100 | Yes |
| More than 100 | x>100 | No |
| Between 0 and 1, inclusive | 0<=w<=1 | Both |
| Within 0.1 of a target a | abs(w-a)<=0.1 | Both deviation boundaries |

Specify units with the condition. A fee limit of 15 currency units and a fee-rate limit of 0.015 are different restrictions. Likewise, a percentage-point limit describes an absolute rate change, not a relative percentage change in the original rate.

The examples here use hypothetical policies. Their purpose is to teach the mathematical translation, not recommend limits for a real account or portfolio. Reference 1 supplies the underlying inequality rules.

## 1.7.3 Why Negative Multipliers Reverse the Sign

Adding the same defined quantity to both sides preserves an inequality. Multiplication or division by a positive number also preserves its direction. Multiplication or division by a negative number reverses it.

For example:

$$-2x\leq6.$$

Dividing by -2 gives:

$$x\geq-3.$$

A quick check confirms the direction: x=0 satisfies the original inequality, while x=-4 does not.

One explanation starts with a<b. The difference b-a is positive. Multiplying that difference by a negative k makes k(b-a) negative, so kb<ka. The order is reversed.

Do not divide by an expression of unknown sign without splitting cases. A factor such as 1+r is positive in our discount-rate domain r>-1, but r itself may be negative, zero, or positive. The domain can justify a sign assumption; the variable's name cannot.

## 1.7.4 Include Zero-Coefficient Cases

For fixed real a,b,B, consider:

$$ax+b\leq B.$$

If a>0, then x<=(B-b)/a. If a<0, then x>=(B-b)/a. If a=0, the condition becomes b<=B and is either true for every x in the underlying domain or false for every x.

For instance, a zero variable-fee rate means the total fee is the fixed fee alone. Dividing by the fee rate would fail to describe that valid case.

An exactly zero coefficient is different from a very small nonzero coefficient. A numerical policy that treats small coefficients as zero changes classification and needs explicit justification. The exact-rational implementation below does not make that substitution.

For strict inequalities, the same sign rules apply, with strict endpoints retained. If a=0, compare b<B instead of b<=B. An equality at the limit satisfies a non-strict rule but not its strict counterpart.

## 1.7.5 Derive a Transaction Fee Limit

Suppose a nonnegative transaction amount x incurs a fixed fee F and a proportional fee kx. Define:

$$c(x)=F+kx.$$

Assume F>=0,k>=0 and require the fee not to exceed budget B>=0. With F=5,k=0.01,B=15:

$$5+0.01x\leq15,$$

$$0.01x\leq10,$$

$$x\leq1000.$$

Including the transaction domain x>=0 gives the feasible interval [0,1000]. A transaction of 1000 has fee 15 and is permitted by the non-strict limit. A transaction of 1000.01 has fee 15.0001 and violates it, before any separately specified contractual rounding.

If the fee policy were strictly less than 15, the interval would be [0,1000). Its supremum would be 1000, but no greatest feasible transaction would exist in this continuous-amount model. A currency-lot or minor-unit restriction could change that conclusion and must be stated separately.

The code uses non-strict fee and amount caps. It does not silently round fees to two decimal places before applying them.

## 1.7.6 Intersect the Policy Constraints

Add a transaction cap x<=800. Feasibility now requires:

$$x\geq0,\qquad x\leq1000,\qquad x\leq800.$$

The intersection is [0,800]. The tightest upper limit is 800; satisfying the fee budget alone is insufficient.

For general nonnegative F,k,B and finite cap M>=0:

- If F>B, no nonnegative amount satisfies the fee budget because kx cannot reduce the fee.
- If F<=B and k=0, every amount in [0,M] satisfies the fee budget.
- If F<=B and k>0, the feasible interval is [0,min(M,(B-F)/k)].

When F=B and k>0, only zero is feasible. When F=B and k=0, all amounts up to M remain feasible. These distinctions follow directly from the assumptions; they should appear in implementation branches and boundary checks.

A missing transaction cap would create a different model. Our implementation requires an explicit finite nonnegative cap rather than inventing one.

## 1.7.7 Read Slack in Its Own Units

For a constraint g(x)<=B, define slack as:

$$s(x)=B-g(x).$$

Positive slack means spare capacity, zero means the constraint binds, and negative slack means the proposed point violates the inequality. This convention assumes the inequality is written in the stated upper-limit form.

At x=800 in our example, fee is 13. Fee-budget slack is 15-13=2 currency units. Transaction-cap slack is 800-800=0, so the cap binds even though the fee budget does not.

At x=900, fee is 14 and fee-budget slack is 1. The transaction-cap slack is -100, so the transaction is infeasible despite passing the fee check.

Report which constraint failed rather than returning only a bare False. Also retain each slack's meaning: one measures fee capacity and another transaction capacity. Even when both are denominated in one currency, they describe different quantities and should not be combined into an unexplained score.

## 1.7.8 Absolute Values Define Deviation Bands

For a nonnegative band width epsilon:

$$|w-a|\leq\varepsilon$$

is equivalent to:

$$a-\varepsilon\leq w\leq a+\varepsilon.$$

With target a=0.5 and half-width epsilon=0.1, the permitted weight band is [0.4,0.6]. If the weight must also be long-only, intersect that band with [0,1].

For a=0.95 and epsilon=0.1, the unconstrained band is [0.85,1.05], but the long-only intersection is [0.85,1]. A band around a target does not automatically enforce nonnegative weights or an upper weight of one.

The width is a policy in this example, not a floating-point tolerance. It describes a permitted decision range. A numerical tolerance for deciding whether a calculated equality is sufficiently accurate serves a different purpose.

For epsilon=0, the band becomes the singleton {a}. A negative half-width is invalid configuration for this interpretation rather than a useful reversed band.

## 1.7.9 Coupled Financial Conditions Can Be Inconsistent

For a fully invested, long-only pair:

$$w_1+w_2=1,\qquad w_1,w_2\geq0.$$

If the first weight must also satisfy |w_1-0.5|<=0.1 and w_1<=0.6, then w_1 belongs to [0.4,0.6], with w_2=1-w_1. The endpoints correspond to (0.4,0.6) and (0.6,0.4).

If the cap on w_1 changes to 0.3, the requirements become inconsistent: the band needs w_1>=0.4 while the cap needs w_1<=0.3. The feasible set is empty. This conclusion follows algebraically; failing to find a point in a small sample would not by itself prove emptiness.

Do not “repair” this conflict by clipping a proposed weight to the cap and calling it feasible. The clipped point would violate the band. The policy must be revised explicitly or the infeasibility reported.

Chapter 17 develops constrained optimization. Here the task is to state and check the feasible set before any objective is considered.

## 1.7.10 Feasible Does Not Mean Optimal

A constraint specifies what is permitted. An objective specifies what is preferred among permitted decisions.

In the transaction example, every x in [0,800] is feasible. If the objective is to maximize transaction amount, 800 is the maximizer. If the objective is to minimize the positive proportional fee with no required transaction minimum, zero is the minimizer. Feasibility alone selects neither preference.

A binding constraint can occur at a feasible point even when that point is not an optimum for some other objective. Reporting zero slack is not an optimality certificate.

Similarly, the maximum-amount helper below computes an upper endpoint for this particular affine-fee model and explicit cap. It is not a general optimizer, and it does not choose a recommended transaction or account policy.

## 1.7.11 Executable Exact-Constraint Lab

This standalone standard-library program uses exact rational arithmetic. It accepts built-in int, Fraction, and strings parsed by Fraction. It rejects booleans, floats, invalid rational text, and negative fee-policy parameters. This deliberate input policy preserves exact decimal quotes from text rather than converting binary approximations silently.

Save it as [code/section_1_07.py](code/section_1_07.py).

```python
from fractions import Fraction


def rational(value):
    if type(value) is int or isinstance(value, Fraction):
        return Fraction(value)
    if type(value) is str:
        try:
            return Fraction(value)
        except (ValueError, ZeroDivisionError) as exc:
            raise ValueError("invalid rational text") from exc
    raise TypeError("use int, Fraction, or rational text; bool/float are excluded")


def maximum_transaction_amount(*, fixed_fee, fee_rate, fee_budget, amount_cap):
    """Maximum feasible amount for nonnegative amounts and non-strict limits.

    Return None when the feasible set is empty. Zero is a valid maximum.
    """
    F, k, B, M = (rational(v) for v in
                  (fixed_fee, fee_rate, fee_budget, amount_cap))
    if min(F, k, B, M) < 0:
        raise ValueError("fee-policy parameters must be nonnegative")
    if F > B:
        return None
    if k == 0:
        return M
    return min(M, (B - F) / k)


def transaction_report(*, amount, fixed_fee, fee_rate, fee_budget, amount_cap):
    """Validate a proposal's domain, then report both policy constraints."""
    x = rational(amount)
    if x < 0:
        raise ValueError("transaction amount must be nonnegative")
    F, k, B, M = (rational(v) for v in
                  (fixed_fee, fee_rate, fee_budget, amount_cap))
    maximum = maximum_transaction_amount(fixed_fee=F, fee_rate=k,
                                          fee_budget=B, amount_cap=M)
    fee = F + k*x
    slacks = {"fee_budget": B - fee, "amount_cap": M - x}
    checks = {name: slack >= 0 for name, slack in slacks.items()}
    return {"fee": fee, "slacks": slacks, "checks": checks,
            "feasible": all(checks.values()), "maximum_amount": maximum}


def weight_band_member(*, weight, target, half_width):
    """Long-only scalar weight and exact policy band; not a full portfolio check."""
    w, a, epsilon = (rational(v) for v in (weight, target, half_width))
    if not 0 <= a <= 1 or epsilon < 0:
        raise ValueError("target must be in [0,1] and half_width nonnegative")
    return 0 <= w <= 1 and abs(w - a) <= epsilon


def main():
    policy = {"fixed_fee": 5, "fee_rate": "0.01",
              "fee_budget": 15, "amount_cap": 800}
    for amount in (800, 900):
        report = transaction_report(amount=amount, **policy)
        print(f"Amount {amount}: fee={report['fee']}, feasible={report['feasible']}")
        print(f"  fee slack={report['slacks']['fee_budget']}, "
              f"amount slack={report['slacks']['amount_cap']}")
    maximum = maximum_transaction_amount(**policy)
    assert maximum == 800
    print(f"Maximum feasible amount: {maximum}")
    empty = maximum_transaction_amount(fixed_fee=16, fee_rate="0.01",
                                        fee_budget=15, amount_cap=800)
    print(f"Fixed fee above budget: {empty}")
    boundary = weight_band_member(weight="0.6", target="0.5", half_width="0.1")
    outside = weight_band_member(weight="0.6001", target="0.5", half_width="0.1")
    assert boundary and not outside
    print(f"Weight 0.6 in band: {boundary}")
    print(f"Weight 0.6001 in band: {outside}")


if __name__ == "__main__":
    main()
```

Expected output:

```text
Amount 800: fee=13, feasible=True
  fee slack=2, amount slack=0
Amount 900: fee=14, feasible=False
  fee slack=1, amount slack=-100
Maximum feasible amount: 800
Fixed fee above budget: None
Weight 0.6 in band: True
Weight 0.6001 in band: False
```

None means there is no feasible transaction. A returned maximum of zero instead means the singleton amount zero is feasible. Those outcomes must not be treated as equivalent by a truth-value shortcut.

transaction_report validates the nonnegative amount domain and configuration with exceptions, then returns the fee and cap checks for a well-formed proposal. An amount above a valid cap is a policy violation reported as infeasible, not a parsing error. The two named checks always exist; the program does not rely on an empty all() to establish completeness. Reference 3 documents all(), including its empty-iterable behavior.

weight_band_member checks one weight only. A full portfolio must additionally satisfy its investment-budget equation and every other holding restriction. Exact rational arithmetic does not supply missing constraints or establish an appropriate real-world policy.

## 1.7.13 Practice Problems and Worked Answers

**Problem 1 — Reverse direction.** Solve -3x+6<=0. Check one permitted point and one excluded point.

**Answer.** Subtract six to obtain -3x<=-6, then divide by -3 and reverse the inequality: x>=2. At x=3 the original left side is -3 and satisfies the condition. At x=1 it is 3 and fails.

**Problem 2 — Combine a fee and cap.** A transaction has fee 4+0.02x, budget 14, cap 400, and domain x>=0. Find the feasible interval and both slacks at its maximum.

**Answer.** The fee implies x<=500. Intersecting with the cap gives [0,400]. At x=400 the fee is 12, fee-budget slack is 2, and cap slack is zero. The cap binds.

**Problem 3 — Zero proportional fee.** With fee rate zero, cap 100, and budget 5, describe the feasible set for fixed fee 4,5,and 6.

**Answer.** For fixed fee 4 or 5, every x in [0,100] is feasible. For fixed fee 6 the feasible set is empty. Equality at the budget is permitted because the fee constraint is non-strict.

**Problem 4 — Absolute deviation.** A long-only weight has target 0.95 and permitted absolute deviation 0.1. Find the feasible interval.

**Answer.** The deviation band is [0.85,1.05]. Intersect with [0,1] to obtain [0.85,1]. The upper deviation boundary alone does not impose the long-only upper bound.

**Problem 5 — Inconsistent conditions.** A weight must satisfy abs(w-0.5)<=0.1 and w<=0.3. Can an optimizer satisfy both, and does clipping w to 0.3 resolve the conflict?

**Answer.** No feasible value exists because the first rule requires w>=0.4. Clipping to 0.3 fails the band, so it does not resolve the inconsistency. The requirements need an explicit revision or an infeasibility report.

**Problem 6 — Strict boundary.** With continuous x>=0, fee 5+0.01x must be strictly less than 15. Is x=1000 feasible? Is there a largest feasible x without another restriction?

**Answer.** The interval is [0,1000), so 1000 is excluded. There is no largest feasible real amount: for any x<1000, the midpoint (x+1000)/2 is larger and still feasible. Its supremum is 1000. A specified transaction increment could produce a discrete maximum, but no such increment is assumed here.

## 1.7.14 Common Mistakes and Summary

| Mistake | Consequence | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Forgetting to reverse an inequality after negative division | The allowed direction is wrong | Determine the divisor's sign first |
| Dividing by a zero fee rate | A valid policy case becomes an arithmetic failure | Handle zero coefficients separately |
| Checking only one of several constraints | An infeasible proposal appears accepted | Intersect all applicable requirements |
| Treating a binding constraint as proof of optimality | Preference is inferred from feasibility | State the objective separately |
| Rounding a fee before an exact threshold check without a rule | A violation may be accepted | Specify contractual rounding before using it |
| Treating None and zero as the same maximum | An empty set and a singleton are conflated | Distinguish infeasibility from zero capacity |
| Relaxing every boundary by an arbitrary epsilon | Mathematical and policy limits change | Assign tolerances only through an explicit numerical policy |
| Clipping a value to one cap and ignoring other rules | The repaired point remains infeasible | Recheck the complete constraint system |

Inequalities specify ranges and directions. Their transformations depend on signs, their feasible sets combine through intersections, and their slacks describe remaining capacity. Exact instructional checks make boundaries explicit, while objectives and real-world policies remain separate choices.
