## Learning objectives

Explain the distinction between a cash-flow amount and a payment rate; construct left, right, and midpoint Riemann sums; recognize how time partitions and sampling rules affect approximation; derive an integral as a limit; and apply accumulated-value sums to deterministic continuous cash-flow rates.

## 1. From discrete payments to a continuous payment rate

A discrete cash flow is a currency amount \(C_i\) paid at a specific date \(t_i\). A **cash-flow rate** \(c(t)\), by contrast, has units of currency per year. Over a short time interval of length \(\Delta t\), the amount paid is approximately \(c(t)\Delta t\), not \(c(t)\) alone.

For example, a constant payment rate of \$1200/year over one quarter of a year generates \$300, not \$1200.

If the payment rate changes with time, partition \([a,b]\) into intervals:

\[
a=t_0<t_1<\cdots<t_n=b,\qquad \Delta t_i=t_i-t_{i-1}.
\]

Choose a sample point \(\xi_i\in[t_{i-1},t_i]\). The **Riemann sum** is

\[
\boxed{S(P,\boldsymbol{\xi})=\sum_{i=1}^n c(\xi_i)\Delta t_i.}
\]

Each term is a rectangle's height (payment rate) times width (time), producing a currency amount.

## 2. Left, right, and midpoint sums

For a uniform partition of \([a,b]\),

\[
\Delta t=\frac{b-a}{n},\quad t_i=a+i\Delta t.
\]

The three common approximations are

\[
L_n=\Delta t\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}c(t_i),
\]

\[
R_n=\Delta t\sum_{i=1}^{n}c(t_i),
\]

\[
M_n=\Delta t\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}c\left(\frac{t_i+t_{i+1}}2\right).
\]

For an increasing payment rate, \(L_n\le R_n\), and the left sum underestimates while the right sum overestimates the accumulated amount when the integral exists. For decreasing rates, the inequalities reverse. Midpoint accuracy depends on the shape and smoothness of the rate function.

## 3. Worked example: linearly growing payment rate

Suppose \(c(t)=100+20t\) dollars/year over \(0\le t\le2\) years. With four equal intervals, \(\Delta t=0.5\):

| Interval | Left rate | Right rate | Midpoint rate |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| [0,0.5] | 100 | 110 | 105 |
| [0.5,1] | 110 | 120 | 115 |
| [1,1.5] | 120 | 130 | 125 |
| [1.5,2] | 130 | 140 | 135 |

Hence

\[
L_4=0.5(100+110+120+130)=230,
\]

\[
R_4=0.5(110+120+130+140)=250,
\]

\[
M_4=0.5(105+115+125+135)=240.
\]

The exact accumulated amount is

\[
\int_0^2(100+20t)\,dt
=[100t+10t^2]_0^2=240.
\]

For an affine payment rate on a uniform partition, midpoint integration is exact in exact arithmetic; this is a special property, not a guarantee for every payment rate.

## 4. Refining the partition

The **mesh size** of a partition is

\[
\|P\|=\max_i\Delta t_i.
\]

For a Riemann-integrable rate \(c(t)\), as \(\|P\|\to0\), Riemann sums converge to the same number independently of how sample points are chosen:

\[
\boxed{\int_a^b c(t)\,dt=\lim_{\|P\|\to0}\sum_i c(\xi_i)\Delta t_i.}
\]

The integral is the total cash amount accumulated over the interval. Refining the partition generally improves accuracy for well-behaved functions, but a smaller step does not automatically guarantee smaller floating-point error or monotone improvement for arbitrary oscillatory rates.

A continuous rate on a closed bounded interval is Riemann-integrable. Bounded functions with only finitely many discontinuities are also Riemann-integrable. Not every bounded function is Riemann-integrable.

## 5. Nonuniform partitions

Actual cash-flow systems may sample more densely around changing payment patterns. The general sum uses each interval's actual width:

\[
S=\sum_i c(\xi_i)(t_i-t_{i-1}).
\]

Using one common \(\Delta t\) for unequal intervals introduces a weighting error. The lab validates strictly increasing partition boundaries and evaluates left, right, and midpoint samples per interval.

## 6. Units and financial interpretation

- \(c(t)\): currency/year.
- \(dt\) or \(\Delta t\): years.
- \(c(t)\,dt\): currency.
- \(\int_a^b c(t)\,dt\): currency.

This section measures **undiscounted accumulation**. Present-value integration introduces a discount factor, such as \(e^{-rt}\), inside the integrand; this is developed in Section 10.5. An integral over time does not automatically represent present value.

## 7. A quadratic example and convergence

For \(c(t)=t^2\) on \([0,1]\),

\[
\int_0^1 t^2\,dt=\frac13.
\]

With \(n\) uniform subintervals,

\[
L_n=\frac{(n-1)n(2n-1)}{6n^3},
\qquad
R_n=\frac{n(n+1)(2n+1)}{6n^3},
\]

and

\[
M_n=\frac{4n^2-1}{12n^2}
=\frac13-\frac{1}{12n^2}.
\]

Thus left and right errors shrink on the order of \(1/n\), while midpoint error shrinks on the order of \(1/n^2\) for this smooth example. These are illustrative rates, not universal guarantees without regularity assumptions.

## 9. Exercises with worked solutions

**1.** A payment rate is \$800/year for three months. What amount accumulates?

*Solution:* Three months is 0.25 year, so \(800(0.25)=\$200\).

**2.** Find the width of each of eight uniform intervals on \([1,3]\).

*Solution:* \(\Delta t=(3-1)/8=0.25\) year.

**3.** Find the left sum for \(c(t)=2t\) on \([0,2]\) with two intervals.

*Solution:* Width 1; left samples 0 and 1 give \(L_2=1(0+2)=2\).

**4.** Find the right sum for Exercise 3.

*Solution:* Right samples 1 and 2 give \(R_2=1(2+4)=6\).

**5.** Find the midpoint sum for Exercise 3.

*Solution:* Midpoints 0.5 and 1.5 give \(M_2=1(1+3)=4\).

**6.** Calculate the exact integral for Exercise 3.

*Solution:* \(\int_0^2 2t\,dt=[t^2]_0^2=4\).

**7.** For an increasing payment rate, which uniform sum is an underestimate?

*Solution:* The left sum, because it samples the minimum rate in each interval.

**8.** For \(c(t)=100+20t\) on \([0,2]\), find the exact amount.

*Solution:* \(100(2)+10(2)^2=240\).

**9.** State the mesh size of the partition \(0,0.2,0.7,1\).

*Solution:* The widths are 0.2, 0.5, and 0.3, so \(\|P\|=0.5\).

**10.** Explain why multiplying every nonuniform sample by the same width is incorrect.

*Solution:* It gives the wrong time weight to intervals of different lengths; use \(\Delta t_i=t_i-t_{i-1}\).

**11.** Evaluate \(\int_0^1t^2\,dt\).

*Solution:* \([t^3/3]_0^1=1/3\).

**12.** For \(c(t)=t^2\) and \(n=2\), calculate the midpoint sum.

*Solution:* Midpoints are 0.25 and 0.75, width 0.5; \(M_2=0.5(0.25^2+0.75^2)=0.3125\).

**13.** What is the exact midpoint error for Exercise 12?

*Solution:* \(1/3-0.3125=1/48\approx0.0208333\).

**14.** Why does an undiscounted cash-flow integral not equal present value?

*Solution:* It adds currency amounts without adjusting for the timing of payments. Present value requires an appropriate discount factor.
