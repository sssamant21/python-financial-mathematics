## Learning objectives

Implement composite midpoint, trapezoidal, and Simpson's quadrature; explain their error behavior; estimate numerical convergence; choose integration resolution responsibly; and apply numerical quadrature to continuous cash-flow valuation.

## 1. Why quadrature is needed

Many financial models require integrals without convenient elementary antiderivatives. A deterministic time-varying discount curve gives

\[
PV=\int_0^T c(t)\exp\left(-\int_0^t r(u)\,du\right)\,dt.
\]

Even when the inner integral is available analytically, the outer integral may require numerical evaluation. Quadrature approximates an integral from finitely many integrand evaluations.

Write \(I=\int_a^b f(t)\,dt\). Partition the interval into \(n\) equal steps \(h=(b-a)/n\), with \(t_i=a+ih\).

## 2. Composite midpoint rule

\[
\boxed{M_n=h\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}f(t_i+h/2).}
\]

This evaluates each interval at its midpoint. For a twice continuously differentiable function,

\[
|I-M_n|\le\frac{(b-a)h^2}{24}\max_{[a,b]}|f''|.
\]

Thus the method is generally second order: halving \(h\) reduces the leading truncation error by about four for sufficiently smooth functions.

## 3. Composite trapezoidal rule

\[
\boxed{T_n=h\left[\frac{f(a)+f(b)}2+\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}f(t_i)\right].}
\]

The method replaces the curve with straight-line segments. For \(f\in C^2[a,b]\),

\[
|I-T_n|\le\frac{(b-a)h^2}{12}\max_{[a,b]}|f''|.
\]

It is exact for affine functions in exact arithmetic.

## 4. Composite Simpson's rule

For an **even** positive number \(n\) of subintervals,

\[
\boxed{S_n=\frac h3\left[f(a)+f(b)+4\sum_{\substack{i=1\\i\text{ odd}}}^{n-1}f(t_i)
+2\sum_{\substack{i=2\\i\text{ even}}}^{n-2}f(t_i)\right].}
\]

For \(f\in C^4[a,b]\),

\[
|I-S_n|\le\frac{(b-a)h^4}{180}\max_{[a,b]}|f^{(4)}|.
\]

This is fourth-order convergence for sufficiently smooth integrands. Simpson's rule integrates polynomials of degree at most three exactly in exact arithmetic. Its even-\(n\) requirement is not optional.

## 5. Worked comparison

Consider \(f(t)=t^2\) on \([0,1]\). The exact integral is \(1/3\).

For \(n=2\):

- Midpoint: \(M_2=\tfrac12[(1/4)^2+(3/4)^2]=0.3125\).
- Trapezoidal: \(T_2=\tfrac12[0/2+(1/2)^2+1/2]=0.375\).
- Simpson: \(S_2=\tfrac16[0+4(1/4)+1]=1/3\).

The different results illustrate approximation order, not a guarantee that one method always wins for every nonsmooth or noisy integrand.

## 6. Financial example: time-varying discount curve

Let \(c(t)=1000+100t\) currency/year and the instantaneous deterministic discount rate be \(r(t)=0.03+0.005t\) per year. The discount factor is

\[
D(0,t)=\exp(-0.03t-0.0025t^2).
\]

Over four years,

\[
PV=\int_0^4(1000+100t)e^{-0.03t-0.0025t^2}\,dt.
\]

The companion lab computes midpoint, trapezoidal, and Simpson estimates for \(n=8,16,32,64,128\). This scenario requires correct rate units and clear separation of cash-flow rates from discount rates.

## 7. Refinement and Richardson-style error estimates

If a method's leading error behaves as \(Ch^p\), then

\[
Q_{2n}-Q_n\approx Ch^p(2^{-p}-1).
\]

A practical estimated fine-grid error is

\[
\boxed{E_{2n}\approx\frac{|Q_{2n}-Q_n|}{2^p-1}.}
\]

Use \(p=2\) for midpoint/trapezoidal and \(p=4\) for Simpson under smoothness assumptions. This is an **estimate**, not a rigorous bound. Oscillatory functions, discontinuities, cancellation, and roundoff can make refinement comparisons misleading.

## 8. Numerical safety

- Require finite endpoints, finite integrand evaluations, and positive integer \(n\); reject booleans as counts.
- Return zero for a zero-width interval and preserve sign for reversed endpoints.
- Enforce even \(n\) for Simpson's rule.
- Use \`math.fsum\` for accumulated terms.
- Detect nonfinite results rather than outputting NaN or infinity.
- Record method, subinterval count, refinement results, and assumptions.
- Split known discontinuities or singular points into separate intervals. Standard smooth-function error estimates do not apply across discontinuities.
- For infinite horizons, establish convergence and bound or estimate the tail separately; a finite quadrature interval is not an infinite-horizon proof.

## 10. Exercises and worked solutions

**1.** State the midpoint formula.  
*Solution:* \(h\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}f(a+(i+1/2)h)\).

**2.** State the trapezoidal endpoint weights.  
*Solution:* One half at each endpoint; weight one at interior nodes.

**3.** Why must Simpson's composite rule use even \(n\)?  
*Solution:* Each parabolic panel spans two adjacent subintervals.

**4.** Evaluate \(\int_0^1t^2dt\) exactly.  
*Solution:* \(1/3\).

**5.** Compute the midpoint estimate with \(n=2\) for Exercise 4.  
*Solution:* \(0.3125\).

**6.** Compute the trapezoidal estimate with \(n=2\).  
*Solution:* \(0.375\).

**7.** Compute Simpson's estimate with \(n=2\).  
*Solution:* \(1/3\).

**8.** Which rule is exact for any affine function?  
*Solution:* Both midpoint and trapezoidal, as is Simpson, in exact arithmetic.

**9.** What smoothness supports the standard Simpson fourth-order error bound?  
*Solution:* A continuous fourth derivative on the closed interval.

**10.** If midpoint error is asymptotically second order, what is the expected error factor after doubling \(n\)?  
*Solution:* Approximately \(1/4\).

**11.** If Simpson error is asymptotically fourth order, what is the expected factor?  
*Solution:* Approximately \(1/16\).

**12.** Derive the discount factor for \(r(t)=0.03+0.005t\).  
*Solution:* \(e^{-0.03t-0.0025t^2}\).

**13.** What is the oriented integral from \(b\) to \(a\)?  
*Solution:* The negative of the integral from \(a\) to \(b\).

**14.** Does agreement between two grids prove the result is accurate?  
*Solution:* No; both grids may share bias, alias oscillations, or encounter roundoff.
