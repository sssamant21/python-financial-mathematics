## Learning objectives

Recognize when to use substitution or integration by parts; transform definite-integral bounds correctly; integrate exponential growth and time-weighted cash-flow functions; verify results by differentiation; and implement numerically stable reference formulas in Python.

## 1. Why integration techniques matter in finance

Financial quantities frequently involve expressions such as

\[
\int_0^T C e^{gt}\,dt,\qquad
\int_0^T t C e^{-rt}\,dt,\qquad
\int_0^T (a+bt)e^{-rt}\,dt.
\]

The first can be evaluated with substitution. The second requires integration by parts. The third combines both ideas and models a linearly changing cash-flow rate under deterministic continuous discounting.

Here \(C,a,b\) describe payment-rate parameters, \(g\) is a continuous growth rate, and \(r\) is a continuous discount rate. The results are deterministic educational examples; a production valuation must also establish cash-flow assumptions, timing conventions, and the appropriate discount curve.

## 2. Substitution: reversing the chain rule

If \(u=g(t)\) is differentiable and \(f\) is continuous on the relevant range, the substitution rule is

\[
\boxed{\int f(g(t))g'(t)\,dt=\int f(u)\,du.}
\]

For a definite integral, **transform the bounds**:

\[
\int_a^b f(g(t))g'(t)\,dt
=\int_{g(a)}^{g(b)} f(u)\,du.
\]

### Worked example A — exponential payment growth

For \(g\ne0\),

\[
\int_0^T C e^{gt}\,dt
=\frac{C}{g}(e^{gT}-1).
\]

Choose \(u=gt\), so \(du=g\,dt\). The bounds become \(0\) and \(gT\). For \(g=0\), the expression has the continuous limit \(CT\), which should be implemented as an explicit branch to avoid division by zero.

For small \(|gT|\), evaluating \`expm1(g*T)\` avoids cancellation in \(e^{gT}-1\).

### Worked example B — shifted exponent

\[
\int_0^T C e^{g(t-t_0)}\,dt
=C e^{-gt_0}\int_0^T e^{gt}\,dt.
\]

For \(g\ne0\), this equals \(C e^{-gt_0}\operatorname{expm1}(gT)/g\). The shifted exponential may represent a payment rate normalized to a reference time \(t_0\); units must remain consistent.

## 3. Integration by parts: reversing the product rule

The product rule \(d(uv)=u\,dv+v\,du\) yields

\[
\boxed{\int u\,dv=uv-\int v\,du.}
\]

For definite integrals,

\[
\boxed{\int_a^b u(t)v'(t)\,dt
=[u(t)v(t)]_a^b-\int_a^b u'(t)v(t)\,dt.}
\]

Choose \(u\) so that differentiating it simplifies the expression, and choose \(dv\) so its antiderivative is manageable.

### Worked example C — time-weighted discounting

For \(r\ne0\), let \(u=t\) and \(dv=e^{-rt}dt\). Then \(du=dt\) and \(v=-e^{-rt}/r\), so

\[
\int t e^{-rt}\,dt
=-\frac{te^{-rt}}r-\frac{e^{-rt}}{r^2}+C.
\]

Consequently,

\[
\boxed{\int_0^T t e^{-rt}\,dt
=\frac{1-e^{-rT}(1+rT)}{r^2}.}
\]

For \(r=0\), the integral is \(T^2/2\).

**Numerical caution:** The boxed expression subtracts nearly equal values for small \(|rT|\). A robust implementation should use a series near zero or stable numerical alternatives rather than rely on direct subtraction.

## 4. Stable time-weighted exponential integral

Define

\[
J(r,T)=\int_0^T t e^{-rt}\,dt.
\]

Write \(x=rT\). Then

\[
J(r,T)=T^2\phi(x),\qquad
\phi(x)=\int_0^1 s e^{-xs}\,ds.
\]

For \(x\ne0\),

\[
\phi(x)=\frac{1-(1+x)e^{-x}}{x^2}.
\]

Expanding the exponential gives a cancellation-resistant series near zero:

\[
\phi(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-x)^k}{k!(k+2)}
=\frac12-\frac{x}{3}+\frac{x^2}{8}-\frac{x^3}{30}
+\frac{x^4}{144}-\cdots.
\]

The companion Python implementation uses this series for sufficiently small \(|x|\), and the closed form elsewhere. Extreme parameter values may still overflow floating-point arithmetic and are rejected rather than silently reported.

## 5. Combining the methods: linear payment rate

Suppose the cash-flow rate is \(c(t)=a+bt\) and a flat continuous discount rate is \(r\). Its present value is

\[
PV=\int_0^T (a+bt)e^{-rt}\,dt.
\]

By linearity,

\[
PV=a I_0(r,T)+bJ(r,T),
\]

where

\[
I_0(r,T)=\int_0^T e^{-rt}\,dt
=\begin{cases}
-\operatorname{expm1}(-rT)/r,&r\ne0,\\
T,&r=0.
\end{cases}
\]

Thus

\[
\boxed{PV=a\frac{1-e^{-rT}}r
+b\frac{1-(1+rT)e^{-rT}}{r^2}}
\]

when \(r\ne0\), and \(PV=aT+bT^2/2\) when \(r=0\).

This formula describes a deterministic flat-rate model, not a claim about the correct market value of a security.

## 6. Worked financial example

Let \(c(t)=1000+100t\) currency/year, \(T=2\) years, and \(r=0.04\). Then

\[
PV=1000I_0(0.04,2)+100J(0.04,2).
\]

The undiscounted amount is

\[
\int_0^2(1000+100t)\,dt=2200.
\]

For positive rates and nonnegative cash-flow rates, the discounted value is smaller than or equal to undiscounted accumulation. The Python lab computes the exact formula and independently approximates the integral using midpoint quadrature.

## 7. Common mistakes

- Forgetting to change the bounds after substituting \(u=g(t)\).
- Applying integration by parts with the wrong sign.
- Confusing the rate \(c(t)\) with the total cash-flow amount.
- Treating the integration constant as part of a definite integral's answer.
- Dividing by \(r\) or \(g\) without handling the zero-rate case.
- Computing \(1-e^{-x}(1+x)\) directly for tiny \(x\), causing cancellation.
- Assuming every payment-rate stream is positive or that every discounted integral represents a market price.

## 9. Exercises with worked solutions

**1.** Evaluate \(\int e^{3t}\,dt\).

*Solution:* \(e^{3t}/3+C\).

**2.** Evaluate \(\int_0^2 500e^{0.02t}\,dt\).

*Solution:* \(500\operatorname{expm1}(0.04)/0.02\).

**3.** Evaluate \(\int_0^T e^{-rt}\,dt\) for \(r=0\).

*Solution:* \(T\).

**4.** Set \(u=2t+1\) in \(\int_0^1(2t+1)^2(2)\,dt\). What are the new bounds?

*Solution:* \(u=1\) and \(u=3\), so the integral is \(\int_1^3u^2du=26/3\).

**5.** Derive \(\int te^{-rt}\,dt\) for \(r\ne0\).

*Solution:* With \(u=t\), \(dv=e^{-rt}dt\), the result is \(-te^{-rt}/r-e^{-rt}/r^2+C\).

**6.** Evaluate \(\int_0^T t\,dt\).

*Solution:* \(T^2/2\).

**7.** Evaluate \(\int_0^T(100+20t)\,dt\).

*Solution:* \(100T+10T^2\).

**8.** Explain why \(J(r,T)\) is continuous at \(r=0\).

*Solution:* The integrand \(te^{-rt}\) approaches \(t\) uniformly on a bounded interval, so the integral approaches \(T^2/2\).

**9.** Find the first three terms of \(\phi(x)\) near zero.

*Solution:* \(\phi(x)=1/2-x/3+x^2/8+O(x^3)\).

**10.** What is the time dimension of \(J(r,T)\)?

*Solution:* Years squared, since \(t\,dt\) has units of years squared.

**11.** If \(c(t)=a+bt\) has units currency/year, what units must \(b\) have?

*Solution:* Currency/year squared, so \(bt\) has currency/year.

**12.** Give the present value formula for \(c(t)=a+bt\) under a flat continuous rate.

*Solution:* \(aI_0(r,T)+bJ(r,T)\).

**13.** Why is \`expm1\` preferable for small exponential changes?

*Solution:* It evaluates \(e^x-1\) accurately when subtracting 1 from a value near 1 would lose significant digits.

**14.** Differentiate \(-te^{-rt}/r-e^{-rt}/r^2\).

*Solution:* The derivative is \(te^{-rt}\), confirming the integration-by-parts result.
