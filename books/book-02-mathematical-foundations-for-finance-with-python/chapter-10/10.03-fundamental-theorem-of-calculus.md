## Learning objectives

Explain both parts of the Fundamental Theorem of Calculus (FTC); differentiate accumulation functions; evaluate definite integrals using antiderivatives; apply the chain rule to variable endpoints; and use analytical identities to validate cash-flow accumulation models.

## 1. Why the theorem matters

Section 10.1 constructed accumulation through Riemann sums. Section 10.2 introduced antiderivatives and definite integrals. The FTC connects these concepts: **differentiation and integration are inverse operations**, subject to appropriate assumptions.

For a continuous cash-flow rate \(c(t)\) in currency/year, define the net amount accumulated between a fixed starting date \(a\) and time \(t\):

\[
A(t)=\int_a^t c(u)\,du.
\]

The variable \(u\) is a dummy integration variable; the upper limit \(t\) is the time at which accumulation is measured.

## 2. FTC Part I — derivative of an accumulation function

If \(c\) is continuous on \([a,b]\), then

\[
\boxed{\frac{d}{dt}\int_a^t c(u)\,du=c(t)}
\]

for interior \(t\in(a,b)\), with appropriate one-sided derivatives at endpoints.

The difference quotient is

\[
\frac{A(t+h)-A(t)}h
=\frac1h\int_t^{t+h}c(u)\,du.
\]

As \(h\to0\), continuity makes the average rate on the short interval approach \(c(t)\). Thus the instantaneous rate of change of accumulated cash is the cash-flow rate itself.

**Financial interpretation:** If \(c(t)=600+50t\) dollars/year, the balance increment changes at \(600+50t\) dollars/year. The accumulated amount and its derivative have different units.

## 3. FTC Part II — evaluation by antiderivatives

If \(c\) is continuous on \([a,b]\) and \(F'(t)=c(t)\), then

\[
\boxed{\int_a^b c(t)\,dt=F(b)-F(a).}
\]

This evaluates the limit of Riemann sums without calculating infinitely many rectangles.

For \(c(t)=600+50t\), an antiderivative is \(F(t)=600t+25t^2\). Over \([0,3]\),

\[
\int_0^3(600+50t)\,dt
=[600t+25t^2]_0^3=1800+225=2025.
\]

The result is currency, not currency/year.

## 4. Initial balances and accumulated net flows

If the initial balance is \(B(a)=B_a\) and \(c(t)\) is the net cash-flow rate,

\[
B(t)=B_a+\int_a^t c(u)\,du,
\qquad B'(t)=c(t).
\]

For \(B(0)=1000\) and \(c(t)=600+50t\),

\[
B(3)=1000+2025=3025.
\]

This simple balance model excludes interest on the balance, fees, and other mechanisms. The equation is a cash-flow accounting identity under the stated assumptions.

## 5. Variable upper and lower endpoints

For differentiable \(g(t)\) and continuous \(c\),

\[
\frac{d}{dt}\int_a^{g(t)}c(u)\,du=c(g(t))g'(t).
\]

The extra factor \(g'(t)\) comes from the chain rule. For example,

\[
\frac{d}{dt}\int_0^{t^2}(1+u)\,du=(1+t^2)(2t).
\]

More generally, if \(a(t)\) and \(b(t)\) are differentiable,

\[
\boxed{\frac{d}{dt}\int_{a(t)}^{b(t)}c(u)\,du
=c(b(t))b'(t)-c(a(t))a'(t).}
\]

The minus sign for the lower endpoint is essential.

## 6. Accumulation from a changing start date

Let

\[
W(t)=\int_{t-1}^{t}c(u)\,du.
\]

This represents net accumulation over a rolling one-year window (when the rate is defined over the required time domain). Differentiating,

\[
W'(t)=c(t)-c(t-1).
\]

A rolling-window amount rises when the current payment rate exceeds the rate leaving the window. It need not rise merely because the underlying rate \(c(t)\) is positive.

## 7. Signed cash-flow rates

The FTC also applies when \(c(t)\) is negative. For \(c(t)=t-1\) over \([0,2]\),

\[
A(t)=\int_0^t(u-1)\,du=\frac{t^2}{2}-t,
\qquad A'(t)=t-1.
\]

Here \(A(2)=0\): early net outflows cancel later net inflows. A zero net amount does not imply zero gross activity.

## 8. Continuity and limitations

Continuity of \(c\) is a sufficient condition for the simple FTC Part I statement. If \(c\) has a jump discontinuity, the accumulated integral may remain continuous and be differentiable away from the jump, but its derivative need not exist at the jump.

For example, \(c(t)=0\) for \(t<1\) and \(c(t)=2\) for \(t\ge1\). Then \(A(t)=0\) for \(t\le1\) and \(A(t)=2(t-1)\) for \(t>1\). At \(t=1\), the left derivative is 0 and the right derivative is 2.

Avoid claiming differentiability at every time for discontinuous payment rates. In financial systems, contractual thresholds and discrete payments require careful modeling; an isolated lump-sum payment is not an ordinary continuous payment rate.

## 9. Independent numerical verification

The FTC provides two useful engineering checks:

1. **Integral check:** Compare \(F(b)-F(a)\) with a convergent numerical quadrature approximation.
2. **Derivative check:** Compare a central finite difference of \(A(t)\) with \(c(t)\) at smooth interior points.

Neither check establishes universal correctness by itself. Finite-difference step sizes must balance truncation error and floating-point cancellation; boundary and discontinuity cases need separate handling.

## 11. Exercises with worked solutions

**1.** Define \(A(t)=\int_0^t(3+2u)\,du\). Find \(A'(t)\).

*Solution:* \(A'(t)=3+2t\) by FTC Part I.

**2.** Evaluate \(A(4)\) in Exercise 1.

*Solution:* \([3u+u^2]_0^4=12+16=28\).

**3.** Find \(\int_1^3 4t\,dt\).

*Solution:* \([2t^2]_1^3=18-2=16\).

**4.** If \(B(0)=200\) and \(c(t)=100\), find \(B(2)\).

*Solution:* \(200+\int_0^2 100\,dt=400\).

**5.** Differentiate \(\int_0^{t^2}(1+u)\,du\).

*Solution:* \(2t(1+t^2)\).

**6.** Differentiate \(\int_t^{t+1}u^2\,du\).

*Solution:* \((t+1)^2-t^2=2t+1\).

**7.** Differentiate \(\int_{2t}^{3t}e^u\,du\).

*Solution:* \(3e^{3t}-2e^{2t}\).

**8.** Evaluate \(\int_0^3(600+50t)\,dt\).

*Solution:* \(1800+225=2025\).

**9.** If \(B(0)=1000\) and the rate is as in Exercise 8, find \(B(3)\).

*Solution:* \(1000+2025=3025\).

**10.** Let \(A(t)=\int_0^t(u-1)\,du\). Find \(A(2)\).

*Solution:* \([u^2/2-u]_0^2=0\).

**11.** Explain why \(A'(t)\) has units of currency/year when \(A(t)\) is in currency.

*Solution:* A time derivative divides the change in amount by elapsed years.

**12.** A payment rate jumps from 0 to 2 at \(t=1\). What are the one-sided derivatives of accumulation there?

*Solution:* Left derivative 0 and right derivative 2; the ordinary derivative does not exist at the jump.

**13.** Show that \(\int_a^b c+\int_b^d c=\int_a^d c\).

*Solution:* By FTC Part II, \([F(b)-F(a)]+[F(d)-F(b)]=F(d)-F(a)\).

**14.** Why is the derivative of a rolling one-year accumulation \(W(t)=\int_{t-1}^t c(u)\,du\) not simply \(c(t)\)?

*Solution:* Advancing the window adds new flow at rate \(c(t)\) and removes old flow at rate \(c(t-1)\), so \(W'=c(t)-c(t-1)\).
