## Learning objectives

Distinguish indefinite from definite integrals, identify antiderivatives and their constants, interpret definite integrals as signed accumulation, compute basic polynomial and exponential integrals, and check results through differentiation and independent numerical sums.

## 1. Two meanings of integration

An **indefinite integral** describes a family of functions whose derivatives equal a specified integrand:

\[
\boxed{\int f(t)\,dt=F(t)+C,\qquad F'(t)=f(t).}
\]

The constant \(C\) is necessary because the derivative of any constant is zero. For example,

\[
\int 2t\,dt=t^2+C.
\]

A **definite integral** returns a number (including units), not a family of functions:

\[
\boxed{\int_a^b f(t)\,dt.}
\]

For an integrable cash-flow rate \(c(t)\) measured in currency/year, \(\int_a^b c(t)\,dt\) is the signed net cash amount over the interval. The link between antiderivatives and definite integrals is the Fundamental Theorem of Calculus, developed systematically in Section 10.3.

## 2. Antiderivatives and the constant of integration

If \(F'=f\), then \(F+C\) is also an antiderivative for every constant \(C\). On a connected interval, any two antiderivatives of the same function differ by a constant.

For \(n\ne-1\),

\[
\boxed{\int t^n\,dt=\frac{t^{n+1}}{n+1}+C.}
\]

The exceptional case \(n=-1\) is

\[
\int\frac1t\,dt=\ln|t|+C
\]

on any interval not crossing zero. Do not apply the power rule with \(n=-1\).

Other basic antiderivatives include

\[
\int e^{kt}\,dt=\frac{e^{kt}}k+C\quad(k\ne0),
\qquad
\int e^{0t}\,dt=t+C.
\]

Always verify an antiderivative by differentiating it.

## 3. Definite integrals and signed area

If \(f(t)\ge0\) throughout \([a,b]\), its integral represents nonnegative accumulation. If \(f(t)<0\) over some intervals, the integral subtracts those contributions.

For example,

\[
\int_0^2(t-1)\,dt
=\left[\frac{t^2}2-t\right]_0^2=0.
\]

This does **not** mean the function is zero: the negative contribution on \([0,1]\) cancels the positive contribution on \([1,2]\). For financial cash flows, positive values may represent receipts and negative values payments.

The definite integral is oriented:

\[
\int_a^b f(t)\,dt=-\int_b^a f(t)\,dt,\qquad
\int_a^a f(t)\,dt=0.
\]

## 4. Fundamental properties

For integrable functions \(f,g\) and constants \(\alpha,\beta\),

\[
\int_a^b(\alpha f+\beta g)\,dt
=\alpha\int_a^b f\,dt+\beta\int_a^b g\,dt.
\]

For \(a\le c\le b\),

\[
\int_a^b f(t)\,dt=\int_a^c f(t)\,dt+\int_c^b f(t)\,dt.
\]

If \(f(t)\le g(t)\) throughout \([a,b]\), then

\[
\int_a^b f(t)\,dt\le\int_a^b g(t)\,dt.
\]

These rules are useful for combining cash-flow streams, splitting reporting periods, and constructing conservative bounds.

## 5. Worked example: a linear cash-flow rate

Suppose \(c(t)=100+20t\) currency units/year over \(0\le t\le2\). An antiderivative is

\[
F(t)=100t+10t^2+C.
\]

The definite accumulated amount is

\[
\int_0^2(100+20t)\,dt
=[100t+10t^2]_0^2=240.
\]

The constant \(C\) cancels when evaluating \(F(2)-F(0)\). This agrees with the midpoint Riemann sum in Section 10.1.

If the initial cash balance is \(B(0)=500\) and the rate represents net inflow, then the balance model is

\[
B(t)=500+\int_0^t c(u)\,du=500+100t+10t^2,
\]

so \(B(2)=740\). The integral itself is the **incremental** accumulation, not the starting balance.

## 6. Worked example: an exponential payment rate

For \(c(t)=500e^{0.03t}\) currency/year, the total undiscounted amount over two years is

\[
\int_0^2 500e^{0.03t}\,dt
=\frac{500}{0.03}(e^{0.06}-1).
\]

In numerical code, use \`math.expm1(0.06)\` to evaluate \(e^{0.06}-1\) accurately. The answer is positive and has units of currency.

For a constant rate \(c(t)=500\), the amount over two years is simply \(1000\).

## 7. Definite integral versus present value

An integral of a payment **rate** accumulates amounts over time. It is not automatically a present value. Under a deterministic flat continuous discount rate \(r\), a future payment-rate stream would instead be discounted using an integrand such as \(c(t)e^{-rt}\). Section 10.5 derives this carefully.

Do not confuse the discount rate \(r\) (per year) with the cash-flow rate \(c(t)\) (currency/year).

## 8. Numerical checks and interpretation

Analytical antiderivatives are useful when available. A midpoint Riemann sum gives an independent numerical approximation to a definite integral:

\[
M_n=\frac{b-a}{n}\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}
f\left(a+\left(i+\frac12\right)\frac{b-a}{n}\right).
\]

For sufficiently smooth functions, midpoint error typically decreases quadratically with the step width; no universal accuracy claim applies to arbitrary integrable functions. An independent check should compare the analytic result with numerical sums for several partition counts and test additivity across subintervals.

## 10. Exercises with worked solutions

**1.** Find \(\int 6t^2\,dt\).

*Solution:* \(2t^3+C\), because \((2t^3)'=6t^2\).

**2.** Find \(\int(100+20t)\,dt\).

*Solution:* \(100t+10t^2+C\).

**3.** Evaluate \(\int_0^2(100+20t)\,dt\).

*Solution:* \(200+40=240\).

**4.** Why is the constant of integration necessary?

*Solution:* Every function \(F+C\) has the same derivative as \(F\).

**5.** Evaluate \(\int_0^2(t-1)\,dt\).

*Solution:* Zero: negative and positive signed contributions cancel.

**6.** Evaluate \(\int_2^0 3t^2\,dt\).

*Solution:* \([t^3]_2^0=0-8=-8\).

**7.** Evaluate \(\int_1^1 e^t\,dt\).

*Solution:* Zero, because the interval has zero width.

**8.** Find \(\int e^{2t}\,dt\).

*Solution:* \(\tfrac12e^{2t}+C\).

**9.** Find \(\int 1/t\,dt\) on \(t>0\).

*Solution:* \(\ln t+C\).

**10.** A cash-flow rate is \(500\) currency/year for two years. Find its total amount.

*Solution:* \(\int_0^2 500\,dt=1000\).

**11.** If the initial balance is 500 and net inflow is \(100+20t\), find the balance at year 2.

*Solution:* \(500+240=740\).

**12.** Split \(\int_0^3 f(t)\,dt\) at \(t=1\).

*Solution:* \(\int_0^1 f(t)\,dt+\int_1^3 f(t)\,dt\).

**13.** Compute \(\int_0^1 t^2\,dt\).

*Solution:* \([t^3/3]_0^1=1/3\).

**14.** Why does \(\int_0^2 c(t)\,dt\) not generally equal present value?

*Solution:* It adds amounts without accounting for payment timing or discounting; present value needs a suitable discount factor.
