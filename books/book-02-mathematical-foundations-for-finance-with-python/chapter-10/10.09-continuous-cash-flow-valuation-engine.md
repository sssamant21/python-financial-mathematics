## Project brief

Build a transparent, standard-library Python valuation engine for deterministic continuous and discrete cash flows. The engine must support constant, linear, and exponential payment-rate streams; flat and affine instantaneous discount-rate curves; exact formulas when available; composite Simpson quadrature otherwise; irregular dated payments expressed as year fractions; parallel rate shifts; and reconciliation evidence.

**Scope boundary:** This is a teaching and analytical reference engine, not a production pricing library. It assumes deterministic cash flows and discount curves. No credit, liquidity, stochastic, calendar, market-calibration, tax, or accounting treatment is implied.

## 1. Contract and units

Let \(c(t)\) be a cash-flow rate (currency/year), \(t\) a year fraction, and \(q(t)\) a deterministic instantaneous discount rate (1/year). Define

\[
D(0,t)=\exp\left(-\int_0^t q(u)\,du\right),
\qquad
PV_{\mathrm{continuous}}=\int_0^T c(t)D(0,t)\,dt.
\]

For discrete amounts \(A_i\) (currency) at times \(t_i\),

\[
PV_{\mathrm{discrete}}=\sum_i A_iD(0,t_i).
\]

If both types occur, total PV is their sum. A continuous rate must **not** be entered as a discrete amount without multiplying by a time interval.

The engine takes all rate inputs as decimal continuously compounded annual rates. A 5% rate is entered as \`0.05\`, not \`5\`.

## 2. Model families

The implementation supports:

| Model | Payment rate \(c(t)\) | Parameters |
|---|---|---|
| Constant | \(C\) | Currency/year |
| Linear | \(a+bt\) | \(a\): currency/year; \(b\): currency/year² |
| Exponential | \(C_0e^{gt}\) | \(C_0\): currency/year; \(g\): 1/year |

Discount curves:

| Curve | Instantaneous rate \(q(t)\) | Discount factor \(D(0,t)\) |
|---|---|---|
| Flat | \(r\) | \(e^{-rt}\) |
| Affine | \(r_0+kt\) | \(e^{-r_0t-\frac12kt^2}\) |

The affine curve is a deterministic pedagogical rate path, not a fitted market term structure.

## 3. Architecture

The companion lab separates:

1. **Validated model definitions:** immutable dataclasses for cash-flow streams and curves.
2. **Discount-factor evaluation:** exact integrated rate for each supported curve.
3. **Analytical valuation:** closed forms for constant, linear, and exponential streams under flat discounting.
4. **Numerical valuation:** composite Simpson's rule for all supported streams and curves.
5. **Discrete-payment valuation:** individually discounted amounts at explicit nonnegative year fractions.
6. **Risk and reconciliation:** parallel rate-shift PV, central-difference sensitivity, and analytic-versus-numeric comparison.
7. **Audit output:** JSON-compatible reports including units, assumptions, tolerances, and validation status.

Use finite numeric inputs; reject invalid horizons, negative payment dates, nonpositive quadrature counts, and odd Simpson subinterval counts. Negative cash flows and negative discount rates are permitted: they may be economically meaningful and should not be silently clipped.

## 4. Exact formulas

For a flat discount rate \(r\), constant stream \(C\):

\[
PV_C=C\frac{1-e^{-rT}}r,\qquad r\ne0,
\]

with zero-rate limit \(CT\).

For exponential growth \(C_0e^{gt}\):

\[
PV_{\exp}=C_0\frac{e^{(g-r)T}-1}{g-r},\qquad g\ne r,
\]

with limit \(C_0T\) when \(g=r\).

For linear \(a+bt\):

\[
PV_{\mathrm{lin}}=aI_0(r,T)+bI_1(r,T),
\]

\[
I_0(r,T)=\int_0^T e^{-rt}dt,\qquad
I_1(r,T)=\int_0^T te^{-rt}dt.
\]

The implementation evaluates \(I_1\) with a near-zero series to avoid cancellation. For affine discount curves, the engine uses numerical integration rather than claiming a universal elementary antiderivative.

## 5. Reference scenario

Use a four-year horizon and these cash flows:

- Continuous constant rate: 1000 currency/year.
- Continuous linear rate: \(200+50t\) currency/year.
- Continuous exponential rate: \(150e^{0.02t}\) currency/year.
- Discrete payment: 500 currency at year 2.
- Flat discount curve: 5% continuously compounded.

The total value is

\[
PV_{\mathrm{total}}=
\int_0^4[1000+(200+50t)+150e^{0.02t}]e^{-0.05t}dt
+500e^{-0.10}.
\]

Evaluate each stream analytically and numerically, sum the components, and reconcile. Repeat under the affine rate curve \(q(t)=0.03+0.005t\), using Simpson's rule for the continuous streams and the corresponding affine discount factor for the discrete payment.

## 6. Numerical accuracy policy

For a smooth integrand, composite Simpson quadrature is formally fourth order:

\[
S_n=\frac h3\left[f(0)+f(T)+4\sum_{i\text{ odd}}f(ih)
+2\sum_{i\text{ even},\,0<i<n}f(ih)\right].
\]

The project compares \(S_n\) and \(S_{2n}\). A refinement-based error estimate is

\[
\widehat E_{2n}=\frac{|S_{2n}-S_n|}{15}.
\]

This estimate assumes the asymptotic smooth-error regime and does not prove accuracy by itself. For flat-rate models, compare against independent analytic formulas. For affine curves, record the refinement difference and label it as estimated numerical evidence, not a proof.

## 7. Parallel-shift sensitivity

For a baseline curve \(D(0,t)\), define a parallel continuous-rate shift \(h\):

\[
D_h(0,t)=D(0,t)e^{-ht}.
\]

A centered finite difference approximates the derivative:

\[
\frac{dPV}{dh}\bigg|_{h=0}
\approx\frac{PV(\delta)-PV(-\delta)}{2\delta}.
\]

For a regular stream, the exact first derivative is the negative time-weighted PV integral. The lab reports a finite-difference sensitivity and states the bump size. A 1 bp bump corresponds to \(0.0001\) in annual rate units.

## 9. Troubleshooting guide

| Symptom | Likely cause | Investigation |
|---|---|---|
| PV is about 100 times too small | Percent entered as whole number | Confirm 5% is \`0.05\` |
| PV increases with positive rate for positive flows | Discount sign reversed | Verify \(D=e^{-\int q}\) |
| Linear stream fails at zero rate | Division by \(r^2\) | Use continuous limit and series |
| Simpson rejects the grid | Odd subinterval count | Use even positive \(n\) |
| Analytic and numeric results disagree | Incorrect cash-flow units or discount curve | Print component-level assumptions |
| Large shifts overflow | Exponential outside float range | Reject nonfinite calculations and narrow scenario |
| PV differs from discrete schedule | Different payment timing assumptions | Compare actual payment dates |

## 10. Acceptance gates

**Functional:** correct PV for all three stream types and discrete payments; correct mixed total; affine-curve support; negative rates and signed payments; zero-horizon handling.

**Mathematical:** analytic flat-rate benchmarks; numerical reconciliation; stable \(r=0\) and \(r=g\) limits; consistent sensitivity sign for nonnegative cash flows.

**Engineering:** immutable model inputs, finite validation, explicit numerical resolution, deterministic JSON output, and no silent NaN/Inf.

**Governance:** distinguish source published, runtime validated, independently reviewed, and canonically approved. Only the first is established by publishing the files.

## 11. Exercises and worked answers

**1.** Define the PV of a continuous stream.  
*Answer:* \(\int_0^T c(t)D(0,t)dt\).

**2.** Define the PV of discrete payments.  
*Answer:* \(\sum_i A_iD(0,t_i)\).

**3.** What is the discount factor for a flat rate \(r\)?  
*Answer:* \(e^{-rt}\).

**4.** What is the discount factor for \(q(t)=r_0+kt\)?  
*Answer:* \(e^{-r_0t-kt^2/2}\).

**5.** Give the PV of constant \(C\) at zero rate.  
*Answer:* \(CT\).

**6.** Give the PV of exponential \(C_0e^{gt}\) when \(r=g\).  
*Answer:* \(C_0T\).

**7.** What is the zero-rate PV of \(a+bt\)?  
*Answer:* \(aT+bT^2/2\).

**8.** How is a discrete amount at time zero discounted?  
*Answer:* Its discount factor is one.

**9.** Why is an odd Simpson grid invalid?  
*Answer:* Composite Simpson panels require pairs of intervals.

**10.** What is the unit of a parallel-rate derivative of PV?  
*Answer:* Currency·years when rates have inverse-year units.

**11.** What is a 1 bp rate bump?  
*Answer:* \(0.0001\) per year.

**12.** How do you reconcile a mixed portfolio?  
*Answer:* Sum independently valued continuous components and discrete amounts.

**13.** Why does \(S_{2n}-S_n\) not prove accuracy?  
*Answer:* Both estimates may share bias or violate the assumed smooth-error regime.

**14.** What remains before production adoption?  
*Answer:* Runtime evidence, independent code and model review, calibration, controls, and operational acceptance.
