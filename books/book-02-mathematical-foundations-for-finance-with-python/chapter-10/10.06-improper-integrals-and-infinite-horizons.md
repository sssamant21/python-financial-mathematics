## Learning objectives

Define improper integrals using limits; distinguish convergence from divergence; derive constant and growing continuous perpetuities; quantify truncation error; handle singular endpoints; and validate infinite-horizon formulas with Python.

## 1. From finite horizons to perpetuities

A continuous payment-rate stream \(c(t)\), measured in currency/year, discounted at a continuously compounded rate \(r\) has finite-horizon present value

\[
PV(T)=\int_0^T c(t)e^{-rt}\,dt.
\]

For an infinite horizon, define the **improper integral** as a limit:

\[
\boxed{PV_\infty=\lim_{T\to\infty}\int_0^T c(t)e^{-rt}\,dt.}
\]

The infinite-horizon value exists as a finite real number only if the limit converges. Writing an infinity symbol as an integration bound does not establish convergence.

## 2. Constant-rate continuous perpetuity

For a constant nonzero payment rate \(c(t)=C\),

\[
PV(T)=
\begin{cases}
C(1-e^{-rT})/r,&r\ne0,\\
CT,&r=0.
\end{cases}
\]

If \(r>0\), then \(e^{-rT}\to0\), so

\[
\boxed{PV_\infty=\frac{C}{r}.}
\]

For \(C>0\), \(r=0\) gives unbounded linear accumulation, and \(r<0\) gives exponentially increasing accumulation. The finite perpetuity formula \(C/r\) must **not** be used for \(r\le0\).

**Example:** A stream paying \$1200/year indefinitely at \(r=0.06\) has \(PV_\infty=1200/0.06=\$20{,}000\). This is a mathematical model, not a guarantee that an actual payer will continue forever.

## 3. Growing continuous perpetuity

Suppose \(c(t)=C_0e^{gt}\) and discounting is flat at rate \(r\). Then

\[
PV_\infty=\int_0^\infty C_0e^{-(r-g)t}\,dt.
\]

Define the **net decay rate** \(\lambda=r-g\). For \(C_0>0\),

\[
\boxed{PV_\infty=\frac{C_0}{r-g}\quad\text{if and only if }r>g.}
\]

If \(r=g\), the discounted integrand is constant and the integral diverges linearly. If \(r<g\), it grows exponentially and diverges. For a signed or zero stream, analyze convergence of the actual integrand rather than applying positive-stream conclusions blindly.

**Example:** With \(C_0=1000\) currency/year, \(r=0.05\), and \(g=0.02\), the value is \(1000/0.03=33{,}333.33\) currency units (rounded). Growth approaching the discount rate makes the value highly sensitive to model assumptions.

## 4. Truncation and residual tail value

A numerical model evaluates only a finite horizon \(T\). For an exponentially growing stream with \(\lambda=r-g>0\),

\[
PV(T)=\frac{C_0(1-e^{-\lambda T})}{\lambda}.
\]

The omitted tail is

\[
\boxed{R(T)=PV_\infty-PV(T)=\frac{C_0e^{-\lambda T}}{\lambda}.}
\]

For positive \(C_0\), the **fraction of total value** beyond \(T\) is \(e^{-\lambda T}\). To achieve a tail fraction no greater than \(\varepsilon\in(0,1)\), choose

\[
\boxed{T\ge\frac{\ln(1/\varepsilon)}{\lambda}.}
\]

For \(\lambda=0.03\) and a 1% tail target, \(T\ge\ln(100)/0.03\approx153.5\) years. This strikingly long horizon illustrates why near-critical perpetuities require scrutiny.

## 5. Convergence tests and comparison

If a nonnegative discounted cash-flow integrand satisfies

\[
0\le c(t)D(0,t)\le M e^{-\lambda t}
\]

for all sufficiently large \(t\), with \(M\ge0\) and \(\lambda>0\), its improper integral converges by comparison. Conversely, if a nonnegative integrand stays above a positive constant eventually, the integral diverges.

A more general tail may behave like \(t^{-p}\). The benchmark

\[
\int_1^\infty t^{-p}\,dt
\]

converges precisely when \(p>1\), and then equals \(1/(p-1)\). This matters for slowly decaying payment or discount patterns that are not exponential.

## 6. Improper integrals at finite endpoints

An integral can also be improper because the integrand is unbounded at an endpoint. For example,

\[
\int_0^1 t^{-1/2}\,dt
=\lim_{\epsilon\downarrow0}\int_\epsilon^1 t^{-1/2}\,dt=2.
\]

But

\[
\int_0^1\frac{1}{t}\,dt
\]

diverges. A singularity is not automatically fatal: the rate of divergence near the endpoint determines integrability.

If singularities occur inside an interval, evaluate each side separately; do not let cancellation of divergent one-sided contributions masquerade as convergence.

## 7. Sensitivity and model risk

For a positive growing perpetuity,

\[
PV_\infty=\frac{C_0}{r-g},\qquad
\frac{\partial PV_\infty}{\partial r}=-\frac{C_0}{(r-g)^2},\qquad
\frac{\partial PV_\infty}{\partial g}=\frac{C_0}{(r-g)^2}.
\]

As \(r-g\to0^+\), both value and sensitivity become large. A finite numerical result at a large truncation horizon is **not** proof that the infinite integral converges.

Real cash flows may terminate, default, change regime, or become capacity-constrained. Infinite-horizon valuation is therefore an assumption-heavy extrapolation, not an unconditional forecast.

## 8. Numerical reliability

For small positive \(x=\lambda T\), use \`-math.expm1(-x)\` rather than \`1-math.exp(-x)\` to avoid cancellation. Compute tail value as \(C_0e^{-x}/\lambda\) only after checking \(\lambda>0\). For enormous \(x\), underflow of \(e^{-x}\) to zero is expected and may be appropriate for a negligible tail, but it should not be mistaken for an exact mathematical zero.

Validate rates, payment scales, horizons, and target tolerances as finite numbers. A zero payment rate has zero integral regardless of the discount or growth rates; the implementation explicitly handles this degenerate case.

## 10. Exercises with solutions

**1.** Define an improper integral over \([0,\infty)\).

*Solution:* It is the limit of the finite-interval integrals as the upper endpoint tends to infinity, if the limit exists.

**2.** Evaluate a continuous perpetuity with \(C=600\) and \(r=0.03\).

*Solution:* \(600/0.03=20{,}000\).

**3.** Does a positive constant payment stream converge at \(r=0\)?

*Solution:* No; accumulated PV grows linearly with \(T\).

**4.** Does \(C_0e^{gt}\) discounted at \(r\) converge when \(g=r\)?

*Solution:* No, for \(C_0>0\); the discounted integrand is constant.

**5.** Find the growing perpetuity value for \(C_0=500\), \(r=0.08\), \(g=0.03\).

*Solution:* \(500/0.05=10{,}000\).

**6.** State the residual tail after time \(T\) for a convergent growing perpetuity.

*Solution:* \(C_0e^{-(r-g)T}/(r-g)\).

**7.** What fraction of total value remains beyond \(T\)?

*Solution:* \(e^{-(r-g)T}\), for a positive exponential stream.

**8.** Find a horizon giving at most 5% tail fraction when \(r-g=0.05\).

*Solution:* \(T\ge\ln(20)/0.05\approx59.91\) years.

**9.** Does \(\int_1^\infty t^{-2}\,dt\) converge?

*Solution:* Yes; its value is 1.

**10.** Does \(\int_1^\infty t^{-1}\,dt\) converge?

*Solution:* No; it diverges logarithmically.

**11.** Evaluate \(\int_0^1t^{-1/2}\,dt\).

*Solution:* 2.

**12.** Explain why \(\int_0^1t^{-1}\,dt\) diverges.

*Solution:* \(\ln t\to-\infty\) as \(t\downarrow0\), so the one-sided limit is infinite.

**13.** Differentiate \(C_0/(r-g)\) with respect to \(r\).

*Solution:* \(-C_0/(r-g)^2\).

**14.** Why can a long-horizon numerical integral be misleading?

*Solution:* A finite truncation can look stable even when the true infinite-horizon limit diverges or its omitted tail remains material.
