## Chapter-wide learning outcomes

This final section assesses integration and accumulation; analytic and numerical present value; deterministic discount curves; convergence and improper integrals; payment timing; and sensitivity analysis. All rates are continuously compounded annual decimals, times are in years, payment rates are currency/year, and discrete payments are currency.

## Part A — Integration fundamentals

### Exercise 1 — Accumulation

A payment rate is \(c(t)=400+60t\) currency/year over three years. Find the undiscounted accumulated amount.

**Solution.**

\[
\int_0^3(400+60t)\,dt=[400t+30t^2]_0^3=1200+270=\boxed{1470}.
\]

### Exercise 2 — Fundamental theorem

Let \(F(T)=\int_0^T(200+10t^2)\,dt\). Find \(F'(T)\) and \(F(2)\).

**Solution.** By the fundamental theorem, \(F'(T)=200+10T^2\). Also,

\[
F(2)=400+\frac{10(8)}3=\boxed{\frac{1280}{3}}.
\]

### Exercise 3 — Substitution

Evaluate \(\int_0^2 2t e^{-t^2}\,dt\).

**Solution.** With \(u=t^2\), \(du=2t\,dt\), giving

\[
\int_0^4e^{-u}du=\boxed{1-e^{-4}}.
\]

### Exercise 4 — Integration by parts

Evaluate \(\int_0^T te^{-rt}\,dt\) for \(r\ne0\), and give its zero-rate limit.

**Solution.**

\[
\int_0^Tte^{-rt}dt=\frac{1-(1+rT)e^{-rT}}{r^2};
\qquad \lim_{r\to0}\int_0^Tte^{-rt}dt=\boxed{T^2/2}.
\]

Use a stable series near \(rT=0\) in numerical code.

## Part B — Continuous cash-flow valuation

### Exercise 5 — Level stream

A payment rate of 1500 currency/year lasts four years at \(r=0.04\). Find its PV.

**Solution.**

\[
PV=1500\frac{1-e^{-0.16}}{0.04}.
\]

The nominal undiscounted amount is 6000, and the PV is lower because \(r>0\).

### Exercise 6 — Growing stream

A rate \(c(t)=800e^{0.025t}\) is discounted at 5% over five years. Find its PV.

**Solution.**

\[
PV=800\frac{1-e^{-0.025(5)}}{0.025}.
\]

### Exercise 7 — Matching growth and discount

Find the five-year PV of \(c(t)=800e^{0.05t}\) when \(r=0.05\).

**Solution.** Growth and discount cancel: \(PV=800(5)=\boxed{4000}\).

### Exercise 8 — Linear stream

A stream \(c(t)=1000+100t\) is paid over two years at zero discount. Find its PV.

**Solution.**

\[
PV=\int_0^2(1000+100t)\,dt=2000+200=\boxed{2200}.
\]

### Exercise 9 — Affine instantaneous discount rate

Given \(q(t)=0.03+0.004t\), find \(D(0,t)\) and write the PV of \(c(t)=900\) over three years.

**Solution.**

\[
D(0,t)=e^{-0.03t-0.002t^2},\qquad
PV=\int_0^3 900e^{-0.03t-0.002t^2}dt.
\]

Numerical quadrature is appropriate.

## Part C — Improper integrals

### Exercise 10 — Level perpetuity

Find the value of a positive constant stream \(C=900\) at \(r=0.045\) for an infinite horizon.

**Solution.** Because \(r>0\),

\[
PV_\infty=\frac{900}{0.045}=\boxed{20{,}000}.
\]

### Exercise 11 — Growing perpetuity

For \(C_0=1200\), \(r=0.06\), and \(g=0.02\), find the infinite-horizon PV.

**Solution.** Since \(r-g=0.04>0\),

\[
PV_\infty=\frac{1200}{0.04}=\boxed{30{,}000}.
\]

### Exercise 12 — Divergence boundary

Does a positive growing stream with \(g=0.06\) and \(r=0.06\) have finite perpetuity value?

**Solution.** No. The discounted rate is constant, so its improper integral diverges.

### Exercise 13 — Tail control

For net decay \(\lambda=0.04\), find a horizon that leaves at most 1% of the total positive perpetuity PV beyond that horizon.

**Solution.**

\[
e^{-0.04T}\le0.01
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
T\ge\frac{\ln100}{0.04}\approx\boxed{115.13\text{ years}}.
\]

### Exercise 14 — Power-law integrability

For which \(p\) does \(\int_1^\infty t^{-p}dt\) converge?

**Solution.** Exactly when \(p>1\); then the integral is \(1/(p-1)\).

## Part D — Numerical quadrature

### Exercise 15 — Three rules

Evaluate \(\int_0^1t^2dt\) exactly, and approximate it with two subintervals using midpoint, trapezoidal, and Simpson rules.

**Solution.** Exact: \(1/3\). Midpoint: \(0.3125\). Trapezoidal: \(0.375\). Simpson: \(1/3\).

### Exercise 16 — Refinement

For a smooth function, a Simpson estimate changes from \(S_n\) to \(S_{2n}\). Give the standard asymptotic estimate of the fine-grid error.

**Solution.**

\[
\widehat E_{2n}=\frac{|S_{2n}-S_n|}{15}.
\]

This is an estimate assuming fourth-order asymptotic behavior, not a rigorous universal bound.

### Exercise 17 — Invalid quadrature setup

Why is \(n=15\) invalid for composite Simpson's rule?

**Solution.** Simpson panels each span two subintervals, requiring positive even \(n\).

## Part E — Payment timing and sensitivities

### Exercise 18 — Discrete schedule

Two payments of 500 currency occur at years 1 and 2, discounted at \(r=0.05\). Find their PV.

**Solution.**

\[
PV=500e^{-0.05}+500e^{-0.10}.
\]

### Exercise 19 — Timing order

For positive payments and \(r>0\), order beginning-, midpoint-, and end-of-interval payments by PV.

**Solution.**

\[
PV_{\mathrm{begin}}>PV_{\mathrm{mid}}>PV_{\mathrm{end}}.
\]

All converge to the corresponding continuous-stream PV as interval width approaches zero with nominal total held fixed.

### Exercise 20 — Rate derivative

For a nonnegative deterministic payment rate \(c(t)\), show the sign of the first derivative of PV with respect to a flat discount rate.

**Solution.**

\[
\frac{dPV}{dr}=-\int_0^Tt\,c(t)e^{-rt}dt\le0.
\]

It is strictly negative when positive cash flows occur at positive times on a set of positive measure.

## Part F — Applied integrated case

An analyst values the following deterministic four-year cash-flow portfolio:

- Constant stream: 1000 currency/year.
- Linear stream: \(200+50t\) currency/year.
- Exponential stream: \(150e^{0.02t}\) currency/year.
- Discrete payment: 500 currency at year 2.
- Base curve: flat \(r=0.05\).

**Task 1 — Model.** Write the total PV as a sum of three integrals and one discounted discrete payment.

**Solution.**

\[
PV=\int_0^4(1000+200+50t+150e^{0.02t})e^{-0.05t}dt
+500e^{-0.10}.
\]

**Task 2 — Analytic decomposition.** Define \(I_0(r,T)=\int_0^T e^{-rt}dt\) and \(I_1(r,T)=\int_0^T te^{-rt}dt\). Express the exact value.

**Solution.**

\[
PV=1200I_0(0.05,4)+50I_1(0.05,4)
+150\frac{1-e^{-0.03(4)}}{0.03}+500e^{-0.10}.
\]

**Task 3 — Numerical reconciliation.** Compute Simpson estimates at \(n=128,256,512\), compare against the exact expression, and document the maximum absolute discrepancy.

**Solution approach.** Apply composite Simpson's rule to each continuous stream, sum with the exactly discounted discrete amount, and calculate \(|PV_{\mathrm{Simpson}}-PV_{\mathrm{analytic}}|\). The companion Python lab provides an executable report.

**Task 4 — Term-structure scenario.** Replace the flat curve with \(q(t)=0.03+0.005t\). Write the total PV.

**Solution.**

\[
PV=\int_0^4(1200+50t+150e^{0.02t})
e^{-0.03t-0.0025t^2}dt
+500e^{-0.03(2)-0.0025(2)^2}.
\]

Use numerical quadrature for the continuous component.

**Task 5 — Sensitivity.** Apply a parallel continuously compounded shift \(h\) and derive the first derivative at \(h=0\).

**Solution.**

\[
\frac{dPV}{dh}\bigg|_{h=0}
=-\int_0^4 t(1200+50t+150e^{0.02t})D(0,t)\,dt
-2(500)D(0,2).
\]

**Task 6 — Model governance.** Identify three reasons the result should not be treated as an independently approved market valuation.

**Solution.** The cash flows and discount curves are assumed rather than calibrated; default/termination and liquidity are omitted; and the implementation lacks independent review and runtime certification evidence.
